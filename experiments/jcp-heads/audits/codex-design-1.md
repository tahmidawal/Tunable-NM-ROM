4. **Head Q1 adaptation — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Differentiating the weighted residual with respect to z is correct, as is the simplified Jacobian \((A+\Delta t\,b_0S\,\partial_cN)\partial_z\tilde h\); it follows the cancellation already used in `Q/p1core.py:139–151`. Rebuilding history and using fresh autodiff Jacobians faithfully implements COMMON-BRIEF §3, and tightening the IC fit is an improvement (`D:125–141`). However, the design does not establish that its “tight” anchors actually satisfy their requested tighter stationarity tolerances: finite outputs and absence of reason 3 still allow budget and tiny-step exits (`hfast.py:95–105`; `Q/p1common.py:294–310`). Two identically stalled anchors can agree. Furthermore, production IC stationarity is merely descriptive, and no explicit IC failure veto is stated. Require independently checked anchor termination/stationarity, explicit invalid-IC handling, and a registered response when an anchor cannot be verified. **Yes, a head can pass these checks while giving a wrong physical trajectory:** a collapsed or rank-deficient head can have \(J_z^\mathsf Tr=0\) at a large residual, with production/tight/tighter all remaining in the same bad basin. That is a limitation of local stationarity—not evidence that z-certification is mathematically wrong. Keep “qualified” distinct from accurate or globally minimized, and add a degeneracy control.

5. **Head Q2 adaptation and rule selection — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Evaluating both rules on the reached coefficients \(\tilde h(z)\), checking successor rollout distance, and choosing a common rule that passes all three seeds are sound (`D:143–159`). Selection excludes E0 error, so it is independent of **reference-accuracy scoring**, although it remains validation-conditioned and solver-dependent. There is a concrete ladder defect: Gauss-192 is selectable, but its successor is 256 and its required second successor is absent (`D:145–150,157–158`). Add a check rule or explicitly limit selectable rungs. State whether selection requires the finer-rule convergence check and valid successor solves, not merely the two q→q⁺ bars; the qualified pipeline distinguishes fail from unverified (`Q/qscore.py:199–212`). Explicitly inherit the separate Fibonacci successor chain and Gauss-160 check sequence for the larger linear rungs (`Q/DESIGN.md:101–104`). Finally, newly minimizing quadrature cost for heads while freezing potentially oversized linear rules can favor heads in the cost comparison. That is acceptable as a **registered deployment-policy comparison**, not a best-achievable head-versus-linear claim. A common rule-selection policy or sensitivity panel is needed for the latter. Sharing a quadrature rule also does not make seeds share a cost: their LM iteration counts and IC fits differ.

6. **M=4K and M=4r — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** This is a defensible deployment policy and matches the incumbent K=16, M=64 head (`D:54–57`; `qcore.py:56–57`). At matched dimension r=K, it also gives both methods the same test-space size. Across K, however, the experiment changes the objective’s test modes as well as the manifold dimension; rule size, solver conditioning, and solution branch may change too. Consequently, an improvement cannot be attributed solely to a larger latent space. Register a small **fixed-M sensitivity**, for example M=128 across all four K values, with one explicit mode list and fresh Q1/Q2; keep it clearly separate from the deployment-policy panel. If this is outside scope, narrow every K claim to “the M=4K deployment policy.” The general campaign already recognizes this identification problem through fixed-M and factorial panels (`PLAN-v2.md:84–92`). Also, `D:57` incorrectly predicts a nonempty mode-set symmetric difference at 4096²: the completed P1 finding was a permutation-only difference there, with set changes at 256². Compute and report the difference rather than asserting it in advance.

7. **Statistical procedures and F5 — WRONG.** Several registered rules conflict or leave consequential choices open. The pooled error is a median over qualified seeds, but equal-cost selection uses seed 0, which may itself be unqualified (`D:182–183,223–224`); this can combine one population’s accuracy with another model’s cost. “More accurate” and “matches” can both hold: a consistent resolved 5% improvement can reject zero while lying comfortably inside the 10% equivalence margin (`D:205–209`). Freeze precedence or retain separate significance/equivalence flags. The stated raw \(d_i\pm\delta_i\) Wilcoxon tests also differ from the inherited implementation’s normalized \(d_i/\delta_i\pm1\) tests (`Q/p1stats.py:68–73,93–95`). H-acc-K calls coverage below 50% a “tie,” whereas inherited AM-7 treats it as inconclusive; its median population and unqualified precedence also need definition (`D:187–191`; `Q/p1stats.py:129–161`). The cost bands themselves are sensible, but **largest r with median ratio≤1.1 is an at-most-budget rule, not equal cost**: it permits arbitrarily cheaper comparators, assumes no accuracy regressions across r, omits the no-affordable-rung case, and does not explicitly restrict selection to qualified rungs. R384 need not be the most accurate affordable rung. Add role qualification using the declared interval band, bracket failures, and an explicit qualified comparator policy. “Matches + equal cost” also lacks an explicit final label in the dominance procedure. Finally, F5’s any-K success needs a multiplicity policy or an exploratory label; “not linear dominates” permits unresolved comparisons, while nonqualification or incomplete work can trigger a scientific negative (`D:230–234`). Report paired-error superiority as such: worst error is only displayed, so this is not dominance over both median and worst accuracy.

The strongest concrete defect is the driver-identity control (`D:102–105,258`): unchanged `base` uses `d["Z_ck"]`, not the incumbent’s emitted `Z_tr` (`S/sep_hfit_run.py:157–160,365–376`). The under-trained head is a scientific sensitivity probe: its oracle is not guaranteed to be 1.5× worse, and the planned smoke does not include the full-budget comparator needed to establish that predicate (`D:106–108,269–271`). The dominance synthetic is mathematically wrong: exactly 10% costlier lies at 1.1, which is inside the equal-cost band, not the strictly costlier category; a sufficiently resolved accuracy improvement therefore does not necessarily yield “neither” (`D:212–216,262`). The reproduction, Jacobian, zero-update, under-resolved-rule, disjointness, and identity-swap checks are reasonable candidates, but their head-specific real-data outcomes have not been demonstrated. Require valid successor evidence for the Gauss-8→16 control. **H-cost-N lacks its own must-fail classification control**: detecting reference drift or a changed identity does not show that a stable but non-flat mesh-cost panel is classified correctly. Q3 also omits the inherited rank-deficient fixture. Add explicit controls for equal-cost rejection, overlapping error labels, unqualified seed 0, empty populations, and missing ladder successors.
