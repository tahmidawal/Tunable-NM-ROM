### 2.1 The latent codes: not collapsed, not extrapolated — but the chart is unstructured

From `analyze_a.json` (training codes `Z_tr` from the checkpoint; oracle codes `Z` from `oracle_N64.npz` / `oracle_N256.npz`):

| dims for 90 / 99 % energy of the head image at oracle codes | 11 / 32 | 13 / 39 | 6 / 15 | 12 / 30 | — |

Reading.
- **No auto-decoder collapse**: the codes fill all K directions (participation ratio ≈ K; singular values flat 1.0 → 0.8), the head's Jacobian has full rank 16/32 at every oracle code and 13/23 directions above 10 % of the leading one; ns303's manifold is locally 6–10-dimensional (its family has 7 intrinsic dims + t), ns203's 12–13 (13 + t). The head is using its latent dimensions.
- **No extrapolation in z**: every held-out oracle code sits *inside* the training cloud (Mahalanobis median 2.4–3.8 against a training median of 3.2–4.5; none outside the per-PC box), and its nearest training code is *closer* (0.66–0.84×) than training codes of different trajectories are to each other. The held-out fits land between training trajectories, where the surface is defined only by the MLP's interpolation.
- **Non-identifiability without harm**: in 45–70 % of states the 8-start LM ends at a different code than the single start, at *identical* error (ratio 1.000) — the head is many-to-one on flat valleys, but this does not cost accuracy.
- **The chart is unstructured**: the true parameters explain only 9–26 % of the code variance under any polynomial degree (deg 3 overfits to R² < 0), and the codes explain 25–49 % of the parameters. Nearby parameters do not get nearby codes. Within a trajectory the codes are continuous (step 0.13–0.46 vs 0.5–1.0 to the nearest other trajectory), so the training set is, in latent space, **512 separate smooth curves placed arbitrarily** in a 16-dim ball. The MLP is asked to interpolate between them. (Burgers column: on Burgers the *same* auto-decoder recipe does learn a structured chart — the codes occupy ≈ 6 effective dimensions (5 parameters + t; participation ratio 6.1 of 16, singular values falling to 0.22), the parameters explain 75 % of the code variance, and trajectories sit 0.31 apart against a within-trajectory step of 0.23, so the training curves touch their neighbours. Whether the structured chart is a cause or a consequence of the dense sampling is settled in §2.5: it is a consequence.)

### 2.2 The held-out residual: driven by the state's nonlinear content, not by distance to the data

From `analyze_a.json` / `analyze_b.json` (ns203 unless stated):

| statistic | ns203 (K16) | ns204 (K32) |
|---|---|---|
| corr(per-state oracle error, per-state POD-K error) | **0.94** | **0.99** |
| corr(per-state residual norm, distance to the nearest training state at the same time) | 0.06 | 0.06 |
| corr(case log ν, case worst oracle error) | −0.77 | −0.84 |
| CV R² of the whitened residual `c − h(z*)` against (params, t), ridge deg 1 / 2 (8 folds over cases) | −0.10 / −0.36 | −0.08 / −0.30 |
| residual energy inside the top-16 / 64 / 128 directions of the head's *own* training image | 16 / 78 / 95 % | 2 / 51 / 84 % |
| error of the head's **linear skip subspace alone** (K-dim linear, from `h_lin`) | 0.547 | 0.372 |
| best K-dim linear subspace inside the bank span (POD-K in bank coordinates) | 0.239 | 0.141 |
| per-time ratio POD-K / oracle, t = 0, 0.2, …, 1 | 1.63, 1.37, 1.10, 1.08, 1.08, 1.17 | 0.83, 1.15, 1.18, 1.09, 1.12, 1.16 |

Reading.
- The head fails on exactly the states where the linear POD-K fails (r = 0.94–0.99) and at the same rate (ratio 1.08–1.18 on evolved times); low-ν (high-Re) cases are worst for both. The head's residual is **not** a smooth function of the parameters (CV R² < 0 with 64 held-out cases) and is uncorrelated with how far the state is from the training set (r = 0.06), i.e. it is not "far from data → bad"; it is "more non-linear content → bad", *for every state alike*.
- The residual lies mostly (78–95 %) inside the span the head can already produce: the failure is interpolation *within* the head's range, not a missing direction. The correction ladder (ns304) confirms this from the other side — the residual directions `C_q` close the gap only at q = R.
- The head's linear part is poor (0.55 vs the 0.24 optimum): the MLP carries the fit, and at t = 0 — where the family is an exact 12-dim linear subspace — the K=32 head is *worse* than POD-32 (0.83) because a free-code MLP has no reason to reproduce a linear subspace exactly.

A smooth interpolant that *knows the parameters* is 2× worse than plain POD-16 and 2.2× worse than the auto-decoder head. The 13-dim parameter → state map is not learnable from 282–512 samples by generic smooth regression; the free-code head does *better* than the true chart because it is free to reuse the near-linear structure of the family. (Burgers control, same script logic on 131 072 states of a 5-dim family: 6.1EG.) This is direct evidence against "the head fails because its chart is unstructured": giving it the perfect chart makes it worse.

The quadratic term explains essentially nothing of the residual beyond POD-K on held-out states (and, at t = 0, makes it worse: 0.19 vs 0.06). The non-POD-K content of a held-out NS state is **not a low-order function of its dominant coordinates**, even with ν and t supplied: states with similar low-mode content have different harmonic content depending on their history. A quadratic manifold — the natural "exploit the degree-2 residual" design — is predicted to fail the 2× bar on this family. On Burgers the same construction *does* pay (1.8× quadratic, 4× cubic over the linear-16 floor): there the manifold is a graph over its dominant coordinates; on decaying NS it is not.

### 2.7 Translation alignment (symmetry reduction)

`analyze_c2.py` (the corrected spectral shift; `analyze_c.py`'s alignment had a missing 2π and is superseded — its raw-POD quadratic rows are still valid). Every field, training and held-out, is translated on the torus so that the Fourier phases of modes (1,0) and (0,1) vanish (exact spectral shift; residual phase 1e-16; the 5th-percentile relative amplitude of those modes is 0.09, so the phase is well defined for ≥ 95 % of states). |---|---|---|---|
| POD-14 (= 16 online unknowns with the 2 shifts) | — | 0.197 | 1.21× vs raw POD-16 |

It does not, by itself, reach 2×, and the quadratic manifold still adds nothing on aligned coordinates.

## 3. Hypotheses, ranked, each with a discriminating test (≤ 1 cluster job or ≤ 15 min local) and a predicted outcome

**H1 — the family is under-sampled per intrinsic dimension and only weakly nonlinear, so no data-driven K-dimensional manifold of any function class can beat POD-K by 2× at this data budget (binding).**
The 12-amplitude family has 13 intrinsic dimensions plus time; 512 trajectories cover it at 1.6 samples per axis, and the nearest training state to a held-out state is 60 % of its norm away (Burgers: 9 %). The state's non-linear content (what POD-K misses) is not predictable from its dominant coordinates (quadratic/cubic manifolds: 1.03× POD-K), nor from the parameters (poly/KRR: 0.5× POD-K), nor from the free-code MLP (1.15–1.19×). The ratio stays 1.2–1.5 under 4× data and under an 8-dim family because both the head and POD improve together — POD-K is a *global average* that 13 k snapshots determine well, a nonlinear manifold needs *local* neighbours that do not exist.
- *Test (1 job, or local on the frozen ns203 bank in whitened space, < 15 min):* the **exact-symmetry augmentation** control. Augment the 512 training trajectories by the D4 group (8 rotations/reflections of the torus, exact for the periodic FOM) and by random translations (exact spectral shifts) — 8× and continuous extra coverage in the 2 translation + 1 discrete directions **at zero data cost** — and retrain the head only (ns301 `plain`, n = 512). Prediction under H1: the held-out oracle improves by the coverage gain in *those* directions only, i.e. ≈ 10–20 % (ratio 1.19 → ≈ 1.35–1.45), still failing; POD-16 of the augmented set is unchanged to < 2 %. If instead the ratio jumps past 2, H1 is wrong and the head simply lacked neighbours.
- *Companion prediction already scored:* ns302 (4× data) was predicted by §A10 at 1.25–1.35 and landed at 1.46; under H1 the next doubling is worth < 5 %.

**H2 — the auto-decoder chart is unstructured (nearby parameters get unrelated codes), so the head interpolates arbitrarily between memorised trajectories; a parameter-conditioned / encoder-regularised chart would fix it (ranked second, but predicted false).**
The chart *is* unstructured (R² 0.1–0.26; the same recipe on Burgers reaches 0.75 with codes occupying ≈ 6 effective dimensions, §2.1), but §2.5 shows that the perfectly structured chart (the true parameters) interpolates 2.2× *worse* than the free codes, and Burgers' `paramcodes` arm (codes fixed at (μ, t)) was 2× worse than free codes there too.
- *Test (< 15 min local, whitened space on the frozen ns203 bank; or 1 job):* train the ns301 `plain` head with **codes frozen at the standardised (a/U_rms, b/U_rms, log ν, t)** (K = 14, no code optimisation) and, as a second arm, with an **encoder** `E(u) → z` (a linear POD-32 read-out followed by a 2-layer MLP) trained jointly. Prediction: fixed-parameter codes give a held-out oracle ≥ 0.30 (worse than POD-16, as KRR was), the encoder arm lands within ±10 % of the auto-decoder (0.18–0.22). Either result rules H2 out as the binding cause; only a held-out oracle ≤ 0.12 would rescue it.

**H3 — head capacity and the fixed 100 k-step full-batch recipe under-fit as the data grows; a wider head with a POD-initialised linear skip and a longer, mini-batched schedule would convert ns302's 16.7 % into the 12 % the bar needs (ranked third, worth ≈ 1.2×).**
The training fit degrades 2.4 → 6.2 → 12.4 % as trajectories go 128 → 512 → 2048 at fixed head and steps; the head's linear skip alone is at 55 % against the 24 % linear optimum; at t = 0 the K = 32 head is worse than POD-32. Against: ns301's small/regularised heads and HFIT's wide/deep/huge heads move the held-out number by ≤ 2× on both PDEs, and ns303 (train fit 1.4 %) keeps the ratio at 1.4.
- *Test (1 A100 job, ≈ 8 h):* ns302's data (2 048 trajectories) with head 1024×3, `h_lin` initialised to the POD-16-in-bank-span map (so the head starts *at* POD-16), 300 k steps at batch 8 192 with the Burgers stage-C `snap` loss and EMA. Prediction: training recon 4–6 %, held-out oracle 0.14–0.15, ratio 1.6–1.75 — the best this head class will do on this family, and still a FAIL at 2.0.

Rejected on the evidence (not ranked): bank floor, LM oracle quality, auto-decoder collapse, code non-identifiability (§2.8).

## 4. Recommended design and its pre-registration

**What the evidence says a "better head" cannot do.** Every interpolant tested on the family's own coordinates — free-code MLP (1.15–1.19×), parameter-conditioned regression (0.5×), quadratic/cubic manifold on POD coordinates with or without (ν, t) (≤ 1.03×), 4× the data (1.46×), an 8-dim sub-family (1.39×) — sits at or below 1.5× POD-K. The 2× bar is a statement about the family's *Kolmogorov width relative to its sampling*: on decaying 2D NS from six low modes at Re 100–1000 the width is small (89 % energy in 16 modes, POD-256 floor 3 %) while the sampling is sparse (1.6 per axis, nearest neighbour at 60 % of the norm). A hypernetwork on (amplitudes, ν, t), a time-continuous latent ODE prior, or encoder-based training changes *how* the K-dim surface is parametrised, not the neighbours it has to interpolate from; the H2/H3 tests of §3 are cheap and predicted to fail, and should be run only as recorded negative controls.

**The design most likely to move the number is the one that removes intrinsic dimensions by exact structure instead of interpolating them: a symmetry-reduced ("shifted") manifold decoder with D4 augmentation.**

$$\omega(\mathbf x;\, z, \mathbf s) = \big[\mathcal T_{\mathbf s}\, G\,(h(z) + C_q y)\big](\mathbf x),\qquad \mathcal T_{\mathbf s}: \text{exact spectral translation by } \mathbf s\in\mathbb T^2,$$

with the shift $\mathbf s$ (2 unknowns) solved online beside $z$ (its Jacobian is the spectral derivative of the decoded field — two extra columns, no new machinery in the LM), the training fields aligned to the canonical phase before the bank/head are trained, the D4 group (8 exact symmetries of the periodic FOM) used as data augmentation, and the head trained head-only in the whitened space on the frozen aligned bank (the ns301/stage-C protocol). The head then interpolates an 11-dimensional family at 8 × 512 = 4 096 effective trajectories.

- *Why (with the §2.7 numbers).* Alignment alone lifts the linear side 1.35× at matched K and 1.21× at matched *online dimension* (14 + 2 shifts vs 16), and closes the coverage gap from 0.64 to 0.45 — the only lever found that moves coverage more than the 4× data set did. D4 augmentation is an 8× data multiplier in the discrete symmetry directions; by the ns301 learning curve (−0.06 to −0.24 per doubling, flattening) three doublings are worth a further ≈ 10–25 %.
- *Expected oracle vs POD-K.* If the head keeps its raw-family advantage over the linear competitor at matched K (1.15–1.19×) on aligned fields, the shifted decoder at 16 online unknowns (K = 14, 2 shifts) lands at ≈ 0.197 / 1.19 ≈ **0.165**, i.e. **1.45× the raw POD-16** (0.239) and 1.2× the aligned POD-14; D4 augmentation is predicted to bring it to ≈ 0.14–0.15 (**ratio 1.6–1.7**). At K = 30 + 2: ≈ 0.09 vs raw POD-32 0.141 (1.55×). The prediction is therefore **FAIL at the 2.0 bar, PASS at the 1.5 marker** — the best any head-side change can do on this family, and worth one job because a ratio ≥ 2 would falsify the coverage model of §2.
- *Pre-registrable pass/fail (one Phase-2 job: `ns2d_phase2.py` with an `ALIGN=1`, `D4_AUG=1` path, K = 14, R = 256, otherwise the ns203 recipe; a second arm K = 30, R = 512).* PASS iff the held-out oracle median of the shifted decoder (LM over z and s jointly, 8 starts, budget 300, errors in field space on the *un-aligned* dev states after un-shifting) is ≤ ½ the **raw POD-16** median on the same 384 states (matched online dimension) and ≥ the aligned bank floor. FAIL otherwise. Recorded beside it, not as the bar: the aligned POD-14 and POD-16 medians (the linear symmetry-reduced controls) and the ratio at 1.5. If the shifted decoder does not beat the aligned POD-14 by ≥ 1.15× the head is declared inert on this family and the paper reports NS as a linear-ROM case, as Poisson and heat. Bar stated before the job; no re-scoring on training trajectories; the sealed cohort untouched. Machinery gates: alignment round-trip ≤ 1e-12; the shift Jacobian against finite differences ≤ 1e-8; D4 images of a dev trajectory must satisfy the FOM to the Newton tolerance (the equivariance gate).

**The only route to a *predicted* pass at 2.0 is a family change, and it should be pre-screened without training.** From the Burgers/NS pair, the two quantities that decide the bar can be computed for a candidate family from 512 FOM trajectories at 64² in minutes (`analyze_b.py`/`analyze_c2.py` logic): the POD-16 floor on held-out states and the same-time nearest-neighbour coverage. Burgers: POD-16-in-bank 0.051, coverage 0.14 → ratio 13.5×; NS: 0.24, 0.64 → 1.19×. A transport-dominated NS family — a vortex dipole or a pair of Gaussian vortices advected across the torus by a uniform mean flow $U$ (parameters: positions, strengths, core radius, $U$, ν: 6–7 intrinsic dims + t), or Kolmogorov forcing with a drifting frame — has a POD-16 floor ≥ 0.4 by construction (translation of a compact structure) and, at 6–7 dims, coverage ≈ 0.15–0.2 with 512 trajectories. Pre-registration for that arm: the lane's H-ORACLE gate unchanged; the **precondition gate**, stated now, is POD-16 (held-out) ≥ 0.35 **and** coverage ≤ 0.25 on the 64² screen, else the family is not run; the prediction is POD-16 ≈ 0.4–0.6 and head ≤ 0.1 on 512 trajectories (the Burgers regime).

## 5. Training on reference solutions restricted to the coarse grid (coordinator addition)

**(a) What the pipelines train on now.** Both train on **same-grid FOM solutions**, not on reference solutions.
- Burgers, stage A: `separable-decoder/sep_burgers_r3.py:196-262` (`build_data_lean`) regenerates the canonical draw with `bc.bf.make_rollout(n)` at the training resolution `n` (line 210) and keeps the picked states on the point pool; the test fields come from `build_test_full` (lines 263-279), same `make_rollout(n)`. Stage B: `sep_coeff_extract.py:198-210` (`sample_params`, `make_rollout(N)`) — same grid. The 4096-interval reference enters only the ladder driver (`b-seeds/seeds_run.py:150-164`, `cfg['reference_mesh']`, `reference_dt`) as an *error column*.
- NS: `ns2d_phase2.py:148-162` (`generate` → `F.make_fom(N, DT, NSTEPS, OUT_EVERY)` at `TRAIN_N`) and `:271-291`; Phase 3 grades against the same-grid converged FOM (`ntol 1e-11`, `R-DATA_reference`). There is no fine-reference column in the NS lane; the Phase-1 F-MESH gate gives the 256² FOM's own discretisation error against 512²: 2.3e-4 at ν = 1e-2 and **4.7e-3 at ν = 1e-3** (report, "Mesh refinement of the family") — comparable to the bank floor.

**(b) Changes to train on reference solutions at the coarse nodes.** Burgers: in `build_data_lean` replace `make_rollout(n)` by `make_rollout(n_ref)` with `n_ref = 4096`, `dt_ref = dt·n/n_ref` (the ladder's `reference_dt` rule), and restrict each state to the coarse interior nodes (`u.reshape(n_ref+1, n_ref+1)[::n_ref//n, ::n_ref//n]`) before the pool/pick; the identical change in `sep_coeff_extract.py` (stage B) and in `build_test_full`. Cost: the reference rollouts are what the ladder job already computes per case; for 4 608 trajectories at 4096² this is the dominant cost (≈ 100× a 256² rollout) and must be chunked and cached (the `no_burgers_20260914/pilot-data01` dataset cache pattern). NS: `generate(N_ref, …)` with `N_ref = 512` and a `[::2, ::2]` restriction — the 512² FOM is already exercised in the F-MESH gate. Everything downstream (bank, head, whitening, directions `C_q`) is unchanged; the ROM residual stays on the coarse grid.

**(c) Expected effect and the risk.** The decoder's manifold then contains restricted *reference* fields, so the "vs ref" column can drop below the coarse FOM's discretisation error **only to the extent the online solve lands on the manifold's reference-like point rather than the coarse-consistent one**. The solve minimises the coarse-grid weak residual, whose zero is the coarse FOM solution; the manifold point closest to the reference is *not* a zero of the coarse residual — it differs from it by the discretisation error (4.03 % on Burgers 256², 0.5 % on NS 256² at Re 1000). With $K+q$ unknowns and $M = 4(K+q)$ test modes the solve will sit between the two: every rung's vs-ref error will be pulled back toward the coarse solution, by an amount that grows with the correction rank (more freedom to satisfy the coarse residual exactly). The prediction is therefore *non-monotone in q for the vs-ref column*: at q = 0 the manifold restriction dominates and vs-ref could fall below 4.03 %; at large q the solve reproduces the coarse FOM and vs-ref returns to ≈ 4.0 %. The Burgers evidence already hints at the mechanism from the other side: the *loose* tuned Newton control `nt1e-2_dt01` is closer to the reference (3.62 %) than the converged coarse FOM (4.03 %) by error cancellation (`b-ladder-top` report, Q2 table). A second risk is the t = 0 compression column: the supplied field is the coarse-grid initial condition, which is on the reference manifold only up to the initial discretisation error. And a third, fatal if ignored: the same-grid error column becomes *meaningless* (it measures the distance to a solution the manifold was trained not to reproduce), so the pre-registered same-grid ladder criterion cannot be reused for this arm.

**(d) Pre-registrable Burgers 256² test (one job, `seeds_run.py` verbatim after retraining stages A–C on restricted 4096-interval references with seed 1).** Ladder `dense_m4`, q ∈ {0, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256}, the six development cases, three timed repetitions, the same-job controls `fft_tight`, `nt1e-2_dt01` (the tuned Newton setting, ≈ 9.3 ms, vs-ref 3.62 %), `nt1e-4_dt005` (37.7 ms, vs-ref 4.03 %). **PASS** iff some rung has worst-over-cases **vs-reference** error below the coarse FOM's **4.03 %** *and* median GPU ms below the tuned Newton setting's 9.3 ms in the same job. **FAIL** otherwise; report which of the two bars failed. Pre-stated expectation: the accuracy bar is reachable at q = 0–32 (manifold restriction dominates, predicted vs-ref 2.5–3.5 % — the head's best-found on reference fields, ≈ 2.5 % on the raw recipe, is the floor), the cost bar is **not** (the q = 0 rung costs 264–332 ms against 9.3 ms; the b-speed lane found the query launch-bound at ≈ 17 µs/step + 141 µs/iteration), so the predicted verdict is FAIL on cost with PASS on accuracy, which would still be a new and publishable row ("beats the reference-error of the coarse FOM at 30× its cost"). A control that must fail: the same ladder with the raw (same-grid-trained) checkpoint must stay at ≥ 4.03 % vs-ref on every rung (it did: 4.036–4.064 %).

## Glossary

- **Bank G / head h / latent code z / K / R**: the R learned spatial functions on the grid; the small MLP mapping the K online unknowns to R coefficients; the unknowns; their count; the bank size.
- **Auto-decoder**: training in which every training snapshot owns a free code that is optimised jointly with the network; no encoder.
- **Chart**: the correspondence between physical parameters and codes that the auto-decoder happens to learn.
- **Oracle / best-found**: the smallest error any code can reach on a known held-out field (multi-start LM fit with the truth supplied); an upper bound on what the ROM can do.
- **Bank floor / POD-K floor**: error of projecting a field on the bank span / on the best K-dim linear subspace of the training snapshots.
- **H-ORACLE bar**: the lane's pre-registered gate: oracle median ≤ ½ the POD-K median on the held-out dev states (ratio POD-K / oracle ≥ 2).
- **Held-out / dev / sealed**: trajectories never trained on (seed 20260918, 64 cases) / the unopened cohort.
- **Whitened coefficient space**: bank coefficients rotated by the thin-QR factor R_b so that Euclidean distance equals the field 2-norm; the head-only refits (Burgers stage C, ns301) train there.
- **Coverage / NN distance**: distance from a held-out state to the nearest training state, relative to ‖u(0)‖ of its trajectory.
- **Intrinsic dimension**: number of independent parameters of the family (12 amplitudes on a sphere + ν = 13 for NS; 5 for Burgers) plus time.
- **Quadratic manifold**: u ≈ V z + W·(monomials of z), a POD basis plus a polynomial correction; here fitted by ridge regression.
- **Alignment / shifted decoder**: translating each field to a canonical phase (torus symmetry) so that the manifold need not represent translations; the shift becomes an online unknown.
- **D4 augmentation**: the 8 rotations/reflections of the square torus, exact symmetries of the periodic FOM, used to multiply the training set.
- **Participation ratio**: (Σσ²)²/Σσ⁴ of the code covariance — the number of latent directions effectively in use.
- **Mahalanobis radius**: distance from the training-code mean in units of the training-code covariance.
- **Correction rank q / C_q**: number of extra linear directions solved with z / the matrix holding them.
- **vs-ref / same-grid error**: error against the fine-mesh reference restricted to the query grid / against the converged FOM on the query grid.
- **`nt1e-2_dt01`, `fft_tight`, `nt1e-4_dt005`**: Burgers full-order controls (loose Newton with 2× time step; converged; tight Newton).
- **LM**: Levenberg–Marquardt damped Gauss–Newton for the latent fit.
