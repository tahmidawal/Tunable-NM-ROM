\begin{frame}{The idea: evaluate the nonlinear term off the mesh}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Before:} the reduced model's nonlinear integral $\int\psi\,u(u_x{+}u_y)$ was computed \emph{on the simulation mesh} (dense sum, fitted EQ, or a giant precomputed tensor). Cost and error both depended on the mesh.
\item \textbf{Hari's idea:} our bank $u(x)=G(x)c$ is a continuous function. Evaluate it and its \emph{exact} autodiff derivative at a few thousand textbook quadrature points (Gauss, lattice):
\[ N(c)=P^{\top}\big[(Bc)\odot(Dc)\big] \]
with $B,D,P$ precomputed once offline.
\item No fitting, no per-mesh retraining, and the same points at every mesh.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{What we did since bringing in Hari's quadrature}
\small
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Plugged Hari's off-mesh rules into our tunable model} for Burgers 2D and 3D, and ran every rule family as a full PDE solve: Gauss, lattices (Fibonacci, CBC, Korobov), Sobol, Smolyak, and the flux form, against the old mesh methods (dense sum, mesh lattice, exact tensor).
\item \textbf{Found why it works} (mechanism study): exact derivative vs.\ mesh differencing.
\item \textbf{Fixed the next bottleneck:} second-order time stepping (CN-R, BDF2, Galerkin).
\item \textbf{Tested what else limits accuracy:} a wider bank (up to 1024 shapes) and smoother or derivative-trained banks.
\item \textbf{Built trustworthy references:} order-verified second-order solutions, cross-checked by a spectral solver; sealed held-out cohorts.
\item \textbf{Combined the winners} (second-order stepping with the wide bank) and scored them against the new references.
\item \textbf{Now running:} 3D speed (one-pass solver, narrower bank, batching, large-mesh comparison against spectral).
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{0.3em}\footnotesize Every step was pre-registered and adversarially checked by an independent reviewer (Codex) before it ran.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{5. Why it works: the exact derivative}
\fig[0.5]{a2_gap_vs_h.png}
\say{The old error was the mesh's own differencing error: slope 1 for upwind, 2 for central, exactly as theory says. Mesh nodes with the exact derivative reproduce the off-mesh result, so the win is the exact derivative, and quadrature makes it cheap (about 14k points against millions of nodes).}
\end{frame}
