## The question, and what changed

Rounds 3 and 4 reduced the Burgers gap to a single rung. The learned
bank's own least-squares floor on fresh test states is far below
target, but the K-dimensional map `h` reaches only a small fraction of
that span, by a factor that is the same at N=256 and N=1024. Round 4
attributed this to h's *capacity or optimisation*. Round 5 shows that
attribution is wrong in a way that matters: h fits its training targets
perfectly well, and what fails is generalisation to fresh mu.

## The method that made this cheap

With the bank `G` frozen, `Gram = G^T G`, `c*` the span least-squares
coefficients of a target `u` and `f` its residual,

```
||G h(z) - u||^2  =  (h(z) - c*)^T Gram (h(z) - c*)  +  f^2
```

exactly, because `G c*` is the orthogonal projection of `u`. So fitting
h against full fields and fitting it against precomputed coefficients
in the Gram metric are the SAME problem, at `O(S r^2)` instead of
`O(S n_pts r)`. Reconstruction, the span floor and the representation
oracle all become computable from a 70 MB file rather than the data
(137 GB at N=1024). One extraction job per (N, checkpoint) then buys
unlimited h arms at minutes each; 30 arms ran in one night.

The identity is gated by computing the source checkpoint's
reconstruction two independent ways -- from the full fields, and from
`(c*, f, Gram)` -- on retained full-resolution states:

| extraction | N | K | cond(G) | rank | identity dev (mean) | identity dev (max) | recon via the identity | fresh-test span floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `2837160` | 1024 | 16 | 1.741e+04 | 512/512 | 6.515e-12 | 8.115e-11 | 7.308e-03 | 2.601e-04 |
| `2837178` | 1024 | 32 | 1.871e+04 | 512/512 | 4.650e-11 | 2.456e-10 | 1.792e-03 | 1.731e-04 |
| `2837159` | 256 | 16 | 2.637e+04 | 512/512 | 3.667e-12 | 1.366e-11 | 7.171e-03 | 2.124e-04 |

Those two `recon via the identity` values reproduce the cluster jobs'
own reported reconstructions, and the two span floors reproduce their
own reported floors, to every digit those jobs printed. The local
coefficient-space problem IS the cluster's field-space problem.

Read the two error columns against each other. Capacity moves the
TRAINING fit by a factor of several and the FRESH oracle barely at all.
`ffz64` -- random Fourier features of the latent -- gives one of the
best training fits and by far the worst oracle, which is the signature
of an image that passes through the training targets and wiggles
between them. Capacity past `1024x3` reverses outright.

Two levers are dead on arrival here, and both were on the inherited
shortlist:

* **The latent codes are already converged.** `code-refit gain` is the
  ratio of an arm's reconstruction to the same reconstruction after
  re-solving every code exactly at frozen h. For the incumbent it is
  1.000. "h is fitting noise because the codes never converged" is
  false. A code-polish arm is among the worst in the table.
* **Latent initialisation is not the limit.** An `oracle_nn` column in
  the generated `HFIT.md` re-runs every oracle with an extra
  initialisation chosen using the test target; it changes nothing.

## Result 5 -- the quadrature is binding only at the first step

From the sibling's r4a6 job (N=1024, K=32, tail-capped NNLS already
applied). `weak-EQ optimum / L2 oracle` at a single step is the
objective-truncation error: what the EQ objective costs on top of
what the manifold can represent.

| EQ set | m | rel fit | worst row | t=1 | t=2 | t=3 | t=5 | t=10 | t=25 | t=50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ctrl` | 256 | 6.961e-03 | 1.426e+02 | 3.271 | 1.231 | 1.095 | 1.026 | 1.013 | 1.005 | 1.002 |
| `M256` | 1024 | 6.548e-04 | 9.409e+03 | 2.256 | 1.040 | 1.017 | 1.004 | 1.001 | 1.001 | 1.001 |

Objective truncation is confined almost entirely to t=1, the first
step off the sharp blob initial condition; from t=2 onward a SINGLE
step is not limited by the EQ objective at the error scale these
decoders reach.

**But a single-step diagnostic understates what the quadrature
costs a ROLLOUT, because the first-step error propagates.** The
same cost-matched decoder measured on both EQ sets, in Result 4
above, gives a rollout error of 8.961e-03 on the control set and
6.184e-03 on M=256/m=1024 -- a factor of 1.45 -- for 45.15 ms
against 55.65 ms and a matched ratio of 0.40x against 0.32x. So the
quadrature IS worth about a third of the remaining rollout error,
and it is bought with speed like everything else here.
And the row-tail statistic points the WRONG WAY: the M=256 set has
a far worse worst row than the control and tracks the oracle
better. The certification metric is this tracking ratio, not the
row tail, and the fix indicated is a first-step-specific
quadrature rather than a globally finer one.

## Verdict

The Burgers gap is a generalisation-in-mu problem, and it has exactly
two levers. K buys accuracy and pays for it steeply at solve time.
mu-density buys the same accuracy and pays nothing at solve time, which
makes it the only lever compatible with keeping the round-4 speed. The
honest arithmetic on the fitted power law is in the lab log; the short
version is that density alone reaches roughly 1e-3 on the oracle at an
order of magnitude more trajectories than the canonical draw, and that
training data at N=256 costs about two minutes per 576 trajectories.

Levers measured and found neutral or harmful, so that nobody spends a
retrain on them again: latent Fourier features, capacity beyond
h=1024x3, code jitter at every scale tried, code-only polish, per-
snapshot loss normalisation, global-versus-snapshot loss normalisation,
weight decay, early-time loss weighting inside the coefficient fit,
better latent initialisation, and more span (R=1024 widened the gap).
