\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every number below is a single training seed on opened development cases, one
checkpoint per PDE. Costs are compared only inside one allocation; where two
numbers come from different jobs the text compares accuracy only. The losses
come first.

\subsection{Nothing reduced is on the frontier at $256^2$}
\label{sec:exp:panel}

Table~\ref{tab:tunability} is the central panel: $\nPanelSubjectCount$ timed
subjects in one allocation on one A100---the correction ladder with dense and
certified-quadrature advection at two tolerances, POD-LSPG at six ranks, the
unrestricted bank, the trained FNO, and eight full-order Newton settings---all
against the same converged reference. On (GPU ms, worst \evolved\ error),
\textbf{$\nPanelReducedNonDomEvolved$ of the $\nPanelReducedCount$ reduced
subjects are non-dominated} once the full-order controls are included, and the
same holds on the \alltimes\ metric ($\nPanelReducedNonDomAll$ of
$\nPanelReducedCount$). The most accurate reduced subject is POD-512 at
$\nPanelPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ and $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms; the cheapest
full-order setting that is at least as accurate, Newton at tolerance $10^{-3}$
with $\Delta t=0.005$, reaches $\nPanelFomNtThreeFineErr\,\%$ at
$\nPanelFomNtThreeFineMs$\,ms. This is the evidence behind the abstract's
sentence that the model does not beat an efficient full-order solver in 2D.

Within the reduced subjects the picture is the one the paper is about. The
dense ladder $q=0,\dots,256$ is monotone on both metrics, every rung converged,
spanning $\nPanelDenseErrSpan\times$ in evolved error for
$\nPanelDenseCostSpan\times$ in cost; but this ladder raises $M=4(K+q)$ with
$q$, and \S\ref{sec:exp:qxm} separates the two. Certified quadrature is a cost
lever at equal error: at $q=0$ the certified rule costs $\nPanelEqMsZero$\,ms
against $\nPanelDenseMsZero$\,ms dense for $\nPanelEqErrZero\,\%$ against
$\nPanelDenseErrZero\,\%$, and at $q=128$ $\nPanelEqMsOneTwoEight$\,ms against
$\nPanelDenseMsOneTwoEight$\,ms for $\nPanelEqErrOneTwoEight\,\%$ against
$\nPanelDenseErrOneTwoEight\,\%$. Loosening the stopping tolerance from
$10^{-6}$ to $10^{-3}$ removes a further $\nPanelTolSavingZero$--$\nPanelTolSavingOneTwoEight\,\%$
of the query at negligible error change. The EQ ladder in this panel is
\emph{not} monotone at the top: with the rules the panel had, $q=256$ regresses
to $\nPanelEqQtwofiftysixEvolved\,\%$ against $\nPanelEqQonetwentyeightEvolved\,\%$
at $q=128$, because those two rungs carried rules certified only on the
secondary bar; \S\ref{sec:exp:eq} shows what fixes that.

Two facts about the classical baseline must sit here. On the \evolved\ metric
POD-512 beats the best dense rung on both axes ($\nPanelPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$
at $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms against $\nPanelBestRungErr\,\%$ at
$\nPanelBestRungMs$\,ms), so the head-ablation statement that no POD rank up to
$k'=128$ matches the neural head (\S\ref{sec:exp:head}) holds but does not
extend to $k'=512$. On the \alltimes\ metric POD-512 is dominated by the
unrestricted bank ($\nPanelFreeAll\,\%$ at $\nPanelFreeMs$\,ms against
$\nPanelPodFiveTwelveAll\,\%$ at $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms), and the
reduced-only frontier is $\nPanelReducedOnlyFrontierEq$ certified-EQ rungs
plus that endpoint, with no POD rank on it. The metric choice decides the
comparison, which is why both are printed. The POD ranks $k'\ge32$ carry a
``strict'' flag of no: their initial fit is an attainable square system whose
residual is at round-off ($\le\nPanelStrictNoIcResidualMax$ relative), so the
stationarity ratio is $0/0$ and no budget can move it; every one of their time
steps exits on the gradient rule and each solve sits on its own projection
floor (solved/best-found $\le\nPanelPodSolvedOverFloorMax$). The classical
baseline is not handicapped.

\subsection{Rank against test count: the family at fixed $M$}
\label{sec:exp:qxm}

The dense ladder confounds two controls, the rank $q$ and the test count $M$.
The crossed grid (Table~\ref{tab:qxm}) runs $q\in\{0,\dots,256\}$ against
fixed $M\in\{256,1088\}$, the schedules $M\in\{4,8,16\}(K+q)$ and bridge cells,
with a saturation sweep in $M$ and a solver control, in three jobs whose
shared cells agree to $10^{-9}$. \textbf{At fixed $M=\nQxmFixedM$ the pure-rank
ladder $q=0,64,128,256$, measured inside one job, is monotone
($\nQxmMonotone$), every rung converged ($\nQxmFixedMConverged$), and spans
$\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in evolved error for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost with
$\nQxmNonDom$ non-dominated points}: it passes the tunability bar fixed on
15~September (monotone, at least three non-dominated points, at least
$2\times$ on both axes, nothing early-stopped) with the test count held
constant, which is the objection the crossed grid was run to answer. The
scheduled $M=4(K+q)$ ladder spans $\nQxmSpanScheduledFour\times$; its log-span
decomposes into $\nQxmShareRankCorner\,\%$ rank and
$\nQxmShareTestsCorner\,\%$ test count along the corner path
($\nQxmShareTestsRung\,\%$ test count along the rung path), and on the balanced
sub-grid the rank main effect carries $\nQxmAnovaRank\,\%$ of the variance in
log error against $\nQxmAnovaTests\,\%$ for the test count. The split is not
uniform: the test count does $\nQxmRungTestShares\,\%$ of the work at
successive rungs, so it buys the bottom of the ladder and the rank buys the
top. The test count does not simply saturate: at $q=0$ it is flat past
$M^\star=\nQxmMstarZero$ but that stretch still costs $\nQxmMstarZeroCost\times$,
and at $q=256$ the $M$ effect is still $\nQxmSpanMatQtwoFiftySix\times$; the
absolute $M$ needed grows with $q$ and no invariance in tests-per-unknown is
claimed. Holding $M$ fixed isolates the rank effect at the price of a dearer
baseline: the fixed-$M$ $q=0$ rung costs $\nQxmFixedQzeroMs$\,ms where the
scheduled one costs $\nQxmScheduledQzeroMs$\,ms in another job. The inner
solver switch at $K+q>64$ is inert (Gauss--Jordan against LU agree to
$\nQxmSolverControlRelDiff$ at $\nQxmSolverControlCost$ the cost). The best
accuracy seen anywhere is $\nQxmBestErr\,\%$ at $(q=256, M=\nQxmBestM)$.

\subsection{Quadrature rules must be certified on reachable states}
\label{sec:exp:eq}

A rule's NNLS fit residual is a property of its fitting set, not a certificate:
the static-snapshot rule at $q=\nEqtopStaticWorstQ$ fits to
$\nEqtopStaticWorstFit$ and reaches $\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopStaticWorstRho$ on
held-out reachable states, four times the primary bar. Certifying every rule by
\eqref{eq:rho} on states the dense solver actually visits, the earlier lanes'
blocker at the top rungs turns out to be the number of fit states rather than
the node count: the parent rules at $q=128$ and $q=256$, fitted on 14 and 8
states, reach $\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopParentOneTwentyEightRho$ and
$\nEqtopParentTwoFiftySixRho$ at $m=2048$ and do not certify, while 64-state
rules at the same $m$ reach $\nEqtopPrimaryOneTwentyEightRho$ and
$\nEqtopPrimaryTwoFiftySixRho$ and do (Table~\ref{tab:eqcert}). The extrapolated
node count at which the parent construction would cross the bar is withdrawn
(it was fitted through draw noise), and so is the inherited fit-state
convention for $q\ge128$. Rebuilt with the cheapest primary-certified rule per
rung and timed in one allocation, the EQ ladder is monotone on the evolved
metric ($\nEqtopLadderMonotone$), every rung converged
($\nEqtopLadderConverged$), running $\nEqtopLadderFirstErr\to\nEqtopLadderLastErr\,\%$
at $\nEqtopLadderFirstMs\to\nEqtopLadderLastMs$\,ms, at
$\nEqtopCostRatioMin$--$\nEqtopCostRatioMax\times$ the same-job dense cost; the
8-state rule at $q=256$ gave $\nEqtopTopOldRuleErr\,\%$ where the 64-state rule
gives $\nEqtopTopEqErr\,\%$ against dense $\nEqtopTopDenseErr\,\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:eqladder}). \textbf{All of this is one draw.} A second draw of
the same construction at $q=64$ moved $\rho_{\max}$ from
$\nEqtopQsixtyFourArchivedRho$ to $\nEqtopQsixtyFourRedrawRho$ and flipped the
flag, so ``64 states'' is not uniformly better and the draw is first-order;
the replication job $\nEqtopPendingJob$ measures that spread, and until it
lands every certified flag here is provisional and the ladder is ``certified
in this draw''.

\subsection{The head earns its place per dimension, not per millisecond}
\label{sec:exp:head}

At matched latent dimension $k=16$ in one frozen bank per PDE, the neural head
is compared with the optimal rank-$k$ affine map inside the same bank, a
quadratic map, the unrestricted bank coefficients, and POD-LSPG at ranks
$8$--$128$, all through the same weak objective, tests, solver and stopping
rule (Tables~\ref{tab:head-burgers}, \ref{tab:head-poisson}). On Burgers the
head reaches $\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$ worst same-grid error against
$\nAblBurgersLinear\,\%$ for the best linear map and
$\nAblBurgersQuad\,\%$ for the quadratic one; \textbf{no POD rank up to
$k'=128$ matches it} (POD-128: $\nAblBurgersPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblBurgersPodOneTwentyEightMs$\,ms against $\nAblBurgersNeuralMs$\,ms), and
the POD rungs run with exact dense advection, which favours them. On Poisson
at $1024^2$ the head wins per dimension ($\nAblPoissonNeural\,\%$ against
$\nAblPoissonLinear\,\%$ linear and $\nAblPoissonPodSixteen\,\%$ POD-16) and
loses per millisecond: POD-128 reaches $\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEightMs$\,ms against the head's
$\nAblPoissonNeuralMs$\,ms, because that query is dominated by projection and
decode rather than by the reduced solve, so an eightfold larger linear
subspace is nearly free. Read in three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}): on
Burgers the bank floor is $\nAblBurgersFloor\,\%$, the best-found fit
$\nAblBurgersBestFound\,\%$, the solved error $\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$---the
solver layer costs $\nAblBurgersSolverGapPp$ percentage points and the
reduction layer sits $\nAblBurgersReductionOverFloor\times$ above the floor.
The gap that matters is the head, and training alone does not close it: no
retrained head on the same bank beats the incumbent's best-found
$\nTrainIncumbentBest\,\%$ (best $\nTrainBestRetrained\,\%$; a like-for-like
retrain of the incumbent's own recipe lands at $\nTrainLikeForLike\,\%$,
$\nTrainLikeForLikeRatio\times$ worse, so that pipeline does not reproduce the
incumbent and its verdict is a caveat, Table~\ref{tab:training}); on Poisson a
wider head moves the best-found/floor ratio from $\nPlinHeadRatioPrimary$ to
$\nPlinHeadRatioBest$ and no further (Table~\ref{tab:head-capacity}).

\subsection{Cached cost is flat in the mesh; nothing crosses over}
\label{sec:exp:mesh}

With one frozen checkpoint per PDE transferred across $64$--$1024$ intervals,
the cached reduced solve costs $\nMeshBurgersCachedFirst\to\nMeshBurgersCachedLast$\,ms
on Burgers and $\nMeshPoissonCachedFirst\to\nMeshPoissonCachedLast$\,ms on
Poisson (ratios $\nMeshBurgersCachedRatio$ and $\nMeshPoissonCachedRatio$)
while the interior unknowns grow $\nMeshBurgersUnknownGrowth\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:mesh}). The complete query does not inherit that: it grows
$\nMeshBurgersCompleteRatio\times$ and $\nMeshPoissonCompleteRatio\times$
through the dense input and output. Against the cheapest same-job full-order
arm that meets the accuracy target there is no crossover at any rung on either
PDE (FOM/ROM $\nMeshBurgersFomOverRomRange$ on Burgers,
$\nMeshPoissonFomOverRomRange$ against the direct transform on Poisson), and the
Poisson error is pinned at its representation floor at every rung
($\nMeshPoissonErrFirst\to\nMeshPoissonErrLast\,\%$). A kernel-level port at
bit-level parity makes the frozen Burgers query $\nSpeedGpuTwoFiftySix$ faster
at $256^2$ and $\nSpeedGpuTenTwentyFour$ at $1024^2$, short of its $2\times$
target; the query is kernel-count bound ($\nSpeedFixedUs\,\mu$s per step plus
$\nSpeedPerIterUs\,\mu$s per iteration), so anything beyond needs fewer
iterations, not faster ones (Table~\ref{tab:speed}).

\subsection{Where the family collapses: linear PDEs}
\label{sec:exp:linear}

When the weak residual is linear in the coefficients the corrections are
eliminated exactly and the ladder has a different shape
(Table~\ref{tab:linear}). On Poisson at $1024^2$ with $R=512$, $K=32$, the
rungs $q=0,\dots,256$ run $\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\to\nPlinTenTwentyFourQtwoFiftySixErr\,\%$
at a flat $\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMin$--$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMax$\,ms, and
the top rung $q=R$---the full-bank linear solve by QR---lands on the bank floor
at $\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopErr\,\%$ and is the \emph{cheapest} point at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopMs$\,ms; the pre-registered degenerate-curve criterion
passes outright ($\nPlinTenTwentyFourDegenerate$), and at $256^2$ its literal
cost-span clause fails only because the top rung is
$\nPlinTwoFiftySixTopOverMidCost\times$ cheaper than the middle rungs. Solver
effort buys nothing: floor $\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor\,\%$, best-found
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourBestFoundQzero\,\%$, solved
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\,\%$. The same-job direct transform is exact at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourDstMs$\,ms. On the reflective wave the top rung, the
learned bank evolved by exact modal propagation with no head, is at every mesh
both the most accurate rung and $\nWaveBankOverHeadCostSixtyFour$--$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostTenTwentyFour\times$
cheaper than the cheapest head rung (error monotone in $q$, cost not: the middle
rungs cost up to $\nWaveMidRungMsTwoFiftySix$\,ms for a solver reason, then
collapse to $\nWaveBankMsTwoFiftySix$\,ms at $q=R$). \textbf{On both linear
PDEs the learned bank is itself worse than a POD basis of the same rank}: POD
$k'=64$ reaches $\nWavePodErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ against the bank's
$\nWaveBankErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ on waves at $256^2$ at the same cost, and POD
$k'=512$ reaches $\nPlinTenTwentyFourPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ against the bank
floor $\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor\,\%$ on Poisson. No reduced arm beats the direct
solve on either. This is the structural condition: the nonlinear manifold is
worth having only where the corrections cost a nonlinear solve, and on these
cells it is not. The L-shaped domain, where no fast transform exists, shows the
same head limit before its solve layer has run: the bank clears its floor
target ($\nLshapeBestFloor\,\%$, the corner enrichment buying only
$\nLshapeEnrichGain\times$, so the re-entrant corner is not what limits the
decoder) and every head sits $\nLshapeHeadRatioMin$--$\nLshapeHeadRatioMax\times$
above it (Table~\ref{tab:lshape}); whether any reduced arm is non-dominated
against a sparse direct solve is $\nLshapeSolve$.

\subsection{Neural operators on the same data}
\label{sec:exp:operators}

A PDEBench-style U-Net and a Transolver were trained on exactly the FNO's
data, split, reference, metric, wall budget and selection rule
(Table~\ref{tab:operators}). On the matched eight-case cohort the worst errors
are U-Net-small $\nOpEightUnetSmall\,\%$, U-Net-medium
$\nOpEightUnetMedium\,\%$, Transolver-refine $\nOpEightTsolRefine\,\%$,
U-Net-refine $\nOpEightUnetRefine\,\%$, \textbf{ROM $\nOpEightRom\,\%$},
FNO-large $\nOpEightFnoLarge\,\%$, and the efficient full-order solver
$\nOpEightFomEfficient\,\%$: $\nOpArmsBeatingRom$ operator arms beat the ROM
and every FNO capacity is worse than it ($\nOpEveryFnoWorse$). The earlier
finding that the ROM is more accurate than a neural operator was an artefact of
the operator family chosen, and it is withdrawn. Operators are also far cheaper
per query (the FNO at $\nPanelFnoMs$\,ms in the same allocation as
Table~\ref{tab:tunability}, though at $\nPanelFnoErr\,\%$ on the six panel
cases), so on Burgers at $256^2$ they dominate the ROM on both axes. Two caveats
travel with the table: $\nOpLaneImproving$ arms of the new families and
$\nOpFnoImproving$ FNO arms were still improving when the budget ended, so every
operator number is a lower bound on that family; and nothing was tuned per
family. On Poisson the gap is larger still (U-Net worst
$\nOpPoissonUnetWorstRange\,\%$ against FNO $\nOpPoissonFnoWorstRange\,\%$,
Table~\ref{tab:operators-poisson}). What survives is the within-reduced-model
claim and the mechanism; the operators' own inference-time knob, evaluation
resolution, is $\nOpResolutionKnob$.
