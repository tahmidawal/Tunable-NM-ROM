A trained neural operator gives one accuracy–cost trade-off per model, and changing it means retraining. We present a nonlinear-manifold reduced-order model (NM-ROM) whose balance between accuracy and speed is chosen when the model is used. Its spatial bank, a set of learned functions of space, is ordered once by importance after training, so a query chooses how many bank functions to use and whether to solve with a small nonlinear head or in the linear span of the bank, without changing a learned weight. Every linear term is precomputed exactly, the discrete PDE residual is solved by least-squares Petrov–Galerkin projection onto fixed tests, and the nonlinear term is evaluated on a sparse set of nodes. We compare every setting with the fastest tested solver that reaches the same accuracy; on the new problems that solver may choose its mesh, integrator and step. On a three-dimensional Navier–Stokes family with sharper interacting vortices and lower viscosity, on 128 fresh test cases and three independently trained models, the head reaches 0.28–0.42% worst-case error at bank width 192, and a faster setting 0.35–0.48% at 5.1–10.2× the speed of the matched solver from a 450-configuration ladder; the linear span of the same bank is unstable (7.7–70%). On unsteady flow past a cylinder, one trained bank meets six acceptance targets at a held-out Reynolds number at widths 16 to 32, with a rollout 25–54× faster than the cheapest finite-element run that meets the same targets. The advantage appears where the matched solver needs a fine mesh and the solution family is low-dimensional; on constant-coefficient box problems transform solvers are mostly faster, and we use those problems as correctness checks.
