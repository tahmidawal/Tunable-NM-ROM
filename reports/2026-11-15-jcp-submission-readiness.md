# Bringing the quadrature study to JCP

The fastest defensible route to the *Journal of Computational Physics* is a paper about **when a continuous reduced basis lets classical quadrature replace a stored nonlinear operator, how to choose enough points, and what that choice costs**. This November 15, 2026 audit uses completed results for the tested configurations. At the time of this audit, sparse-solver collection and the two held-out studies were pending. Their final results are in the [experiment closeout](2026-11-15-experiment-closeout.md).

The October JCP manuscript document is an outline. A new title and another mesh-scaling plot would leave substantial overlap. ## Plain-language glossary

**Bank** means the reusable collection of spatial functions used to represent a field. **Width** or **rank** counts how many functions are retained. **ROM** means a reduced-order model that evolves a smaller set of unknowns. **POD** means proper orthogonal decomposition, a classical way to build a basis from example solutions. A **head** maps a small set of coordinates to the coefficients of a bank.

**Quadrature** approximates an integral with weighted sample points. **Hyper-reduction** cuts the work needed to evaluate the original model’s equations. **Empirical cubature** learns sample points and weights from data. A **Gauss rule** uses weighted integration nodes. A **lattice rule** uses a regular point pattern. A component-by-component (**CBC**) construction chooses the lattice one dimension at a time. **GB** counts billions of bytes. A **tensor contraction** evaluates a nonlinear term using stored precomputed coefficients. A **stencil** estimates derivatives using nearby grid values. A **plateau** is the range where adding more quadrature points changes a measured error by less than the study’s threshold.

A **cohort** is a named group of test cases. **Held-out** means excluded from fitting. **Sealed** means access is controlled until the team fixes its choices. A **reference floor** describes the uncertainty in the high-resolution comparison solution. A **timing-order check** tests whether the order of timing measurements materially changes the result. A **full field query** includes producing the answer at all requested mesh points. **cuDSS** and **AmgX** are GPU sparse linear solvers. **CROM**, **CoLoRA** and **U-Net** name learned model families used for comparison. **Slurm** is the cluster scheduler.
