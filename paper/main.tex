\begin{abstract}

Neural operators such as Fourier Neural Operators \citep{Li2020FNO} and
DeepONets \citep{Lu2021DeepONet} deliver one (accuracy, speed) point per
trained model; at deployment only the evaluation grid can be changed,
which moves cost but not what the model can represent. We present a non-linear manifold reduced order
model (NM-ROM) for elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic PDEs that exposes a
family of accuracy/cost operating points from a single trained
decoder, controlled at inference time by one primary knob, the rank $q$ of a
linear correction the solver may switch on, and three solver-side knobs
(iteration cap, tolerance, empirical-quadrature sample count). The framework combines a matrix-free least-squares Petrov--Galerkin
projection of the discrete residual onto fixed weak tests
\citep{Bradbury2018JAX}, NNLS-based EQ hyper-reduction
\citep{Hernandez2017EQ,YanoPatera2019LPEQ} validated on the states the
solver actually reaches, exact boundary enforcement inside the decoder,
and a separable decoder --- a per-node spatial bank from a
Fourier-feature coordinate network \citep{Tancik2020FourierFeatures}
times a small neural head with a linear skip, plus nested correction
directions. Across 2D Burgers, Poisson, heat and waves, the trained-once family's
scheduled ladder is monotone in $q$ on Burgers on each of three training
seeds, and its fixed-test-count ladder, where $q$ is the only control,
meets a bar fixed before any run on one checkpoint, on the same-grid
error: against a fine reference every rung's
error is at most $\nPanelRungRefOverDiscMax\times$ the mesh's own
discretisation error, so the knob moves the reduction error, not the
physical error, at the meshes we ran; its quadrature rules are validated on reachable states, not by their
fitting residual, and confirmed on re-draw at $q\le32$ only;
and its cost results are same-job: on an L-shaped Poisson domain, where
no fast transform applies, the head is
$\nLshapeNeuralCheaperVsCheapestTwoFiftySix\times$ and
$\nLshapeNeuralCheaperVsCheapestFiveTwelve\times$ cheaper than the cheapest
full-order solve at $256^2$ and $512^2$, and plain POD-128 cheaper still
for $\nLshapePodOverHeadErrFiveTwelve\,\%$ more error; at $1024^2$ on
Burgers the cheapest rungs are non-dominated on evolved times. Nothing reduced is on the frontier at $256^2$ or $512^2$, where a
tuned full-order solver (and at $256^2$ a neural operator trained on the
same data) is cheaper and more accurate; and on
Poisson, heat and waves the family collapses to a linear model, which
says when the nonlinear manifold is worth having.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Neural operators for partial differential equations (PDEs)
\citep{Li2020FNO,Lu2021DeepONet,Kovachki2023NeuralOperator,Li2024PINO,BoulleTownsend2024} have
become a strong empirical baseline, but each trained model produces
essentially one (accuracy, wall-clock) operating point: the only
deployment-time knob, the evaluation grid, moves cost without changing
what the model can represent (\S\ref{sec:results:operators}). Classical Full Order Methods (FOMs)
\citep{Hughes2000FEM} solved iteratively \citep{HestenesStiefel1952CG}
expose this knob through their tolerance, and where a fast transform
applies they are hard to beat on wall-clock.

Two families of methods sit on either side of this gap. Neural
operators and PDE foundation models
\citep{HerdePoseidon2024,McCabeMPP2024,HaoDPOT2024,Chen2024UnsupervisedNO} deliver fast
amortised inference but fix what the model can represent at training
time. Reduced order models (ROMs) address the same problem from the
opposite direction: linear projection-based ROMs
\citep{BennerGugercinWillcox2015,Sirovich1987POD} inherit the FOM's
guarantees but hit the Kolmogorov $n$-width barrier on
advection-dominated regimes \citep{CohenDeVore2015}, and non-linear
manifold ROMs (NM-ROMs) \citep{LeeCarlberg2020,KimChoi2022NMROM} lift
this barrier with a learned manifold but have rarely been measured
head-to-head against neural operators and tuned full-order solvers in
the same job. The open question is whether a single trained NM-ROM can expose a
deployment-time accuracy/cost tradeoff that neither alternative offers,
and where, if anywhere, it is worth having once the comparators are
measured in the same allocation.

This paper answers that question with measurements on 2D Burgers,
Poisson, heat and waves, and the answer is mixed. We present an NM-ROM
framework whose distinguishing property is a \emph{deployment-time
accuracy/cost family traced by a single trained model}: at inference,
one primary knob --- the rank $q$ of a linear correction the solver may
switch on --- and three solver-side knobs --- the iteration cap, the
stopping tolerance, and the Empirical Quadrature sample count --- trade
accuracy for cost. The machinery is a decoder that enforces Dirichlet
conditions exactly, a damped Levenberg--Marquardt latent solver
\citep{Marquardt1963}, Empirical Quadrature
\citep{Hernandez2017EQ,YanoPatera2019LPEQ} validated on reachable states,
and a matrix-free JAX implementation \citep{Bradbury2018JAX}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A tunable NM-ROM with a deployment-time accuracy/cost
      family from a single trained model, and a matched-dimension
      result.} With the test count $M$ held fixed so that $q$ is the
      only control, the Burgers $256^2$ ladder is monotone with every
      rung converged, spanning $\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in same-grid error
      for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost inside one allocation
      (\S\ref{sec:results:tunability}); at matched $k=16$ inside one bank
      the neural head reaches $\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$ where the best
      linear map reaches $\nAblBurgersLinear\,\%$ and POD-16
      $\nAblBurgersPodSixteen\,\%$ (\S\ref{sec:results:head}).
\item \textbf{Architectural choices that make this family
      achievable.} We identify the three constraints a decoder must
      satisfy --- cold-start convergence, EQ compatibility, and
      mesh-independent per-node evaluation --- and show that a separable
      decoder (coordinate-network bank, head with a linear skip, nested
      corrections) meets all three.
\item \textbf{Precomputed weak operators and validated quadrature that
      make the framework practical.} Exact preassembly of every linear
      term, Empirical Quadrature for the one nonlinear term, and a rule
      that a fitted quadrature is accepted only by its held-out error on
      reachable states and by independent re-draws, never by its fitting
      residual.
\item \textbf{Where the family is not worth having, reported as such.}
      At $256^2$ on the square no reduced subject is on the
      non-dominated set once a tuned full-order solver and a neural
      operator on the same data are in the job; on Poisson, heat and
      waves the top rung is the linear model and also the cheapest
      point; on an L-shaped Poisson domain, where no fast transform
      applies, reduced models are cheaper than the cheapest same-job
      full-order solve, POD-128 more so than the head
      (\S\ref{sec:results:operators}, \S\ref{sec:results:collapse}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} No speedup over an efficient full-order
solver on the square; no accuracy superiority over neural operators; no
frontier over POD at every rank; no cost ratio across jobs; no theory;
and every number is development-cohort evidence
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Linear and non-linear manifold ROMs.}
Linear projection-based ROMs
\citep{BennerGugercinWillcox2015,Sirovich1987POD,Berkooz1993,QuarteroniManzoniNegri2015,HesthavenRozzaStamm2016}
suffer from the Kolmogorov $n$-width barrier on advection-dominated and
moving-front problems
\citep{CohenDeVore2015,GreifUrban2019,OhlbergerRave2016}. NM-ROMs lift this barrier with a non-linear trial manifold:
\citet{LeeCarlberg2020} introduced convolutional-autoencoder NM-ROMs with
LSPG projection \citep{Carlberg2011LSPG}, followed by shallow masked
autoencoders with sample-mesh hyper-reduction \citep{KimChoi2022NMROM},
reduced over-collocation \citep{RomorStabileRozza2023}, domain-decomposed
sparse autoencoders \citep{Diaz2024DomainDecompNMROM}, quadratic
manifolds \citep{GeelenWrightWillcox2022,BarnettFarhat2022},
feature-tracking variants with EQ \citep{MirhoseiniZahr2023},
POD-pre-compressed DL-ROMs \citep{FrescaManzoni2022PODDLROM} and
neural-field ROMs \citep{KimWenLeeChoiCNFROM2024}. We differ from these by a separable decoder --- a coordinate-network
bank \citep{Tancik2020FourierFeatures} times a small head with a linear
skip --- augmented by nested \emph{correction} directions chosen offline
once, so that a rank $q$ is selected at run time without refit, a family
from one trained decoder analogous to runtime-tunable networks
\citep{YuSlimmable2019,Cai2020OnceForAll}. NN-augmented projection ROMs
\citep{BarnettFarhatMaday2023} and online-adaptive bases
\citep{PeherstorferWillcox2015Adaptive,Carlberg2015hRefinement} add a
nonlinear correction to a linear basis; ours is the reverse composition,
the nonlinear head inside the linear bank with the linear part as the
correction. The projection is least-squares Petrov--Galerkin
\citep{Carlberg2011LSPG} and the linear terms are preassembled as in
tensorial POD \citep{StefanescuSandu2014,WederSchwerdtnerPeherstorfer2024};
we claim no novelty for either.

\paragraph{Neural operators, hyper-reduction, and differentiable programming.}
Neural-operator surrogates
\citep{Li2020FNO,Lu2021DeepONet,Kovachki2023NeuralOperator,LiGeoFNO2023,TranFFNO2023,Cao2021GalerkinTransformer,LiOFormer2023,HaoGNOT2023,WuTransolver2024,WangLNO2024,WenGAOT2025,HuLinearNO2025,Shikhman2026BoundaryIndexed}
and PDE foundation models
\citep{HerdePoseidon2024,McCabeMPP2024,HaoDPOT2024,HolzschuhPDETransformer2025}
deliver one fixed (accuracy, speed) point per trained model and
typically approximate boundary conditions via penalty terms, with
partial fixes via distance functions or hard constraints
\citep{SukumarSrivastava2022,BerroneBC2022,BrechtHardConstraints2023,WangBENO2024,LippePDERefiner2023};
hybrid solver-in-the-loop methods
\citep{UmSolverInTheLoop2020,Kochkov2021PNAS,KopanicakovaKarniadakis2024,EshaghiNOWS2025,Raissi2019,Karniadakis2021PIML}
embed operators in classical iterations. We instead use the network solely for manifold learning with a
least-squares Petrov--Galerkin projection \citep{Carlberg2011LSPG} for
the residual, and compare against an FNO, a U-Net
\citep{Ronneberger2015UNet,Takamoto2022PDEBench} and a Transolver
\citep{WuTransolver2024} on identical data at an equal wall budget
(\S\ref{sec:results:operators}). For hyper-reduction we retain classical NNLS-based EQ
\citep{Hernandez2017EQ,YanoPatera2019LPEQ,LawsonHanson1974NNLS} over DEIM
\citep{ChaturantabutSorensen2010}, ECSW
\citep{FarhatECSW2014,Chapman2017AcceleratedECSW}, GNAT
\citep{Carlberg2013GNAT} and their neural and high-order variants
\citep{NegriManzoniAmsallem2015,HirschPichiHesthavenNEIM2024,Nguyen2024HighOrderEIM};
the difference is that only the nonlinear term is sampled and that a
fitted rule is accepted by its held-out error and by re-draws, not by
its fit residual. On separable cells the discrete sine transform \citep{Swarztrauber1977}
is a direct solve faster than every reduced model we measure;
preconditioned Krylov methods \citep{HestenesStiefel1952CG,Saad2003} are
the iterative comparator.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

We turn a learned manifold into a practical PDE solver through three
components: a trial manifold whose decoder enforces Dirichlet conditions
exactly and carries nested linear correction directions
(\S\ref{sec:method:manifold}); a least-squares Petrov--Galerkin
projection of the discrete residual onto fixed weak tests
(\S\ref{sec:method:weak}); and exact preassembly of every linear term
with Empirical Quadrature for the one nonlinear term
(\S\ref{sec:method:precomp}); \S\ref{sec:decoder} gives the architecture.
Throughout, $u \in \Real^{n}$ is the
full-order state on a uniform grid of $N$ intervals per axis,
$\Aop \in \Real^{n \times n}$ the negative five-point Laplacian on
$[0,1]^2$; $R$ is the bank width, $k$ the latent dimension, $M$ the number
of weak tests and $m$ the number of quadrature nodes.
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} (Appendix~\ref{app:arch}) shows the data flow;
Appendix~\ref{app:method} gives the per-PDE derivations and exit codes.

\subsection{Trial Manifold with Exact Dirichlet Enforcement}
\label{sec:method:manifold}

We train a decoder $\mathcal{D} : \Real^k \to \Real^n$, $k \ll n$, with
latent state $z$ and no encoder in the deployed path, separable into a
frozen spatial \emph{bank} $\bank\in\Real^{n\times R}$ times a small
neural \emph{head} $\head:\Real^{k}\to\Real^{R}$, $k\le R\ll n$, the bank
built once per mesh from a random-Fourier-feature coordinate network
$g_\phi$ \citep{Tancik2020FourierFeatures},
\begin{equation}
  \bank_{x,:} = \bcmask(x)\,g_\phi(x)\T,
  \qquad
  \bcmask(x) = 16\,x_1(1-x_1)\,x_2(1-x_2).
  \label{eq:bank}
\end{equation}
To strictly enforce the homogeneous Dirichlet condition on the boundary
$\Gamma$, we utilize the smooth vanishing factor $\bcmask$, zero on
$\Gamma$, folded into every column of the bank, so that
$\partial u/\partial z = 0$ on $\Gamma$ at every resolution (homogeneous
data only).

\paragraph{The correction ladder.}
\label{sec:method:ladder}
The deployed model augments the head's output with $q$ fixed directions
$\corr\in\Real^{R\times q}$ and solves for the latent code and the
correction coefficients together,
\begin{equation}
  u(\latent,y) \;=\; \bank\big(\head(\latent) + \corr\,y\big),
  \qquad \latent\in\Real^{k},\; y\in\Real^{q},\; 0\le q\le R .
  \label{eq:ladder}
\end{equation}
The columns of $\corr$ are chosen offline once per checkpoint by a
principal component analysis of the head's own residual on the training
codes, ordered so that $C_{q}$ is a prefix of $C_{q'}$ for $q<q'$; the
ladder is nested and no rung needs retraining. At $q=0$ the model is the
frozen head; at $q=R$ the head is irrelevant and the model is the linear
reduced model on the bank's span, solved without a nonlinear iteration;
in between, $q$ moves the reachable set from the head's image towards
the bank's span, and the solved dimension from $k$ to $k+q$. Two consequences follow: the bank's projection error is a floor on the
achievable error whatever the head or the solver does, so nonlinearity
in $\head$ buys a smaller \emph{solved} dimension, not an escape from the
span; and all $x$-dependence factors through $\bank$, which makes node
sampling and exact preassembly available from one decoder.

\subsection{Projecting the PDE onto the Manifold}
\label{sec:method:weak}

Each PDE we consider gives a discretised residual $\rfull(u)$ that
should vanish at the true solution. We substitute the trial manifold
$u(\latent,y)$ into $\rfull$ and project onto a fixed, latent-independent
test space: $\tests\in\Real^{M\times n}$ collects the $M$ lowest
tensor-product sine vectors of the square, eigenvectors of the five-point
operator, $\tests\Aop=\rowscale\tests$. The reduced problem solved online
is
\begin{equation}
  (\latent^{\star},y^{\star})
  =
  \argmin_{\latent,\,y}\;
  \tfrac12\,\norm{\rweak(\latent,y)}_2^2,
  \qquad
  \rweak(\latent,y) = \rowscale_\star^{-1}\,\tests\,\rfull\!\big(u(\latent,y)\big)
  \in\Real^{M},
  \qquad M>k+q ,
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
with a diagonal row scaling $\rowscale_\star$ stated per PDE in
Appendix~\ref{app:method}: a least-squares Petrov--Galerkin condition
with an explicit test space \citep{Carlberg2011LSPG,LeeCarlberg2020}, not
tangent Galerkin, overdetermined rather than square, solved by a method
appropriate to each PDE's structure.

\paragraph{Elliptic (Poisson).}
The Poisson residual is $\rfull(u) = \Aop u - f$, and projection gives
$\rweak(\latent,y)=B_0(\head(\latent)+\corr y)-b_0$, $B_0=\tests\bank$
(Appendix~\ref{app:method:poisson}); the projector onto
$\operatorname{range}(B_0\corr)$ does not depend on $\latent$, so $y$ is
eliminated exactly \citep{GolubPereyra1973}, the iteration stays
$k$-dimensional at every $q$, and $q=R$ is a linear solve.

\paragraph{Parabolic (heat).}
The heat semi-discretisation $du/dt = -\kappa\, \Aop u$ is advanced with
Crank--Nicolson; the reduced step substitutes the manifold into the fully
discrete equation \emph{before} projecting and solves
$\latent_{n+1}=\argmin_{\latent}\norm{B_0\head(\latent)-D\,B_0\head(\latent_n)}_2$
(Appendix~\ref{app:method:heat}), a nonlinear least-squares problem, not
a linear system; at $q=R$ the trajectory is the exact modal propagation
of the bank coefficients.

\paragraph{Hyperbolic (Burgers).}
For $u_t+u(u_x+u_y)=\nu\Delta u$ with a sign-upwind stencil and backward
Euler (Appendix~\ref{app:method:burgers}) the weak residual is quadratic
in the coefficients, so $y$ is not eliminated in closed form; we damp the
$(\latent,y)$ blocks separately inside one Levenberg--Marquardt step
(\emph{block-damped} variable projection), which exits stationary
everywhere where a joint LM leaves $\nBlockJointBudgetExits$ budget exits
(Table~\ref{tab:solver-variants}).

\paragraph{Solver and exits.}
Each attempt solves the damped normal system
$(H+\lambda\,\diag(\diag H))\,\delta=-g$, $H=\jac{}\T\jac{}$,
$g=\jac{}\T\rweak$, accepting the step only if it strictly decreases the
residual: damped Levenberg--Marquardt with a monotone test
\citep{Marquardt1963}. Three exit families are recorded and never conflated: the scale-free
stationarity measure
$\station{\latent}{y}=\norm{\jac{}\T\rweak}_2/(\normF{\jac{}}\norm{\rweak}_2)\le\eta_{\mathrm{tol}}$
\refstepcounter{equation}\label{eq:stationarity}(\theequation), a
residual threshold, and the stalls, reported as early-stopped, never as
converged. No query uses the solution it predicts: the elliptic solve starts from
the cached training code nearest the projected source, the Burgers query
fits $(\latent,y)$ to the supplied initial field
(Appendix~\ref{app:method:codes}).

\subsection{Hyper-reduction}
\label{sec:method:precomp}

The non-linear manifold reduces the DOF count from $n$ to $k+q$, but a
projected term such as $\tests N(\bank c)$ still costs $O(n)$. For a fixed linear operator
the tested residual is exactly precomputable,
$\tests(\Aop\,u-f)=B\,(\head(\latent)+\corr y)-b$ with
$B=\rowscale\,\tests\bank$ assembled offline by two sine transforms, so
linear terms are never approximated; the Burgers advection
$\tests N(\bank c)$ is the only term that resists preassembly, evaluated
\emph{densely}, $O(nR)$ per residual, or on an \emph{empirical quadrature}
rule of $m$ nodes \citep{Hernandez2017EQ,YanoPatera2019LPEQ}, $O(mR)$. The rule is a non-negative \emph{per-node} weight vector, \emph{independent
of the snapshot}, fitted by NNLS \citep{LawsonHanson1974NNLS} to reproduce
the projected advection term at $n_{\rm fit}$ stored codes with a hard cap
of $m$ nodes (Appendix~\ref{app:method:eq}); changing $m$ re-solves the
fit, rules are not nested, and deployment selects among stored rules. \emph{A rule is never accepted on its NNLS fit residual}; we score it by
the held-out relative error $\rho$ of the projected advection term
\eqref{eq:rho} over states the solver actually reaches on trajectories
disjoint from the fit and evaluation cases, against a primary bar
$\rho_{\max}\le\nEqtopBar$ fixed before any certification job ran and a
tight bar $\nEqtopTightBar$, and then re-draw the construction: a rule is
\emph{confirmed} only if every re-draw passes
(\S\ref{sec:results:tunability}).

\subsection{Model Architecture}
\label{sec:decoder}

Three properties of the problem motivate our architecture: the latent
iteration is initialised cold, so the decoder Jacobian must retain a
well-conditioned linear component, which motivates a \emph{linear skip}
in the head; hyper-reduction retains a sparse subset of mesh nodes, so
the decoder must evaluate at any node in mesh-independent time, which
motivates a \emph{per-node bank}; and the family must reach from the
head's image to the bank's whole span without retraining, which
motivates the nested \emph{correction directions}.

\paragraph{Linear skip, for cold-start convergence.}
The head is a two-layer SiLU MLP plus a linear skip,
$\head(\latent)=\varphi_\theta(\latent)+W\T\latent$, so its Jacobian
retains a latent-independent component along which the cold solve can
descend; the elliptic solver checks the numerical rank of the projected
Jacobian at every query. The skip is a design choice, not ablated here.

\paragraph{No encoder; per-node bank.} No encoder is needed: the query
fits $(\latent,y)$ against the supplied input. The decoder restricted to
a rule's support is a cached block times the coefficients, and the
network's parameters do not depend on the mesh.

\paragraph{What is fixed, what is chosen, and how error is reported.}
\label{sec:method:tuning}
Every operating point uses one frozen artefact per PDE; selected at run
time are the rank $q$, the quadrature (dense or a stored rule) and the
stopping tolerance and budget; $M$ is held fixed along the headline ladder.
\label{sec:method:layers}
Every panel reports three numbers: the \emph{bank floor} (projection
error onto $\operatorname{range}\bank$), the \emph{best-found} error (the
smallest any point of the augmented manifold attains, from a multistart
oracle) and the \emph{solved} error the iteration returns.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Matrix-free Projected Operators via JAX}
Every linear term is preassembled once per mesh as the $M\times R$
matrix $B$, so the online residual and Jacobian of a linear PDE are
$B\,(\head(\latent)+\corr y)-b$ and $B\,[\dhead\;\;\corr]$, with $\dhead$
from a forward-mode Jacobian-Vector Product \citep{Bradbury2018JAX}; the
sampled Burgers advection uses the cached stencil block. The damped
normal system is solved directly; no Krylov solve is applied to the
projected operator.

\subsection{Training Protocol}

The bank and the head are trained in two stages, both as auto-decoders:
the coordinate network $g_\phi$ is fitted to the training states through
\eqref{eq:bank}, then frozen, and the head is fitted with a code library
$Z$ on the bank-projected states; $\corr$ is then the principal
components of the head's residual on the training codes. Sizes, cohorts
and offline cost are in Appendix~\ref{app:training-schedule}; every
headline uses one checkpoint per PDE, named by hash in
Table~\ref{tab:provenance}.

\section{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Problems}
\label{sec:setup}

All full-order comparators and reduced solves are benchmarked in one
Slurm allocation on one GPU per job to provide a direct
hardware-to-hardware comparison; \emph{no ratio is ever formed across
jobs or GPUs}. Table~\ref{tab:problems} specifies each cell and
Table~\ref{tab:spec} the sampled families.

\paragraph{Problems.}
\emph{Burgers (hyperbolic, the hero):} $u_t+u(u_x+u_y)=\nu\Delta u$ on
$(0,1)^2$ at $256^2$ intervals with a frozen-checkpoint ladder from
$64^2$ to $1024^2$; errors are against the same job's converged
full-order solve on the same grid (discretisation error
$\nPanelRefDiscretisation\,\%$ at $256^2$ against a $4096^2$ reference).
\emph{Poisson (elliptic):} $-\Delta u = f$, ground truth the verified
discrete solution from the direct sine transform, at $256^2$ and $1024^2$
on the square and on the L-shaped domain $(0,1)^2\setminus[\tfrac12,1)^2$
at $64^2$ to $512^2$, where a sparse direct solve replaces the transform.
\emph{Heat and waves:} $\partial_t u - \kappa \Delta u = 0$ with
Crank--Nicolson and $u_{tt}=c^2\Delta u$ with reflective walls.

\paragraph{Metrics and timing.}
We report the relative $L^2$ error against the reference of each cell,
maximised over cases and output times; for Burgers two maxima are
reported everywhere and neither is chosen, worst over \emph{all} times
(bounded below by the decoder's compression of the input at $t=0$) and
worst over the \emph{evolved} times $t>0$. Every cost is the median over repetitions of a completed computation
after a burn-in, arms interleaved in a recorded random order, in
float64; ``device ms'' is the device time with inputs and outputs
resident, ``complete-query ms'' the host-to-host time charging the dense
input and output, and every column says which; offline setup is charged
separately.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
FNO, a PDEBench-style U-Net and a Transolver are trained on the
identical dataset and split at an equal wall budget; only the FNO is
timed in the same allocation as the ROM. POD-LSPG runs through the same
weak objective, tests, solver and stopping rule. Full-order comparators
are always in the same job: preconditioned Newton (Burgers); the direct
sine transform and tuned CG (Poisson, heat, waves); sparse direct, CG and
IC(0)-PCG (L-shape). The NM-ROM of \citet{KimChoi2022NMROM} is not run
under this protocol and is not compared. Every table names its job
id, GPU and checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:provenance}), every solve carries
its exit reason, and every number is emitted by one generator from
audited records.

\section{Numerical Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate NM-ROM against an FNO, a U-Net and a Transolver trained on
the same data, against POD-LSPG, and against tuned full-order solvers in
the same job; every number is one checkpoint per PDE on opened
development cases except where three seeds are stated, and costs are
compared only inside one allocation. The losses come first.

\subsection{Comparison against Neural Operators and Full-Order Solvers}

\subsection{Best Configurations per Problem and Resolution}
\label{sec:results:best}

The same trained decoder serves every resolution. With one frozen

\label{sec:results:tunability}

quadrature rule and the tolerance move cost; the head's training data
and capacity move the floor and need retraining (Tables~\ref{tab:qxm},
\ref{tab:knobs}, \ref{tab:training}).

\paragraph{Rank against test count: the family at fixed $M$.}
The rank alone moves the error: hold the test count fixed, add correction
directions, and the error falls at every step while the cost rises
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder-main}). The ROM approximates the discrete system,
so the same-grid error is what the knob controls. Against the fine reference the knob moves the error only from
$\nPanelQzeroRef$ to $\nPanelBestRungRef\,\%$ ($\nPanelRefSpan\times$),
because at $256^2$ every rung's reference error is
$\nPanelRungRefOverDiscMin$--$\nPanelRungRefOverDiscMax\times$ the mesh's
own discretisation error of $\nPanelRefDiscretisationTwo\,\%$: the
reduction adds little on top of the discretisation. And a same-job full-order setting at $\nPanelFomBestRefMs$\,ms
reaches $\nPanelFomBestRef\,\%$ against the reference, more accurate than
every reduced subject at a small fraction of the top rung's cost; at
$512^2$ the ladder runs $\nPanelFiveTwelveQzeroRef$ to
$\nPanelFiveTwelveBestRungRef\,\%$ against the reference over a converged
same-grid error of $\nPanelFiveTwelveRefDiscretisationTwo\,\%$, all
\nPanelFiveTwelveReducedAboveDiscCount{} reduced subjects above it
(closest \nPanelFiveTwelveClosestReduced{} by
$+\nPanelFiveTwelveClosestReducedExcessPp$\,pp), and the \nPanelFiveTwelveFomBelowDiscCount{} full-order settings below it
(\nPanelFiveTwelveFomBelowDiscList) are coarser solves cancelling against
the reference, not better ones; at $1024^2$
the rungs run $\nPanelTenTwentyFourQzeroRef$ to
$\nPanelTenTwentyFourBestRungRef\,\%$ over
$\nPanelTenTwentyFourRefDiscretisationTwo\,\%$. The knob is a knob on the reduction error, not on the physical error at
these meshes. At fixed $M=256$ the ladder spans only $\nQxmTwoFiftySixErrSpan\times$
in error and \textbf{fails} the pre-registered bar
(Table~\ref{tab:qxm}; monotone on the evolved metric, every rung
converged, error span $\ge2\times$, plus two generator clauses as
implemented: $\ge3$ non-dominated points and cost span $\ge2\times$). \textbf{At $M=\nQxmFixedM$ the ladder $q=0,64,128,256$,
inside one job, is monotone, every rung converged, and spans
$\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in evolved error for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost
with $\nQxmNonDom$ non-dominated points}, and passes. $M=1088$ is the lane's pre-registered pure-rank ladder (the largest fixed
$M$ that holds every rung to $q=256$); the $q=\nQxmExtQtop$ extension did not converge and enters no span, and raising $M$ at $q=256$
saturates at $M^\star=\nQxmMstarTwoFiftySix$ with diminishing return past
it.

Seeds summary (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}): on \nSeedsCount{} fresh seeds the
scheduled ladder is monotone on \nSeedsMonotoneEvolved{}, converged on
\nSeedsAllConverged{}, spans \nSeedsErrSpanMin--\nSeedsErrSpanMax{} in
error and \nSeedsCostSpanMin--\nSeedsCostSpanMax{} in cost, and meets the
knob bar on \nSeedsKnobBar{} of \nSeedsCount, the pre-registered pass; the
third seed fails on convergence at one rung, not on span. The incumbent checkpoint that every headline uses beats all three fresh
seeds at $q=\nSeedsIncumbentBetterQs$: a favourable draw, and the
fixed-$M$ ladder is that one checkpoint.

\paragraph{Quadrature and tolerance move cost.}
Quadrature is a cost lever at equal error ($\nPanelEqSpeedupZero\times$
at $q=0$, $\nPanelEqSpeedupOneTwoEight\times$ at $q=128$) and the
tolerance $10^{-6}\to10^{-3}$ removes a further
$\nPanelTolSavingZero$--$\nPanelTolSavingOneTwoEight\,\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:tunability}). Of the two rule sets, the earlier ladder's
breaks upward at $q=256$ (its rule is marginal); the replication-selected
set is monotone at both tolerances and
$\nPanelEqtopTwinSpeedupMin$--$\nPanelEqtopTwinSpeedupMax\times$ cheaper
than its dense twins, its $q=\nPanelEqtopSingleDrawQs$ rules single-draw
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder-main}). Transferred to $512^2$ (Table~\ref{tab:tunability-fivetwelve}) that set
stays monotone at both tolerances; its $q=128$ and $256$ transfers pass
the primary bar there ($\rho_{\max}$
$\nPanelFiveTwelveEqtopxferQOneTwentyEightRho$,
$\nPanelFiveTwelveEqtopxferQTwoFiftySixRho$) although their sources are
single-draw, the ladder-rule transfers at $q\ge64$ do not, and at $q=256$
the transfer reaches $\nPanelFiveTwelveEqtopxferQTwoFiftySixErr\,\%$ in
$\nPanelFiveTwelveEqtopxferQTwoFiftySixTwinSpeedup\times$ less time than
its dense twin. Solver knobs alone move cost, not accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:knobs}).

\paragraph{Validate on reachable states, then re-draw.}
\label{sec:exp:eq}
A quadrature rule has to be judged on the states the solver reaches, not
on how well it fits the states it was built from. The NNLS fit residual predicts nothing about held-out error
(Figure~\ref{fig:cert}: a rule fitting to $\nEqtopStaticWorstFit$ reaches
$\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopStaticWorstRho$ held-out, $\nEqtopStaticWorstOverBar\times$
the primary bar).
\textbf{The pre-registered replication re-drew every construction}
(Table~\ref{tab:replication}): $\rho_{\max}$ moves by
$\nEqtopSpreadMin$--$\nEqtopSpreadMax\times$ within one construction, and
the ladder's rules are \textbf{confirmed at $q=\nEqtopConfirmedRungs$ and
marginal at $q=\nEqtopMarginalRungs$}; a single passing rule is not a certificate of its construction --- a
re-draw is (Appendix~\ref{app:extended:eq}). One gate is reported as failed: same-source rule pairs transferred to
$512^2$ are not bitwise identical (each transfer refits on its own
fit-state draw), and one $q=32$ source passes the primary bar in one
transfer and only the secondary in the other, a
$\nPanelFiveTwelveTransferRhoSpreadQthirtyTwo\times$ spread in
$\rho_{\max}$, measured and unexplained; no falsification clause fired.

\subsection{The Head against Linear Maps at Matched Dimension}
\label{sec:results:head}

At the same latent dimension the neural head is more accurate than any
linear map in the same bank; per millisecond it is not. At matched $k=16$ in one frozen bank per PDE it is compared with the
optimal rank-$k$ affine map, a quadratic map, the unrestricted bank and
POD-LSPG through the same solver (Tables~\ref{tab:head-burgers},
\ref{tab:head-poisson}). \textbf{The
matched-dimension result is the claim}: on Burgers the head reaches
$\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$ against $\nAblBurgersLinear\,\%$ for the best
linear map and $\nAblBurgersPodSixteen\,\%$ for POD-16; on Poisson at $1024^2$ it wins per dimension ($\nAblPoissonNeural\,\%$ against
$\nAblPoissonLinear\,\%$) and loses per millisecond to POD-128
($\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEightMs$\,ms against $\nAblPoissonNeuralMs$\,ms).
In three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}) the reduction layer sits
$\nAblBurgersReductionOverFloor\times$ above the floor and the solver
layer costs $\nAblBurgersSolverGapPp$\,pp: the head is the binding layer.

\subsection{Where the Family Collapses: Linear PDEs, Navier--Stokes, and the L-shaped Domain}
\label{sec:results:collapse}

On the linear PDEs there is no trade to make: the corrections are solved
exactly and the top rung is both the most accurate point and the
cheapest. Poisson at $1024^2$ (Table~\ref{tab:linear}): the rungs run
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\to\nPlinTenTwentyFourQtwoFiftySixErr\,\%$ at
flat cost and the top rung $q=R$ lands on the floor at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopErr\,\%$ as the \emph{cheapest} point
($\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopMs$\,ms against the exact transform's
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourDstMs$\,ms); heat and the reflective wave behave the
same way (Appendix~\ref{app:extended:linear}). \textbf{The collapse is
confounded with a weak bank, and we say so}: on both linear cells the
learned bank is $\nLinearBankOverPodMin$--$\nLinearBankOverPodMax\times$
worse than a POD basis of the same rank. No reduced arm beats the direct
solve on any of the three.

\textbf{Navier--Stokes collapses for a different reason.} On 2D
incompressible flow (a nonlinear residual) the family was gated before
any ladder ran (Table~\ref{tab:ns}): at $K=16$ and $K=32$ the head's
held-out oracle beats POD-$K$ by only $\nNsOracleRatio\times$ and
$\nNsKthirtyTwoOracleRatio\times$ against a pre-registered bar of
$\nNsOracleBar$ (oracle values are upper bounds: some fits hit the LM
budget), and at $t=0$ POD-32 is the more accurate (POD/oracle
$\nNsKthirtyTwoPodOverOracleTzero$): linear beats nonlinear where the data
lie in a low-dimensional linear subspace. Retraining only the head on the frozen $K=16$ bank with 128, 256 and
512 trajectories moves the held-out oracle from
$\nNsScalePlainNOneTwentyEight$ to $\nNsScalePlainNTwoFiftySix$ to
$\nNsScalePlainNFiveTwelve\,\%$ ($\nNsScaleGainFirstDoubling\,\%$ then
$\nNsScaleGainSecondDoubling\,\%$ per doubling), regularisation or a
smaller head moving the 512 value by under $\nNsScaleRegMoveMaxPct\,\%$;
the pre-registered rule reads this as ``\nNsScalePlainVerdict''
(Table~\ref{tab:ns-scaling}), the flattening leaning towards the head
function class, with the caveat that the frozen bank saw all 512
trajectories, so the small-$n$ arms are optimistic. A trade between rungs
therefore needs a residual nonlinear in the coefficients and a manifold
that beats POD held-out; Poisson, heat and waves lack the first, this
cell the second, and Burgers on the square had both yet still lost on
cost. Whether a reduced solve wins on cost at all is a separate question,
decided by whether a fast transform exists.

$\nLshapePodCheaperVsCheapestTwoFiftySix\times$ and

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
(i) Every headline rests on one checkpoint per PDE, and the Burgers one
is a favourable draw: it beats all three fresh seeds at
$q=\nSeedsIncumbentBetterQs$ (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}); the fixed-$M$
ladder is single-seed and the sealed cohort is not yet opened. (ii)
Uniform Cartesian finite differences, 2D only; small development cohorts
(Table~\ref{tab:problems}). (iii) No cold-start comparison against \citet{KimChoi2022NMROM}; the
linear skip is not ablated. (iv) Operator numbers are lower bounds (\nOpLaneImproving{}
U-Net/Transolver and \nOpFnoImproving{} FNO arms still improving);
quadrature rules above $q=32$ are marginal and transfer draw variance
(\nPanelFiveTwelveTransferRhoSpreadQthirtyTwo$\times$ in $\rho_{\max}$ at
one rung) is unexplained; cross-job spread of an identical cell is
$\nQxmAnchorSpreadPct\,\%$; the $1024^2$ frontier statement rests on one
job and the evolved metric. (v) The knob moves the same-grid error; against the fine reference it
moves the error only $\nPanelRefSpan\times$ at $256^2$ and a same-job
full-order setting is more accurate there. (vi) No theory: no
convergence guarantee, no quadrature-error bound on unseen states.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We built an NM-ROM for elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic PDEs with a
deployment-time accuracy/cost family from one trained model: the rank
$q$ and three solver knobs span $\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in same-grid error
for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost on 2D Burgers in one allocation. It does
not beat a tuned full-order solver or a neural operator on the same data
at $256^2$; where no fast transform applies reduced models are cheaper,
plain POD most of all. Next: lower-viscosity Burgers and unstructured
meshes.
