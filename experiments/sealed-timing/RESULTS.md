# Post-closeout lattice timing and end-to-end cost on validation inputs

Final numbers for this lane, generated by `report.py` from `results/summary.json`. **Every new number here is measured on exposed validation inputs** (2D `val32`, 3D `val64`, cases 0–5), timing only, with the frozen closeout settings and the closeout-selected matched spectral solver. It is not prospective and is not part of the sealed confirmation; the closeout's sealed timing verdicts stand as recorded (PROTOCOL.md §1).

## Headline

- **(a)** 3 of 3 previously failing 2D meshes (256², 1024², 4096²) pass every closeout timing check on validation inputs. Lattice ROM / matched spectral query ratio: 256² 6.67×, 1024² 6.49×, 4096² 5.65×. The spectral solver is faster on every mesh.
- Across all 8 meshes the prepared-input ratio passes on 8 and ranges 4.56–7.79×.
- **(b)** ROM input preparation ranges 1.41–350.8 ms and grows with the mesh (it builds the initial field on every mesh node on the host); spectral preparation stays 9.9–12.3 ms. End-to-end ratio: 256² 2.60×, 512² 2.69× (checks FAIL), 1024² 3.12×, 2048² 6.73×, 4096² 23.27×, 64³ 2.52×, 128³ 4.30×, 256³ 24.40×.
- End-to-end checks pass on 7 of 8 meshes; failures: 512²: rom_prep ['main/confirm fold 1.1230 > 1.1'].

## What these numbers support and what they do not

- They support: on validation inputs of the same family, same meshes, same GPU types and frozen settings, the matched spectral solver is about 4.6–7.8× faster than the lattice ROM per prepared-input query, with every timing check passing on the three 2D meshes that failed in the closeout; and, including input preparation as the pipelines are built, the spectral solver is about 2.5× faster at the smallest meshes and over 20× faster at 4096² and 256³.
- They do not support: converting the closeout's failed sealed timing checks into passes; any prospective claim; accuracy matching on validation inputs (established on the sealed cohort by the closeout, not re-tested); a claim about an optimised ROM preparation. The ROM preparation measured here evaluates the analytic initial field on every mesh node in NumPy on the host and copies it to the GPU, exactly as the frozen driver does; a device-side evaluation would be faster, and the spectral preparation is likewise an unoptimised host quadrature.
- The validation errors in the provenance table are six-case sanity values (they match the frozen pipeline's behaviour); they were not used for any selection.


## Glossary

- **Lattice ROM query**: the reduced-order model's full query (projection of the initial field, reduced solve, decode onto the 257² or 63³ scoring lattice).
- **Matched spectral solver**: the full-order pseudospectral configuration ($N$ modes per direction) that the closeout selected as the fastest one matching the lattice ROM's accuracy on the sealed cohort. Not re-selected.
- **Prepared input / preparation**: the solver's starting data on the GPU (initial field on the mesh for the ROM; sine coefficients for the spectral solver), and the work of producing it from the case description.
- **Prepared-input ratio**: ROM query time / spectral query time, both from prepared inputs.
- **End-to-end ratio**: (ROM preparation + query) / (spectral preparation + query).
- **Fused**: preparation and query timed as one call; should equal the component sum within 10%.
- **Block drift**: largest deviation of one repetition block's median from the median over all blocks.
- **Fold**: main-block median vs confirmation-block median, larger over smaller.
- **Paired per-case range**: minimum to maximum over the six cases of the per-case ratio.
- **Sealed / validation**: sealed cases were opened once in the closeout; validation cases were used for historical selection and are already exposed.
- **ST median error**: median relative trajectory error against the space-time reference, shown only to confirm the frozen pipeline reproduces on these inputs.
- **(a)**: the three 2D meshes whose closeout timing checks failed.
