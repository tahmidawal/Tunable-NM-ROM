Abstract 1 (233 words)

Each trained neural operator gives one accuracy-and-speed point. Changing the evaluation grid at deployment moves cost without changing what it can represent. A single trained decoder gives our nonlinear-manifold reduced-order model a family of operating points. From the negative results, we state when the nonlinear manifold is worthwhile. The decoder is a coordinate-network bank times a small head. The solver may activate a rank-q correction in the bank's coefficient space, with q the primary knob. Iteration cap and tolerance are secondary knobs, as is quadrature count. The residual is a matrix-free least-squares Petrov-Galerkin projection. We validate empirical quadrature on states the solver actually reaches rather than by its fitting residual. On 2D Burgers, the ladder in q is monotone on three training seeds and a sealed cohort opened once, meeting a bar fixed before any run. We time every comparison in the same job. Against a tuned full-order solver, nothing reduced is on the frontier at 256^2 or 512^2. On an L-shaped Poisson domain, where no fast transform exists, the reduced model is 2.8x and 6.1x cheaper than the cheapest full-order solve at 256^2 and 512^2. Plain POD is cheaper still for 15% more error. Neural operators trained on the same data are more accurate. On Poisson the family collapses to a linear model, as it does on heat and waves. On Navier-Stokes the learned manifold never beats linear POD at matched dimension.
