Burgers improvement campaign

The finished Burgers campaign found three weak spots in our reduced model: a coarse spectral solver is faster at equal accuracy, a tuned standard reduced model (POD with hyper-reduction) beats us, and we fail on fronts steeper than training. This campaign tries to fix each one. Each experiment was registered in advance with a rule that says when to stop.

So far: four experiments have finished and every one hit its stop rule. One paper claim is withdrawn (a cost advantage when one model serves several meshes). Three experiments are still running.

Problem 2 · tuned POD with hyper-reduction beats us

Faster time stepstop rule fired
E5 · 1.4 GPU-h

A Galerkin model with an exponential (ETDRK4) step evolves 14–24× faster than our current solver and is more accurate on 36 of 38 cases. But its worst case is slightly worse (1.82 % against 1.78 %), which the rule does not allow. POD with the same step is still faster.

Cheaper nonlinear termboth stop rules fired
E6 · 1.5 GPU-h

Smaller quadrature rules either fail the accuracy check or lose accuracy. Best gain 1.24× (1.5× needed). Fewer points do not save much because the per-step cost is not driven by the number of points.

Cost over several meshesclaim withdrawn
E7 · 3.2 GPU-h

A plain POD basis built once on the coarse mesh and resampled to the others is as accurate as ours at about one tenth of our total cost, on every workload tested (1–3 meshes, 10–1000 queries). The paper drops the comparative multi-mesh cost claim; mesh-independent cost stays as a property of the method.

Problem 3 · steeper fronts than training

Diagnostics, then a better-trained bankretrain running
E8 + E9 · CPU checks done

The CPU checks found that the learned functions put large high-frequency content right at the walls, where the true solution has none. Adding sine functions to the bank did not bring the steep-front error low enough. Now retraining the bank with steeper, lower-viscosity training data and comparing it against a control retrain.

Two other retrain ideas (cleaner training data, a wall-compatible bank) do not fit the budget and are deferred.

Things to know

  • All errors are measured against second-order reference solutions and carry their small, estimated error. POD comparisons are in-sample.
  • Compute so far: about 9 GPU-hours, against roughly 30 planned for wave 1.

Words used here

Reduced model
A fast approximate solver that works with a few hundred unknowns instead of millions of grid values.
Bank
Our set of learned functions that the reduced model combines to build a solution.
POD
The standard way to build a reduced model: a basis taken from snapshots of full solutions.
Hyper-reduction
Evaluating the nonlinear term at a small set of weighted points (ECSW is one method).
Spectral solver
A full solver built on sine waves and fast transforms; very efficient on a square box.
Worst error
The largest error over all test cases; the campaign's main accuracy measure.
Stop rule
A condition written down before the experiment; if it fires, that line of work ends.
GPU-hour
One hour of one cluster GPU.
