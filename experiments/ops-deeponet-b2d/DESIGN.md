## 1. The question

The paper's abstract names FNO, DeepONet, U-Net and Transolver. Three of the four have been
trained on 2D viscous Burgers at 256²; **DeepONet has never been trained in 2D anywhere in
this project**. It exists only as the JAX `deeponet3d` arm of the 3D lanes. This lane closes
that hole, and nothing else: one PDE, one mesh, one seed, one budget.

Two things this lane can settle and nothing more:

- **Q1.** Where does a DeepONet land against the FNO, U-Net and Transolver already measured
  on exactly this data, split, metric and wall budget?
- **Q2.** Does the matched eight-case ROM/FOM cohort verdict change? `no-second` found that
  four of its arms beat the ROM's worst case while every FNO capacity was worse — i.e. the
  FNO lane's "the ROM beats the neural operator" was an artefact of the family chosen. A
  fourth family is a fourth draw from that same question.

### 2.1 The DeepONet

The 2D analogue of this project's own 3D `deeponet3d`
(`worktrees/2026-09-20-paper-b3d/experiments/paper-b3d/operators/extra_models3d.py`), so the
paper describes one DeepONet recipe in 2D and 3D rather than two. Branch/trunk after Lu et
al. (Nat. Mach. Intell. 2021):

- **Branch**, over the supplied field and parameter channels: `levels = 3` levels of two
  3×3 convolutions with GELU, each followed by 2×2 average pooling; an adaptive average pool
  to `pool_bins × pool_bins = 4 × 4`; a GELU hidden layer of width `trunk_width`; a linear
  read to `cout × rank` coefficients, its weights scaled by 0.1 at initialisation as in 3D.
- **Trunk**, over the coordinate channels: sinusoidal features at frequencies $(1, 2, 4)$
  concatenated with the coordinates, then three linear layers with $\tanh$ between, to
  `rank` basis functions. `model.features` builds the coordinates on $[0, 1]$ and the trunk
  maps them to $[-1, 1]$ first, because the declared frequencies are relative to that range
  in 3D (audit finding M1, §A2).
- **Output**: $u_c(y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{rank}}}\sum_k B_{ck}(u)\, T_k(y) + \beta_c$,
  the same contraction and the same $1/\sqrt{\text{rank}}$ scaling as 3D.

It sits on the FNO lane's exact contract: it consumes `model.features` and emits the same
five evolved channels, which `model.predict` masks to the zero-Dirichlet boundary and
prepends the supplied initial state to. Nothing about the input, the output, the boundary
mask, the normalisation, the loss or the metric differs between families.

**One efficiency, and its guard.** The trunk is evaluated once per forward on the coordinate
channels of the *first* batch element, because `model.features` builds those channels from
the field shape alone and they are therefore bitwise identical across a batch. That is not
assumed: `smoke_second.py` asserts `features(...)[:, -2:]` equals its own first element
broadcast, on a batch of three. Evaluating the trunk per sample would cost 8× the same
arithmetic and change nothing.

**Declared deviations from `deeponet3d`**, all recorded before the job:

- The 3D pooling step is `reduce_window(..., 'SAME') / 8`, which divides a truncated edge
  window by the full window size; `F.avg_pool2d(..., 2, ceil_mode=True)` divides by the
  elements actually present. Shapes agree exactly (257 → 129 → 65 → 33 either way) and only
  the last row and column of each pooled map differ, behind a zero-Dirichlet boundary and a
  subsequent 4×4 pool. PyTorch's behaviour is kept.
- Convolution and linear weights use PyTorch's default initialisation rather than 3D's
  explicit `N(0, 1)/\sqrt{\text{fan-in}}`; both are fan-in scaled. The read-out's 0.1 scaling
  **and** its zero bias are matched explicitly.
- The DeepONet compresses the 257² field through a 4 × 4 × 8·width global bottleneck before
  the trunk, while the FNO, U-Net and Transolver it is tabulated beside are full-resolution
  field-to-field maps. That is what a DeepONet is, not an implementation shortfall, and the
  report says so.

**Capacities**, chosen to span the same parameter range as the families already measured
(FNO 1.2–17.9 M, U-Net 4.4–17.5 M, Transolver 3.1–12.3 M), and fixed before any training:

| arm | width | rank | trunk width | real parameters |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `don-small` | 48 | 256 | 384 | 2 569 349 |
| `don-medium` | 64 | 384 | 512 | 4 694 789 |
| `don-large` | 96 | 512 | 768 | 10 258 181 |

Everything else in the configs is copied from `configs/unet/medium.json`: float32 network
(float64 everywhere outside it), 4000-epoch cap, patience 250, batch 8, AdamW at 1e-3 with
weight decay 1e-4, `ReduceLROnPlateau(0.5, patience 20, min 1e-5)`, seed 20260914, no warm-up.

## 3. Protocol — matched to jobs 3780138 (`unet01`) and 3780139 (`tsol01`)

**Equal wall budget, not equal epochs, and not early stopping.** Every Burgers arm in this
comparison ended on its wall budget rather than by early stopping, so the budget is what is
being held equal. `don01` gives **3000 s per capacity** and the same 3e-4 refinement of the
validation-selected capacity, in one allocation with a 15600 s global budget and a 900 s
reserve, exactly as `unet01` and `tsol01` did.

Every arm's `result.json` records `stop_reason` and the epoch curve. **What ended each arm is
generated from those records, never asserted** (§A3). The report carries a
generated **`still_improving`** column (best epoch within the last 5 % of completed epochs —
`no-second`'s own definition, reused unchanged so the four families are graded identically).
**No number in this lane is an architecture ceiling**, and the report says so beside the table
rather than in a preamble. Where the budget binds, the report says the number is a lower bound;
where an arm early-stopped, it says that instead.

Float32 networks get more epochs per second than the float64 FNO. That is favourable to this
lane and to `no-second`, and the epoch counts are reported for every arm so the reader can see it.

### 3.1 Pre-registered selection metric

**The selected arm is `argmin` over all arms, including `refine`, of the mean over the 32
validation cases of the per-case maximum over the six output times of the fixed-initial
relative error.** This is the FNO lane's own rule, applied unchanged, and it is pre-registered
here *before* the job because `no-second` recorded the pitfall it creates: the mean-selected
arm had a *worse* worst case than an unselected sibling in both families (`unet-refine`
7.52 % vs `unet-medium` 3.96 %). Therefore, also pre-registered:

- the report gives **every** arm's mean, median, worst and >5 % count, not only the selected one;
- if the selected arm's worst case is worse than any unselected sibling's, the report says so
  **beside the verdict**, naming both arms;
- the selection rule is not changed after seeing the numbers, and no alternative rule is
  introduced post hoc. If a different rule would have chosen differently, that is reported as
  an observation about the rule, not used as the verdict.

### 3.2 Cohorts

- **Validation-32** — the 32 validation cases of the shared Burgers dataset. Selection and the
  headline accuracy table. Never used for anything the final cohort is for.
- **Matched eight-case cohort** — rebuilt in-job by `prepare_diagnosis_cohort.py` from the same
  4096-interval anchors the Burgers ROM lane used (job 3702709), disjoint from training by
  seed and by input content. Its index SHA256 is asserted equal to the FNO job's by `audit.py`.
- **Final / held-out cases are never opened.** `dataset.py` refuses those splits by
  construction, and `PROVENANCE.json` records `final_cohort_opened: false`.

### 3.3 Data

The pinned cluster cache `/cluster/tufts/paralab/tawal01/no_burgers_20260914/pilot-data01`,
copied into the attempt directory by the sbatch preamble and re-verified against its own
`DATA.sha256` before any training; the cache itself is never modified. `audit.py` asserts the
train and validation index hashes equal the FNO job's (`5333584b…` / `468b9e70…`), derived
from the archived manifest rather than from literals. **This is the same data, byte for byte,
that every other Burgers operator arm used.** (`no-second` recorded a provenance deviation for
its *Poisson* data; Burgers has none, and this lane trains no Poisson.)

### 3.4 Time convention

These operators are **direct multi-time outputs**, not autoregressive rollouts: the output
time set $\{0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25\}$ is fixed by training and the supplied initial
state is returned bitwise. That convention is kept unchanged so the comparison is like-for-like.

## 4. Pre-registered criteria

- **D1 (competitive).** The DeepONet is *competitive with the FNO* iff the selected DeepONet
  arm's validation-32 worst and median are each within **1.5×** `fno-large`'s. This is
  `no-second`'s V1 bar, unchanged, so the four families are graded identically.
- **D2 (cohort).** On the matched eight cases, whether the selected DeepONet arm's worst
  fixed-initial error is below the ROM's 1.8671 %. Reported as a fourth draw on Q2; a single
  case decides this column and the report says so.
- **D3 (honest negative).** If the DeepONet is the weakest of the four families, that is the
  result and it is reported as such — with the explicit statement that a 3000 s budget on
  which every arm was still improving cannot distinguish "this architecture is weaker here"
  from "this architecture needed longer", and that no DeepONet-specific tuning was attempted.

**No hypothesis is retired by this lane.** One PDE, one mesh, one seed, one budget, one
family implementation.

## 5. Timing — what would be admissible

A speed number is admissible **only** from a same-allocation panel in which the ROM, the
operator and the FOM are timed in one job on one GPU. `no-second` states this for its own
checkpoints and makes no speed claim; the only admissible route in this repository is the
`b-panel` harness, which prepares an `fno-cohort` in its JAX phase and then times a PyTorch
checkpoint in a **second process of the same allocation** (`fno_panel.py`).

Plan: **if and only if** `don01` returns a usable checkpoint and the lane's job budget allows,
submit a reduced `b-panel` configuration timing the selected DeepONet checkpoint beside the
ROM and the FOM in one allocation. Otherwise **this lane reports accuracy only and states
explicitly that no speed statement about the DeepONet is admissible.** Under no circumstance
is a time from `don01` divided by a time from any other job.

## 6. Gates (a job whose log fails any of these is void)

1. `jax_backend=gpu` and `torch_backend=cuda` in the logs; the run ends with `ALL-DONE`.
2. `sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256` and the data `DATA.sha256` both pass in the preamble.
3. Train and validation index hashes equal the FNO job's, derived from the archived manifest.
4. Every arm in the spec is present, complete, cohort-scored and timed (`audit.py` asserts the
   full arm set; a dropped arm fails the audit rather than passing quietly).
5. Every reported error is recomputed from the saved prediction fields by `audit.py`, which
   imports neither torch nor jax. The batch-8 selection score and the batch-1 reported score
   are allowed to differ by at most **1e-5** for a float32 network (the pre-registered
   tolerance `no-second` set after finding the observed gap reaches 2.5e-6; float32 kernels are
   not batch-invariant, so a tighter gate is unmeetable rather than more rigorous).
6. The supplied initial state is returned bitwise and the boundary is exactly zero in every
   saved prediction.
7. Exactly one job per submit directory, confirmed in `squeue` before and after submission.

## 7. Risks

- **The trunk is a plain coordinate MLP with sinusoidal features.** That is the recipe this
  project already uses in 3D and the canonical DeepONet, but a coordinate-MLP trunk is the
  known weak point of DeepONet on high-frequency 2D fields. If the result is poor this is the
  first thing a reviewer will name, so the report states the trunk form explicitly and does not
  present the number as the best a DeepONet can do.
- **Hyperparameters are inherited, not tuned per family** — as for the U-Net and the
  Transolver, whose own published recipes were also not used. `refine` is the only
  family-level tuning.
- **Single seed.** `no-second`'s `ctrl01` showed the seed and precision controls for the U-Net
  and Transolver; this lane has no budget for its own and does not claim seed robustness.
