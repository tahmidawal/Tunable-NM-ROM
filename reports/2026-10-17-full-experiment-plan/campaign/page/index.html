Does the reduced model's cost depend on bank width, and not on the mesh?

Priority 1 is done and the 3D speed study is done. In 2D, cost and accuracy do not depend on the mesh, and accuracy improves with bank width up to r = 384. In 3D, cost is also flat in the mesh, but a spectral full-order solver is faster at equal accuracy. Priority 2 (baselines, 2D full-solver comparison) is starting.

The four core questions

Cost vs meshpass2D: 8/8 settings flat from 256² to 4096² (largest max/min 1.016, bar 1.25). 3D: flat from 64³ to 384³ (max/min 1.007–1.091).
Accuracy vs meshpass2D: 8/8 settings give the same error on every mesh, within margin.
Accuracy vs bank widthmonotoneError drops from r=128 to 256 to 384 on every mesh. Going to 512 does not improve.
Cost vs bank widthfailCost does grow with r, but 7 of 8 kernel timings grow more slowly than their operation counts predict.

Against the full simulation (3D)

The kill criterion K2 fires: at 384³ a spectral full-order solver reaches the reduced model's accuracy faster. The reduced models run at 0.008–0.16× its speed. Against our own earlier 3D setups the new configurations are 2.0×, 5.4× and 17.2× faster.

Per the plan, the paper reports this plainly and leads with mesh-independence. 2D comparison against full solvers is next (priority 2).

Words used here

  • Bank width r: how many basis coefficients the reduced model solves for (the "k" in the question).
  • Mesh: grid points per side of the full simulation (256² up to 4096² in 2D).
  • Full-order / spectral solver: the conventional simulation we compare against; "spectral" uses fast Fourier-type transforms.
  • K2: the plan's pre-registered "we do not beat the best full solver" outcome.
  • BE, CN-R: first- and second-order time stepping.
