## 1. Question, inherited evidence, and hypotheses

Can a reusable ordered coordinate-network velocity bank, geometry-aware off-mesh weak
integration, and a stable pressure treatment solve fixed-obstacle incompressible flow
accurately at useful total query cost? The first target is steady solutions, not transient
trajectories or turbulence. An admissible failure is an intended scientific outcome.

The completed Burgers campaign, improvement campaign (especially geometry E2, hrcost E6,
continuous POD E7), and final 3D refinement all preclude assuming competitive advantage.
The historical spectral geometry crossover estimates are scenarios, not obstacle-flow
measurements. Periodic NS2D vorticity and NS3D curl/Leray/co-moving solvers are algorithms to
inspect, not validated obstacle references. The frozen main heat rollout is not a reference.
The current experimental fork is `93f399faa`, code-only mirror parent `5a64899fd`.

- H-ref: curved body-fitted mixed FE references meet independently tested spatial,
  nonlinear, mass, boundary, force, outlet and domain checks.
- H-rep: ordered bank prefixes improve held-out weighted velocity approximation, with
  pressure/force/derivative error reported separately. No monotonicity is assumed.
- H-int: positive geometry quadrature approaches the full mapped-element reduced weak
  operator and its Jacobian on both fitted and reached states.
- H-query: admitted bank settings meet target output accuracy at lower total query cost
  than the fastest admitted FOM and POD settings. Failure is recorded without replacement
  by an over-solved comparator.
- H-output: fixed-operator/fixed-output work is insensitive to reconstruction mesh size;
  full-field output cost is measured separately and is expected to grow.

## 2. PDE, pressure, stress and dimensions

In the fluid region, with fixed stationary solid boundaries,
$$\partial_t\boldsymbol u+(\boldsymbol u\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol u
=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta\boldsymbol u,\qquad\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u=0.$$
Density is constant and set to $\rho=1$. The stored pressure is kinematic pressure
$p=p_{\rm physical}/\rho$. For reproducibility, every output stores dimensional scales:
$x=D\hat x$, $u=U\hat u$, $t=(D/U)\hat t$, $p=U^2\hat p$; hence
$\mathrm{Re}=UD/\nu$. The 2D benchmark is computed in its published dimensional units;
the 3D case uses $D=U=1$. Reduced residual scaling uses these fixed scales, never a
candidate-dependent or viscosity-dependent reweighting.

The benchmark uses the gradient-form outlet condition
$$t_g=(\nu\nabla\boldsymbol u-pI)\boldsymbol n=0.$$
This fixes the pressure level: no extra mean-zero or pinned-pressure constraint is imposed.
It is NOT zero symmetric physical traction. The physical stress per density is
$$\sigma=-pI+\nu(\nabla\boldsymbol u+\nabla\boldsymbol u^T).$$
Force on the obstacle is $F=\rho\int_{\partial B}\sigma n_B\,ds$ with $n_B$ pointing
from solid into fluid (opposite the fluid outward normal). Both physical symmetric stress
and published gradient-stress force are stored; they agree at an exact smooth stationary
no-slip incompressible surface, but their finite-discretization difference is a diagnostic.
A reaction/weak force and independently integrated surface force are also compared.

For momentum and energy checks include inlet/outlet flux and traction. In particular,
$$\frac{d}{dt}\frac12\int_\Omega |u|^2
+2\nu\int_\Omega\epsilon(u):\epsilon(u)
=\int_{\partial\Omega}u\cdot\sigma n
-\frac12\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^2u\cdot n,$$
for the incompressible continuum problem, with $\epsilon=(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)/2$.
Under $t_g=0$, the physical outlet traction is generally $\nu\nabla u^Tn$, and is counted.
Finite divergence contributions are saved rather than silently interpreted as dissipation.
No unforced periodic-box energy-decay criterion applies.

## 3. Fixed geometries and parameter populations

### 3.1 Established 2D benchmark and first steady family

DFG/Schäfer–Turek 2D-1 specification: rectangle $[0,2.2]\times[0,0.41]$ excluding the
circle of radius $0.05$ centered at $(0.2,0.2)$. No-slip on circle and horizontal walls.
Inlet $u_x=4U_{\max}y(H-y)/H^2$, $u_y=0$, $U_{\max}=0.3$,
$U=U_{\rm mean}=0.2$. Outlet $t_g=0$ at $x=2.2$. Cylinder diameter $D=0.1$;
linear blockage $D/H$, not projected-area blockage. Published benchmark uses $\nu=0.001$
and $\mathrm{Re}=20$. Published drag/lift normalization is $2F/(\rho U^2D)$ per unit span.
Pressure drop is evaluated at exact boundary points $(0.15,0.2)$ and $(0.25,0.2)$.

Primary source: https://wwwold.mathematik.tu-dortmund.de/~featflow/en/benchmarks/cfdbenchmarking/flow/dfg_benchmark1_re20.html
Original provenance: Schäfer and Turek (1996), *Benchmark computations of laminar flow
around a cylinder*, NNFM 52, 547–566; the hosted high-accuracy stationary values are
attributed there to Nabh (1998). Save source values machine-readably, not manually in results.

First family varies viscosity only: $\nu=0.02/\mathrm{Re}$, fixed inlet and geometry.
Training Reynolds numbers: $15+1.25j$, $j=0,\ldots,8$; development: $16.875,23.125$;
validation: $16,18,22,24$. These are disjoint fixed design populations. Mesh seed 27102026;
learning seed 27102027; quadrature seed 27102028 (deterministic rules when possible).
Validation is opened only after architectures, rules, stopping policies and budgets freeze.
No existing sealed/OOD cohort is read. These tiny populations support pilot conclusions only.

The first cost contract is a converged steady query. Newton starts from the fixed-inlet
Stokes lift; its reusable setup and any per-query work are separately charged. There is no
physical time horizon or inlet ramp for a stationary solve; the sole output is the converged
state. A stationary computation is not evidence of a validated transient pipeline.

Domain checks hold the obstacle and local mesh resolution fixed: extend inlet to $x=-0.2$
with the same parabolic inlet data, and separately outlet to $x=4.4$. Compare on the common
original domain. The canonical DFG geometry remains the literature benchmark even if its
upstream length sensitivity fails; do not relabel an extended geometry as DFG. A failed

### 3.2 Project-defined 3D confined sphere

Candidate geometry: sphere diameter $D=1$ centered at the origin inside a circular pipe
radius $2.5$, axial coordinate $x\in[-5,15]$. Inlet profile
$u_x=2U(1-(y^2+z^2)/2.5^2)$, $u_y=u_z=0$, with cross-sectional mean $U=1$.
No-slip pipe wall and sphere; outlet $t_g=0$, no extra pressure gauge. Linear blockage
$D/(2R)=0.2$, projected-area blockage $D^2/(2R)^2=0.04$; both stored.
Pilot $\mathrm{Re}=10$, $\nu=0.1$; conditional family $\mathrm{Re}=8,10,12$ for training,
$9$ for development, $11$ for validation. Three training states constrain interpretation:
only ranks supported by numerical data rank are eligible, and this is not a broad family.
Force coefficients use $2F/(\rho U^2 A)$, $A=\pi D^2/4$, for axial and both transverse forces.
Pressure probes are at the sphere poles $(-0.5,0,0),(0.5,0,0)$.

This is a new project-defined case, not a reproduced published benchmark. Yin and Ong,
*Physics of Fluids* 32, 103605 (2020), DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0017349, study the
same class of confined stationary-sphere flow but use different Reynolds conventions and
regimes; their transition observations are not transferred to this pilot.
Barber and Emerson, Daresbury report DL-TR-01-001 (2001), continuum no-slip rows only,
https://epubs.stfc.ac.uk/manifestation/255/dltr-2001001.pdf, supply a separate low-Re control.
Their radius-based Reynolds number must be doubled for our diameter convention. The
finite source geometry and source outlet assumptions must be matched before a numerical
agreement claim. No slip-flow table is evidence for a no-slip calculation.

Check $[-10,15]$ and $[-5,30]$ separately, comparing local wake/forces and common-domain
fields with adequate pressure-drop interpretation. Empty-pipe Poiseuille is an exact
geometry/viscosity/outlet control. Full three-component 3D solves are required; symmetry
and optional axisymmetric controls do not substitute for them.

After stationary admission, short transient verification starts from zero with smooth
inlet ramp $a(t)=\sin^2(\pi t/(2t_r))$ for $0\le t<t_r=1$, then unity; $T=2$ in $D/U$
units, saved every $0.1$. BDF2 with a separately checked startup and initial steps
$0.04,0.02,0.01$; these are design settings, not a claim of resolved unsteady wake. Starting
zero with a ramp is a verification case, not the stationary timing baseline.

## 5. Reduced architecture, pressure stability and POD controls

Initial model is a frozen ordered linear learned velocity bank with solved coefficients,
not a nonlinear head. It learns the admitted steady training snapshots only.
$$u(x)=u_L(x)+\sum_{i=1}^r a_i\,b(x)f_i(x;\theta)
+\sum_{j=1}^{p_r}s_j\,v^{\rm sup}_j(x),\qquad
p(x)=p_L(x;\nu)+\sum_{j=1}^{p_r}c_j\psi_j(x).$$
Here $u_L$ is a shared FE Stokes lift for the fixed inlet; its pressure scales with viscosity.
The pressure POD modes and supremizer velocity enrichments are computed using training
only. Rank deficiencies are handled by a fixed numerical-rank rule, reported, not hidden
regularization. The first implementation is a hybrid neural-velocity/FE-lifting-pressure-
supremizer model: it does not establish a fully mesh-free pipeline.

For 2D, the normalized product $b(x,y)=x\,y(H-y)((x-c_x)^2+(y-c_y)^2-a^2)$ vanishes
on inlet, walls and circle, but not outlet. This proves zero correction trace only; it does
not prove divergence-free flow. Coordinates/factors are nondimensionalized to avoid arbitrary
scale conditioning. The 3D counterpart uses inlet distance, pipe radial factor and sphere
signed squared-distance; poles/derivatives require separate checks.

First prefixes $r=2,4,8$, pressure count $p_r=\min(r,\text{training pressure numerical rank})$,
one supremizer per pressure mode; unknown count $d=r+2p_r$. At maximum $d=24$, fixed tests
are 64 momentum vector modes and 16 scalar continuity modes (80 rows). FE smooth modes
and the entire maximum-r supremizer set form common tests. Constant-pressure/flux control
is retained; no sine-box test substitution. Numerical test rank and nonzero outlet flux
ability must be verified. Residual is the steady momentum weak form plus tested divergence,
whitened with fixed nondimensional H1/L2 test Riesz matrices, and minimized by damped
Gauss–Newton/QR. This is **weak-residual least squares**, not Galerkin or full nodal
residual minimization. $M>d$ throughout. No pressure penalty is added to conceal instability.

Pressure stability gate: whitened divergence inf-sup singular value ≥1e−3 and at least
half its full-velocity/same-pressure supremizer reference. Steady Jacobian condition number
≤1e10 after fixed whitening; independent finite-difference Jacobian relative agreement
≤1e−6 at nondegenerate directions. Both norm definitions and any rank trimming are frozen
before case evaluation. Boundary, mass, divergence and pressure errors are separately scored.

POD sees identical training data, weighted inner product, lifting, pressure, supremizers,
output schedule and tuning opportunities. Mandatory controls: unsampled full mapped weak
operator, and exact precontracted quadratic operators for the small mixed reduced space.
A square mixed Galerkin control may be included under its correct name; it is not confused
with the overdetermined LS arms. POD gets the same nonlinear stopping policies and any
later time-integration improvement. Fitted-rule failure leaves unsampled/exact POD eligible.
Report optimal weighted projection floors and gradient/pressure/force diagnostics before
attributing solved errors to representation or dynamics. Projection is a descriptive
best-fit geometry diagnostic, not a feasible PDE solution.

## 6. Quadrature admission

First control is mapped positive element Gauss quadrature on actual curved fluid cells,
with separate curved-boundary rules for force and flux. Increase geometry and quadrature
orders independently. A box mask, random collocation or Fibonacci lattice is not a substitute.
Fitted positive rules use decoder-output snapshots/moments from training only, never residual
snapshots. Candidate budgets m=320,640,1280,2560 (M=80); refit for bank/test changes. This
sequence is a pilot design, not a universal m/M scaling law. No fit on validation/reached
validation states; rejection is allowed. Save support, weights, fit/KKT diagnostics and seed.

Compare each candidate to successively refined full mapped rules on training, development,
and then frozen validation reached states: normalized operator error ≤1e−4, Jacobian ≤1e−4;
returned u change ≤2e−4 and p ≤5e−4; drag coefficient change ≤1e−3 relative and transverse
≤1e−4 absolute. Near-zero residuals use a fixed characteristic term norm denominator,
not division by the tiny net residual. Directional Jacobian consistency is distinct from
quadrature-Jacobian accuracy. Boundary-force quadrature is separately refined. A quadrature
pass is not a PDE-solution pass. NNLS convergence failure stops that rule; one predetermined
larger support is allowed within the listed ladder, not an unbounded fit search.

## 7. Accuracy, work and fair timing contracts

Primary steady pilot targets on every validation case: velocity relative weighted L2 ≤1%,
pressure ≤2%, drag relative ≤2%, transverse force coefficient absolute error ≤0.002,
normalized divergence ≤0.005 and relative inlet/outlet flux mismatch ≤0.001. Reference
uncertainty must be smaller than one fifth of the corresponding comparison tolerance,
otherwise matching is unresolved. These are feasibility targets, not a speed-win condition.
Keep medians, maxima, and counts beyond each target; never replace worst cases with means.

Three separate matched outputs: full u/p fields on common requested points; fixed probes;
integrated forces. Each query saves initialization, coefficient solve/evolution, output
reconstruction, total time, nonlinear iterations, Jacobian evaluations and peak memory.
Offline costs include data generation, lift/test/basis construction, training, quadrature
fitting, validation qualification and compilation. Report amortized and cold contracts.
Common geometry/offline cached preparation is available to FOM/POD too, including reused
factorizations/preconditioners and previous-query starts only in a declared ordered workload.

Primary timing is paired **same CPU cores in one allocation**: NGSolve FOM and CPU
NumPy reduced algebra/frozen tanh network evaluation, thread counts fixed. GPU training is
offline. Optional GPU reduced timings are heterogeneous-platform diagnostics and cannot
be substituted in same-device speed claims. Cluster GPU allocation/preflight is retained
and allocated GPU-hours count even when FOM kernels execute on CPU. Store both CPU and GPU
hardware identifiers. Run burn-in immediately before each timed block (GPU burn-in before
any GPU block), discard documented compilation/warmup calls, and store seven repetition
arrays with paired alternating method order. Cost and accuracy are outputs of the same
solver invocation; each repetition retains result identity/field checksum and work counts.

FOM ladder: admitted coarse/fine curved mesh and polynomial settings; match to bank accuracy,

Break-even uses complete offline cost divided by positive per-query saving; if saving is
nonpositive report no break-even, not a negative query count. No cross-job timing ratio.

## Glossary

FOM: full spatially resolved PDE solver. ROM: reduced model solving few coefficients.
POD: weighted snapshot singular-vector basis. Bank: frozen learned spatial functions;
r: number of selected learned velocity columns; p: pressure-mode count; d: all online
unknowns; M: weak test row count; m: fluid integration point count; N: requested output
mesh resolution. Lifting: fixed field carrying nonzero boundary values. Supremizer:
velocity enrichment paired to a pressure mode to stabilize pressure coupling. Inf-sup:
smallest scaled velocity-pressure coupling, not a solution-accuracy certificate. Weak
residual: PDE tested against smooth functions after integration by parts. Quadrature:
weighted numerical integration. Reference: independently checked numerical approximation,
not exact truth. No-slip: zero fluid velocity on a stationary solid surface. Kinematic
pressure: physical pressure divided by density. Traction: force per area on a surface.
Blockage: stated obstacle-to-pipe width or area ratio. Reached state: a solution actually
returned by the reduced solve. Gate: prospective acceptance rule. Query: all work needed
to return the declared output. Offline: reusable preparation, still charged in break-even.
