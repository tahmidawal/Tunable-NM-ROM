## Title

**Tunable Nonlinear-Manifold ROMs from One Trained Decoder**

Alternative: *Tunable Nonlinear-Manifold Reduced-Order Models from a Single Trained Decoder: Bank,
Head, and Correction Rank*

## Abstract (about 200 words)

A learned PDE surrogate usually gives one accuracy–cost point; getting another means retraining.
We show a nonlinear-manifold reduced-order model that turns one trained decoder into a whole
family of operating points chosen at run time. The decoder writes the solution as a fixed spatial
bank times a small neural head. At inference, the solver can add $q$ extra bank directions on top
of the head's output; $q$ sets accuracy, while precomputed quadrature rules and the solver
tolerance set cost. Three ingredients make this work: a bank that evaluates per node, so cost is
flat in mesh size; a linear skip in the head, so the solve starts reliably; and the split between
bank, head, and corrections, so the rank dials between the head's compression and the bank's
floor. On 2D Burgers the family is monotone, meets a bar fixed before any run, and beats linear,
POD, and FNO alternatives at matched size. On Poisson and heat the family collapses to a linear
model, which tells you when the nonlinear manifold is worth having. We also show that quadrature
rules must be checked on states the solver actually visits, not on their fit residual. The model
does not beat an efficient full-order solver in 2D.

## Evidence landed 2026-09-17 (coordinator notes)

- **Frontier, 256^2, one allocation, 35 timed subjects (`b-panel`, job 3780638).** **Zero of 27 reduced
  subjects are non-dominated** once the same-job full-order controls are included: Newton at `ntol 1e-3,
  dt 0.005` reaches 0.0489 % at 31.4 ms and `ntol 1e-4` 0.0338 % at 36.7 ms, against the best reduced arm
  (dense q=256) at 0.5194 % / 4164.6 ms. The abstract's sentence "does not beat an efficient full-order
  solver in 2D" is now backed by one-allocation evidence and should stay.
- **Empirical quadrature is a real, same-job cost lever at equal error.** At matched (q, M): q=0/M=64
  dense 284.8 ms / 1.8890 % vs certified EQ 57.9 ms / 1.8891 %; q=128/M=576 dense 1223.2 ms / 0.8930 % vs
  EQ 247.0 ms / 0.8925 %. Roughly 5x cheaper at four-decimal-identical error. Loosening the tolerance from
  1e-6 to 1e-3 removes a further ~25 % at negligible error change.
- **Open, must not ship unresolved:** the POD-LSPG arms at k' >= 32 are flagged not converged (final
  gradients 1.8e-1 down to 4.5e-2) while POD-512 prints 0.2184 % at 2928.9 ms, i.e. better than the best
  ROM rung on both axes. Either converge them under the shared rule or say plainly why they cannot.
- **EQ top-rung certification (`b-eqtop`, job 3780164, in flight).** The parent lane's blocker was the
  **number of fit states, not m**: at q=256 a rule with 64 fit states certifies on the primary bar at
  m=2048 (rho_max 0.1074) where the 8-fit-state rule gave 0.1678. `q-ridge`'s m ~ 2522 extrapolation was
  fitted through draw noise. Treat as provisional until the draw-replication job (3783811) bounds that noise.
