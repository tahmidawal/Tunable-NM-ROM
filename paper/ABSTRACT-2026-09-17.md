## Title

**Tunable Nonlinear-Manifold ROMs from One Trained Decoder**

Alternative: *Tunable Nonlinear-Manifold Reduced-Order Models from a Single Trained Decoder: Bank,
Head, and Correction Rank*

## Abstract (about 200 words)

A learned PDE surrogate usually gives one accuracy–cost point; getting another means retraining.
We show a nonlinear-manifold reduced-order model that turns one trained decoder into a whole
family of operating points chosen at run time. The decoder writes the solution as a fixed spatial
bank times a small neural head. At inference, the solver can add $q$ extra bank directions on top
of the head's output; $q$ sets accuracy, while precomputed quadrature rules and the solver
tolerance set cost. Three ingredients make this work: a bank that evaluates per node, so cost is
flat in mesh size; a linear skip in the head, so the solve starts reliably; and the split between
bank, head, and corrections, so the rank dials between the head's compression and the bank's
floor. On 2D Burgers the family is monotone, meets a bar fixed before any run, and beats linear,
POD, and FNO alternatives at matched size. On Poisson and heat the family collapses to a linear
model, which tells you when the nonlinear manifold is worth having. We also show that quadrature
rules must be checked on states the solver actually visits, not on their fit residual. The model
does not beat an efficient full-order solver in 2D.

### CLAIM CHANGE — the neural-operator comparison (`no-second`, jobs 3780138 / 3780139)

The abstract currently says the family "beats linear, POD, and FNO alternatives at matched size".
**The neural-operator half of that is no longer defensible.** A PDEBench-style U-Net and a Transolver,
trained on the *same* data, split, reference, metric, wall budget and selection rule as the FNO, beat the
ROM on the matched eight-case diagnosis cohort (worst relative error, accuracy is comparable across jobs
on these cases; timing is not, so no speed claim is attached):

| method | worst % |
|---|---|
| `unet-small` | 1.4712 |
| `unet-medium` | 1.5189 |
| `tsol-refine` | 1.5224 |
| `unet-refine` | 1.7110 |
| **`rom`** | **1.8671** |
| `fno-large` | 2.4829 |

So the ROM beats the FNO and loses to three U-Net capacities and one Transolver capacity. The earlier
"more accurate than a tuned FNO on Burgers" was true only because the FNO was the weakest of the three
operator families. Neural operators are also far cheaper per query, so on Burgers at 256^2 they dominate
the ROM on both axes.

**What survives:** the ROM beats POD-LSPG at matched latent dimension (a within-reduced-model claim), and
the tunability contribution is unaffected, since each trained operator still gives one operating point.
**What must change:** drop any claim of accuracy superiority over neural operators; say plainly that
well-tuned operators are more accurate and cheaper here, and that the reduced model's case rests on the
mechanism, the structural condition, and residual-based physical consistency rather than on winning.
This is exactly why the second operator was worth running: shipping with an FNO-only comparison would
have left the paper open to a reviewer who trains a U-Net.

## Abstract, revision 2 (supersedes the text above; ~205 words, no ratios)

**Title unchanged:** Tunable Nonlinear-Manifold ROMs from One Trained Decoder

A learned PDE surrogate usually gives one accuracy-cost point; getting another means retraining. We show
a nonlinear-manifold reduced-order model that turns one trained decoder into a family of operating points
chosen at run time. The decoder writes the solution as a fixed spatial bank times a small neural head. At
inference the solver may add $q$ extra bank directions on top of the head's output: $q$ sets accuracy,
while offline-certified quadrature rules and the solver tolerance set cost. Three ingredients make this
work: a bank that evaluates per node, so cached cost is flat in mesh size; a linear skip in the head, so
the solve starts reliably; and the split between bank, head and corrections, which lets the rank dial
between the head's compression and the bank's floor. On 2D Burgers the family is monotone and meets a bar
fixed before any run, with the test count held constant so the rank alone is the control, and it beats
the classical linear reduced model at matched latent dimension. It does not win outright: a well-tuned
full-order solver, and neural operators trained on the same data, are both cheaper and more accurate
here, and we report that. On Poisson and heat the family collapses to a linear model, which says when the
nonlinear manifold is worth having. We also show that quadrature rules must be validated on states the
solver actually reaches, not on their fitting residual.
