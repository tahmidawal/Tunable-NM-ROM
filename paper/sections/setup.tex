\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Problems.} Table~\ref{tab:problems} (appendix) specifies each PDE,
domain, discretisation, mesh, reduced sizes, reference and cohort. Burgers 2D
at $256^2$ with one frozen checkpoint is the hero; Poisson, heat and the
reflective wave are the linear cases; the L-shaped Poisson domain is the cell
where no fast transform exists.
\textbf{Metrics.} Burgers errors are relative $L^2$ trajectory errors against
the same job's converged full-order solve on the same grid (removing the
mesh's own discretisation error, $\nPanelRefDiscretisation\,\%$ at $256^2$
against a $4096^2$ reference), maximised over cases and output times. Two
maxima are reported everywhere and neither is chosen: worst over \emph{all}
times, which includes $t=0$ and is bounded below by the decoder's compression
of the supplied field, and worst over the \emph{evolved} times $t>0$, with the
$t=0$ compression as its own column. Poisson and wave errors are against the
exact discrete solution.
\textbf{Timing.} Every cost is the median over retained repetitions of a
completed device computation, timed in one Slurm allocation on one GPU after a
burn-in, with device synchronisation around every region, arms interleaved in a
recorded random order, float64 and highest matmul precision. Device query
(input and outputs resident) and complete host-to-host query are both reported.
\emph{No ratio is ever formed across jobs or GPUs.} Offline setup is charged
once per mesh and reported separately.
\textbf{Comparators} are always in the same job: Newton with a modal Helmholtz
preconditioner at several tolerances for Burgers; the direct sine transform
and tuned conjugate gradients for Poisson, heat and waves; sparse direct and
IC(0)-PCG on the L-shape; POD-LSPG at several ranks through the same weak
objective, tests, solver and stopping rule. Neural operators are trained on
the identical dataset and split with recorded index hashes, an equal wall
budget per capacity and the same selection rule.
\textbf{Provenance.} Every table names its job id, GPU and checkpoint in its
caption or in Table~\ref{tab:provenance}; every solve carries its exit reason;
every error is recomputed from the saved fields by an independent NumPy audit;
every number in the paper is emitted by one generator from those records.
