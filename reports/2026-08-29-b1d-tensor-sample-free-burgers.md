## The idea being tested

Poisson is quadrature-free because its residual is linear in the field and the field
$u = G\,h(z)$ is linear in the head output. Burgers' advection $u\,u_x$ is quadratic in the
field, so it is quadratic in $h$, and a quadratic in $R=32$ numbers is one fixed table:

$$
\Phi^\top\big(u \odot D^- u\big) = \tfrac12\, h^\top Q\, h,\qquad
T_{ijk} = \sum_x \Phi_{xi}\,G_{xj}\,(D^-G)_{xk},\quad Q = T + T^{(j\leftrightarrow k)},
$$

built once on the full grid from the frozen bank ($32^3$ entries), with Jacobian
$(Qh)\,\partial h/\partial z$. The FOM's sign-upwind stencil is not polynomial, but
sign-upwind is exactly *central difference $+\,\tfrac{\Delta x}{2}|u|\,\Delta_h u$*, and on
this family (positive blobs, zero walls) the truth is non-negative, so the backward-difference
tensor equals the full-grid oracle wherever the *decoded* field is positive. The only
discrepancy can come from ROM undershoots below zero.

## Findings

**F1 — sample-free at oracle accuracy, four for four.** The `tensor` arm reproduces the
full-grid `oracle` arm's rollout error to $\le 1.2\times10^{-6}$ absolute (per trajectory,
worst case at $N{=}128$; $\le 2.5\times10^{-7}$ at $N \ge 512$), with **identical
stop-reason histograms** at every $N$, in total and per trajectory. It uses zero sample
points, no NNLS fit, no node training. Against the sampled rules it is 1–9 % better than
NNLS-32 and equal to learned-32 (ratios 0.91–0.99 and 0.995–1.016).

**F2 — exact where it should be, and the deviation is fully accounted for.** Gate T0: on
the ~2900–3100 training states whose decoded field is entirely positive, $h^\top T h$ equals
$\Phi^\top N_{\text{upwind}}(Gh)$ to $\le 8.2\times10^{-15}$. The algebraic identity
against the fixed backward stencil holds on all 8192 states to $1.3\times10^{-14}$; two
independent accumulation orders agree to $10^{-15}$. The residual difference from the
oracle is therefore *only* the undershoot term: 6.7–7.0 % of decoded grid points sit at or
below zero (min $u \approx -0.02$ on training states, $-0.008$ along rollouts), and the
sign audit at **every LM candidate** of the oracle rollout (1339–1642 per $N$, accepted and
rejected) finds 60–68 % of candidates touch such a point. That is why parity is
$10^{-6}$–$10^{-7}$ and *not* $10^{-9}$: the tensor is not bit-exact against the
sign-switching oracle, and this report does not call it that. Gradient cosine vs the oracle
$\ge 0.999996$ on non-stationary candidates; Jacobian relative mismatch $\le 5\times10^{-5}$.

**F3 — cost parity with the sampled rule; no 1D speedup, as predicted.** End-to-end the
tensor arm is 0.98–1.05× NNLS-32 and 0.80–0.90× the oracle (whose residual touches all
$N{-}2$ points). In 1D everything is launch-bound, so this is an exactness-and-simplicity
result, not a speed result; the FOM comparison of the 08-27 report is unchanged. The
"lean" fold of the tensor into the head's last layer matters (2–4 ms faster than the
unfolded variant, `tensor_nolean`).

**F4 — what it removes.** For this problem class the tensor deletes the NNLS quadrature
fit, the $m$ sweep, the stage-2 node learning, and the checkpoint-dependence of node sets,
while matching the best of them. The Poisson quadrature-free result (linear) and this one
(quadratic) are the first two rungs of one ladder.

## What this supports, and what it does not

- **Supports:** a sample-free Burgers residual at oracle accuracy on non-negative data, at
  the same online cost as the tuned sampled rule, across $N = 128 \ldots 1024$.
- **Does not support:** bit-exactness against the sign-upwind oracle (undershoots exist and
  are measured); any 1D speed claim; sign-changing data (the $|u|$ term is not tabulated —
  the ideation synthesis lists the routes: polynomial FOM, split form, lifting); other $N$,
  other seeds, 2D. The $N{=}256$ job ran on a 40 GB A100 (others 80 GB) — only within-job
  ratios are comparable there.
- Single seed, 8 trajectories; the tensor-vs-NNLS percentages are at seed-noise scale; the
  tensor-vs-oracle agreement and the gate values are not.

## Glossary

- **Tensor arm / $T$, $Q$** — the advection term evaluated as a precomputed 3-index table
  contracted with the head output twice; $Q$ is the symmetrized table used for the Jacobian.
- **Oracle** — the advection term summed over every grid point; the best any sampled rule
  can do with this decoder.
- **NNLS-32 / learned-32** — the sampled rules: 32 nodes with non-negative least-squares
  weights, at grid positions (NNLS) or gradient-learned positions (learned).
- **Sign-upwind / backward difference** — the FOM's stencil looks left where $u>0$ and right
  where $u<0$; the tensor always looks left. They coincide wherever $u>0$.
- **Undershoot** — decoded field values slightly below zero where the truth is non-negative;
  the only source of tensor-vs-oracle mismatch.
- **LM candidate** — a latent state tried by the Levenberg–Marquardt solver, accepted or
  rejected; the sign audit covers all of them.
- **Stop-reason histogram** — how each of the 400 time steps' solves terminated
  (0 = iteration budget, 2 = stall); identical histograms mean the solver took the same
  decisions.
- **Gates T0 / TB / TA / TQ** — exactness on all-positive states; build-order agreement;
  algebraic identity vs the fixed stencil; tensor-vs-oracle at 32 random latent states
  (recorded, not asserted).
- **Lean / nolean** — whether the table is folded into the head's last layer before the
  rollout (fewer kernels) or applied separately.
- **e2e** — initial-condition fit + 50-step latent solve + decoding all states, per
  trajectory, median over persisted repetitions.
