1. **Blocker — reporting does not enforce acceptance gates.** `summarize.py:55–74` writes the summary and table even when the audit, order gate, reproduction gate, or recomputation agreement fails. The historical reference is optional (`:11`), and reproduction checks only intersecting names (`:64–67`), allowing missing arms to escape verification. **Fix:** require the complete expected arm set and mandatory reference, enforce every gate before generating accepted results, and label failed runs diagnostic-only.

2. **Blocker — “independent recomputation” does not validate the headline full-grid errors.** `audit_panel.py:66–70` recomputes headline statistics from the original reported error arrays. `panel.py:181–185` never saves the promised full fields; `make_configs.py:16` requests 50,000 random nodes instead of 100,000. For operators/FOM, cases beyond the first two have no full-error cross-check. Furthermore, NaNs can evade `d > threshold` checks (`audit_panel.py:46–65`), and missing random samples are silently accepted. **Fix:** enforce finite values, expected shapes/cases/sample coverage, save the promised full fields, independently recompute their errors, and explicitly distinguish sampled estimates from exact full-grid verification.

3. **Major — the order gate’s positive control does not test the registered contamination mechanism.** `panel.py:328–342` queues dense matrix multiplications instead of the FOM query, then tests their queueing delay. That can establish that queued work delays a sentinel, but does not establish sensitivity to the slow-arm carryover motivating the gate. The audit also trusts stored sentinel medians instead of deriving them from repetitions (`audit_panel.py:71`). **Fix:** implement and document a control that exercises the actual FOM carryover mechanism, verify that it creates the intended contamination, and recompute sentinel medians and expected block coverage from raw arrays.

4. **Major — checkpoint loading does not enforce cohort or mesh provenance.** `panel.py:309–320` accepts any loadable checkpoint at the configured path. `heatops.py:161–168` does not compare its mesh/configuration against the panel; `train_ops.py:158–159` omits training/validation cohort provenance from the checkpoint. A misplaced checkpoint can therefore be reported as a target-mesh, cohort-matched operator. **Fix:** bind each checkpoint to training provenance and require matching mesh, family, cohorts, PDE, output times, and frozen configuration before evaluation.

5. **Major — unsupported speedup claims and comparator eligibility.** `summarize.py:50–53,86` calls the unmatched-accuracy ratio a **lower bound**. That does not follow: an untested, more accurate CN–CG setting could also be faster. Additionally, the named comparator bypasses failure filtering (`:40–43`), and controls receive a “FOM chosen” entry despite the design explicitly forbidding it (`:44–53`). **Fix:** label unmatched ratios “unmatched-accuracy reference ratio; no bound established,” suppress named ratios when that FOM fails, and exclude controls from comparator selection.

6. **Major — FNO precision contradicts the declared operator protocol.** `heatops.py:110–117` always constructs FNO in float64/complex128, whereas DESIGN §5 declares float32 operator networks. This changes both achievable training progress within the wall budget and inference cost. **Fix:** either implement the registered FNO precision or explicitly amend the protocol and report FNO’s precision separately before treating the comparison as compliant.

7. **Major — accuracy and failure statistics come from a different invocation than timing.** `panel.py:174–180` records only the warm invocation’s fields/statistics; timed outputs are discarded at `:188–189`. Consequently, reported failures do not cover timed executions, and execution equivalence is assumed. **Fix:** retain timed outputs until timing stops, then validate their errors and solver status outside the timed region; associate these checks with each repetition.

8. **Minor — zero-ridge quadratic fitting is numerically fragile.** `podfac.py:85–86` solves the normal equations even for `gamma=0`, squaring the feature matrix’s condition number without a rank safeguard. A singular or poorly conditioned candidate can abort selection or produce unreliable reconstruction scores. **Fix:** use SVD/QR least squares for zero ridge and an augmented least-squares formulation for positive ridge; record numerical rank and reject nonfinite candidates.

The inspected algebra is otherwise consistent: the separable core yields target-mesh POD modes, orthonormal up to the declared truncation, with the correct row-major ordering; both POD CN matrices are correct. Quadratic centring, monomial ordering, ridge scaling, trajectory holdout, all-data refit, and core-coordinate QR construction match the intended formulation. JAX/PyTorch synchronization and untimed DLPack conversion are correctly placed. Operator loss, microbatch weighting, wall-time cosine, and validation-only checkpoint selection are consistent, with no selection leakage found in the inspected training path. The principal blockers are evidence validation and reporting enforcement, rather than the POD mathematics. This was a static, read-only review; no files were changed or code executed.
