**Overall verdict: WRONG as a production-ready pre-registration.** The question is useful, but the current design contains mathematical errors, an already-failing training-cost validation, overstated certification coverage, and negative controls that need not fail. These can change the stop-rule outcome.

This was a read-only audit. I made no file changes, submitted no jobs, and created no scratch arrays. Findings below distinguish source-confirmed defects from checks that still require a smoke run.

1. **Stop rule and decision rule — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The stop-rule text is copied verbatim, apart from Markdown presentation. Defining “us” as all six frozen configurations and allowing a different CP variant to match each configuration is a defensible comparison of attainable fronts. Allowing per-mesh competitor configurations also favours the control appropriately. However, §5’s instruction to choose the “cheapest” configuration independently per mesh conflicts with shared setup costs: configurations must be selected **jointly across the workload**, charging each shared basis/path once. Specify what happens when an X is unqualified, a CP arm is deferred, or a measurement is missing; none should silently disappear from a universal quantifier. The global error-tie allowance can make the rule fire without satisfying the binding casewise rule, while incomplete competitors or omitted costs can prevent it firing. Finally, “stop did not fire” establishes no comparative advantage by itself: it may mean only that one comparison was unresolved. [Design §§5–6](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-21-imp-multimesh/experiments/imp-multimesh/DESIGN.md)

2. **Cost model and bank-fit projection — WRONG.** The accounting skeleton is sensible, but it is not yet full-cost accounting. Rotation and our rule selection are explicitly zeroed; validation/selection is charged asymmetrically only in a sensitivity. Those omissions favour ours, as labelled. Conversely, omitting competitor Q2 gates favours controls, and omitting competitor configurations favours ours. Source-mesh CP preparation is incompletely specified: CPR’s source ECSW charge must include states, contributions and NNLS, and both CP variants need source arm-data preparation and compilation when the source is a queried mesh. Charge snapshot preparation, POD/certificate, state generation, candidate construction, NNLS retries, resampling/QR, initialization data and compilation through explicit shared-artifact dependencies, without duplication. Most decisively, the [bank log](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-20-jcp-consolidated/experiments/separable-decoder/runs/push_r3a/logs/2835788.out) records 144 seconds at step 5,000 and 409 seconds at 15,000: extrapolation gives **7,950 seconds**, **25.6% below 10,684.65**, failing the registered 5% check. The last two 5,000-step intervals jump to approximately 1,507 and 1,491 seconds, versus approximately 132–133 earlier; their cause is unknown. Scaling that entire heterogeneous total by an early-window H200 rate is unsupported. Separate steady training, compilation, terminal work and unexplained slowdown; measure or bound each. Excluding the later head refit is defensible only after checking every deployed dependency, including trust/initialization metadata, rather than just `g` and `B`.

3. **Continuous-POD resampling, coefficients and rule mapping — WRONG.** The underlying sine-transfer construction works, but the normalization and coefficient claims do not. The repository’s [DST-I is orthonormal and self-inverse](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-21-imp-multimesh/experiments/mr-burgers2d/engines.py:54). With orthonormal two-dimensional transforms \(U_s,U_L\) and coefficient embedding \(E\), the Euclidean-orthonormal prolongation is \(\Phi_L=U_LEU_s^\top\Phi_s\), **without another \(s/L\) multiplier**. Its physical nodal values already equal \(s/L\) times the source sine interpolant. Applying the design’s extra multiplier breaks orthonormality; another DST convention needs its own explicit normalization formula. Consequently, coefficients representing the same physical interpolated field satisfy \(c_L=(L/s)c_s\), so source snapshot codes are not mesh-independent and the proposed unchanged-code trust radii are wrong. After restriction and QR, transform the codes by the QR factors too, and detect rank loss before accepting an orthonormal completion. Truncation is a low-pass projection, not exact evaluation of the original high-frequency sine series at coarse nodes. The physical-node mapping is correct for one-based interior coordinates; for stored zero-based flat index \(p\), use \(i=p//(s-1)+1\), \(j=p\bmod(s-1)+1\), and \(p'=(iL/s-1)(L-1)+(jL/s-1)\). Unchanged nodes/weights constitute the same discrete sampling measure, but the FD stencil and residual change with mesh: quadrature accuracy does not transfer automatically. A common weight scale preserves the exact minimizer, but finite LM damping and stopping behaviour also require verification.

4. **Competitor tuning and qualification; inherited evidence and J5 — WRONG.** The grid tunes rank/support but fixes scheme, step and solver, and omits previously tested ranks 32/64, BE, native solves, Q-DEIM and the accurate-end rank-512 candidate. Calling these limitations out is good; it permits “against the strongest tested competitor,” not a general fair-front claim. The Q1 procedure largely follows the binding contract, provided it includes complete valid tight/tighter anchors and exact cohort coverage. However, a reported-only rollout comparison is not COMMON-BRIEF’s own-state Q2 certificate, and cannot confer Q1+Q2 numerical qualification; retain Q1-only competitors as an explicitly separate conservative comparison if desired. The inherited-certification claim is factually false: [p1’s analysis](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-21-imp-multimesh/experiments/jcp-p1-scaling/checks/analysis.json) gives **Q2 population 16 for every one of these configurations**, including those with 38 accuracy cases. `p1acc.py` also defaults `q2_cases` to `sub16`. J5 therefore closes the R128/R256 accuracy gap only if configured correctly; it does not close all remaining Q2 coverage gaps. Historical certificates are prior evidence under COMMON-BRIEF §3. Matching scalar errors to \(10^{-8}\) cannot replace in-job certification or establish identical trajectories, iterations and solver exits in J6.

5. **Scoring contract — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Dev6+val32, explicit developmental/in-sample labelling, unopened test/sealed cohorts, E0 ST, fixed-initial-norm error, and store-derived eligibility are appropriate. Restrict full fields with zero walls to the \(257^2\) lattice; do not stride the interior-only flattened array directly. Pooling worst and median over mesh–case pairs is mathematically well-defined, but also report per-mesh results because a pooled worst can conceal a degradation on one mesh and the pooled median counts each physical case repeatedly. The rule \(W_{\rm CP}\le W_X+2\max_c\varepsilon_c\) is a conservative aggregate allowance, **not the binding casewise \(2\varepsilon_c\) rule**: uncertainty on an unrelated easy case can excuse degradation on the worst case. Register a casewise matching requirement or obtain an explicit amendment for an aggregate uncertainty rule, and report casewise resolved differences alongside it. Distinguish reference-ineligible cases from solver failures; failures must not be removed from the accuracy population. Reporting median without matching it also falls short of the lane brief’s “accuracy-matched (worst and median)” wording, although the stop rule itself explicitly uses worst.

The most important missing control is **physical field/coefficient consistency across meshes**, including the trust-radius transformation: orthonormality and round-trip checks alone cannot detect a consistently wrong representation convention.

7. **Feasibility, budget and timing — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The basis-size estimates are credible, but array sizes alone do not establish feasibility. At 4096, \(\Phi_{384}\) occupies **51.51 GB**; simultaneously retaining \(\Phi,\Delta\Phi,D\Phi\) requires **154.54 GB before residual/Jacobian temporaries**, exceeding usable H200 memory. Blocking contributions must avoid full derivative tables and full Jacobians, not merely block the final candidate matrix. [`stencil_rows`](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-21-imp-multimesh/experiments/jcp-hr-baselines/hrcore.py:250) also concatenates a zero row onto the full basis, introducing another potentially large allocation. Account for DST workspaces, resampling copies, states, outputs, NNLS workspaces and host memory. Ours’ 68.7-GB table and a CP basis cannot safely be assumed co-resident; reloads/process isolation need an explicit schedule. The 4.6-hour estimate is unsupported by phase-level projections: the [1024 tuning record](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-21-imp-multimesh/experiments/jcp-hr-baselines/inputs/tuning_json/t2d1024a.tune.json) spends **2,286.50 seconds** on `ecsw_r384`, and its path terminates at a KKT optimum with **3,457** active nodes, not 6,144. Stopping at 3,072 cannot simply be assumed to halve that cost. J4 must cover two source bases, six refit rank paths, FOM states and qualification; dense Q2 analogues are not explicitly budgeted. Same-job H200 A–B–A is appropriate, but burn-in after reloads/host work, repetitions, saved timing arrays, synchronized output checks and cold-compilation cache policy must be registered. A single long B block between A endpoints cannot detect all intervening drift. Deferring competitors must produce “incomplete/inconclusive,” not evidence that ours wins.

8. **PM at 4096 as a cost lower bound — WRONG.** The approximately 0.93-TB snapshot matrix demonstrates that this dense implementation is unsuitable for the proposed host allocation, not that the recipe is intrinsically unconstructible: streaming/out-of-core methods can implement it differently. More importantly, \(576\) times the mean time of **24 sampled trajectories is an estimate**, not a guaranteed lower bound on the sum of 576 trajectory times. Setting subsequent phases to zero does not repair that statistical issue. The state generator also alternates BE and CNR in [`ecsw_states`](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-21-imp-multimesh/experiments/jcp-hr-baselines/build.py:238), whereas the snapshot recipe is CNR, so the proposed sample is not even automatically the same workload. Call it an estimated snapshot-only cost with uncertainty, or construct a justified lower bound. As written, it could decide the descriptive PM verdict on any of the **12 workloads containing 4096²**; without measurements, no particular crossing is established. It must never determine the CP stop rule, and “loses regardless of accuracy” is warranted only for a valid cost bound scoped to the exact recipe.

9. **Other threats to the conclusion — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The experiment can establish an implementation-specific developmental comparison, not a universal advantage of coordinate-network representations. The CP control currently pays for fine-grid initial projection and FD evolution even though a continuous POD representation may support reusable reduced evolution and cheaper initialization; the registered control is legitimate, but it is not the strongest possible mesh-transferable POD method. Similarly, six full-field outputs define a specific workload, not mesh-free evaluation generally. Input construction/transfer and output transfer/storage boundaries must be identical and explicit. The statement “our worst error is mesh-independent” should be an empirical observation with tolerance, not a premise allowing missed measurements. Break-even cost is also generally a **lower envelope of configuration-dependent lines**, so there may be multiple switches, a switch below the registered range, or no switch—not necessarily one \(q^*\). Finally, small cost differences need timing/projection uncertainty treatment; an exact floating-point `<=` is an unstable scientific decision boundary.
