\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every number below is a single training seed on opened development cases, one
checkpoint per PDE; costs are compared only inside one allocation, and where two
numbers come from different jobs the text compares accuracy only. The losses
come first.

\subsection{Nothing reduced is on the frontier at $256^2$}
\label{sec:exp:panel}

Table~\ref{tab:tunability} and Figure~\ref{fig:family} come from one
allocation on one A100 with $\nPanelSubjectCount$ timed subjects: the
correction ladder with dense and certified-quadrature advection at two
tolerances, POD-LSPG at six ranks, the unrestricted bank, the trained FNO, and
eight full-order Newton settings, all against the same converged reference.
On (GPU ms, worst \evolved\ error) \textbf{$\nPanelReducedNonDomEvolved$ of the
$\nPanelReducedCount$ reduced subjects are non-dominated} once the full-order
controls are included, and likewise on the \alltimes\ metric
($\nPanelReducedNonDomAll$ of $\nPanelReducedCount$). The most accurate reduced
subject is POD-512 at $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ and
$\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms; Newton at tolerance $10^{-3}$, $\Delta t=0.005$,
reaches $\nPanelFomNtThreeFineErr\,\%$ at $\nPanelFomNtThreeFineMs$\,ms. This is
the evidence for the abstract's sentence that the model does not beat an
efficient full-order solver in 2D.

Within the reduced subjects: the dense ladder $q=0,\dots,256$ is monotone on
both metrics with every rung converged, spanning $\nPanelDenseErrSpan\times$ in
evolved error for $\nPanelDenseCostSpan\times$ in cost, but it raises
$M=4(K+q)$ with $q$ (\S\ref{sec:exp:qxm} separates the two). Certified
quadrature is a cost lever at equal error: at $q=0$, $\nPanelEqMsZero$\,ms
against $\nPanelDenseMsZero$\,ms dense for $\nPanelEqErrZero$ against
$\nPanelDenseErrZero\,\%$; at $q=128$, $\nPanelEqMsOneTwoEight$ against
$\nPanelDenseMsOneTwoEight$\,ms for $\nPanelEqErrOneTwoEight$ against
$\nPanelDenseErrOneTwoEight\,\%$. Loosening the tolerance from $10^{-6}$ to
$10^{-3}$ removes a further $\nPanelTolSavingZero$--$\nPanelTolSavingOneTwoEight\,\%$
at negligible error change. The EQ ladder of this panel is \emph{not}
monotone at the top ($q=256$: $\nPanelEqQtwofiftysixEvolved\,\%$ against
$\nPanelEqQonetwentyeightEvolved\,\%$ at $q=128$) because those rungs carried
rules certified only on the secondary bar; \S\ref{sec:exp:eq} shows the fix.

Two facts about the classical baseline sit here. On the \evolved\ metric POD-512
beats the best dense rung on both axes ($\nPanelPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ at
$\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms against $\nPanelBestRungErr\,\%$ at
$\nPanelBestRungMs$\,ms), so the head-ablation statement that no POD rank up to
$k'=128$ matches the head holds but does not extend to $k'=512$. On the
\alltimes\ metric POD-512 is dominated by the unrestricted bank
($\nPanelFreeAll\,\%$ at $\nPanelFreeMs$\,ms) and the reduced-only frontier is
$\nPanelReducedOnlyFrontierEq$ certified-EQ rungs plus that endpoint, with no
POD rank on it; the metric decides the comparison, which is why both are
printed. The POD ranks $k'\ge32$ carry a ``strict'' flag of no because their
initial fit is an attainable square system at round-off
($\le\nPanelStrictNoIcResidualMax$ relative), so the stationarity ratio is
$0/0$; every one of their steps exits on the gradient rule and each solve sits
on its projection floor (solved/best-found $\le\nPanelPodSolvedOverFloorMax$).
The baseline is not handicapped.

\subsection{Rank against test count: the family at fixed $M$}
\label{sec:exp:qxm}

The crossed grid (Table~\ref{tab:qxm}) runs $q\in\{0,\dots,256\}$ against fixed
$M\in\{256,1088\}$, the schedules $M\in\{4,8,16\}(K+q)$ and bridge cells, a
saturation sweep in $M$ and a solver control, in three jobs whose shared cells
agree to $10^{-9}$. \textbf{At fixed $M=\nQxmFixedM$ the pure-rank ladder
$q=0,64,128,256$, inside one job, is monotone, every rung converged, and spans
$\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in evolved error for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost with
$\nQxmNonDom$ non-dominated points}: it passes the bar fixed on 15~September
(monotone, at least three non-dominated points, at least $2\times$ on both
axes, nothing early-stopped) with the test count held constant. The scheduled
ladder spans $\nQxmSpanScheduledFour\times$; its log-span is
$\nQxmShareRankCorner\,\%$ rank and $\nQxmShareTestsCorner\,\%$ test count
along the corner path, and on the balanced sub-grid the rank main effect
carries $\nQxmAnovaRank\,\%$ of the variance in log error against
$\nQxmAnovaTests\,\%$. The test count does $\nQxmRungTestShares\,\%$ of the work
at successive rungs: it buys the bottom of the ladder and the rank buys the
top. It does not simply saturate: at $q=0$ it is flat past
$M^\star=\nQxmMstarZero$ but that stretch costs $\nQxmMstarZeroCost\times$, and
at $q=256$ the $M$ effect is still $\nQxmSpanMatQtwoFiftySix\times$; the
absolute $M$ needed grows with $q$ and no tests-per-unknown invariance is
claimed. The solver switch at $K+q>64$ is inert (Gauss--Jordan against LU,
relative difference $\nQxmSolverControlRelDiff$). Best accuracy anywhere:
$\nQxmBestErr\,\%$ at $(q=256,M=\nQxmBestM)$.

\subsection{Quadrature rules must be certified on reachable states}
\label{sec:exp:eq}

A rule's NNLS fit residual is a property of its fitting set, not a certificate:
the static-snapshot rule at $q=\nEqtopStaticWorstQ$ fits to
$\nEqtopStaticWorstFit$ and reaches $\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopStaticWorstRho$ on
held-out reachable states, four times the primary bar. Certifying every rule by
\eqref{eq:rho} on states the dense solver visits, the top-rung blocker of the
earlier lanes turns out to be the number of fit states, not the node count:
the parent rules at $q=128$ and $256$, fitted on 14 and 8 states, reach
$\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopParentOneTwentyEightRho$ and $\nEqtopParentTwoFiftySixRho$
at $m=2048$ and fail, while 64-state rules at the same $m$ reach
$\nEqtopPrimaryOneTwentyEightRho$ and $\nEqtopPrimaryTwoFiftySixRho$ and pass
(Table~\ref{tab:eqcert}); the extrapolated crossing node count of the parent
construction is withdrawn (fitted through draw noise), as is the inherited
fit-state convention for $q\ge128$. Rebuilt with the cheapest primary-certified
rule per rung and timed in one allocation, the EQ ladder is monotone on the
evolved metric, every rung converged, running
$\nEqtopLadderFirstErr\to\nEqtopLadderLastErr\,\%$ at
$\nEqtopLadderFirstMs\to\nEqtopLadderLastMs$\,ms, $\nEqtopCostRatioMin$--$\nEqtopCostRatioMax\times$
the same-job dense cost; at $q=256$ the 8-state rule gave
$\nEqtopTopOldRuleErr\,\%$ where the 64-state rule gives $\nEqtopTopEqErr\,\%$
against dense $\nEqtopTopDenseErr\,\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:eqladder}). \textbf{All
of this is one draw}: a second draw at $q=64$ moved $\rho_{\max}$ from
$\nEqtopQsixtyFourArchivedRho$ to $\nEqtopQsixtyFourRedrawRho$ and flipped the
flag, so the draw is first-order; job $\nEqtopPendingJob$ measures that spread,
and until then the ladder is ``certified in this draw''.

\subsection{The head earns its place per dimension, not per millisecond}
\label{sec:exp:head}

At matched $k=16$ in one frozen bank per PDE, the head is compared with the
optimal rank-$k$ affine map inside the same bank, a quadratic map, the
unrestricted bank, and POD-LSPG at ranks $8$--$128$, through the same weak
objective, tests, solver and stopping rule (Tables~\ref{tab:head-burgers},
\ref{tab:head-poisson}). On Burgers the head reaches $\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$
worst same-grid error against $\nAblBurgersLinear\,\%$ for the best linear map
and $\nAblBurgersQuad\,\%$ quadratic; no POD rank up to $k'=128$ matches it
(POD-128: $\nAblBurgersPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblBurgersPodOneTwentyEightMs$\,ms against $\nAblBurgersNeuralMs$\,ms), with
the POD rungs run on exact dense advection, which favours them. On Poisson at
$1024^2$ the head wins per dimension ($\nAblPoissonNeural\,\%$ against
$\nAblPoissonLinear\,\%$ linear, $\nAblPoissonPodSixteen\,\%$ POD-16) and loses
per millisecond: POD-128 reaches $\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEightMs$\,ms against the head's
$\nAblPoissonNeuralMs$\,ms, because that query is dominated by projection and
decode. In three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}): on Burgers the floor is
$\nAblBurgersFloor\,\%$, best-found $\nAblBurgersBestFound\,\%$, solved
$\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$; the solver layer costs $\nAblBurgersSolverGapPp$
percentage points and the reduction layer sits
$\nAblBurgersReductionOverFloor\times$ above the floor. The head is the binding
layer, and training alone does not close it: no retrained head on the same bank
beats the incumbent's best-found $\nTrainIncumbentBest\,\%$ (best
$\nTrainBestRetrained\,\%$; the like-for-like retrain lands at
$\nTrainLikeForLike\,\%$, so that pipeline does not reproduce the incumbent and
the verdict is a caveat, Table~\ref{tab:training}); on Poisson a wider head
moves the best-found/floor ratio from $\nPlinHeadRatioPrimary$ to
$\nPlinHeadRatioBest$ and no further (Table~\ref{tab:head-capacity}).

\subsection{Cached cost is flat in the mesh; nothing crosses over}
\label{sec:exp:mesh}

With one frozen checkpoint per PDE transferred across $64$--$1024$ intervals,
the cached reduced solve costs $\nMeshBurgersCachedFirst\to\nMeshBurgersCachedLast$\,ms
on Burgers and $\nMeshPoissonCachedFirst\to\nMeshPoissonCachedLast$\,ms on
Poisson while the unknowns grow $\nMeshBurgersUnknownGrowth\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:mesh}); the complete query grows
$\nMeshBurgersCompleteRatio\times$ and $\nMeshPoissonCompleteRatio\times$
through dense input and output. Against the cheapest same-job full-order arm
meeting the target there is no crossover at any rung (FOM/ROM
$\nMeshBurgersFomOverRomRange$ on Burgers, $\nMeshPoissonFomOverRomRange$
against the direct transform on Poisson), and the Poisson error is pinned at
its floor at every rung. A kernel-level port at bit-level parity makes the
frozen Burgers query $\nSpeedGpuTwoFiftySix$ faster at $256^2$ and
$\nSpeedGpuTenTwentyFour$ at $1024^2$, short of its $2\times$ target; the query
is kernel-count bound ($\nSpeedFixedUs\,\mu$s per step plus
$\nSpeedPerIterUs\,\mu$s per iteration), so anything beyond needs fewer
iterations (Table~\ref{tab:speed}).

\subsection{Where the family collapses: linear PDEs}
\label{sec:exp:linear}

When the residual is linear in the coefficients the corrections are eliminated
exactly and the ladder has a different shape. Poisson at $1024^2$ ($R=512$,
$K=32$; Table~\ref{tab:linear}): the rungs $q=0,\dots,256$ run
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\to\nPlinTenTwentyFourQtwoFiftySixErr\,\%$ at a flat
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMin$--$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMax$\,ms, and the top
rung $q=R$, the full-bank QR solve, lands on the floor at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopErr\,\%$ and is the \emph{cheapest} point at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopMs$\,ms; the pre-registered degenerate-curve criterion
passes outright, and at $256^2$ only its literal cost-span clause fails,
because the top rung is $\nPlinTwoFiftySixTopOverMidCost\times$ cheaper than
the middle rungs. Solver effort buys nothing (floor
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor$, best-found $\nPlinTenTwentyFourBestFoundQzero$,
solved $\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\,\%$); the direct transform is exact at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourDstMs$\,ms. Heat at $1024^2$ (an earlier cell of the same
family, Table~\ref{tab:heat}): the bank evolved linearly reaches
$\nHeatBankErrTenTwentyFour\,\%$ at $\nHeatBankMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms against the
head's $\nHeatHeadErrTenTwentyFour\,\%$ at $\nHeatHeadMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms, with
the direct solve at $\nHeatFomMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms. The reflective wave
(Table~\ref{tab:waves}): the top rung, the bank under exact modal propagation
with no head, is at every mesh both the most accurate rung and
$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostSixtyFour$--$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostTenTwentyFour\times$
cheaper than the cheapest head rung (error monotone in $q$, cost not: the
middle rungs cost up to $\nWaveMidRungMsTwoFiftySix$\,ms for a solver reason,
then collapse to $\nWaveBankMsTwoFiftySix$\,ms). \textbf{On both Poisson and
waves the learned bank is itself worse than a POD basis of the same rank}:
POD-64 reaches $\nWavePodErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ against the bank's
$\nWaveBankErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ on waves at the same cost, and POD-512
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ against the bank floor
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor\,\%$ on Poisson. No reduced arm beats the direct solve
on any of the three. This is the structural condition: the nonlinear manifold
is worth having only where the corrections cost a nonlinear solve. On the
L-shaped domain, where no fast transform exists, the bank clears its floor
target ($\nLshapeBestFloor\,\%$; corner enrichment buys $\nLshapeEnrichGain\times$,
so the re-entrant corner is not the limit) and every head sits
$\nLshapeHeadRatioMin$--$\nLshapeHeadRatioMax\times$ above it
(Table~\ref{tab:lshape}); its solve layer is $\nLshapeSolve$.

\subsection{Neural operators on the same data}
\label{sec:exp:operators}

A PDEBench-style U-Net and a Transolver were trained on exactly the FNO's data,
split, reference, metric, wall budget and selection rule
(Table~\ref{tab:operators}). Worst error on the matched eight-case cohort:
U-Net-small $\nOpEightUnetSmall$, U-Net-medium $\nOpEightUnetMedium$,
Transolver-refine $\nOpEightTsolRefine$, U-Net-refine $\nOpEightUnetRefine$,
\textbf{ROM $\nOpEightRom$}, FNO-large $\nOpEightFnoLarge$, efficient full-order
solver $\nOpEightFomEfficient\,\%$: $\nOpArmsBeatingRom$ operator arms beat the
ROM and every FNO capacity is worse than it. The earlier finding that the ROM
is more accurate than a neural operator was an artefact of the family chosen
and is withdrawn. Operators are also far cheaper per query (the FNO at
$\nPanelFnoMs$\,ms in the panel's allocation, at $\nPanelFnoErr\,\%$ on its six
cases), so on Burgers at $256^2$ they dominate the ROM on both axes. Every
operator number is a lower bound: $\nOpLaneImproving$ new-family arms and
$\nOpFnoImproving$ FNO arms were still improving at their budget, and nothing
was tuned per family. On Poisson the gap is larger (U-Net worst
$\nOpPoissonUnetWorstRange\,\%$ against FNO $\nOpPoissonFnoWorstRange\,\%$,
Table~\ref{tab:operators-poisson}). What survives is the within-reduced-model
claim and the mechanism; the operators' own inference-time knob, evaluation
resolution, is $\nOpResolutionKnob$.
