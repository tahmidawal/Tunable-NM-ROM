\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Problems.} Table~\ref{tab:problems} specifies each PDE, domain,
discretisation, mesh, reduced sizes, reference and cohort. Burgers 2D at
$256^2$ intervals with one frozen checkpoint is the hero; Poisson, heat and the
reflective wave are the linear cases; the L-shaped Poisson domain is the one
cell where the fast direct transform does not exist; 2D Navier--Stokes has a
certified full-order solver and dataset but no reduced model yet.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Burgers errors are relative $L^2$ errors of the trajectory
against the same job's converged full-order solve on the same grid (which
removes the mesh's own discretisation error, $\nPanelRefDiscretisation\,\%$ at
$256^2$ against a $4096^2$ reference), maximised over cases and over output
times. Two maxima are reported everywhere and neither is chosen: worst over
\emph{all} times, which includes $t=0$ and is therefore bounded below by the
decoder's compression of the supplied initial field, and worst over the
\emph{evolved} times $t>0$, with the $t=0$ compression printed as its own
column. Which one heads the paper is an open decision and both are given.
Poisson and wave errors are relative $L^2$ errors against the exact discrete
solution.

\paragraph{Timing protocol.} Every cost is the median over retained repetitions
of a completed device computation, timed inside one Slurm allocation on one
GPU after a burn-in, with device synchronisation around every measured region,
arms interleaved in a recorded random order, float64 and highest matmul
precision throughout. We report the device query (input resident, outputs
resident) and the complete host-to-host query. \emph{No ratio is ever formed
across jobs or GPUs}; where two numbers come from different allocations the
text says so and compares accuracy only. Offline setup (bank evaluation,
operator assembly, quadrature fits, POD bases, factorisations) is charged once
per mesh and reported separately, never amortised into a query.

\paragraph{Comparators.} Full-order controls are always in the same job: Newton
with a modal Helmholtz preconditioner at several tolerances and time steps for
Burgers; the direct discrete sine transform and tolerance-tuned conjugate
gradients for Poisson and waves; sparse direct and incomplete-Cholesky PCG on
the L-shape. Classical POD-LSPG at several ranks runs through the same weak
objective, tests, solver and stopping rule as the ROM. Neural operators (FNO,
U-Net, Transolver) are trained on the identical dataset and split with recorded
index hashes, an equal wall budget per capacity, and the same selection rule.

\paragraph{Provenance.} Every table names its job id, GPU and checkpoint hash
in its caption or in Table~\ref{tab:provenance}; every reported solve carries
its exit reason; every error is recomputed from the saved fields by an
independent NumPy audit that imports neither the driver nor JAX. Every number
in this paper is emitted by one generator from those records.
