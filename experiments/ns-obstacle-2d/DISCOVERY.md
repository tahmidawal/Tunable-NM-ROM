## Authoritative starting case

Use the official [DFG 2D-1 specification](https://wwwold.mathematik.tu-dortmund.de/~featflow/en/benchmarks/cfdbenchmarking/flow/dfg_benchmark1_re20.html), with historical attribution to Schäfer–Turek (1996). Its fixed fluid region is

$$\Omega=([0,2.2]\times[0,0.41])\setminus\overline{B_{0.05}(0.2,0.2)}.$$

The cylinder is intentionally not centered vertically. No-slip applies to both channel walls and the cylinder. Inlet velocity is $(4U_{\max}y(H-y)/H^2,0)$, with $H=0.41$, $U_{\max}=0.3$, mean speed $U=2U_{\max}/3=0.2$, diameter $D=0.1$, and $\nu=0.001$, giving $\mathrm{Re}=UD/\nu=20$. The outlet condition is $\nu\partial_nu-pn=0$. Compare $C_D=2F_D/(U^2D)$, $C_L=2F_L/(U^2D)$ and $p(0.15,0.2)-p(0.25,0.2)$ with the official spectral reference values. Their numeric targets are recorded separately in `benchmark.json`; force is per unit span. Blockage means diameter/channel-height, $D/H$, not area fraction. This benchmark is a bounded-channel problem, not unconfined cylinder flow.

The original paper PDF was discoverable through indexed text but direct retrieval returned 404. `SOURCES.json` records that limitation. The official benchmark page is directly available and supplies the operational definition. Do not claim the inaccessible PDF was fully inspected.

## Physical and pressure conventions

We solve the user's Newtonian equations with constant density $\rho=1$ and kinematic pressure $p=p_{\mathrm{physical}}/\rho$. Coordinates and velocities above retain benchmark dimensional units; nondimensional reporting uses $x/D$, $tU/D$, $u/U$, and $p/U^2$. The weak momentum form is

$$\int_\Omega \nu\nabla u:\nabla v+((u\cdot\nabla)u)\cdot v-p\nabla\cdot v\,dx=0,\qquad
\int_\Omega q\nabla\cdot u\,dx=0.$$

Velocity tests vanish on inlet, walls, and obstacle but are free at the outlet. The natural outlet already fixes the pressure level: adding a mean-zero pressure constraint or pin is not allowed. Save that absolute pressure convention and the gauge-independent pressure difference. Do not silently replace the outlet by symmetric-stress traction; although the bulk equations agree at zero divergence, the natural boundary conditions differ.

The physical stress is $\sigma=-pI+\nu(\nabla u+\nabla u^T)$. On an exact incompressible no-slip obstacle the extra transpose-gradient traction vanishes, so the integrated physical force agrees with the benchmark's Laplacian stress $\tau=-pI+\nu\nabla u$. At finite discretization report both and their discrepancy. With $n$ pointing out of the fluid into the obstacle, force **on the obstacle** is $-\int_{\Gamma_o}\tau n\,ds$. The variational reaction is the negative of the unconstrained weak residual evaluated on a test equal to the corresponding unit vector at the obstacle and zero on other Dirichlet boundaries. Compute direct surface and variational forces separately, refining surface quadrature independently. Evaluate pressure probes using the actual FE interpolation at the exact coordinates, never nearest-node lookup.

## Solver recommendation and dependency evidence

Use Netgen/NGSolve curved body-fitted triangles, cubic geometry, and inf-sup-stable Taylor–Hood velocity/pressure degrees $3/2$. Cross-check with degrees $4/3$ and quartic geometry before admitting a reference. Solve the steady mixed equations by damped Newton from a Stokes lift, with stored residual and step norms and an explicit iteration cap. Use a sparse direct factorization as the first trusted small-problem solver; add a tuned iterative comparator only after it agrees. No pressure penalty and no grad-div stabilization in the first implementation. Taylor–Hood satisfies discrete continuity but is not pointwise divergence-free, so divergence is a measured quantity with its own convergence gate.

[Official NGSolve documentation](https://ngsolve.org/ngsolve/docs/i-tutorials/unit-3.2-navierstokes/navierstokes.html) supports curved geometry, Taylor–Hood pairs, and variational reaction extraction. Its tutorial uses channel length **2.0**, not DFG **2.2**, and the displayed force-normalization paragraph conflicts with the DFG diameter and squared-speed convention. Copy neither those numerical settings nor that displayed normalization. Its illustrative first-order IMEX step is also not our reference integrator.

Local and cluster read-only probes found no installed DOLFINx, legacy FEniCS, NGSolve, Netgen, scikit-fem, Gmsh, meshio or petsc4py. NumPy/SciPy exist; cluster Apptainer exists. `DEPENDENCIES.json` pins candidate wheel URLs, sizes and SHA256 from official PyPI metadata for CPython 3.13 x86_64: NGSolve/Netgen 6.2.2608, netgen-occt 7.8.1, and ngsolve-openblas 0.3.33. Install only those explicit wheels using the designated cluster Python's pip with `--no-deps --target <lane>/deps`, after design/dependency approval. Reuse the existing NumPy. Do not replace JAX or modify the shared environment. Metadata is availability evidence, not a successful import. Stop after one dependency repair attempt if imports still fail; scikit-fem with curved geometry is a fallback design requiring a new review, not an unannounced substitution.

## First bounded pilot and provisional acceptance contract

The first model represents **steady solutions only**. Its FOM comparator is a direct steady solve with the same requested output, not an unnecessarily long transient. Start at the exact DFG anchor, then a narrow viscosity family with fixed inlet speed; the coordinator freezes the train/development/validation split. A steady solve has no physical time horizon or inlet ramp. Optional transient verification starts from rest with a smooth ramp and is a separate run, never mixed into steady cost.

A first allocation is capped at twenty minutes, sixteen CPUs and 64 GiB, in the GPU partition, with GPU/JAX preflight and f64/highest flags. NGSolve's FE solve is **CPU work within that allocation**; no GPU acceleration claim. It covers package import, mesh/geometry measures, a manufactured/analytic control and one small steady anchor. Record import/version checks, source/wheel hashes, allocated elapsed time, wall/CPU work, DOFs, element counts, peak RSS and nonlinear iteration histories. A successful smoke permits a separately audited reference ladder; it is not itself reference admission.

Proposed ladders use deterministic Netgen meshing with global target sizes $0.08,0.04,0.02$ and obstacle target size no larger than one quarter of the global value, plus the degree-$4/3$ cross-check. These are starting resolutions, not asserted converged meshes. Gate them independently; no training data until the final reference pipeline passes review.

- Geometry: relative fluid-area and obstacle-perimeter errors below $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-6}$, no inverted cells, verified boundary labels and normals. Increase curved order or integration order if needed under the original threshold.
- Analytic control: unobstructed Poiseuille profile, pressure gradient and flux; manufactured mixed-flow control with nonzero convection to check sign and Jacobian. Manufactured forcing and outlet traction must be derived consistently, not reused from the physical no-forcing problem. Three mesh levels must show decreasing errors; expected spatial order is checked where asymptotic and above roundoff.
- Algebra: mixed residual relative to its initial Stokes state at most $10^{-10}$, cap thirty Newton iterations; tightening nonlinear tolerance by a factor of one hundred and the linear solve tolerance must change each admitted physical output by less than one tenth of its spatial uncertainty. A budget exit is failure, not a valid endpoint.
- Conservation: global flux imbalance divided by inlet flux at most $10^{-8}$; $D\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^2}/\|u\|_{L^2}\le10^{-3}$ and decreasing under refinement. Dirichlet mismatch normalized by $U$ below $10^{-10}$ at independent boundary samples. Record strong divergence and weak continuity separately.
- Field reference uncertainty: successive common-point, common-quadrature velocity differences below $5\times10^{-4}$ relative $L^2$; pressure differences below $2\times10^{-3}$ in RMS units of $U^2$, retaining absolute outlet convention. Demonstrate decreasing differences across at least three resolutions and agreement of degree refinement; use a conservative maximum of observed final differences, not unqualified Richardson extrapolation.
- Forces/reference anchors: relative drag and pressure-drop differences below $5\times10^{-4}$; lift absolute difference below $2\times10^{-5}$. Apply these both against the official anchor and between final refinements. Surface-vs-reaction and Laplacian-vs-symmetric force discrepancies must fit within the same output tolerance. A failed lift gate does not become a passed drag-only reference for force ranking.
- Quadrature: raising integration order changes each velocity, pressure, and force diagnostic by less than one tenth of the corresponding reference threshold. Record volume and boundary quadrature separately.
- Momentum and energy: evaluate boundary momentum and pressure/viscous power fluxes, including inlet and outlet. For the advective/Laplacian convention the steady energy residual includes $\nu\int|\nabla u|^2$, $\frac12\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^2u\cdot n$, minus boundary traction work, minus $\frac12\int|u|^2\nabla\cdot u$ and minus $\int p\nabla\cdot u$. The momentum residual is $\int_{\partial\Omega}[u(u\cdot n)-\tau n]-\int_\Omega u\nabla\cdot u$. Normalize each by the sum of absolute contributing flux/dissipation terms (with declared nonzero physical floor), require below $10^{-3}$ and decreasing with refinement. Do not test unforced periodic energy decay.
- Domain sensitivity: preserve the exact DFG domain for literature comparison. Separately double the upstream gap by moving the inlet to $x=-0.2$, and extend the outlet to $x=4.2$, one change at a time. Compare a fixed common wake window and force outputs at the same local resolution. If velocity/drag/pressure changes exceed $0.5\%$ or lift changes exceed $10^{-4}$, the original case remains a finite-domain benchmark but is ineligible for a domain-independent wake/force claim. An alternative outlet is another named problem, not a reference correction.

Stop the reference branch if the ladders and one predeclared resolution extension cannot meet these thresholds in its budget. Preserve failure fields/logs and report which output is unresolved. The coordinator's total initial envelope is twelve allocated GPU-hours, including CPU FE work: eight for references and four conditionally for ROM work, no more than two simultaneous campaign allocations, and at most sixty GiB of new local unique data. Resource measurements, not guesses, determine any later allocation within that ceiling.

## Unsteady extension is gated, not a transition guess

[DFG 2D-2](https://wwwold.mathematik.tu-dortmund.de/~featflow/en/benchmarks/cfdbenchmarking/flow/dfg_benchmark2_re100.html) supplies evidence for a periodic wake at its own Re=100 configuration. Its guidance requires a developed periodic state and provides later improved force-integration data; those downloads must be inspected before registering acceptance bounds. Only after the steady pipeline passes: specify ramp, initialization, BDF2 or fully implicit midpoint startup, horizon, output cadence, temporal refinement, phase-sensitive errors, force statistics and frequency. A short transient does not prove a periodic statistical reference. No numerical transition threshold is inferred from an unconfined cylinder.

## Risks and claims

The practical risks are package ABI compatibility, direct-solver memory at fine resolution, force sensitivity to geometry/pressure traces, weak rather than exact divergence control, outlet backflow, and ROM loss of inf-sup stability. The intended first result is a verified reference/data path and a fair steady pilot. Passing it permits claims only on the declared fixed geometry and narrow family. A stronger full solver or unsampled POD remains an admissible competitor even if learned-bank or fitted-quadrature attempts fail.

## Plain-language glossary

**DFG**: the published German research-program cylinder benchmark. **FOM**: the full finite-element flow solver. **ROM**: a reduced model with fewer online unknowns. **POD**: a linear reduced basis extracted from training snapshots. **Taylor–Hood**: paired velocity and pressure finite-element spaces designed to avoid unstable pressure modes. **Inf-sup stability**: the compatibility requirement between velocity and pressure spaces. **DOF**: one independent discrete unknown. **Weak continuity**: divergence tested against pressure functions; it is weaker than zero divergence at every point. **Gauge**: the convention fixing an otherwise arbitrary pressure level; here the outlet traction does that. **Natural outlet**: a boundary condition arising from the weak form. **Reaction force**: force recovered from equations replaced by the obstacle's fixed-velocity constraint. **Reference uncertainty**: an estimated remaining numerical error, not proof of the exact continuum solution. **Blockage**: the obstacle diameter divided by channel height in this two-dimensional case. **RSS**: the process's resident memory. **Anchor**: the exact published parameter setting used for an external check.
