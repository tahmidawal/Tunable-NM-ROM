2. DEFINE THE PHYSICS PRECISELY

Use incompressible Newtonian flow:

∂t u + (u · ∇)u = −∇p + νΔu,
∇ · u = 0.

Define the pressure convention, nondimensionalisation and density convention explicitly.

For each dimension specify:
- Domain dimensions and obstacle geometry.
- Obstacle position.
- Inlet velocity profile and its characteristic-speed convention.
- No-slip walls and obstacle surface.
- Outlet condition and pressure gauge.
- Initial condition and any inlet ramp.
- Viscosity and Reynolds number Re = U D / ν.
- Blockage ratio and its definition.
- Simulation horizon and output schedule.
- Force-coefficient normalisation.

The first geometries are fixed and stationary. No moving obstacles, fluid–structure interaction, or varying geometry in the first production campaign.

Choose a modest steady-flow regime first. Select unsteady regimes using benchmark evidence and verified simulations; do not guess transition thresholds. Geometry and confinement affect those thresholds.

Check upstream and downstream domain-length adequacy before treating the wake or forces as reference data.

7. ADAPT THE ROM TO THE GEOMETRY

Do not reuse the Burgers box envelope, sine tests, or tensor quadrature unchanged.

Explicitly address:
- Nonhomogeneous inlet data through a suitable lifting or equivalent treatment.
- Zero velocity on walls and obstacle.
- Incompressibility.
- Pressure representation or recovery.
- Geometric integration over the fluid region.
- Conditioning of the reduced system.

If using a divergence-free bank, verify the property numerically and analytically where possible. Do not assume that divergence-free functions alone satisfy all boundary conditions or give pressure and forces automatically.

If using a velocity–pressure formulation, check reduced stability and any stabilisation. Avoid an arbitrary pressure basis with no stability assessment.

If using streamfunctions or vector potentials, document their boundary constraints and derivative costs.

Compare a small number of justified constructions. Change one ingredient at a time.

8. VERIFY OFF-MESH QUADRATURE

Construct quadrature over the actual fluid domain, excluding the obstacle. Treat boundary integrals separately where required.

Compare credible rules suited to the geometry. Possible candidates include mapped element quadrature, composite Gauss rules, geometry-aware point sets, and fitted positive-weight quadrature.

Do not assume Fibonacci lattices or box Gauss rules remain efficient around curved excluded regions.

Check:
- Geometric integration and simple exactness controls.
- Reduced nonlinear operator error.
- Operator Jacobian consistency.
- Pressure/divergence contributions where applicable.
- Boundary-force integration.
- Reached-state accuracy, not just training-state accuracy.
- Rollout convergence as the quadrature is refined.

Keep integration error, solution error and force error as separate metrics. A quadrature threshold alone does not certify the PDE solution.
