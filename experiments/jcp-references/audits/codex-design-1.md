**Overall verdict: WRONG as a ready-to-run pre-registration.** The primary reference method is defensible, and the mesh/cohort arithmetic largely checks out. The blocking problems are the spectral de-aliasing claim, incomplete cross-check decision rules, unjustified numerical-error floor, non-prefix-stable 2D draws, and ineffective access separation.

I audited [DESIGN.md](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-06-jcp-references/experiments/jcp-references/DESIGN.md), the specified source files, campaign requirements, and historical reference JSONs. Small read-only NumPy checks tested arithmetic and two counterexamples. No files were modified; no GPU experiments were run.

| Item | Verdict | Concrete reason and fix |
|---|---|---|
| **1. PDE, domain, viscosity, output times (§2)** | **CORRECT** | The code implements scalar Burgers with velocity components all equal to \(u\), zero Dirichlet walls, final time \(0.25\), and six outputs separated by \(0.05\). The stated viscosity range matches both generators. |
| **2. 2D parameter ranges and wall jump (§2)** | **CORRECT** | `engines.params_draw` matches the listed ranges. The largest possible wall trace is \(a\exp[-0.15^2/(2\,0.20^2)]=0.7548396a\). `initial` then zeros boundary nodes. |
| **3. Consequence of incompatible 2D IC** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Incompatibility does **not automatically destroy second-order convergence at fixed positive output times**. Parabolic smoothing can recover it. But “every discretisation samples it the same way” is false for the proposed continuous-coefficient spectral initialization. State the common continuum IC and distinguish its discrete representations; test the actual incompatible family, not only a smooth MMS. |
| **4. 3D IC and normalization (§2)** | **CORRECT** | `b3d_common` draws 1–3 blobs, centers in \([0.2,0.8]^3\), widths in \([0.10,0.20]\), weights \(\rho_b\in[0.5,1]\), and amplitude in \([0.5,2]\). The mask is \(64\prod_a x_a(1-x_a)\); normalization uses a **single fixed 257-node-grid maximum**. Preserve that same `s_star` at every resolution. |
| **5. Claimed bound \(\lvert u\rvert\le2\)** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | A maximum normalized on the 257-node grid does not strictly bound the continuous or finer-grid peak by 2. CN and spectral approximations also lack a general discrete maximum principle. Use measured initial/stage maxima with a safety margin for stability calculations. |
| **6. Central stencil and CN equation (§3.1)** | **CORRECT** | The signs and nonconservative advection match the intended continuum PDE. The centered first derivative and 5-/7-point Laplacian are second-order for smooth solutions. This changes the discretization, not the continuum equation. |
| **7. Four BE half-steps replacing two CN steps** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | For smooth finite-dimensional dynamics, a fixed number of BE startup steps introduces \(O(\Delta t^2)\) accumulated error, so subsequent CN remains second-order. That elementary argument is not uniform in the stiff mesh limit with incompatible data. Rannacher smoothing is well founded, but the relevant published analysis does not prove this nonlinear multidimensional case automatically. Keep the method; qualify the guarantee and verify positive-time convergence. [Giles–Carter](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/~gilesm/files/giles_carter.pdf). |
| **8. Helmholtz preconditioner notation (§3.1)** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | For CN step \(\Delta t\), the diffusion preconditioner is \(I+(\Delta t/2)\nu\Lambda_h\). A BE startup step of length \(\Delta t/2\) has **the same** diffusion factor. The parenthetical wording can be read as halving twice. Specify the step length and implicit coefficient separately. |
| **9. \(\Delta t\propto h\), level constants, output alignment** | **CORRECT** | The proposed constants give the listed nominal 200/400/800 and 100/200/400 steps, with exact output alignment. Refinements give 1200 and 600 nominal steps. CN-R actually performs two additional nonlinear solves because two steps become four. Coupled refinement is sensible if both leading errors are second-order. |
| **10. CFL 5.12 and 0.64; implicit stability interpretation** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The arithmetic \(2\Delta t/h\) is correct **per direction**. The summed nominal advective numbers are 10.24 and 1.92. Implicit CN avoids the usual explicit linear CFL restriction, but this does not establish nonlinear stability, positivity, accuracy, or Newton convergence. Treat these as diagnostics, not a safety proof. |
| **11. \(R_S\) target (§3.2)** | **CORRECT** | Fixed-step BE with spatial refinement converges to the spatially continuous, time-discrete BE solution. It is a useful BE-matched diagnostic. It is not the time-matched reference for the campaign’s newer CN/BDF2 arms; label that limitation explicitly. |
| **12. Uniform-ratio observed order (§4)** | **CORRECT** | \(p=\log_2(d_{12}/d_{23})\) follows from a common leading vector error \(C(t)h^p\). It is an observed asymptotic indicator, not proof that all error components obey that expansion. |
| **13. Nonuniform-ratio solve** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The equation is correct. For the retained refinement triple, widths are proportional to \(3,1.5,1\), giving ratio **5.4 at \(p=2\)**. Bisection on \([0.25,8]\) only brackets ratios approximately **[1.96277, 265.35369]**. Specify rejection for no bracket, zero differences, NaNs, and infinities; never clip to an endpoint or silently substitute \(p=2\). |
| **14. Richardson uncertainty and extrapolated field** | **CORRECT** | Both formulas have the correct factor and sign under the common-leading-error assumption. At \(p=2\), the finest-error multiplier is \(1/3\) for doubling and **0.8** for the final 1.5 refinement ratio. |
| **15. Three-level norm ratio establishes the asymptotic regime** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Norm ratios can look second-order despite cancellation or changing error directions. Coupling time and space also permits leading-error cancellation. Add a vector consistency diagnostic, such as agreement between \(u_1-u_2\) and the predicted multiple of \(u_2-u_3\), plus selected separate time/space refinements. Three levels alone cannot certify a bound. |
| **16. \([1.8,2.2]\) gate at every evolved output** | **CORRECT** | This is a clear, deliberately conservative acceptance policy consistent with the main plan. It may reject superconvergent or cancellation-dominated cases that are actually accurate; report them as **unverified**, not necessarily inaccurate. Excluding \(t_0\) is essential. |
| **17. \(10^{-10}\) “solver-tolerance floor”** | **WRONG** | A per-step relative residual tolerance near \(10^{-11}\) does not establish a trajectory-error floor below \(10^{-10}\). Residual-to-state conditioning and accumulation over hundreds of steps are missing. Even the simple product \(800(2\times10^{-11})=1.6\times10^{-8}\) shows why that assertion needs evidence. Establish a field-level tolerance-sensitivity estimate and require differences comfortably above it. |
| **18. Refining cases that fail the floor** | **WRONG** | Refinement generally makes \(d_{23}\) **smaller**, so it does not repair unresolved differences. Tighten solver tolerances first or classify the order as unresolved below the measured numerical floor. Do not treat finer meshes as a universal remedy. |
| **19. Péclet gate and ability to fire** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | With the nominal amplitude bound, coarsest-grid maxima are only **0.09765625 in 2D** and **1.5625 in 3D**, including the proposed OOD viscosity range. Thus this gate is not an active family discriminator under that assumption. It remains a useful sanity check, but should use the running maximum already recorded, not only six outputs. Add an explicit violating test if its rejection path is meant to be validated. |
| **20. Solver retry and retained evidence** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The retry changes the Newton budget and linear tolerance but leaves the nonlinear target unchanged. Define outcomes for persistent solver failures separately from order failures. Preserve failed attempts and all original levels; “three accepted levels” cannot describe rejected cases or retain the full refinement history. |
| **21. Triangle inequality and \(2\epsilon_{\rm ref}\)** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The **mathematical inequality is correct** for the fixed-\(n_0\), max-over-time metric: \(\lvert E_R(f)-E_U(f)\rvert\le\max_k\lVert R_k-U_k\rVert/n_0\). Two arms therefore have a \(2\delta\) uncertainty bound. But substituting a conditional Richardson estimate for \(\delta\) makes this an **estimated resolution threshold**, not “moves by at most.” Fix that wording and freeze the common normalization. |
| **22. Sine basis, continuum eigenvalues, cosine derivatives (§3.3)** | **CORRECT** | Sine expansions enforce Dirichlet values; differentiation replaces the sine by \(k_a\pi\cos(k_a\pi x_a)\). DCT-I synthesis along that axis is appropriate with correct endpoint weights, zero/Nyquist slots, and continuous-versus-discrete normalization. Explicitly specify those conventions and test individual modes. |
| **23. “3/2 padding is de-aliasing equivalent to 2/3 truncation”** | **WRONG** | The usual Fourier argument does not transfer to this tensor-sine projection. For \(u=\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\), \(u\,u_x=(\pi/2)\sin(2\pi x)\sin^2(\pi y)\). The transverse \(\sin^2\) factor has an **infinite sine expansion**. Its exact unnormalized \((2,1)\) coefficient is \(4/3\); the proposed padded-grid projection gives errors **\(7.9591\times10^{-5}\)** at \(N=8,M=12\) and **\(4.9134\times10^{-6}\)** at \(N=16,M=24\). Padding is oversampling here, not exact de-aliasing. Audit projection quadrature independently or use an accurately integrated sine-Galerkin formulation. |
| **24. Separability of the 3D masked-blob IC** | **CORRECT** | Each masked isotropic Gaussian is a product of three one-dimensional factors. The full IC is a sum of at most three such products, multiplied by the scalar \(A/s_\star\). Thus its coefficient tensor can be assembled from one-dimensional integrals without a \(4096^3\) quadrature array. It is generally rank at most three, not one. |
| **25. “Exact” coefficients from 4096-point Gauss–Legendre** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Gauss–Legendre is not exact for Gaussian–sine products. The count may be ample, but accuracy must be checked, including the highest retained modes at the escalation resolution. Call them numerically integrated continuous coefficients and verify against a higher quadrature order or an independent formula. |
| **26. Spectral \(t_0\) versus shared sampled IC** | **WRONG** | A finite sine expansion of the continuous incompatible 2D IC does not equal the exact sampled IC at lattice nodes. Therefore “\(t_0\) identical at every level” applies to the FD levels, not all proposed solvers. Store canonical sampled \(u_0\) for normalization and distinguish it from the spectral solver’s actual initialized field. Do not disguise initialization error by overwriting the latter. |
| **27. ETDRK4 and 32-point contour coefficients** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | This is a legitimate independent time integrator; 32-point contour evaluation is a standard starting choice. Classical fourth order does not guarantee uniform stiff fourth order for this boundary/regularity problem. Check contour convergence and temporal convergence separately. [Kassam–Trefethen](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/fourth-order.pdf), [Hochbruck–Ostermann](https://na.math.kit.edu/download/papers/rkexp.pdf). |
| **28. Expected spectral spatial order “about 4”** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Algebraic convergence is plausible, but the exponent depends on boundary compatibility, norm, projection quadrature, and temporal contamination. At a smooth flat wall, \(u=0\) and the PDE imply \(u_{nn}=0\); higher even derivatives can be nonzero. A blanket fourth-order inference is insufficient. Measure spatial convergence with temporal error controlled; two resolutions do not determine its order. |
| **29. Explicit-advection timestep limits** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The stated arithmetic is right: approximately **\(1.0880\times10^{-4}\)** and **\(5.8025\times10^{-4}\)**. At escalation, the corresponding values are **\(7.2532\times10^{-5}\)** and **\(3.8683\times10^{-4}\)**, still above the proposed steps. But the RK4 imaginary-axis heuristic is not a stability theorem for ETDRK4 with variable-coefficient nonlinear advection. Monitor stages and test timestep sensitivity. |
| **30. One timestep-halving case “bounds the time error”** | **WRONG** | It bounds neither that case rigorously nor the other seven cases empirically. Both spatial resolutions can share nearly identical time bias, giving tiny \(s_{PS}\) while being wrong together. Specify case selection, a numerical temporal-error bar, and temporal checks for every cross-check case or a justified conservative coverage protocol. Include initialization and projection errors in the PS uncertainty assessment. |
| **31. Independence of the spectral cross-check** | **CORRECT** | Continuum eigenvalues, continuous-coefficient initialization, and ETD stepping provide genuine independence from FD/CN. Sharing the PDE descriptors is necessary. Independence does not remove the need to audit normalization, nonlinear projection, and forcing separately. |
| **32. Cross-check case selection (§6)** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Four lowest viscosities plus four remaining lowest indices is reproducible once ties are specified. Lowest viscosity alone does not identify the hardest incompatible-wall cases; width, amplitude, and wall proximity matter. Keep the predeclared population but avoid claiming it exhausts worst-case behavior. |
| **33. PASS/FAIL/INCONCLUSIVE coverage** | **WRONG** | If \(s_{PS}\le\epsilon/2\) and \(2\epsilon<x_{PS}\le2\epsilon+s_{PS}\), **none of the three rules applies**. For example \(s=0.4\epsilon,x=2.2\epsilon\). Moreover, the main plan treats disagreement beyond \(2\epsilon\) as K0, whereas this design delays FAIL. Define a complete decision table and explicitly reconcile it with K0. |
| **34. PS escalation and final disposition** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Specify replacing \((N,2N)\) by \((2N,3N)\), the fine solution used thereafter, and the changed refinement ratio. Persistent unresolved PS error, invalid FD uncertainty, or PS solver failure must have explicit terminal outcomes. None may be promoted to PASS by exhausting the budget. |
| **35. Dimension-level versus case-level K0** | **WRONG** | §6 says cross-check FAIL triggers K0 **for the dimension**; §10 says failures affect cases. Those can produce different downstream rankings. Define whether one independent-family disagreement suspends all references in that dimension until diagnosed, and encode that policy in loader metadata. |
| **36. 2D scoring-lattice containment (§5)** | **CORRECT** | Every listed interval count—256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, **12288**—is divisible by 256. The last has stride **48**. Spectral interval counts 1024, 2048, 3072 also contain the lattice. |
| **37. 3D scoring-lattice containment and flattening** | **CORRECT** | Interval counts 64, 128, 256, 384, 512, **768** are divisible by 64. Thus 769 nodes give stride **12**. Spectral counts 128, 256, 384 also qualify. `lattice65_index` uses the stated \(63^3\) interior points and x-slowest ordering. |
| **38. Restriction for excluded meshes** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | A 128-interval 2D mesh shares a 129-node lattice; a 48-cell 3D mesh shares only the **15³ interior points** of the 16-interval lattice. Those are different metrics. Restrict **all compared arms and reference levels** to one common lattice and recompute \(n_0,p,\epsilon_{\rm ref}\); merely restricting the reference field is insufficient. |
| **39. G1 vendor parity** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Useful and capable of failing, but specify identical timesteps, tolerances, initialization, output conventions, and solver limits. The 3D vendor accepts tolerances as constructor arguments, while the 2D query accepts them at execution. A tolerance mismatch can defeat a \(10^{-12}\) field-parity bar without an operator bug. |
| **40. G2 manufactured-solution order** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | No manufactured solution, forcing, timestep ladder, or temporal forcing quadrature is specified. “Every \(t_k\)” includes \(t_0\), where exact initialization makes order/effectivity \(0/0\). Pre-register analytic forcing independently of the numerical operator; gate only positive times; ensure neither space nor time error vanishes accidentally. |
| **41. G3 spectral linear test** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | It can catch transform/eigenvalue errors but cannot exercise nonlinear projection or ETDRK4’s nonlinear coefficients. Specify exact finite-mode data and score positive times so decaying norms do not make the relative criterion ill-defined. Keep it alongside stronger nonlinear tests. |
| **42. G4 spectral MMS** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Error need not decrease with \(N\) once fixed-step temporal error dominates. A finite-sine MMS can also have zero spatial truncation error. Specify an MMS with a nontrivial tail and independent forcing, and separate spatial, temporal, and projection-quadrature ladders. Include the tensor-sine counterexample from item 23. |
| **43. G5 independent recomputation and faults** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | A consistently swapped field-plus-metadata record can remain internally self-consistent. Recomputing \(p,\epsilon\) will not detect its identity without an independently pinned descriptor/case mapping or IC check. Define exactly which nonzero record is perturbed and keep expected values fixed. Lattice fields alone cannot reconstruct every-step Newton acceptance. |
| **44. G6 hash corruption tests** | **CORRECT** | Swapped unequal files and genuinely changed field bytes fail against an unchanged trusted manifest. Pin manifest version and cohort/case identity; do not regenerate expected hashes after injection. This tests integrity, not numerical correctness. |
| **45. X1 first-order rejection** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | First-order asymptotics suggest \(p\approx1\), but do not guarantee rejection on every finite-resolution case; leading-error cancellation can produce accidental higher order. Timesteps are also unspecified. Freeze those values and failure policy. Add a deterministic \(h\)-scaling injected defect to prove the order gate’s rejection path separately. |
| **46. X2 old-reference cross-check must FAIL** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Specify that thresholds retain the **new reference’s** uncertainty. If uncertainty is instead recomputed for the old first-order sequence, asymptotically its PS discrepancy is about its own Richardson error and need not exceed twice that error—so the intended control cannot fire in that regime. Even with new uncertainty, failure on every case is a hypothesis, not guaranteed. Require the PS resolution condition too. |
| **47. X3 old bias exceeds uncertainty on 90%** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The threshold is a valid descriptive hypothesis, but failing it does not prove the bias “immaterial”: the new uncertainty may simply be large. Specify reference kind and non-sealed population. Scope it away from sealed cohorts, where old-arm scoring is prohibited by §9. |
| **48. X4 doubled middle timestep** | **CORRECT** | This can fail to move the order gate when spatial error dominates. The explicit sensitivity-probe warning acknowledges that correctly. It should not sit under an unqualified heading saying every control “must fail”; define its case, dimension, and reference kind. |
| **49. Cohort seeds, historical counts, totals (§9)** | **CORRECT** | `qcore.cohort` confirms the exact dev6 concatenation, val32 and test64 seeds/counts. The 3D definitions/configs agree with dev4, 923801×64 and 923901×32. Including new sealed/OOD cohorts gives **262 2D cases** and **180 3D cases**. |
| **50. OOD ranges and prefix stability** | **WRONG** | The viscosity endpoint \(0.01\,10^{0.1}=0.01258925\) and proposed narrower widths are well defined. But `engines.params_draw(seed,count)` draws whole parameter columns and is **not prefix-stable**. For seed 7090702, changing count 4→6 changes row 0’s \(c_y\) from 0.63660848 to 0.59606426. Preserve historical tables with their exact counts; define a new row-wise or indexed RNG protocol for new 2D cohorts. The 3D row-wise generator is prefix-stable. |
| **51. Sealed storage permissions and G7** | **WRONG** | `chmod a-w` prevents writes, not reads. Agents sharing `tawal01` can read `SEEDS.json` and NPZs directly, bypassing the loader; a hash token is not an access boundary. G7 only tests cooperative API behavior. Use a separate custodian/account or encrypted artifacts with keys unavailable to consuming lanes. |
| **52. Global freeze/opening protocol** | **WRONG** | A token matching one committed `selection.json` does not establish that **every lane’s** rules, baselines, FOM settings, and thresholds are frozen. Require a coordinator-controlled global manifest enumerating all selections and the reference version, an immutable opening record, and the explicit rule that post-opening method changes require a fresh cohort. |
| **53. QC-only access and seed lifecycle** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | Predeclared aggregate QC is compatible with reference generation, but an authorized custodian must actually be separated from model-selection workers. Freeze the permitted aggregate schema, forbid outcome-driven seed replacement, enforce seed distinctness, and record failed generation attempts. Keep sealed case identities/details private until opening. |
| **54. Store contents and provenance (§11)** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The schema omits an explicit canonical IC/norm, times, lattice ID, descriptor/table hash, solver configuration, per-step acceptance evidence, cross-check disposition, and complete refinement history. It also promises `u_R` without specifying whether it is stored or derived. Define rejected-case records and reconcile the separate `sealed/` path with the advertised `store/` layout. |
| **55. Loader `rankable`, Richardson field, and authorization API** | **WRONG** | `score(field,…)` cannot establish that **two arms** are rankable without their gap. It can return reference eligibility and uncertainty, with a separate pairwise-ranking function. The finest-field uncertainty is not automatically the uncertainty of the extrapolated field. The advertised signatures also omit the opening credential/context required by §9. |
| **56. \(2\epsilon\) versus plan’s \(0.1\times\) bar** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | “Gap \(>2\epsilon\)” and “worst uncertainty \(\le0.1\) times smallest claimed gap” are different standards. The latter requires a tenfold margin. Specify which claims require which bar; reporting median/worst uncertainty does not itself enforce the stronger campaign requirement. |
| **57. \(\delta_{\rm old}\) bounds rescoring changes (§10)** | **CORRECT** | For the **same arm fields, same lattice/times, same fixed initial normalization**, old and new FD references share \(t_0\), so the triangle inequality gives the stated bound even though \(\delta\) excludes \(t_0\). This does not cover rerun arms, current-state-normalized metrics, speedups, or changed scoring lattices. |
| **58. Median/max \(\delta_{\rm old}\) interpretation** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The cohort maximum bounds changes in cohort maxima and medians of paired errors. The **median of per-case bounds does not generally bound the change in the median error**, because ordering can change. Label median \(\delta\) descriptive and recompute actual aggregate scores. |
| **59. Historical “reference error about 1.7–2.3%”** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The historical evidence is a **1.70% probe discrepancy** and a **2.28% worst held-out discrepancy/extrapolation displacement**, not verified true-error endpoints. Say that explicitly. The old 3D reference was also tolerance-limited in its recheck; separate that contribution from discretization bias. |
| **60. Historical runtime numerals (§12)** | **CORRECT** | Archived JSONs give 2D ST means **355.683 s** and **344.565 s**, and 3D means **61.133 s** and **57.396 s**. These support the rounded ranges. The old 3D run used nonlinear/linear tolerances **\(10^{-6}/10^{-7}\)**, materially looser than the proposed reference. |
| **61. GPU-hour addition** | **CORRECT** | Listed rows sum to **91.5 GPU-h**, rounding to 92. Products also round consistently: 262×7.5 min = 32.75 h; 262×2 min = 8.73 h; 180×5.5 min = 16.5 h; 180×1.5 min = 4.5 h. Four continuously occupied GPUs imply an ideal minimum of about **22.9 hours**, excluding queueing and dependencies. |
| **62. Cost model, spectral subtotal, contingency** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | The 2D ST geometric factor \(1+1/8+1/64\) is correct under ideal \(L^2\)-per-step scaling. The corresponding 3D factor is \(1+1/16+1/256\). Tightened 3D tolerances, FFT costs, compilation, startup solves, and retries are unpriced. The stated PS per-case figures total **7.2 h** for eight cases per dimension; explicitly allocate the remaining 1.8 h of the 9 h row. Calibrate before claiming the 120 h budget is sufficient. |
| **63. Memory feasibility and solver storage** | **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT** | A 511³ f64 interior vector is **1.06746 GB**, correctly stated. At 769 nodes it is **3.60974 GB**. But vendor `common.make_fom` retains every step: 400 full 513-node states alone exceed **426 GB**. Require the streaming/lean implementation and measure FFT/JVP/BiCGStab workspace. `--mem 240G` requests host RAM, not H200 device memory. |

The design should be amended and re-audited before implementation. The highest-priority repairs are items **17–18, 23, 30, 33–35, 50–52, and 55**. The central-plus-CN-R reference strategy itself does not need to be abandoned.
