Does the reduced model's cost depend on bank width, and not on the mesh?

Yes for the mesh, partly for the bank width. Cost and accuracy are the same on every mesh we tried, in 2D and 3D, and that holds across retraining seeds and GPU types. Accuracy improves with bank width up to r = 384. But the method is not faster than the best conventional solver, a standard POD reduced model beats it, and it fails on steeper solutions than it was trained on. What the paper can stand on is mesh-independent cost, evaluation without a mesh, and memory.

E0 R_ST conditional Richardson estimates; development/validation cohorts only; sealed and OOD cohorts unopened. Total compute: 169 GPU-hours over 10 lanes.

Corrections made during the campaign

  • The first 3D speed-ups over our own earlier setups used settings that failed the quadrature check. Re-timed with fully checked settings, a real gain remains against the W768 setup, but against the original deployment it is below the bar.
  • An earlier large 2D speed-up held only against a same-mesh upwind solver, not against the best full solver.
  • Some earlier "flat accuracy across meshes" comparisons used slightly different test functions on the 256² mesh. Fixing them moved errors by hundredths of a percentage point.
  • Several lanes' first analyses had errors (wrong dominance rule, under-counted budgets, double-counted GPU-hours). Each was caught by an audit, corrected and re-audited. The full list is in the report.

Words used here

  • Bank width r: how many basis coefficients the reduced model solves for. This is the "k" in the advisor's question; the other reading, the nonlinear head's size K, is also tested.
  • Mesh: grid points per side of the full simulation.
  • Full solver / spectral solver: the conventional simulation; "spectral" uses fast Fourier-type transforms.
  • K2: the plan's pre-registered outcome "we are not at least 2× faster than the best full solver".
  • POD baseline: the standard reduced model built from simulation snapshots, with hyper-reduction (ECSW or Q-DEIM) to make it fast.
  • Qualified: passed the solver and quadrature checks, so its numbers can be trusted.
  • Seed: the random starting point of training; a seed-robust result holds for every retrained model.
  • Development / validation cases: the test problems used for choosing and checking; the sealed final test set is still unopened.
