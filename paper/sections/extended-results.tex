\section{Extended results}
\label{app:extended}

\subsection{Neural operators: controls and the resolution knob}
\label{app:extended:operators}
Both pre-registered controls were run on the selected U-Net
(Table~\ref{tab:op-controls}): its float64 twin lands at
$\nOpCtrlMediumFSixtyFour\,\%$ and its second-seed twin at
$\nOpCtrlMediumSeedTwo\,\%$, both below the ROM, so that comparison is not
a seed or precision artefact --- though the float64 margin is
$\nOpCtrlMediumFSixtyFourMargin$\,pp and, at equal wall clock, the float64
twins ran
$\nOpCtrlMediumFSixtyFourEpochRatio$--$\nOpCtrlTsolSmallFSixtyFourEpochRatio\times$
of their parents' epochs, so precision is confounded with an epoch effect.
The Transolver arm below the ROM has no twin and remains a single-seed
float32 result. Every operator number is a lower bound
(\nOpLaneImproving{} of this lane's arms and \nOpFnoImproving{} FNO arms
were still improving at their budget). The operators' own knob,
evaluation resolution, was tested under a pre-registered rule
(Table~\ref{tab:resolution}; speedups are within one model's own curve in
one job and carry no comparison to the ROM or the solver):
\nResUsableList{} is usable --- one step, $256\to128$, buys the whole gain
($\nResFnoSpeedup\times$ its own speed at $\nResFnoErrRatio\times$ its own
error), below which the query is launch-bound
($\nResFnoMsOneTwentyEight$, $\nResFnoMsSixtyFour$,
$\nResFnoMsThirtyTwo$\,ms) while the error rises to
$\nResFnoErrRatioSixtyFour\times$ and $\nResFnoErrRatioThirtyTwo\times$
--- while \nResDegenerateList{} are not (the U-Net breaks at once, the
Transolver is cost-flat). At rung 128 no family is within
$\nResMinFloorRatioOneTwentyEight\times$ of the interpolation floor, so the
off-resolution error is the network's, not the grid's.

\subsection{Quadrature: the fit-state count and the replication}
\label{app:extended:eq}
Scored by \eqref{eq:rho} on reachable states, the top-rung blocker is the
number of fit states, not the node count: 14- and 8-state rules at $q=128$
and $256$ fail at $m=2048$ ($\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopParentOneTwentyEightRho$,
$\nEqtopParentTwoFiftySixRho$) where 64-state rules pass in that draw
($\nEqtopPrimaryOneTwentyEightRho$, $\nEqtopPrimaryTwoFiftySixRho$;
Table~\ref{tab:eqcert}). Rebuilt with the cheapest rule passing the
primary bar per rung and timed in one allocation, the EQ ladder runs
$\nEqtopLadderFirstErr\to\nEqtopLadderLastErr\,\%$ at
$\nEqtopLadderFirstMs\to\nEqtopLadderLastMs$\,ms
(Table~\ref{tab:eqladder}; offline cost in Table~\ref{tab:offline}). In
the replication (pool and fit-state subset, four draws at fixed $q$, $m$,
fit-state count; Table~\ref{tab:replication}) the rules are confirmed at
$q=\nEqtopConfirmedRungs$ and marginal at $q=\nEqtopMarginalRungs$ (draws
passing / draws); at $q=256$ the rule the ladder ran is the only one of
$\nEqtopTopDraws$ draws of its recipe to meet the bar (re-draws
$\nEqtopTopRedrawRange$). ``Every rung carries a certified rule'' is
withdrawn as a statement about the construction, and four earlier
readings are withdrawn by the same data.

\subsection{The head at matched dimension: metric dependence and the three layers}
\label{app:extended:head}
On Burgers, on the all-times metric no POD rank up to $k'=128$ matches the
head ($\nAblBurgersPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$), while on evolved times POD-128
reaches $\nPanelPodOneTwentyEightErr\,\%$ against the $q=0$ rung's
$\nPanelQzeroEvolved\,\%$. In three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}): floor
$\nAblBurgersFloor\,\%$, best-found $\nAblBurgersBestFound\,\%$, solved
$\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$. On Poisson a wider head moves the
best-found/floor ratio only from $\nPlinHeadRatioPrimary$ to
$\nPlinHeadRatioBest$ (Table~\ref{tab:head-capacity}).

\subsection{Navier--Stokes: the head-only data-scaling diagnosis}
\label{app:extended:ns-scaling}
Job \provNsScaleJob{} retrained only the head on the frozen $K=16$,
$R=256$ bank with 128, 256 and 512 training trajectories under three
recipes and scored the held-out oracle at $256^2$
(Table~\ref{tab:ns-scaling}). The pre-registered rule
(\provNsScalingBandSource) returns ``\nNsScalePlainVerdict'' for every
recipe; the decelerating slope and the inertness of regularisation lean
towards a head-limited reading, which the lane does not upgrade beyond
ambiguous. Caveat, pre-registered: the frozen bank was trained on all 512
trajectories, so the small-$n$ arms are optimistic.

\subsection{The linear cells in full}
\label{app:extended:linear}
Poisson at $1024^2$ ($R=512$, $K=32$; Table~\ref{tab:linear}): the middle
rungs cost a flat
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMin$--$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMax$\,ms; the
pre-registered degenerate-curve criterion passes (at $256^2$ only its
literal cost-span clause fails, because the top rung is
$\nPlinTwoFiftySixTopOverMidCost\times$ cheaper than the middle rungs);
solver effort buys nothing at $q=0$ (floor $\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor$,
best-found $\nPlinTenTwentyFourBestFoundQzero$, solved
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\,\%$ at $M=129$). Heat at $1024^2$ (an
earlier cell of the same family, Table~\ref{tab:heat}): the bank evolved
linearly reaches $\nHeatBankErrTenTwentyFour\,\%$ at
$\nHeatBankMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms against the head's
$\nHeatHeadErrTenTwentyFour\,\%$ at $\nHeatHeadMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms, with
the direct solve at $\nHeatFomMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms. The reflective wave
(Table~\ref{tab:waves}): the top rung, the bank under exact modal
propagation with no head, is not strictly the most accurate rung (strict
reading: \nWaveTopStrictBestTwoFiftySix{}) but matches the $q=32$ rung
within the pre-registered integrator tie band of
$\nWaveTieBandPpTwoFiftySix$\,pp at every mesh ($256^2$:
$\nWaveBankErrTwoFiftySix$ against $\nWaveQthirtytwoErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$),
and is
$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostSixtyFour$--$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostTenTwentyFour\times$
cheaper than the cheapest head rung on device ms (on complete-query ms
the dense input and output dominate both, Table~\ref{tab:waves}); the head ladder is monotone in $q$
(\nWaveHeadLadderMonotoneTwoFiftySix{}), cost is not. On waves POD $k'=64$
reaches $\nWavePodErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ against the bank's
$\nWaveBankErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ at the same cost. On the L-shaped domain
the sparse direct solve costs $\nLshapeSpluMsSixtyFour$,
$\nLshapeSpluMsOneTwentyEight$, $\nLshapeSpluMsTwoFiftySix$,
$\nLshapeSpluMsFiveTwelve$\,ms at $64^2$, $128^2$, $256^2$, $512^2$; at
$64^2$ it dominates everything and at $128^2$ only POD rungs join the
non-dominated set (\nLshapeOneTwentyEightBestReduced{} at
$\nLshapeOneTwentyEightBestReducedErr\,\%$,
$\nLshapeOneTwentyEightBestReducedCheaper\times$ the direct solve's cost),
cheapness rather than a trade; at $512^2$ the best POD rung,
\nLshapeFiveTwelveBestPod{}, reaches $\nLshapeFiveTwelveBestPodErr\,\%$ at
$\nLshapeFiveTwelveBestPodMs$\,ms. In a separate job at $M=1024$, whose
costs are not comparable with those above, the free rung $q=R$ is the one
place the learned bank beats the linear baseline on both axes, at
$\nLshapeFreeOverFloor\times$ its own bank floor with no solver iterations
(Table~\ref{tab:lshape-free}).
