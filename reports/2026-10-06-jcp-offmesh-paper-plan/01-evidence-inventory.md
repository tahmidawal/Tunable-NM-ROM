## 2. Known weaknesses and open items

1. **First-order refined reference (A2, A4, A6, B2).** Both lanes compare against a refined first-order upwind FOM. In 3D the reference differs from u_R by up to 2.28 % (R3D 43), the same order as the off-mesh error (2.83 %). The R'=256 256³ gap of 0.58 pp is explicitly "inside the reference's own uncertainty" (R3D 316). In 2D, ST includes the backward-Euler time error, about 1–2 % (LOG 18799). FOM `fft_tight` vs ST at 4096² is 2.698 % but only 0.148 % vs S (R2D 494). The ST numbers are therefore dominated by time error shared by every arm. "More accurate than the tensor/FOM" needs a second-order or spectral reference.
2. **Off-mesh is measured as more accurate only because the FOM is first-order upwind.** The gain is the stencil's O(h) gap, since the off-mesh rule integrates the continuum term (R3D 315). A JCP referee will ask why a smooth viscous Gaussian-bump problem uses a first-order FOM. Against a second-order FOM the accuracy argument shrinks to cost alone. The 3D bank was trained on fields of ≤129 nodes per axis (R3D 316).
3. **The head setting is not mesh-invariant in 2D** (B3, 1.91 on test). Test cases 36/48/60 move by up to 4.7 pp for every arm, dense included. The cause is not isolated (LOG 18790). The dev result "head B2 ≤1.007" was retracted on test (LOG 18799).
4. **The bank needs ~640² points for a certified continuum target**, against 64² for Hari's bank. Practical rules need Gauss 96² (acc) and ~1.6–4k Fibonacci (fast). The cause (128 Fourier features up to 11 cycles) is not isolated. A cheaper certified target was never searched (LOG 18797).
5. **Some cells are slower than the FOM.** In 3D at 64³ every reduced arm runs at 0.06–0.32× (R3D 256–262), and at 128³ R'=512 at 0.20–0.54×. In 2D at 256² the acc query is ~3.6× slower than `lean_nt3e-3` (R2D 1369). There is no matched-accuracy off-mesh speedup vs the FOM in 2D at all.
6. **Reference tolerance limit, 3D.** The re-check gave up to 1.022e-5 against a 1e-5 threshold, so refined-error differences below 1.0e-4 are unresolved (R3D 43, 325).
7. **Fixed-rule ρ grows with the mesh.** lat4096 at R'=512 crosses the 0.116 bar at 256³, so certification must be redone per mesh (R3D 318; LOG 18779).
8. **Selection instability.** Re-applying the 3D rule to held-out data picks lat8192 instead of the frozen gl24 (R3D 306–310, 327). The 2D pre-registered selection returned "none". The rules used are post hoc (A3, A5 amendments).
9. **Cohort reuse.** The 3D held-out cohort was evaluated earlier by the retry lane, and test64 is a historical cohort (R3D 330; R2D 1448). Nothing was chosen on either.
10. **"NM-ROM" framing.** Every off-mesh headline is a *linear span* of a coordinate-network bank. In 3D no head was admitted ("head still generalises badly (16–41 %)", LOG 18272). In 2D the only nonlinear setting (head) is the one that fails invariance and confirmation. This is the "when is the nonlinear manifold needed" objection (LOG 18282).
11. **Metric mismatch with earlier tables.** Table-1/ICLR rows use same-grid error (2D acc 0.22 %). The off-mesh lanes use refined error (~2.6 %), and on same-grid error the off-mesh arms look worse (3D 64³ 10.26 % vs tensor 1.52 %, R3D 115–117).
12. **Errors scored on subsampled nodes** (257² / 63³ lattice), not the full mesh (R2D 1446). In 2D, dense at 4096² ran on 6 cases only. In 2D, GPU types differ by mesh (A100 vs H200), so only within-job ratios are valid.
13. **Not done in either lane:** 512²/2048², the 3D k-head (iv), shift-averaged lattice statistics, and decomposition of the remaining 2.83 % (bank, Δt, test space, solver).
14. **Old manuscript inconsistency.** TEX §3.3 describes NNLS empirical quadrature, but the Burgers 2D Table-1 code uses the fixed `lat64` sub-lattice with no fit (LOG 18357, 18345). The 3D and NS rows use a precomputed tensor. A JCP paper must describe the deployed rule exactly.

**Retraction-touching numbers (flag in any reuse):**
- The Burgers speedup history: "Published ladder 0.72x -> 1.57x -> 4.46x -> 7.96x becomes 0.19x -> 0.36x -> 0.93x -> 1.83x" (memory `warmstart-hybrid-result-2026-08-17.md` 48). The note says the Burgers denominator was "formally unsettled". The current lanes use the 15-setting Newton–BiCGStab tolerance grid with a "fastest setting at least as accurate" rule, which supersedes the old one. Never quote a pre-09 Burgers speedup.
- The ICLR "21.7×/27.5×" fast speedups divide by an FOM matched to the *accurate* arm. The own-matched value is 13.76× (LOG 18292, 18346).
- Dev-cohort Burgers numbers (0.22 %, head 2.29 %) are superseded by test64 (1.35 %, 8.42 %) (LOG 18540, 18624).
- The heat rollout-freeze bug and the Poisson analytic-data inconsistency invalidate only `main`-package heat/Poisson numbers. Neither touches the Burgers off-mesh lanes. The coordinate-decoder claims "never rising" and "lossless mesh transfer" were retracted (memory `coord-decoder-resolution-findings.md`). Do not cite them as motivation.
- One heat comparison is held back by user instruction (memory `heat-pod-comparison-excluded.md`). It is not listed here.

## 3. Other PDEs and cells: can they carry the off-mesh story?

- **Poisson 2D/3D, L-shape, heat 2D/3D** (TEX Table 1, test rows): all linear. Their residual is precomputed exactly, so there is no nonlinear term to hyper-reduce. They are not off-mesh evidence. At most they show cost scaling of the linear part.
- **NS 3D:** has quadratic advection and an analytic div-free coordinate-net bank (E2), so off-mesh is technically possible. But the FOM is pseudo-spectral (CNAB2), which has **no O(h) consistency gap**, so only the cost story would carry. The coordnet bank floor is 8–10× worse than POD-64. Off-mesh quadrature on NS was never run. This is the best new-experiment candidate for generality.
- **Wave 2D:** reflective ROM fails on every head/arm (memory `wave2d-mechanism-outcome-2026-09-03.md`), and the wave equation is linear. Not usable.
- **Hari: Allen–Cahn, HJ, Bratu** (CPU, R=128, 6 cases): ladders and mesh invariance reproduce the Burgers picture. HJ is the hardest integrand (Fibonacci 4181 worst 1.5e-1). Stiff Allen–Cahn is unusable end-to-end (>100 % error with exact evaluation, HS 131–135). Mild Allen–Cahn is stable but its errors are 19–21 %. Allen–Cahn has no stencil gap (pointwise nonlinearity, ρ_mesh 5e-5), which makes it a clean pure-quadrature test. All need full-scale GPU reruns with held-out cohorts before journal use. HJ (monotone first-order stencil) would repeat the Burgers O(h) argument. Bratu (steady) shows every rule within 0.02 % of dense.
- **Low-viscosity Burgers** (LOG 72, item 7): under-resolved FOM, reduced-vs-reduced only. Not usable.

## 4. Missing entirely for a journal paper

1. **Hyper-reduced classical baselines at matched cost:** POD-LSPG/POD-Galerkin with DEIM/Q-DEIM, GNAT, ECSW/EQP. The existing POD-LSPG (E1) uses a dense residual at k=16 only. The masked-AE NM-LSPG had no hyper-reduction reproduced (TEX Table 3 caption).
2. **CROM-style baseline** (INR decoder with residual-driven greedy sampling on mesh nodes), the closest prior work per Hari's LITERATURE.md. Never run. Neither was Weder–Schwerdtner–Peherstorfer.
3. **A mesh-fitted EQ (NNLS) arm in 3D at full scale.** Hari's 3D NNLS reached only ρ = 0.6 at 2048 nodes (HS 287). Our 3D lanes compared only against the tensor.
4. **Second-order or spectral FOM and reference** (weakness 1/2). This is needed for any accuracy claim, and also for a fair "faster than FOM" claim (a second-order FOM at a coarser mesh could be much cheaper).
5. **Error analysis:** an a priori bound linking ρ (quadrature error of the tested term) to rollout error. The empirical finding that "quadrature error is small next to model error once converged" (R3D 317) has no theory. Nor is there any convergence-rate theory for lattice/Gauss rules on the decoded integrand (the super-algebraic claim is empirical, HS 69–72).
6. **Multiple lattice shifts / randomized QMC error bars;** multi-seed banks. Every lane uses one bank and one shift.
7. **Matched-accuracy speedup curves** (time-to-target-error) for off-mesh arms in 2D. Meshes 512²/2048² are also missing.
8. **Off-mesh with a nonlinear head that works** (3D head never admitted; 2D head not invariant), or an honest reframing as a learned linear trial space.
9. **A cheaper certified continuum target** and a study of bank bandwidth vs required m (Hari's decoder ablation hints that smoothness controls it).
10. **Memory/time scaling at very large N with full-mesh error scoring,** plus profiling of the mesh-side projection and decode, which dominate query time at 256³ (R3D 319).

## Strongest defensible claims today

1. In 3D Burgers (R=512 span), off-mesh lattice/Gauss rules give held-out refined errors flat across 64³–256³ (ratio 1.001–1.003), while the mesh tensor varies 2.1–2.7× (R3D 227–248).
2. At equal point count, in 3D, CBC rank-1 lattices beat tensor Gauss by 1–2 orders in ρ. Smolyak and lattice-256 controls fail (R3D 51–65, 291–298; HS 270–278).
3. The off-mesh advection data take 12.6× (gl24) to 43× (lat4096) less memory than the tensor at R'=512 (10.7× for lat4096 at R'=256). The selected rule's query is faster than the tensor's in all six 3D cells, by 1.06–1.40× (R3D 11, 256–276).
4. Off-mesh solves remove the FOM stencil's O(h) error from the ROM. The off-mesh–tensor distance tracks the dense-upwind continuum ρ (0.195/0.098/0.052) (LOG 18774).
5. In 2D Burgers linear rungs, resolved off-mesh rules are mesh-invariant case by case on test64 (≤0.011 pp spread). The deployed lattice is not (2.0×) (R2D 57, 74, 82).
6. 2D off-mesh solve time is flat from 256² to 4096² (ratio 0.97–1.04, 40 arms) and linear in m (R2D 1322–1365).
7. Post-hoc 2D rules (acc Gauss 96², fast Fibonacci 1597) confirmed on frozen test64 (R2D 66–67).
8. At 256³ the selected off-mesh rule gives 2.59× (same-grid rule) and 6.45× (refined rule) over Newton–BiCGStab, against 2.21× for the tensor. The 6.45× depends on a first-order reference (R3D 271).
9. Decoder smoothness, not the network type, controls off-mesh convergence. Interpolated POD loses 2–4 orders (HS 224–243; scaled-down CPU, qualitative only).
10. All of the above are Burgers-only at full scale, linear-span only, and against first-order references. Generality to other PDEs and nonlinear heads is not yet established.
