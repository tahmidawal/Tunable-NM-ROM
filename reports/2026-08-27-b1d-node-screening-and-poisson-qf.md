# 1D Burgers node-learning screening, and the Poisson quadrature-free confirmation

This report covers two one-seed screening experiments run 2026-08-27 (branch
`exp/2026-08-27-b1d-poissonqf`, cluster jobs 2967085–2967094, all COMPLETED):
(A) frozen-decoder quadrature-node learning on a new **1D viscous Burgers**
testbed at $N = 128/256/512$, and (B) the long-designed **quadrature-free
exact residual** for the project's 2D linear Poisson problem at the same
resolutions. Numbers are **final for these checkpoints but single-seed
screening results** — no multi-seed confirmation has run (deliberately held
back pending review, per the experiment brief). Tables are generated by
`gen_2026-08-27-b1d-nodes-and-poisson-qf.py` from the run JSONs.

## Part A — 1D Burgers: does node learning transfer to 1D, and where does it pay?

**Setup.** New self-contained 1D testbed (`b1d_common.py`,
`sep_b1d_screen.py`; nothing 1D existed in this repository): the 2D
discretization one dimension down — sign-upwind advection $u\,u_x$, centered
diffusion, backward Euler $\Delta t = 0.005 \times 50$ steps, Newton with a
dense interior Jacobian, truth-residual gate $10^{-8}$. Separable decoder
$u(x;z) = bc(x)\,\langle g(x), h(z)\rangle$ with $K{=}8$, $R{=}32$, trained
normally in-job (40k steps, 512 trajectories, seed 0) and then **completely
frozen**. Weak form on $M{=}32$ sine test modes (exact eigenvectors of the
1D discrete Laplacian), all linear terms exact via $A = \Phi^\top G$; only
advection is sampled. Budgets: $m = M = 32$ (tight, as briefed),
$m = \min(4M, N{-}2)$ (generous, as briefed — 126 at $N{=}128$, i.e. every
interior point, 128 at $N{=}256/512$), plus an added $m = M/2 = 16$ pair
because the $N{=}64$ smoke showed $m{=}M$ binds only mildly in 1D. Six arms
per resolution, one instrument: full-grid oracle, NNLS and learned nodes at
each tight budget, NNLS at the generous budget; 8 fresh test trajectories,
gates E/F/C/D all passed at every $N$, `jax_backend=gpu` in every log.

**F-A1 — the 1D decoder is resolution-flat and good.** Held-out
reconstruction is $3.7\text{–}3.8\times 10^{-3}$ and the oracle rollout
$4.9\text{–}6.1\times 10^{-3}$ at every resolution (T-B2) — the separable
architecture behaves in 1D exactly as it does in 2D.

**F-A2 — at $m{=}M$ the quadrature barely binds in 1D.** The NNLS baseline
at 32 nodes sits only $+2$ to $+10\%$ above the oracle (T-B2, "quadrature
share"), unlike 2D where $m{=}M$ left an 80–100% residual mismatch. In 1D,
32 points sample a 1D advection field well.

**F-A3 — learned nodes recover almost the whole removable gap at $m{=}M$,
at every resolution.** Rollout error vs the same-budget NNLS baseline:
$-8.4\%$ / $-1.8\%$ / $-6.1\%$ at $N{=}128/256/512$, which is **84–97% of
the gap to the oracle** (T-B2); the learned-node arms land within $0.4\%$ of
the oracle. The win is real but small because the gap itself is small.

**F-A4 — where the quadrature binds hard ($m{=}M/2{=}16$), node learning is
worth a factor of about two, resolution-stably.** The 16-node NNLS baseline
is $+318$ to $+446\%$ above the oracle; learned nodes cut rollout error by
$\mathbf{-52\% / -58\% / -61\%}$ at $N{=}128/256/512$ (held-out gradient
rung 3–4$\times$ better, cosine 0.50–0.65 $\to$ 0.65–0.74). This is the
clean 1D replication of the 2D rule: **node learning pays in proportion to
how badly the budget binds.**

**F-A5 — learned nodes still do not substitute for budget (1D replication
of the 2026-08-27 matched-accuracy refutation).** Learned 16-node sets
($1.0\text{–}1.1\times 10^{-2}$) never reach the 32-node NNLS baseline
($5.2\text{–}6.3\times 10^{-3}$) — roughly $2\times$ worse at half the
budget, at every resolution.

**Caveats, ranked.** (1) One seed, one checkpoint per resolution, 8 test
trajectories. (2) The per-trajectory times in T-B1 (~0.4–0.5 s) are
host-dispatch-bound at these tiny sizes and do not resolve $m$; they are
recorded for completeness (burn-in + 3 persisted repetitions, accuracy from
the same invocation), not as a speed claim. (3) The generous arm at
$N{=}128$ uses all 126 interior points, so it coincides with the oracle by
construction; at $N{=}256/512$ the 128-node generous fit is also essentially
exact (rel fit $\sim 2\times 10^{-6}$), so the generous rung adds no
information beyond "4M is enough in 1D".

## Part B — Poisson 2D: the quadrature-free exact residual, confirmed

**Setup.** The cell designed on 2026-08-26 and never run: with the separable
decoder, the whole weak residual of the linear Poisson problem collapses to
$r(z) = W_\ell\,(B\,h(z)) - f_m$ with $B = \Phi^\top \mathrm{diag}(w)\,G$ an
$(M{\times}R)$ matrix precomputed once — **no empirical-quadrature sample
points at all**. Driver `sep_poisson_qf.py` runs three residual paths
through the *same* incumbent LM solver: FULL (all interior nodes), EQ (the
incumbent NNLS rule, $m = 4M = 256$), and QF. Frozen decoders inherited from
the 2026-08-23 scaling arms ($K{=}16$; provenance and SHA-256 in
`runs/inherited_qf/`); truth regenerated in-job by CG at $10^{-13}$ from the
recorded seeds; held-out + fresh-seed cohorts (16 sources each); balanced
interleaved timing after GPU burn-in, every repetition persisted.

**F-B1 — exactness, the confirmation itself.** Gate Q: the QF residual and
gradient equal the full-grid reference to $\le 1.3\times 10^{-15}$ at random
states and $\le 2.9\times 10^{-13}$ at every solver solution, **zero
failures at all three resolutions** (T-P3). Linear Poisson does not require
empirical quadrature under this architecture — the elliptic case is now a
measured fact, not a design note.

**F-B2 — identical accuracy, fastest online path, N-independent.** All
three paths reach the same solve error (the decoder floor,
$3.06\text{–}3.15\times 10^{-2}$) with the same iteration counts. The QF
solve is the fastest at every resolution (2.7–2.9 ms, flat in $N$) while
FULL grows 4.0 → 11.1 ms and EQ sits at 2.8–3.1 ms (T-P1).

**F-B3 — QF removes the entire quadrature-fitting stage.** One-time setup:
25–37 s of NNLS fit + node bank for EQ versus **under one second** for the
QF matrix build (T-P2). There is nothing to fit, nothing to certify, and no
quadrature error to track.

**F-B4 — what EQ was actually costing (fidelity, not final error).** At the
solver's solutions the EQ residual is 5.5–7.9% wrong and its gradient can
point *away* from the true gradient (cosine down to $-0.65$; the huge
relative gradient numbers in T-P2 are a cancellation artifact — the true
gradient is nearly zero at solutions, see glossary). At these checkpoints
the decoder floor hides that from the final error; QF makes the question
moot.

**Caveats.** All $\tau{=}10^{-3}$ solves stop on convergence/stall before
reaching $\tau$ (100% censored — the long-standing behavior of these
checkpoints, recorded in T-P1); the accuracy comparison is
like-for-like across paths and unaffected. One seed per cohort draw;
checkpoint configurations differ slightly across $N$ (R=96 at $N{=}128$,
R=64 elsewhere — inherited, disclosed).

## Recommendation

- **Adopt the quadrature-free residual as the default for linear Poisson**
  (and any purely linear weak term): exact by construction, fastest online,
  and it deletes the NNLS stage entirely. This is the elliptic endpoint of
  the exlin rule.
- On Burgers-type problems, the 1D screening supports the existing 2D rule
  set: learned nodes are worth adopting **only at budgets where the
  quadrature demonstrably binds** (here $m < M$ in 1D), where they buy
  50–60%; they are not a substitute for a larger budget.
- Multi-seed confirmation of Part A is designed and cheap (each job was
  ~3.5 min on one A100) but **has not been launched**, per the brief.

## Glossary — every term and table column, in plain language

- **FOM / ROM** — the full-order model (the PDE solved on the whole grid;
  the expensive truth) / the reduced-order model (the cheap surrogate that
  solves for a small latent vector and decodes it to the grid).
- **Decoder** — the trained network mapping a latent vector $z$ to a full
  solution field. Frozen everywhere in these experiments after its normal
  training.
- **Oracle (arm)** — the ROM run with the advection term evaluated on the
  entire grid: zero sampling error. Its rollout error is the decoder's own
  floor; every sampled arm's excess over it is quadrature error.
- **m vs M** — $M$ is the number of weak test functions (sine modes, 32 in
  Part A, 64 in Part B); $m$ is how many grid points the ROM samples to
  approximate the one nonlinear term. Budgets here: tight $m{=}M$, halved
  $m{=}M/2$, generous $m{=}4M$ (capped at the interior size).
- **NNLS (base arms)** — non-negative least squares: the standard convex fit
  that picks sample points and their weights from grid candidates. "base"
  rows use these.
- **Learned nodes ("nodes" arms)** — starting from the NNLS points,
  gradient descent slides the points continuously (weights re-solved by
  NNLS every 500 steps) to make the sampled advection term and its
  latent-gradient match the exact full-grid versions.
- **Rollout err** — mean relative error of the ROM trajectory against the
  FOM truth over 50 time steps, averaged over 8 fresh test trajectories no
  fit ever saw. The number that decides everything in Part A.
- **Held (b)** — relative error of the sampled advection projection vs the
  exact full-grid one, on states excluded from every fit. 0.47 means 47%
  wrong (badly binding); $10^{-6}$ means essentially exact.
- **Held (c1) / (c1) cos** — the same comparison for the gradient the
  solver actually follows: relative error and direction agreement (1.0 =
  same direction). In T-P2's "at solutions" rows the *relative* gradient
  error is huge for EQ because the true gradient is nearly zero there
  (the solver stopped at a minimum) — direction (cos) and the absolute
  gate are the meaningful measures at solutions.
- **EQ rel fit** — how well the NNLS weights reproduce the training
  projections. Certifies nothing about solver behavior (a standing project
  rule); shown for completeness.
- **vs base / vs oracle** — percent change in rollout error against the
  same-budget NNLS baseline / against the oracle arm.
- **Quadrature share at m=M** (T-B2) — the fraction of the tight-budget
  arm's rollout error that sampling added: (base − oracle)/base.
- **Learned-nodes recovery** (T-B2) — how much of that removable share the
  learned nodes eliminated: (base − learned)/(base − oracle).
- **FULL / EQ / QF** (Part B) — three ways to evaluate the same weak
  residual: on all interior points; on the 256 NNLS points; or exactly,
  through one precomputed matrix, with no points at all (possible because
  Poisson is linear, so the whole residual is linear in the decoder's
  coefficient vector).
- **Gate Q** — the exactness check: the quadrature-free residual and
  gradient must equal the full-grid reference to machine precision, at
  random states and at every solver solution.
- **Censored** — the solver stopped for a reason other than reaching the
  requested tolerance $\tau$ (converged/stalled first). 100% at
  $\tau{=}10^{-3}$ is the long-standing behavior of these Poisson
  checkpoints; the comparison across paths is unaffected because all three
  paths behave identically.
- **Setup s** — one-time offline cost: for EQ the NNLS fit plus node-bank
  build; for QF the single matrix product. The shared full-grid readout
  bank (needed by every path to decode the final field) is excluded from
  both.
- **Gates E/F/C/D (Part A)** — pre-flight identity checks: sine modes are
  exact discrete-Laplacian eigenvectors; the weak operators equal the
  projected FOM residual; the continuous node machinery equals the grid
  machinery at the grid initialization; the training loss's autodiff
  gradient matches finite differences. All passed at every resolution.
- **One-seed screening** — a single random seed for data, training, and
  fits; enough to rank arms and decide follow-ups, not to quote error bars.
