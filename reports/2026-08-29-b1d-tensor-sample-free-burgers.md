## What this is, in the literature's words

The decoder is $u = G\,h(z)$: a frozen $n\times R$ bank $G$ ($R=32$) and a nonlinear head
$h:\mathbb R^{8}\to\mathbb R^{32}$. The ROM is therefore a **32-dimensional linear-subspace
Galerkin ROM restricted to an 8-dimensional learned submanifold of coefficient space**. For
a polynomial nonlinearity such a ROM has *precomputable reduced operators* — the standard
intrusive construction of POD-Galerkin for Burgers (Kunisch–Volkwein), the object DEIM was
invented to avoid at large $R$ (Chaturantabut–Sorensen), and the object operator inference
learns instead of projecting (Peherstorfer–Willcox; Kramer–Willcox lift-and-learn;
quadratic-manifold variants of Barnett–Farhat and Geelen–Wright–Willcox). Nothing in the
tensor itself is new. **The delta is the setting**: a learned bank with an MLP coefficient
map, weak form on Laplacian eigenmodes with exact linear terms, and the observation that
linearity in $h$ buys back precomputability that a generic nonlinear decoder
(Lee–Carlberg style) loses. The correct description is "exact projection of a polynomial
nonlinearity — no hyper-reduction needed"; the project's shorthand "sample-free" means
that and nothing more.

$$
\Phi^\top\big(u \odot D^- u\big) = \tfrac12\, h^\top Q\, h,\qquad
T_{ijk} = \sum_x \Phi_{xi}\,G_{xj}\,(D^-G)_{xk},\quad Q = T + T^{(j\leftrightarrow k)},
\qquad \partial_z\big(\tfrac12 h^\top Q h\big) = (Qh)\,\partial_z h .
$$

$T$ is not symmetric (bank vs differenced bank), so the symmetrised $Q$ is what the
Jacobian needs. The FOM's sign-upwind stencil is not polynomial, but decomposes exactly as

$$
N_{\text{upwind}}(u) = u\,D^{c}u - \tfrac{\Delta x}{2}\,|u|\,\Delta_h u,
$$

so on non-negative fields $|u|=u$ and the backward-difference tensor is the full-grid
projection exactly. The 1D family (positive blobs, zero walls) is non-negative on the truth;
the only discrepancy comes from *decoded* fields undershooting zero.

## Findings

**F1 — the tensor arm equals the full-grid oracle, six checkpoints and two fresh decoders,
$N = 128 \ldots 65\,536$.** Per-trajectory rollout error agrees to $\le 1.2\times10^{-6}$
absolute at $N{=}128$, falling to $\le 2.5\times10^{-7}$ at $N \ge 512$ and $\le
6\times10^{-10}$ at $N \ge 16\,384$ (T-2, T-6, T-9). Review R3 reproduced every printed
digit on CPU and measured the stricter observable — the direct relative difference between
the two arms' decoded fields — at $\le 9\times10^{-6}$ (worst trajectory, $N{=}128$). The
stop-reason histograms are identical at every $N$, but that is a coarse statistic (98–99 %
of steps end on the stall rule for every arm, sampled ones included); the finer statistic in
the JSONs — accepted Jacobian evaluations per step — is identical on 31 of 32 trajectories
at $N \le 1024$ and on all trajectories at $N \ge 2048$ (T-7). Zero sample points, no NNLS
fit, no node training. **Against the sampled rules the tensor is not distinguishable from
NNLS-32 or learned-32 at $n = 8$ trajectories** (paired sign test $p \ge 0.07$, bootstrap
intervals on the error ratio touch 1; R3) — the oracle already sat 1–9 % under NNLS-32 in
the 08-27 ladder, and the tensor inherits that margin, which is seed-noise scale.

**F2 — exact on the positivity cone; the deviation is first order in the undershoot and
bounded.** Gate T0: on the ~2900–3100 training states whose decoded field is entirely
positive, $h^\top T h$ equals $\Phi^\top N_{\text{upwind}}(Gh)$ to $\le 8.4\times10^{-15}$
at every $N$ (T-3, T-11). The algebraic identity against the fixed backward stencil holds
on all 8192 states to $1.3\times10^{-14}$; two accumulation orders agree to $10^{-15}$. The
residual difference from the oracle is exactly
$$
q_T - q_{\text{or}} = -\Delta x\,\Phi^\top\big[\mathbb 1[u\le 0]\;u\;\Delta_h u\big],
$$
(verified to $3\times10^{-13}$ on all states by R3). It is **first order** in the
undershoot depth $\delta$ (measured log-log slope 1.1–1.4), multiplied by $\Delta x$ and by
the blob's own curvature at its feet ($|\Delta_h u| \approx 1.6$–$1.9$ there) — small
because $\Delta x$ is small, not because $\delta$ enters squared; the measured medians scale
as $1/N$. The per-state bound $|q_T-q_{\text{or}}|_i \le \Delta x\,\delta\sum_{u\le0}
|\Phi_{xi}||\Delta_h u_x|$ holds with the measured mismatch at a median 0.4 of it. The sign
audit along the oracle's rollout (every residual-evaluated, in-radius LM candidate, accepted
or rejected: 1339–1642 per $N$) finds 60–68 % touch a point with $u\le0$ (min $u \approx
-0.008$; 6.7–7.0 % of decoded grid points on training states, min $\approx -0.02$), which is
why parity is $10^{-6}$–$10^{-7}$ and **not** bit-exact. Off-manifold states beyond the
trust radius (gate TQ, perturbed half) reach residual/Jacobian mismatches of $10^{-3}$ at
min $u = -0.3$; the trust region (0.017 in $z$) keeps rollouts away from them. **f64 is
required**: cancellation in the contraction costs ~2.5 digits (R3), and in f32 the tensor's
roundoff ($\sim10^{-6}$) would exceed the undershoot term at $N{=}1024$.

**F3 — the latent solve does not grow with $N$; the full-grid oracle does once it leaves the
launch floor.** On one A100 in one process, resolutions alternating and repetitions
outermost (T-6), the tensor arm's latent solve is 14.3–20.2 ms per 50-step trajectory from
$N{=}128$ to $4096$ with fitted exponent $-0.08$ (oracle $-0.04$, NNLS-32 $-0.07$): every
arm is under the launch floor there, including the $O(N)$ oracle. At $N = 16\,384$ and
$65\,536$ — separate jobs on the same GPU model, fresh decoders trained in-job at the same
$3.76\times10^{-3}$ floor as every smaller $N$ — the oracle's solve rises 21.4 → 32.9 ms
while the tensor's stays 14.2 → 13.8 ms (T-10). **These two points are cross-job and give a
ratio, not an exponent** (R4, R5): they show qualitatively that the oracle has begun its
$O(N)$ climb and the tensor has not. The claim is scoped exactly as the reviews require:
*the cached latent solve is $N$-independent by construction and measured flat; the IC fit
($G^\top u_0$) and the final decode still touch the grid* — 6–9 ms and <1 ms here, flat only
because they too are launch-bound at these sizes. The "µs per LM attempt" column in T-8
divides by loop attempts only (the per-step initial evaluation is not counted), so its
slopes are not meaningful (R5); it is kept as an amortisation, not a per-iteration cost.

**F4 — cost: within about 7 % of the sampled rule; no 1D speedup.** End-to-end, tensor /
NNLS-32 is 0.93–1.07 across the one-GPU ladder and 1.00–1.02 at large $N$ (T-7, T-10).
The like-for-like implementation comparison against the oracle is `tensor_nolean` (the
oracle path has no lean fold): 0.88–0.95× at $N \le 1024$, growing to 0.59× at
$N{=}65\,536$. The `tensor` arm's 0.78–0.84× includes the fold. Arm order in the original
four jobs was sequential (ref/fast paths interleaved within an arm), and IC timing drifted
7–10 % between arm blocks there; the one-GPU ladder fixes the $N$ order but keeps a fixed
arm order, so percent-level ratios carry that noise. In 1D everything is launch-bound and
the FOM comparison of the 08-27 report is unchanged.

**F5 — what it removes, and where it stops.** On this problem class the tensor deletes the
NNLS quadrature fit, the $m$ sweep, the stage-2 node learning, and the checkpoint-dependence
of node sets, at the accuracy of the best of them. It does **not** extend as written to
sign-changing data (the $|u|$ term is not tabulated), to non-polynomial physics, or to the
2D Burgers bank as it stands: **the 2D headline runs used $R = 512$, not 64**, so the 2D
tensor would be $128\times512^2 \approx 3.4\times10^{7}$ entries (270 MB f64) against
$\sim1.6\times10^{5}$ flops for 256-node sampling — the synthesis' 2 MB estimate was wrong
(R2; corrected there). A 2D port needs compression (projecting $h$ onto the directions it
spans) before it is a cost story.

## What the reviews changed (all five verdicts: "legitimate with corrections")

R1 (Fable, code audit): no leakage — $T$ from bank and modes only; oracle is the committed
baseline path; error against regenerated truth on a seed-disjoint set; perturbation test
shows the arms diverge when the field goes negative. Corrections: "arms interleaved" was
false; like-for-like oracle ratio is `tensor_nolean`; disclose the off-manifold TQ maximum;
cite the Jacobian-count statistic. R2 (Fable, methodology): reposition against the
literature; drop "beats NNLS-32"; first-order bound; report min of the truth; $R{=}512$ in 2D;
stall-dominated stopping. R3 (Fable, numerics): "second order" wrong; `njac_equal=False` on
one trajectory; NNLS margin not distinguishable from zero; f64 required; direct field
difference $\le 9\times10^{-6}$. R4 (Codex, code audit): large-$N$ exponents are cross-job
ratios; timed e2e is steady-state online cost (the $Q$ build, 304 ms at $N{=}65\,536$, is
offline); "every LM candidate" means every residual-evaluated in-radius candidate; tensor
artefacts record $m=n_i$ in metadata (harmless, wrong). R5 (Codex, cost): constant-time
scoped to the latent solve; per-attempt normalisation excludes the per-step initial
evaluation; parity stated as "within about 7 %"; the oracle's large-$N$ growth is a
transition, not its asymptotic exponent (which is 1). Every item is applied above. Still
open from the reviews: a sign-changing family (expected failure, needed to make the scope
credible), $\ge 3$ seeds $\times \ge 32$ trajectories with paired CIs, and the numerical
statement that the *truth* is non-negative on every snapshot.

## Glossary

- **Tensor arm / $T$, $Q$** — the advection term evaluated as a precomputed 3-index table
  contracted with the head output twice; $Q$ is the symmetrised table used for the Jacobian.
- **Exact projection / no hyper-reduction** — the literature's name for what the tensor
  does: the reduced nonlinear operator is computed once, exactly, so no sampling
  (DEIM/GNAT/EQ) approximation is needed. "Sample-free" is the project's shorthand for it.
- **Oracle** — the advection term summed over every grid point with the sign-upwind
  stencil; the best any sampled rule can do with this decoder; costs $O(N)$ per evaluation.
- **NNLS-32 / learned-32** — the sampled rules: 32 nodes with non-negative least-squares
  weights, at grid positions (NNLS) or gradient-learned positions (learned).
- **Sign-upwind / backward difference** — the FOM's stencil looks left where $u>0$ and right
  where $u<0$; the tensor always looks left. They coincide wherever $u>0$.
- **Undershoot** — decoded field values slightly below zero where the truth is non-negative;
  the only source of tensor-vs-oracle mismatch.
- **LM candidate** — a latent state at which the solver evaluated the residual (inside the
  trust radius), whether the step was then accepted or rejected.
- **Stop-reason histogram** — how each of the 400 time-step solves terminated (0 = iteration
  budget, 2 = stall = relative decrease below $10^{-3}$; the absolute tolerance is
  unreachable for a 32-equation / 8-unknown least-squares residual, so stall is the
  de-facto convergence rule).
- **Latent solve / IC fit / decode / e2e** — the 50 implicit steps in latent space (the
  $N$-independent part); the projection of the initial condition ($O(N)$, once); decoding
  all states ($O(N)$, once); their sum, per trajectory, median over persisted repetitions.
- **Launch floor** — at small problem sizes each GPU operation finishes faster than the next
  can be launched, so wall time counts operations, not arithmetic; every arm here sits on it
  up to $N \approx 10^4$.
- **Lean / nolean** — whether the table is folded into the head's last layer before the
  rollout (fewer kernels) or applied separately; the oracle path has no fold, so
  `tensor_nolean` is its like-for-like comparison.
- **Gates T0 / TB / TA / TQ / J / T2 / E / F / C / G / V** — exactness on all-positive states;
  build-order agreement; algebraic identity vs the fixed stencil; tensor-vs-oracle at 32
  random latent states (recorded, not asserted); and the inherited solver/data gates.
