# Classical spatial-tree PDE solvers

Research synthesis, October 27, 2026. These are primary-source findings from the completed Astra literature review, not project measurements. Literal spatial trees are distinguished from block trees and generic hierarchical algorithms. No literature speedup is transferred to this project.

## Verified examples and source scope

**Stéphane Popinet, “Gerris: a tree-based adaptive solver for the incompressible Euler equations in complex geometries” (JCP, 2003).** [Full paper](https://gerris.dalembert.upmc.fr/gerris.pdf), particularly sections 2, 4, and 6–8. Literal quadtree in two dimensions and octree in three; adaptive cell-based projection, embedded solid boundaries, and a multilevel pressure-Poisson solver. Coarse/fine pressure gradients are constructed to match fluxes, with interpolation between differently sized cells. Vorticity drives dynamic refinement. This is an actual governing-PDE solve. The refinement indicator is not a rigorous complete solution-error bound; the cell-centered projection is approximate, refinement is isotropic, and tree bookkeeping has a cost.

**Frank Losasso, Frédéric Gibou, Ronald Fedkiw, “Simulating Water and Smoke with an Octree Data Structure” (ACM TOG, 2004).** [Full paper](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs667/2005sp/readings/losasso04.pdf), sections 3–6, especially 4.3. Unrestricted spatial octree for three-dimensional incompressible water/smoke, pressure Poisson, semi-Lagrangian advection, and level-set interfaces. Face divergence and a symmetric pressure formulation support preconditioned conjugate gradients; characteristic sampling uses trilinear interpolation. Refinement can follow interfaces and vorticity. Section 4.3 identifies first-order advection/Poisson accuracy. This graphics-oriented result must not be presented as evidence of a high-order conservative transport method.

**Rahul S. Sampath, Santi S. Adavani, Hari Sundar, Ilya Lashuk, George Biros, “Dendro: Parallel algorithms for multigrid and AMR methods on 2:1 balanced octrees” (SC, 2008).** [Full paper](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~hari/files/pubs/sc08.pdf), sections I–V. Balanced spatial octrees, trilinear finite elements for variable-coefficient elliptic problems, and a parabolic moving-feature demonstration. Matrix-free operators, transfer, geometric multigrid, hanging-node constraints, and parallel partitioning are already part of this conventional architecture. Section IV evaluates existing finite-element shape functions at new coordinates for transfer. Crucial limit: the paper explicitly does not supply an error estimator; the moving-mesh example uses the exact solution. Robustness for discontinuous coefficients, complex geometry, and load balance has scoped limitations.

**Stéphane Popinet, “Quadtree-adaptive tsunami modelling” (Ocean Dynamics, 2011).** [Full paper](https://gerris.dalembert.upmc.fr/papers/tsunami.pdf), sections 3.1–3.4 and 4. A literal planar quadtree solves Saint-Venant/shallow-water equations with wetting/drying. The finite-volume method uses a Godunov formulation, HLLC flux, hydrostatic reconstruction, and predictor-corrector stepping. It preserves lake-at-rest balance, nonnegative depth, and coarse/fine conservation; free-surface gradients guide refinement and bathymetry is multiscale. The threshold is problem dependent and the timestep is global rather than locally subcycled.

**Michael Kazhdan, Hugues Hoppe, “An adaptive multigrid solver for applications in computer graphics” (Computer Graphics Forum, 2019).** [Full paper](https://hhoppe.com/adaptivemultigrid.pdf); [author project page](https://hhoppe.com/proj/adaptivemultigrid/), sections 3–5. Quadtree/octree finite-element spaces for Poisson-like symmetric positive-definite systems, including Laplacian/biharmonic reconstruction and image applications. Continuous, compactly supported B-splines, variable degree/dimension, weak integral and point constraints, sparse support coupling, and multigrid V-cycles establish the most direct classical counterexample to claiming “continuous functions plus trees plus weak solves” as new. Refinement-boundary ringing is treated heuristically in section 5. This is not a universal certified nonlinear PDE solver.

**Jannis Teunissen, Rony Keppens, “A geometric multigrid library for quadtree/octree AMR grids coupled to MPI-AMRVAC” (CPC, online 2019).** [Full paper](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/28841/28841.pdf), sections 2.2–2.6, 3, 4. This is explicitly a **tree of blocks**, balanced in two and three dimensions, rather than an assertion that every individual unknown is an octree leaf. Second-order elliptic solves and magnetohydrodynamic divergence cleaning use parallel V-cycles/full multigrid, boundary handling, and transfers. The adaptive application supplies the tree; the library is not a complete estimator. Refinement boundaries can slow convergence; supported operators and smoothers constrain use. Algebraic residual reduction is distinguished from discretization error.

## Fair comparison and implementation consequences

Strong adaptive finite-element/finite-volume solvers already supply local resolution, sparse interactions, hierarchical corrections, and solved coefficients. Finite elements already evaluate continuous fields and their appropriate derivatives at arbitrary coordinates. A neural bank must earn a benefit through family-trained approximation or reduced iterations, not by renaming these capabilities. Compare learned local features with polynomial, spline, wavelet, and local-POD alternatives, with matched acceptance and output. Ordinary methods can also reuse meshes, geometry, setup, factorizations, preconditioners, and preceding solutions across queries.

A compact cost comparison is

$$
T_{\mathrm{FOM}}=T_{\mathrm{adapt/setup}}+n_{\mathrm{it}}C_{\mathrm{sparse}}+T_{\mathrm{output}},
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{bank}}=T_{\mathrm{select/assemble}}+T_{\mathrm{reduced\ solve}}+T_{\mathrm{check}}+T_{\mathrm{decode}}+T_{\mathrm{fallback}}.
$$

Effective multigrid can approach linear work in active unknowns under suitable assumptions. A smaller dense reduced system can nevertheless lose: global tests, dense QR/Jacobians, or dense contractions can erase spatial locality. Full-field output and unrestricted coefficient-input scans remain proportional to their requested sizes. Solution-trained modes need not span the late errors that slow an iterative solver. Training, cache construction, adaptation, and failure recovery require separate accounting and eventual amortization.

Repeated localized heterogeneous diffusion or quasi-steady conduction is a defensible candidate because repeated family structure could be reusable. Geometry and contrast alone do not imply a neural advantage. First use a reduced weak-solve guess followed by an independently accepted conventional solve; this requires appropriate diffusion data/banks, not transferring Burgers weights without justification. Start with a correct two-dimensional reference and a fixed adaptive mesh, tune the conventional solver, compare representation and warm-start controls, then include dynamic adaptation. Continue only when prediction, mapping, and checks cost less than the conventional work they save. Stop or redirect if POD dominates, setup/output dominates, almost every patch activates, or fallback is frequent.

## Glossary

A quadtree splits a planar region into four; an octree splits a volume into eight. A block tree stores small structured grids at its nodes/leaves. FE/FV mean finite element/finite volume. AMR changes spatial resolution. A hanging node needs coarse/fine compatibility constraints. POD is data-based linear compression. Multigrid removes errors through multiple spatial scales. A warm start is an initial guess. A fallback is a validated alternative after failure. The two cost expressions distinguish setup/adaptation, iterations, sparse work, reduced assembly/solve, checks, and requested output; they are accounting identities, not performance predictions.
