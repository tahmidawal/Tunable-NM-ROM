**Overall verdict: NOT-READY.** A1 resolves substantial parts of round 1, but leaves blocking reference, qualification, selection and stop-rule issues. It also introduces contradictions around rule construction and coverage. This was read-only: no files changed, jobs submitted, or arrays copied.

1. **Problem, families, pairing and cohorts — CORRECT.** A1.1 resolves the round-1 concerns: distinct split seeds, descriptor checks, freezing all retained cells before test opening, paired-row inference, and explicit acknowledgment that width changes both translation variability and wall proximity. Pairing across cells is beneficial and does not itself cause leakage. `train-c` is legitimately derived from training rows. **Concrete safeguard:** implement disjointness using descriptor rows, not merely unequal whole-array hashes; retain base-row identifiers after reference eligibility filtering so cross-cell comparisons remain properly paired.

2. **References — WRONG as a binding-contract resolution; NEEDS-RESTATEMENT numerically.** A1.2 improves the estimator substantially: continuous coefficient-space gates, a spatial decay check, padding uncertainty, the correct high-resolution IC rule, and conditional labeling. Nevertheless, COMMON-BRIEF §2 explicitly requires the second-order/Richardson recipe for new problems. The lane brief’s “build on” references and approximate \(N_{\rm ref}=768\) motivate spectral references but do not explicitly replace that uncertainty contract; A1’s assertion does not resolve the round-1 objection. A4 remains unvalidated—the pilot did not run it. There are also new specification gaps: the introduction says gates cover “every split,” but padding populations name only dev/val; \(U\) is defined only over eligible **selection** cases, leaving test allowances undefined. Excluding individual reference failures also replaces the original whole-cell failure policy and can remove the hardest cases from worst-error scoring. **Fix:** record an explicit governing amendment authorizing the spectral conditional estimator; define separate selection/test allowances and padding populations, eligibility counts and coverage consequences. State that sampled padding maxima are extrapolated estimates. Add a roundoff-floor branch to the decay test, so two negligible numerical differences need not exhibit a fourfold ratio. Keep \(N=768\) provisional until its gates pass; \(2\max\varepsilon_{\rm ref}\) is a conditional allowance, not a certified bound.

3. **Arms 4.1–4.6 — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT overall.**

   - **4.1 F-SPEC — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** A1 defines the step ladder and adds useful spatial endpoint checks. But extension is triggered by the *smallest feasible* \(N\), not the fastest candidate sitting at the spatial boundary; a larger \(N\) can permit cheaper stepping even when smaller sizes meet the target. Step-cap and two-invalid-rung exits also need coverage labels. **Fix:** inspect the selected optimum’s boundaries, specify extension or explicit truncation labels for each binding knob, and distinguish an exhausted search from a demonstrated lack of feasible configurations.

   - **4.2 F-SPEC-AD — CORRECT algorithmically, with coverage clarification needed.** Integer ticks, aligned coarsening, \(j_0=2\), coefficients through \(J+1\), normalization, rejected-trial costs and the attempt cap complete the main round-1 requirements. The dyadic alignment preserves exact output times. Accepting a failed finest-level trial and then invalidating the entire run is awkward but cannot qualify an inaccurate result if enforced. **Fix:** terminate immediately on that failure, and explicitly say whether the spatial extension rule also applies to AD. Report tolerance/level boundary hits as search limitations.

   - **4.3 R-BANK — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Retraining on the prescribed eight cells and retaining both steppers is faithful. A1 correctly replaces the impossible universal \(4r\) test prefix with \(\min(4r,R_{\rm eff})\), scopes the incumbent tolerance, and improves training-resolution disclosure. However, the common rule construction now depends on a **trained bank Gram matrix**, while POD/ECSW must run at widths where no bank is trained. Rank handling is also unfinished: `bank.rotation` returns a data-dependent \(R_{\rm eff}\), and `rom.make_query` asserts \(r\le R_{\rm eff}\). **Fix:** define rule construction independently for each representation/cell, explicitly skip unavailable ranks, and specify the exact box adapter interface and coordinate scaling.

   - **4.4 R-PODG — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The coefficient-space adapter and Parseval/operator checks address the incorrect FD-POD reuse claim. But the rule’s construction remains dependent on the unresolved bank policy. The geometry POD implementation also retains Gram eigenvalues down to \(10^{-26}\) relative, which is not a defensible numerical-rank threshold for a Gram matrix accumulated in f64; a new adapter should not inherit that blindly. **Fix:** register stable numerical-rank handling and an independent POD rule policy. Treat G-PILOT as a convergence comparison between the Gauss and pseudo-spectral nonlinear operators; establish that their finite discretizations can meet the proposed \(10^{-8}\) bar.

   - **4.5 R-TRANS — CORRECT as a deliberately limited model.** A parameter-dependent translated basis is transport-aware. A1 now explicitly identifies the bounded-domain approximation, charges shifted synthesis, specifies ladders, and reports boundary defects. It supports conclusions about this static-shift model, not transport-aware models generally. **Fix:** apply reference/training gates to `train-c` as well, and retain the sign and zero-shift controls.

   - **4.6 R-ECSW — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** A1 correctly replaces the reused `run_cell.ecsw_q2`, which only compares support successors and does not gate selection, with a full-parent vector-and-rollout check. However, it calls \(q=12\) the “R-PODG reference rule,” while A1.3 identifies the frozen reference rule as \(q=16\). **Fix:** distinguish the ordering/reference rule, ECSW parent rule, and its \(q=14\) or other prescribed successor. Pin training-row/snapshot indices and fit-candidate policies, and require valid parent/successor rollouts rather than only their numerical distance.

4. **Metric, matching, selection, timing, crossings and \(r_\times\) — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The metric, representative re-screening, raw paired timings, timed-output checks, component reconciliation, max-over-ROM/min-over-FOM definition, and missing-crossing treatment are improvements. The new six-candidate limit creates a coverage hole: six cheap candidates may fail qualification while candidate seven qualifies, yet A1.7 can classify the fully visited ladder as arm-censored. The finalist search must either continue or report incomplete qualification. Moreover, \(r_\times\) compares four-case screen costs with eight-case paired costs, uses merely “valid” bank configurations without defining whether that includes Q1/Q2, and replaces the pilot’s **nonlinear-evaluation-cost** definition with **whole-query cost** without naming that deviation. Setting \(r_\times=0\) measures a zero-model floor but does not establish anything about an affordable trained rank. **Fix:** define candidate-exhaustion behavior, use the same case population and timing statistic for both sides of the affordability test, distinguish numerical qualification from validity, and name query-cost versus evaluation-cost \(r_\times\) separately. Report no affordable tested rank explicitly. Scope “upper bound” to improving the FOM denominator for the selected ROM; this is not a bound on the globally optimized ROM-versus-FOM ratio.

5. **Q1/Q2 for every selectable arm — WRONG as a completed symmetric contract.** Withdrawing ROM-only \(4n/8n\) requirements correctly removes forced time oversolving; independent ETDRK4 stages and updates are a sensible **discrete-update correctness** certificate. But A1 calls the policy symmetric while FOM ETDRK4 receives only finite-output and coefficient checks, with no analogous stage/update replay. The COMMON-BRIEF’s tight/tighter requirements also need an explicit interpretation for non-Newton methods, rather than being silently replaced. Finally, A1 specifies test-stage Q1 only: Q2 on validation reached states does not establish Q2 on test reached states. The imported LSPG anchor routine checks `stats_valid` for tight/tighter solves but does not freshly certify their stationarity. **Fix:** state the audited non-Newton substitution explicitly, apply stage finiteness and independent update checks to both FOM variants, certify implicit anchor solves, and recheck Q2 for every reduced finalist on test states. Failed test qualification must remain a failure of the frozen finalist, not trigger reselection.

6. **Must-fail controls — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Reclassifying physical ablations as diagnostics resolves much of round 1. Several remaining controls still do not prove what their labels claim: MF-NU establishes viscosity dependence, not that coefficient setup occurs inside the timed query; a NaN viscosity does not test detection of an intermediate-stage NaN; and fixed-size perturbations to an iterate, tensor slice or coefficient need not exceed the checker threshold on every real state. MF-AD-cap with an extreme tolerance can encounter an exactly zero floating-point difference. **Fix:** verify the injected effect exceeds the relevant threshold on a specified real state, inject stage NaNs directly, and test setup placement through timed-region instrumentation or an equivalent observable. Add a selector fixture with six rejected candidates followed by a qualifying seventh, test-Q2 failure, and partial reference eligibility. Keep positive controls alongside rejection controls.

7. **Stop rules — WRONG.** A1.7 still converts missing speed evidence into a stop: it explicitly fires when every arm is censored and no matched speed-up exists. That is a feasibility stop, not the quoted \(S<0.5\) criterion; the pilot explicitly calls censoring “not evidence either way.” Coverage also uses “every run wide cell,” permitting unrun required cells to disappear, and its Cartesian product includes BANK at \(W=0.28\) and TRANS there even though those arms are not scheduled at that width. These choices respectively bias toward firing and toward unresolved outcomes. Excluding deferred arms can support a scoped result, not the original universal claim. The bank predicate improves uncertainty handling but inherits the undefined/changed \(r_\times\) semantics. **Fix:** freeze an explicit required set of scheduled arm–cell combinations; require measured matched comparisons and declared non-vacuity coverage for the speed stop. Return **UNRESOLVED** for insufficient evidence. If stopping for universal infeasibility is desired, authorize and name it separately. Evaluate the bank stop only with a defined affordability measurement and report absent ranks separately.

8. **Budget and defer order — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT, with the release framework substantially resolved.** The cap, concurrency limit, defer order, committed-job accounting and headroom match the brief’s intent. A1 defines the second-data-size diagnostic and sensibly excludes it from the primary stop rule. A smoke-derived certificate is a later production-release requirement, not something that must already exist for design approval. However, the proposed measurement list must explicitly cover centred training data, POD Gram/basis construction, bank ordering, feature derivatives, tensor construction, all qualification searches, reference-gate populations, test Q2 and storage/memory peaks. Measuring the largest-rank row alone does not bound every compilation or fit cost. The two-cell data-size diagnostic is also a scope reduction from the pilot’s per-cell pair. **Fix:** enumerate complete retained work in the certificate, account for these operations and failed jobs, and label the narrower data-size scope. The preliminary 54 GPU-hour estimate remains unsubstantiated.

9. **Other interpretability and fairness threats — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** A1 appropriately limits claims to the fitted banks, adds finer-grid diagnostics, and introduces operator checks. But individual reference exclusions can make cells’ reported statistics describe different paired populations; disclose eligibility masks and use common eligible rows for paired cross-cell comparisons. The fixed scoring lattice remains the authoritative metric, so finer-grid disagreement must qualify any broader field-accuracy claim. Report test-once success separately from validation-selected speed-up: validation timing does not itself establish test accuracy matching. **Fix:** define these reporting rules, deterministic selection ties, timing outlier counts, and artifact/code provenance. Treat the reused files as components requiring the stated adapters: their existing selector and ECSW qualification paths do not implement A1.

**Round-1 closure:** pairing/cohort safeguards and the transport model’s interpretation are resolved. Adaptive scheduling is substantially resolved. ECSW qualification, timing, crossings, controls and budget accounting are improved but incomplete. The reference-contract substitution, qualification contract, \(r_\times\), and stop predicates remain unresolved. A1 introduces additional conflicts through bank-dependent rule construction, \(q=12\)/\(q=16\) reference-rule naming, six-candidate coverage, selection-only allowances, and the arm–cell coverage product.
