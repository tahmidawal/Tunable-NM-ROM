\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every number below is a single training seed on opened development cases, one
checkpoint per PDE; costs are compared only inside one allocation, and where two
numbers come from different jobs the text compares accuracy only. The losses
come first.

\subsection{Nothing reduced is on the frontier at $256^2$}
\label{sec:exp:panel}

Table~\ref{tab:tunability} and Figure~\ref{fig:family} come from one
allocation on one A100 with $\nPanelSubjectCount$ timed subjects: the
correction ladder with dense and certified-quadrature advection at two
tolerances, POD-LSPG at six ranks, the unrestricted bank, the trained FNO, and
eight full-order Newton settings, all against the same converged reference.
On (GPU ms, worst \evolved\ error) \textbf{$\nPanelReducedNonDomEvolved$ of the
$\nPanelReducedCount$ reduced subjects are non-dominated} once the full-order
controls are included, and likewise on the \alltimes\ metric
($\nPanelReducedNonDomAll$ of $\nPanelReducedCount$). The most accurate reduced
subject is POD-512 at $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ and
$\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms; Newton at tolerance $10^{-3}$, $\Delta t=0.005$,
reaches $\nPanelFomNtThreeFineErr\,\%$ at $\nPanelFomNtThreeFineMs$\,ms, and
the FNO, timed in the same allocation, $\nPanelFnoErr\,\%$ at
$\nPanelFnoMs$\,ms. This is the evidence for the abstract's sentence that the
model does not beat an efficient full-order solver in 2D.

Within the reduced subjects: the dense ladder $q=0,\dots,256$ is monotone on
both metrics with every rung converged, spanning $\nPanelDenseErrSpan\times$ in
evolved error for $\nPanelDenseCostSpan\times$ in cost, but it raises
$M=4(K+q)$ with $q$ (\S\ref{sec:exp:qxm} separates the two). Certified
quadrature is a cost lever at equal error: at $q=0$ the rule costs
$\nPanelEqMsZero$\,ms against $\nPanelDenseMsZero$\,ms dense
($\nPanelEqSpeedupZero\times$) for $\nPanelEqErrZero$ against
$\nPanelDenseErrZero\,\%$ and $\nPanelEqSpeedupOneTwoEight\times$ at $q=128$: about $5\times$ cheaper at
four-decimal-identical error. Loosening the tolerance from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-3}$ removes a further
$\nPanelTolSavingZero$--$\nPanelTolSavingOneTwoEight\,\%$ at negligible error change. The EQ ladder of this panel is \emph{not} monotone at the top ($q=256$:
$\nPanelEqQtwofiftysixEvolved\,\%$ against $\nPanelEqQonetwentyeightEvolved\,\%$
at $q=128$) because those rungs carried rules certified only on the secondary
bar; \S\ref{sec:exp:eq} shows the fix (cross-job spread of an identical cell:
$\nQxmAnchorSpreadPct\,\%$, \S\ref{sec:limitations}).

The classical baseline: on the \evolved\ metric POD-512 beats the best dense
rung on both axes ($\nPanelPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ at $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveMs$\,ms
against $\nPanelBestRungErr\,\%$ at $\nPanelBestRungMs$\,ms); on the \alltimes\
metric it is dominated by the unrestricted bank and the reduced-only frontier is
$\nPanelReducedOnlyFrontierEq$ certified-EQ rungs plus that endpoint, with no
POD rank on it. Both are printed because the metric decides. The POD ranks
$k'\ge32$ carry a ``strict'' flag of no only because their initial fit is
attainable at round-off ($0/0$ stationarity); each sits on its projection floor
(solved/best-found $\le\nPanelPodSolvedOverFloorMax$), under a completion rule
pre-registered for this panel and post-dating the head-ablation job.

\subsection{Rank against test count: the family at fixed $M$}
\label{sec:exp:qxm}

The crossed grid (Table~\ref{tab:qxm}) runs $q\in\{0,\dots,256\}$ against fixed
$M\in\{256,1088\}$, the schedules $M\in\{4,8,16\}(K+q)$ and bridge cells, a
saturation sweep in $M$ and a solver control, in three jobs whose shared cells
agree to $10^{-9}$. Two fixed-$M$ ladders were run and both are reported. At
$M=256$ the ladder $q=0,\dots,\nQxmTwoFiftySixQtop$ inside one job spans
$\nQxmTwoFiftySixErrSpan\times$ in error for $\nQxmTwoFiftySixCostSpan\times$
in cost and \textbf{fails} the pre-registered bar (monotone, at least three
non-dominated points, at least $2\times$ on both axes, nothing early-stopped).
\textbf{At $M=\nQxmFixedM$ the ladder $q=0,64,128,256$, inside one job, is
monotone, every rung converged, and spans $\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in evolved error
for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost with $\nQxmNonDom$ non-dominated points}, and
passes. The choice of $M=1088$ as the headline was made after the crossed grid
was run, by the lane's decision rule (the largest fixed $M$ that holds every
rung to $q=256$), not pre-registered; at $M=256$ the $q=256$ rung is
under-determined ($M<K+q$) and cannot exist. The scheduled ladder spans $\nQxmSpanScheduledFour\times$, of which
$\nQxmShareRankCorner\,\%$ of the log-span is rank and $\nQxmShareTestsCorner\,\%$
test count. The test count does
$\nQxmRungTestShares\,\%$ of the work at successive rungs: it buys the bottom
of the ladder and the rank buys the top, and the absolute $M$ needed grows with
$q$ (at $q=256$ the $M$ effect is still $\nQxmSpanMatQtwoFiftySix\times$). The
solver switch at $K+q>64$ is inert (relative difference
\nQxmSolverControlRelDiff{}).

\subsection{Quadrature rules: validate on reachable states, then re-draw}
\label{sec:exp:eq}

A rule's NNLS fit residual predicts nothing about its error on the states the
solver visits (Figure~\ref{fig:cert}): the static-snapshot rule at
$q=\nEqtopStaticWorstQ$ fits to $\nEqtopStaticWorstFit$ and reaches
$\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopStaticWorstRho$ held-out, four times the primary bar. Scored by \eqref{eq:rho} on reachable states, the top-rung blocker is the
number of fit states, not the node count: 14- and 8-state rules at $q=128$ and
$256$ fail at $m=2048$ ($\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopParentOneTwentyEightRho$,
$\nEqtopParentTwoFiftySixRho$) where 64-state rules pass in that draw
($\nEqtopPrimaryOneTwentyEightRho$, $\nEqtopPrimaryTwoFiftySixRho$;
Table~\ref{tab:eqcert}). Rebuilt with the cheapest rule passing the primary
bar per rung and timed in one allocation, the EQ ladder is monotone on the
evolved metric, every rung converged, running
$\nEqtopLadderFirstErr\to\nEqtopLadderLastErr\,\%$ at
$\nEqtopLadderFirstMs\to\nEqtopLadderLastMs$\,ms, $\nEqtopCostRatioMin$--$\nEqtopCostRatioMax\times$
the same-job dense cost (Table~\ref{tab:eqladder}) (offline cost in Table~\ref{tab:offline}).

\textbf{Then the pre-registered replication re-drew every construction}
(pool and fit-state subset, four draws at fixed $q$, $m$, fit-state count;
Table~\ref{tab:replication}): $\rho_{\max}$ moves by
$\nEqtopSpreadMin$--$\nEqtopSpreadMax\times$ within one construction, and the
ladder's rules are \textbf{confirmed at $q=\nEqtopConfirmedRungs$ and marginal
at $q=\nEqtopMarginalRungs$} (draws passing / draws); at $q=256$ the rule the
ladder ran is the only one of $\nEqtopTopDraws$ draws of its recipe to meet
the bar (re-draws $\nEqtopTopRedrawRange$). The ladder's errors therefore stand
only as measured with the SHA256-identified rules that happened to pass;
``every rung carries a certified rule'' is withdrawn as a statement about the
construction. The finding is stronger for it: the fit residual predicts
nothing, and held-out $\rho$ is itself draw-sensitive, so a single passing rule
is not a certificate of its construction---a re-draw is. Four earlier readings
are withdrawn by the same data (Table~\ref{tab:replication}).

\subsection{The head earns its place per dimension, not per millisecond}
\label{sec:exp:head}

At matched $k=16$ in one frozen bank per PDE, the head is compared with the
optimal rank-$k$ affine map inside the same bank, a quadratic map, the
unrestricted bank, and POD-LSPG at ranks $8$--$128$, through the same weak
objective, tests, solver and stopping rule (Tables~\ref{tab:head-burgers},
\ref{tab:head-poisson}). \textbf{The matched-dimension result is the claim}: on
Burgers the head reaches $\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$ worst same-grid error against
$\nAblBurgersLinear\,\%$ for the best linear map, $\nAblBurgersQuad\,\%$ for
the quadratic one and $\nAblBurgersPodSixteen\,\%$ for POD-16. Larger POD ranks
are a different question and the answer depends on the metric: on the
all-times metric no POD rank up to $k'=128$ matches the head (POD-128
$\nAblBurgersPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$), while on the evolved metric in the panel
job POD-128 reaches $\nPanelPodOneTwentyEightErr\,\%$ against the $q=0$ rung's
$\nPanelQzeroEvolved\,\%$ and POD-256 $\nPanelPodTwoFiftySixErr\,\%$. On
Poisson at $1024^2$ the head wins per dimension ($\nAblPoissonNeural\,\%$
against $\nAblPoissonLinear\,\%$ linear, $\nAblPoissonPodSixteen\,\%$ POD-16)
and loses per millisecond (POD-128: $\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEightMs$\,ms against the head's
$\nAblPoissonNeuralMs$\,ms), because that query is dominated by projection and
decode. In three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}): on Burgers the floor is
$\nAblBurgersFloor\,\%$, best-found $\nAblBurgersBestFound\,\%$, solved
$\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$; the solver layer costs $\nAblBurgersSolverGapPp$
percentage points and the reduction layer sits
$\nAblBurgersReductionOverFloor\times$ above the floor. The head is the binding layer; retraining it on the same bank does not
close the gap (Table~\ref{tab:training}), and on Poisson a wider head moves the
best-found/floor ratio only from $\nPlinHeadRatioPrimary$ to
$\nPlinHeadRatioBest$ (Table~\ref{tab:head-capacity}).

\subsection{Cached cost is flat in the mesh; nothing crosses over}
\label{sec:exp:mesh}

With one frozen checkpoint per PDE transferred across $64$--$1024$ intervals,
the cached reduced solve costs
$\nMeshBurgersCachedFirst\to\nMeshBurgersCachedLast$\,ms on Burgers while the
unknowns grow $\nMeshBurgersUnknownGrowth\times$ (Table~\ref{tab:mesh}); the
complete query does not inherit that, growing
$\nMeshBurgersCompleteRatio\times$ through dense input and output. There is no
crossover against the cheapest same-job full-order arm meeting the target at any
rung (FOM/ROM \nMeshBurgersFomOverRomRange{} on Burgers,
\nMeshPoissonFomOverRomRange{} against the direct transform on Poisson), and a
kernel port at bit-level parity buys $\nSpeedGpuTwoFiftySix$ at $256^2$, short
of its $2\times$ target, because the query is kernel-count bound
(Table~\ref{tab:speed}).

\subsection{Where the control collapses: linear PDEs}
\label{sec:exp:linear}

When the residual is linear in the coefficients the corrections are eliminated
exactly and the ladder has a different shape. Poisson at $1024^2$ ($R=512$,
$K=32$; Table~\ref{tab:linear}): the rungs $q=0,\dots,256$ run
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\to\nPlinTenTwentyFourQtwoFiftySixErr\,\%$ at a flat
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMin$--$\nPlinTenTwentyFourMidMsMax$\,ms, and the top
rung $q=R$, the full-bank QR solve, lands on the floor at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopErr\,\%$ and is the \emph{cheapest} point at
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopMs$\,ms; the pre-registered degenerate-curve criterion
passes (at $256^2$ only its literal cost-span clause fails, because the top
rung is $\nPlinTwoFiftySixTopOverMidCost\times$ cheaper than the middle rungs).
Solver effort buys nothing at $q=0$ (floor $\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor$,
best-found $\nPlinTenTwentyFourBestFoundQzero$, solved
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\,\%$ at $M=129$; Table~\ref{tab:layers} gives the
$M=257$ solve). The direct transform is
exact at $\nPlinTenTwentyFourDstMs$\,ms. Heat at
$1024^2$ (an earlier cell of the same family, Table~\ref{tab:heat}): the bank
evolved linearly reaches $\nHeatBankErrTenTwentyFour\,\%$ at
$\nHeatBankMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms against the head's
$\nHeatHeadErrTenTwentyFour\,\%$ at $\nHeatHeadMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms, with the
direct solve at $\nHeatFomMsTenTwentyFour$\,ms. The reflective wave
(Table~\ref{tab:waves}): the top rung, the bank under exact modal propagation
with no head, is not strictly the most accurate rung (strict reading:
\nWaveTopStrictBestTwoFiftySix{}) but matches the $q=32$ rung within the
pre-registered integrator tie band of $\nWaveTieBandPpTwoFiftySix$\,pp at every
mesh ($256^2$: $\nWaveBankErrTwoFiftySix$ against
$\nWaveQthirtytwoErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$), and is
$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostSixtyFour$--$\nWaveBankOverHeadCostTenTwentyFour\times$
cheaper than the cheapest head rung; the head ladder is monotone in $q$
(\nWaveHeadLadderMonotoneTwoFiftySix{}), cost is not.

\textbf{The collapse is confounded with a weak bank, and we say so.} On
Poisson POD $k'=512$ reaches $\nPlinTenTwentyFourPodFiveTwelveErr\,\%$ against
the learned bank's floor of $\nPlinTenTwentyFourFloor\,\%$, and on waves POD
$k'=64$ reaches $\nWavePodErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ against the bank's
$\nWaveBankErrTwoFiftySix\,\%$ at the same cost: on both linear cells the
learned bank is three to four times worse than a POD basis of the same rank.
On Burgers, in the panel job, the free bank and POD-512 are comparable as
banks ($\nPanelFreeAll\,\%$ against $\nPanelPodFiveTwelveAll\,\%$ on the
all-times metric). The exact elimination is a property of the linear residual; the size of the
collapse is at least as much a property of these banks. No reduced arm beats
the direct solve on any of the three. On the L-shaped domain (no fast transform) the bank floor is
$\nLshapeBestFloor\,\%$ (development) and $\nLshapeSelectedFloorCommon\,\%$
(selection cohort), corner enrichment buys $\nLshapeEnrichGain\times$, and every
head sits $\nLshapeHeadRatioMin$--$\nLshapeHeadRatioMax\times$ above its floor
(Table~\ref{tab:lshape}); its solve layer has not run.

\subsection{Neural operators on the same data}
\label{sec:exp:operators}

A PDEBench-style U-Net and a Transolver were trained on exactly the FNO's data,
split, reference, metric, budget and selection rule (Table~\ref{tab:operators}). Worst error on the matched eight-case cohort:
U-Net-small $\nOpEightUnetSmall$, U-Net-medium $\nOpEightUnetMedium$,
Transolver-refine $\nOpEightTsolRefine$, U-Net-refine $\nOpEightUnetRefine$,
\textbf{ROM $\nOpEightRom$}, FNO-large $\nOpEightFnoLarge$, efficient full-order
solver $\nOpEightFomEfficient\,\%$: $\nOpArmsBeatingRom$ operator arms beat the
ROM and every FNO capacity is worse than it. An earlier FNO-only finding that the ROM is the more accurate model was an
artefact of the family and is withdrawn. Only the FNO was timed against the ROM
in one allocation, where it dominates on both axes (\S\ref{sec:exp:panel}); the
U-Net and Transolver were not, so no cost statement is made for them. Both pre-registered controls were run on the selected U-Net
(Table~\ref{tab:op-controls}): its float64 twin lands at
$\nOpCtrlMediumFSixtyFour\,\%$ and its second-seed twin at
$\nOpCtrlMediumSeedTwo\,\%$, both below the ROM, so that comparison is not a
seed or precision artefact. Two caveats: the float64 margin is thin
($\nOpCtrlMediumFSixtyFourMargin$\,pp), and at equal wall clock the float64 twins
ran $\nOpCtrlMediumFSixtyFourEpochRatio$--$\nOpCtrlTsolSmallFSixtyFourEpochRatio\times$
of their parents' epochs, so precision is confounded with an epoch effect. The Transolver arm below the ROM has no twin and remains a single-seed float32
result, and its float64 twin trips the lane's precision-sensitivity flag.
Every operator number is a lower bound
(\nOpLaneImproving{} U-Net/Transolver arms and \nOpFnoImproving{} FNO arms were
still improving at their budget); on Poisson the gap is larger
(Table~\ref{tab:operators-poisson}).
The operators' own knob, evaluation resolution, was tested under a
pre-registered rule (Table~\ref{tab:resolution}; speedups are within one
model's own curve in one job and carry no comparison to the ROM or the solver):
\nResUsableList{} is R-USABLE---one step, $256\to128$, buys the whole gain
($\nResFnoSpeedup\times$ its own speed at $\nResFnoErrRatio\times$ its own
error), below which the query is launch-bound ($\nResFnoMsOneTwentyEight$,
$\nResFnoMsSixtyFour$, $\nResFnoMsThirtyTwo$\,ms) while the error rises to
$\nResFnoErrRatioSixtyFour\times$ and $\nResFnoErrRatioThirtyTwo\times$---while
\nResDegenerateList{} are not (the U-Net breaks at once,
$\nResUnetErrRatioOneTwentyEight\times$ error at rung 128; the Transolver is
cost-flat, $\nResTsolSpeedOneTwentyEight\times$). At rung 128 no family is
within $\nResMinFloorRatioOneTwentyEight\times$ of the interpolation floor, so
the off-resolution error is the network's, not the grid's. ``One accuracy--cost
point per trained operator'' is withdrawn; what distinguishes $q$ is that it
moves what the model can represent, which a coarser grid does not.
