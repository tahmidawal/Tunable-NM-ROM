\section{Additional analysis of the correction settings}
\label{app:interpretation}
\paragraph{Rank against test count: the family at fixed $M$.}
The rank alone moves the error: hold the test count fixed, add correction
directions, and the error falls at every step while the cost rises
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder-main}). The ROM approximates the discrete system,
so the same-grid error is what the knob controls. Against the fine reference the knob moves the error only from
$\nPanelQzeroRef$ to $\nPanelBestRungRef\,\%$ ($\nPanelRefSpan\times$),
because at $256^2$ every rung's reference error is
$\nPanelRungRefOverDiscMin$--$\nPanelRungRefOverDiscMax\times$ the mesh's
own discretisation error of $\nPanelRefDiscretisationTwo\,\%$: the
reduction adds little on top of the discretisation. And a same-job full-order setting at $\nPanelFomBestRefMs$\,ms
reaches $\nPanelFomBestRef\,\%$ against the reference, more accurate than
every reduced subject at a small fraction of the top rung's cost; at
$512^2$ the ladder runs $\nPanelFiveTwelveQzeroRef$ to
$\nPanelFiveTwelveBestRungRef\,\%$ against the reference over a converged
same-grid error of $\nPanelFiveTwelveRefDiscretisationTwo\,\%$, all
\nPanelFiveTwelveReducedAboveDiscCount{} reduced subjects above it
and the \nPanelFiveTwelveFomBelowDiscCount{} full-order settings below it
coarser solves cancelling against the reference, not better ones; at $1024^2$
the rungs run $\nPanelTenTwentyFourQzeroRef$ to
$\nPanelTenTwentyFourBestRungRef\,\%$ over
$\nPanelTenTwentyFourRefDiscretisationTwo\,\%$. Correction rank chiefly changes reduction error here; discretisation
limits the gain against the fine reference. At fixed $M=256$ the ladder spans only $\nQxmTwoFiftySixErrSpan\times$
and \textbf{fails} the pre-registered bar
(Table~\ref{tab:qxm}: monotone on evolved, every rung converged, error
span $\ge2\times$, and two generator clauses, $\ge3$ non-dominated points
and cost span $\ge2\times$). \textbf{At $M=\nQxmFixedM$ the ladder $q=0,64,128,256$,
inside one job is monotone, every rung converged, spans
$\nQxmErrSpan\times$ in evolved error for $\nQxmCostSpan\times$ in cost
with $\nQxmNonDom$ non-dominated points} and passes. $M=1088$ is the pre-registered pure-rank ladder (the largest fixed
$M$ holding every rung to $q=256$); the $q=\nQxmExtQtop$ extension did not converge and enters no span; $M$ at $q=256$
saturates at $M^\star=\nQxmMstarTwoFiftySix$.

Sealed cohort and seeds (Tables~\ref{tab:sealed}, \ref{tab:seeds}). The
sealed cohort was opened once, after every choice was frozen; on it all
\nSealedCheckpoints{} checkpoints (the incumbent and \nSeedsCount{} fresh
seeds) give a ladder monotone on both metrics, \nSealedKnobBarCount{} of
them meeting the knob bar, with top-rung errors of
\nSealedAllTopMin--\nSealedAllTopMax\,\%. The one
checkpoint-specific number is the incumbent's uncorrected $q=0$ rung,
\nSealedIncQzero\,\% sealed against \nPanelQzeroEvolved\,\% in
development (the seeds: \nSealedSeedsQzeroMin--\nSealedSeedsQzeroMax\,\%):
one sealed case converged to a wrong branch, and the first correction rung
removes it ($q=16$: \nSealedAllQsixteenMin--\nSealedAllQsixteenMax\,\% on
every checkpoint). The pre-registered sealed-over-development ratio thus
fails at $q=0$ for the incumbent alone (\nSealedCtwoIncQzeroRatio; worst
seed-mean ratio \nSealedCtwoWorstSeedRatio); the sealed numbers are the
headline and the development table records seed variability (the incumbent
beats every seed there at $q=\nSeedsIncumbentBetterQs$). Rungs not
converged everywhere (\nSealedUnconvergedCells{} sealed, the one bar
failure; one seed at $q=256$ in development) are reported as run, not tuned; the fixed-$M$
ladder, one development-cohort checkpoint, is unchanged.

\paragraph{Quadrature and tolerance move cost.}
Quadrature is a cost lever at equal error ($\nPanelEqSpeedupZero\times$
at $q=0$, $\nPanelEqSpeedupOneTwoEight\times$ at $q=128$) and the
tolerance $10^{-6}\to10^{-3}$ removes a further
$\nPanelTolSavingZero$--$\nPanelTolSavingOneTwoEight\,\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:tunability}; those arms are not admissible). The earlier ladder's rule set breaks
upward at $q=256$; the replication-selected set is monotone at both
tolerances,
$\nPanelEqtopTwinSpeedupMin$--$\nPanelEqtopTwinSpeedupMax\times$ cheaper
than its dense twins, its $q=\nPanelEqtopSingleDrawQs$ rules single-draw
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder-main}). Transferred to $512^2$ (Table~\ref{tab:tunability-fivetwelve}) it stays
monotone; its $q=128$ and $256$ transfers pass the primary bar there
though single-draw at source, the ladder-rule transfers at $q\ge64$ do not, and at $q=256$
the transfer reaches $\nPanelFiveTwelveEqtopxferQTwoFiftySixErr\,\%$ in
$\nPanelFiveTwelveEqtopxferQTwoFiftySixTwinSpeedup\times$ less time than
its twin. Solver knobs chiefly change cost in these accurate comparisons
(Table~\ref{tab:knobs}); changed numerical settings can also change error.

\paragraph{Validate on reachable states, then re-draw.}

A quadrature rule must be judged on the states the solver reaches, not
on its fit to the states it was built from. The NNLS fit residual says nothing about held-out error
(Figure~\ref{fig:cert}: a rule fitting to $\nEqtopStaticWorstFit$ reaches
$\rho_{\max}=\nEqtopStaticWorstRho$ held-out, $\nEqtopStaticWorstOverBar\times$
the primary bar).
\textbf{The pre-registered replication re-drew every construction}
(Table~\ref{tab:replication}): $\rho_{\max}$ moves
$\nEqtopSpreadMin$--$\nEqtopSpreadMax\times$ within one construction; the
ladder's rules are \textbf{confirmed at $q=\nEqtopConfirmedRungs$ and
marginal at $q=\nEqtopMarginalRungs$}; one passing rule does not certify its construction; a re-draw does
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended:eq}). One gate failed: same-source rule pairs transferred to
$512^2$ are not bitwise identical (each transfer refits its own
draw), and one $q=32$ source passes the primary bar in one
transfer and only the secondary in the other, an
$\nPanelFiveTwelveTransferRhoSpreadQthirtyTwo\times$ spread in
$\rho_{\max}$, unexplained; no falsification clause fired.

\subsection{Detailed matched-dimension comparisons}


At matched $k=16$, the Burgers neural head outperforms the tested affine
and POD comparators in achieved error, without a per-millisecond
advantage. Comparisons use a training-fitted rank-$k$ affine map,
a quadratic map, the free bank and
POD-LSPG in the same solver (Tables~\ref{tab:head-burgers},
\ref{tab:head-poisson}). On Burgers the head reaches
$\nAblBurgersNeural\,\%$ against $\nAblBurgersLinear\,\%$ (fitted affine
map) and $\nAblBurgersPodSixteen\,\%$ (POD-16); on Poisson at $1024^2$ it wins per dimension ($\nAblPoissonNeural\,\%$ against
$\nAblPoissonLinear\,\%$) and loses per ms to POD-128
($\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEight\,\%$ at
$\nAblPoissonPodOneTwentyEightMs$ vs $\nAblPoissonNeuralMs$\,ms).
In three layers (Table~\ref{tab:layers}) the reduction layer sits
$\nAblBurgersReductionOverFloor\times$ above the floor, the solver
layer costs $\nAblBurgersSolverGapPp$\,pp on the all-times metric, where
the $t=0$ term dominates: the head binds.

\subsection{Detailed linear and nonlinear controls}


On linear PDEs the full bank removes the nonlinear solve. On Poisson the
top rung is the most accurate and cheapest. At
$1024^2$ (Table~\ref{tab:linear}) the rungs run
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourQzeroErr\to\nPlinTenTwentyFourQtwoFiftySixErr\,\%$ at
flat cost, and $q=R$ lands on the floor at $\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopErr\,\%$
as the \emph{cheapest} point ($\nPlinTenTwentyFourTopMs$ vs
$\nPlinTenTwentyFourDstMs$\,ms exact). Heat and the reflective wave
show similar cost collapse, with a small wave accuracy exception
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended:linear}). Against unpreconditioned
CG at $10^{-6}$, the previous comparator, reduced rungs are
\nCgPoissonSpeedupMin--\nCgPoissonSpeedupMax$\times$ faster on Poisson in the
same job; the transform is faster still by
\nCgPoissonDstFasterMin--\nCgPoissonDstFasterMax$\times$. Both algorithms are retained as named
comparators (Appendix~\ref{app:extended:cg}). \textbf{The collapse
is confounded with a weak bank}:
$\nLinearBankOverPodMin$--$\nLinearBankOverPodMax\times$ worse than a POD
basis of equal rank on both cells. No reduced arm beats the direct
solve.

\textbf{Navier--Stokes collapses differently.} On 2D
incompressible flow the confirmatory ladder gate failed (Table~\ref{tab:ns}): at $K=16$ and $32$ the
held-out oracle beats POD-$K$ by only $\nNsOracleRatio\times$ and
$\nNsKthirtyTwoOracleRatio\times$ against a bar of
$\nNsOracleBar$ (oracle values are upper bounds), and at $t=0$ POD-32 is more accurate (POD/oracle
$\nNsKthirtyTwoPodOverOracleTzero$): linear wins where data lie in a
low-dimensional subspace. Retraining the head on 128--512
trajectories moves the oracle
$\nNsScalePlainNOneTwentyEight$ to $\nNsScalePlainNTwoFiftySix$ to
$\nNsScalePlainNFiveTwelve\,\%$ (gains $\nNsScaleGainFirstDoubling\,\%$ then
$\nNsScaleGainSecondDoubling\,\%$; ``\nNsScalePlainVerdict''
by the pre-registered rule, Table~\ref{tab:ns-scaling}; the bank saw all
512). A family of dimension \nNsFamDimLow{} instead of
\nNsFamDimBase{} lowers every error \nNsFamDropMin--\nNsFamDropMax$\times$ yet
leaves the head's edge over POD-16 at \nNsFamOracleRatio$\times$.
With $\nNsDataFactor\times$ the data (\nNsDataTrainN{} trajectories),
that ratio reaches \nNsDataOracleRatio. Training and held-out errors
use different normalisations; their ratio cannot establish a shift
from generalisation to capacity limits. Across $K=16$ and $32$,
family dimension \nNsFamDimBase{} and \nNsFamDimLow, and
128--\nNsDataTrainN{} trajectories, this head class never beats POD-$K$
by the pre-registered $\nNsOracleBar\times$ on held-out decaying 2D
Navier--Stokes (ratios \nNsCellRatioMin--\nNsCellRatioMax). An exploratory ladder on that
failed manifold (Table~\ref{tab:ns-ladder}) closes the
manifold gap only at $q=R$, where the head no longer matters; POD-LSPG at
matched dimension is more accurate at every rung and cheaper below the
top, no neural rung non-dominated: the falsification clause's
named outcome. A trade between
rungs needs a residual nonlinear in the coefficients and a manifold that
beats POD held-out: Poisson, heat and waves lack the first, this cell the
second, Burgers had both and still lost on cost. At ten-fold lower viscosity
(Appendix~\ref{app:extended:lowvisc}) linear floors collapse
$\nLvLinearCollapseMin$--$\nLvLinearCollapseMax\times$ while the head degrades
$\nLvNeuralDegradeMin$--$\nLvNeuralDegradeMax\times$, every neural rung beats
every POD-LSPG rank, $q\ge\nLvReducedOnlyNeuralMinQ$ is on the
reduced-only frontier: the manifold's edge appears where the Kolmogorov
width is worst. Nothing reduced is on the full-order frontier,
the fixed-$M$ ladder spans \nLvLadderErrSpan{} and the mesh is
under-resolved (discretisation error $\nLvDiscMin$--$\nLvDiscMax\,\%$):
reduced-versus-reduced only.

at $\nLshapeCheapestFomErrFiveTwelve\,\%$, more accurate than any reduced

$\nLshapeNeuralCheaperVsCheapestTwoFiftySix\times$ and
$\nLshapeNeuralCheaperVsCheapestFiveTwelve\times$ cheaper, POD-128
$\nLshapePodCheaperVsCheapestTwoFiftySix\times$ and
$\nLshapePodCheaperVsCheapestFiveTwelve\times$; the head is non-dominated
only on accuracy
($\nLshapeHeadOverPodCostTwoFiftySix$--$\nLshapeHeadOverPodCostFiveTwelve\,\%$
more cost than POD-128, $\nLshapePodOverHeadErrFiveTwelve\,\%$ less error).
Nothing is claimed beyond $512^2$; the banks' reconstruction degrades from
$\nLshapeDevelopmentWorst$ to $\nLshapeValidationWorst\,\%$ on unswept
validation sources (Table~\ref{tab:lshape}).
