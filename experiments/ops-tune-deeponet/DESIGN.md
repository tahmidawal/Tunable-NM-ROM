## 1. The question

`ops-deeponet-b2d` trained four DeepONet arms on 2D viscous Burgers at 256². On its 32 held-out
**validation** cases they reached 14.79–18.22 % mean and 11.75–14.80 % median fixed-initial
relative error, against 1.35–2.28 % mean for the selected U-Net, Transolver and FNO arms on the
same 32 cases. (Our NM-ROM's 1.87 % is a **worst** case on a different, eight-case cohort and is
not comparable with those means; §5.2 T4 handles that cohort separately.) All four DeepONet arms
ended by **early stopping**, and both of that lane's audits named the same two unresolved
explanations: **128 training cases**, and **an inherited schedule that was never tuned for this
family**.

A reviewer will ask whether the paper's DeepONet was simply under-trained and under-fed. This
lane answers that, and only that:

- **Q1 (data parity).** What does each side of the comparison actually train on — how many
  trajectories, and how many solution states — stated precisely for the operators and for our
  NM-ROM bank and head?
- **Q2 (data starvation).** How does DeepONet's validation error move as the training set
  grows from 128 toward parity with our model's 4608 trajectories, at fixed compute?
- **Q3 (tuning).** With the data question separated, how much does DeepONet-specific tuning of
  the learning rate and schedule, the stopping rule, the trunk, the branch bottleneck and the
  output normalisation buy?
- **Q4.** What is the strongest DeepONet this lane can honestly build, and where does it land
  against the other three operator families and against our NM-ROM?

**What this lane cannot settle, and will not claim:** an architecture ceiling in either
direction, a speed number of any kind (§7), or anything about a second PDE, mesh or seed.

## 2. Q1 — the data-parity accounting, stated before any job

The two sides of the paper's comparison are trained on different amounts of data. This is the

### 2.2 What our NM-ROM trains on

The frozen Burgers checkpoint the paper reports, `sep_hfit_dense_mid_N256_dense.pkl`:

| component | trajectories | states | source |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| spatial bank $G$ | 576 | FOM state snapshots, early-time-weighted, capped at `max_snaps` = 16384 (a cap, not an audited realised count) | job 2835788; `sample_params(seed=0)`, `n_traj = 576` |
| head $h_\theta$ | **4608** | **131072 retained decoder codes** | job 2837431; those 576 plus `sample_params(seed=1000, m=4032)`; `hfit_n_traj = 4608`, `hfit_extra_traj = 4032` |

The bank is a neural auto-decoder fit to those snapshots; POD appears in that lane as a
comparator, not as the bank's training method. Each trajectory carries `num_steps = 50` steps,
so 51 states; 4608 × 51 = 235008 states exist and the incumbent checkpoint retains 131072 of
them (LAB-LOG 2026-09-16, the head-training lane's correction; the 4608 / 4032 / 576 / 50 /
16384 configuration figures are read from
`reports/2026-09-10-historical-poisson-and-burgers-cost-audit.json`, and the 131072 from that
log entry, which is the only record of it this repository holds).

**The disparity, stated the way the paper must state it.** The headline is the trajectory
count: **4608 / 128 = 36×**. The state counts are reported as **two separate inventories, not as
a ratio**: 131072 fitted latent codes for a reconstruction objective is not the same kind of
object as 640 supervised output fields for an initial-condition-to-future-fields map, and
dividing them would assert an information equivalence this lane cannot support. At full
trajectory parity (4608 operator cases) the operator still sees 23040 supervised output fields.

**The asymmetry the trajectory count hides.** Trajectory parity does not make the two sides
equally cheap or equally informed. Each operator training case cost ≈ 130 GPU-seconds to
produce at the pinned anchor, where the ROM's snapshots come from 50-step 256² solves; and at
query time the ROM is handed the governing equations and solves a residual, while the operator
is a feed-forward map with no access to them. The report states both beside the counts, because
a reviewer weighing "36× less data" should also see what the 36× costs and what the other side
gets instead.

**What is and is not shared.** `engines.params_draw` and `burgers2d_film.sample_params` are the
same sequential draw over the same ranges (LAB-LOG 2026-09-21), so both sides draw from **the
same parameter family**. That is the extent of the equivalence: the two sides differ in
numerical protocol ($\Delta t = 0.005$ and 50 steps for the ROM's snapshots against the
operators' refined-anchor targets at six output times), in snapshot selection (early-time
weighted against fixed output times), in learning objective, and in whether the model has online
access to the PDE residual at query time. The report tabulates all of those beside the counts.

### 2.3 Parity at the pinned reference protocol is unaffordable; what is done instead

The pinned 128 cases were solved at the 4096-interval, $\Delta t = 1.5625\times10^{-4}$ anchor
and restricted to 256. The generating job's own recorded
`wall_seconds_including_first_compile` averages **130.33 s per case** over the 128 records
(median 122.06, max 179.51; `reports/accounting.py` derives all four), so 4608 cases at that
setting extrapolates to ≈ **167 GPU-hours** — more than this lane's entire budget. That is a historical
extrapolation from one job's recorded times, not a proof about every possible generation
strategy; it is the number this lane acts on.

The protocol's own calibration (`pilot-data01/refinement/index.json`, gate status `calibrated`,
budget $10^{-3}$) measured the cheaper candidates at output 256 over its 8 calibration cases.
Note what the gate's `worst_empirical_margin` is: the candidate's difference from the anchor
**plus** the anchor's own space and time refinement differences (`data.py: evaluate_gate`), so
the third column is a margin, not a difference:

| reference setting | work proxy $L^2 n_t$ | worst empirical margin (incl. the anchor's own 9.588e-4) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4096, $\Delta t = 1.5625\times10^{-4}$ (**pinned**) | 2.684e10 | 9.588e-4 |
| 1024, $\Delta t = 3.125\times10^{-4}$ | 8.389e8 | 2.781e-3 |
| 512, $\Delta t = 6.25\times10^{-4}$ | 1.049e8 | 5.251e-3 |
| 256, $\Delta t = 1.25\times10^{-3}$ | 1.311e7 | 1.006e-2 |

**`gen_more.py` is a new generation protocol, not a larger `--count`.** It reuses `data.py`'s
low-level pieces unchanged — `case_seed`, `case_record`, `make_solver`, `restrict`, `save_case`,
`fixed_initial_errors`, the four solver tolerances, and every convergence, Newton-cap, boundary
and dtype assertion — and supersedes three things, named here as superseded: `allowed_count`'s
128-case cap, `read_calibration`'s requirement that the setting passed the $10^{-3}$ gate, and
`protocol.json`'s `future_train_prefixes` list of [512, 1024]. **The generated data therefore
does not inherit the "empirically calibrated" status of the gated 4096 setting**, and nothing in
this lane says it does.

**The reference-setting rule, executable and fixed before the job** (`gen_more.generate`):
profile each candidate in the declared order on the **same two cases**; take the **second**
solve's wall time, so first compilation is excluded; require
`steady_seconds × count ≤ budget − elapsed − reserve`; choose the **finest** candidate that
passes; if none passes, choose the cheapest and generate the largest nested prefix the budget
allows. Both profiles are recorded before the choice, the choice is made once, and the setting
is never switched inside an accepted prefix. No setting coarser than 512 is offered.

Three things keep the cheaper targets honest, and one thing they do not do:

1. **The ladder is a nested prefix of one draw.** `gen01` generates train cases
   $0, 1, 2, \dots$ with `data.py`'s own `case_seed('train', i)`, so cases 0–127 are *the same
   physical draws* as the pinned 128. Rungs [128, 512, 2048, 4608] are prefixes of that one
   sequence, so training-set size is the only variable across rungs.
2. **The target difference is measured on the training distribution, not assumed.** Because
   cases 0–127 are the same draws, `gen01` asserts that each regenerated case reproduces the
   pinned case's generation descriptors and supplied input field **bitwise**, and records the
   fixed-initial difference between the new and pinned evolved targets for all 128 — per case
   and per output time.
3. **A trained control measures its effect at 128 cases.** `c-pinned128` trains the base
   configuration on the pinned 128 and `c-new128` trains it on the newly generated 128: same
   draws, same schedule, same budget, different target protocol.
4. **What none of that establishes.** That the label difference has the same effect at 4608
   cases, or after tuning, or on cases beyond the first 128. Coarser targets smooth exactly the
   sharp early structure the trunk struggles with, which could help or hurt. The ladder therefore
   measures **the effect of more approximate-label data at fixed compute**, and the report says
   that in those words. No bound on the true-label result is claimed in either direction.

**Everything is graded against the pinned validation-32 and the pinned 8-case cohort**, which
are untouched refined-anchor data. Only training targets change.

### 3.1 What DeepONet is being given that the other three families were not

Stated here so the report and the paper can state it without hedging. The U-Net, Transolver and
FNO arms each got: 128 training cases, an inherited schedule, a 3000 s per-arm wall budget, one
seed, and one learning-rate refinement of the validation-selected capacity. This lane gives
DeepONet:

1. up to **4608** training cases instead of 128;
2. a **hyperparameter sweep** over eleven one-factor arms plus a composed arm;
3. a **tuned schedule** and a stopping rule expressed in steps;
4. a **3× longer** final wall budget (9000 s) for the selected arm;
5. a **POD trunk** (§A4): the POD basis of its own training output fields, in place of a learned
   coordinate MLP. That is extra *structure* rather than extra data — the modes come from the
   same training targets and no validation or cohort field ever enters them — but it is
   information about the solution manifold that the U-Net, Transolver and FNO arms were not
   handed, and the report says so beside every POD row.

This is a deliberate asymmetry in the baseline's favour, relative to the historical rows the
paper tabulates. (The sibling lane `ops-tune-grid` is doing the same for the FNO, U-Net and
Transolver, so the *final* paper comparison may be less asymmetric than this list; what this
lane can state is the asymmetry against the rows that exist today.) Every number in the
comparison table carries its arm's data size and wall budget so a reader can see which rows are
like-for-like: **the like-for-like row against the published families is `ops-deeponet-b2d`'s
`don-small`** — 128 cases, inherited schedule, 3000 s — which stays in the table as the
historical recipe-matched row.

Two further inherited exceptions, declared rather than hidden: the network is **float32** (as
the U-Net and Transolver are; everything outside the network is float64, so the repository's f64
rule holds outside the network but not inside it), and the arms are **single-seed**.

## 4. Protocol

### 4.1 Stopping and validation cadence — in steps, not epochs

At 128 cases an epoch is 16 optimisation steps; at 4608 it is 576. The inherited rule (validate
once per epoch, stop after 250 epochs without a new best) is therefore not one rule but four
different rules across the ladder, and a fixed *step* cadence is not one rule either: it would
give the small rung 36× more epochs between evaluations and — because the inherited driver
writes a 62 MB checkpoint at every evaluation — it would also spend a different share of an
equal wall budget on I/O at each rung, which biases the ladder in the direction the ladder is
measuring.

This lane therefore validates on a **fixed fraction of each arm's wall budget**:
`validations_per_budget` = 200 evaluations, so every arm at every rung gets 200
checkpoint-selection opportunities and 200 evaluations' worth of I/O inside its own budget. An
arm stops after `patience_evaluations` = 50 consecutive evaluations without a new best selection
score — a quarter of its budget with no improvement. The `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler, when
used, steps on the same cadence with `plateau_patience_evaluations` = 16; PyTorch reduces when
bad evaluations *exceed* patience, so that fires on the seventeenth consecutive bad evaluation,
i.e. after 8.5 % of the budget. `last.pt` is written once, at the end. These constants are
declared and held fixed across every rung and arm; **no equivalence to the inherited
epoch-based constants is asserted**, because none is defined.

Consequences, declared in advance:

- `c-pinned128` is **not** `don-small` re-run. It changes three things at once — validation
  cadence, stopping patience and scheduler patience — and runs on different hardware. It is a
  **new-schedule control**, the report labels it that way, and it is not offered as the
  measurement of any single one of those changes. `don-small` remains the historical
  recipe-matched row. Note the likely direction: 200 evaluations over 3000 s is *fewer*
  checkpoint-selection opportunities than `don-small`'s 539 over 552 s, so this control is if
  anything handicapped against the historical row, not flattered by it.
- An arm that never accumulates 50 non-improving evaluations is reported as **patience
  inactive**, which is a statement about the run and never evidence that it was still improving. `result.json` records realised steps, evaluations,
  epochs, examples seen, the stop reason and the evaluations spent at the learning-rate floor,
  so which of these applies is generated, never asserted.
- The epoch cap is set high (`epochs` = 100000) so that the wall budget and the patience rule
  decide every arm; the realised epoch count is recorded per arm.

### 4.2 Schedule

`schedule: "plateau"` (default) is the inherited `ReduceLROnPlateau(0.5, min 1e-5)` on the
cadence of §4.1. `schedule: "cosine"` is, exactly:

$$\eta(s, t) = \begin{cases} \eta_0 \dfrac{s+1}{S_w} & s < S_w \\[4pt] \eta_{\min} + (\eta_0 - \eta_{\min})\,\tfrac12\left(1 + \cos \pi \min(t/T, 1)\right) & s \ge S_w \end{cases}$$

with $s$ the optimisation step, $S_w$ = `warmup_steps`, $t$ the seconds elapsed since training
began and $T$ the arm's wall budget, $\eta_0$ = `learning_rate`, $\eta_{\min}$ = 1e-6. The clock
is wall time, so validation, checkpoint writes and any stall advance the decay; that is the
price of a schedule that must finish inside a wall budget, and it is why the realised schedule
progress is recorded. If an arm's budget ends before warm-up completes, the arm never decays and
is reported as **warm-up incomplete**. A cosine arm **can** early-stop under §4.1; what it
cannot do is finish its schedule early. Changing an arm's budget changes its learning-rate
trajectory, so a 9000 s final run is a **different schedule**, not more of the same one, and the
report says so where it compares them.

### 4.3 Output normalisation

Inherited: one global scale, the RMS of all training targets, multiplying the network output.
`output_scale_mode: "per_time"` uses one scale per evolved output channel, shaped
$(1, 5, 1, 1)$ so it broadcasts over channels. Both are computed from training data only, on the
arm's own prefix.

### 4.4 The pool and the prefix ladder

`build_pool.py` runs `dataset.load_pair` over the whole split — the per-case checksum, schema,
dtype, boundary, output-time, duplicate-input and train/validation disjointness gates — asserts
that `case_index` is a contiguous prefix starting at 0, and writes `input.npy`, `target.npy`,
`parameters.npy` and `cases.json` (ordered case ids, both index hashes, each source file's
hash, and each packed array's hash). Those array hashes are appended to the cache's
`DATA.sha256`, so **every job's preamble verifies the arrays themselves**. `train.py --pool`
asserts the pool's recorded train and validation index hashes against the indices it was handed
and trains on the **first N cases in index order**. This is what makes it one verified read per
job instead of one per arm; the verification is not weakened, it is moved.

### 4.5 Everything else is unchanged

Input/output contract, the boundary mask, the float64-outside-the-network precision, the loss
(mean squared fixed-initial relative error over the five evolved times), the error metric, the
seed (20260914), batch size 8 unless an arm declares otherwise, AdamW with weight decay 1e-4
unless an arm declares otherwise, and the 32-case validation set.

## 5. Pre-registered selection and criteria

### 5.1 The selection metric and the selection graph

**The metric, unchanged from the parent lane:** the mean over the 32 validation cases of the
per-case maximum over the six output times of the fixed-initial relative error. Lower is better;
`argmin` selects.

**The selection graph, frozen here.** Each decision names its candidate set, and a candidate is
a *recipe* (configuration + data prefix + budget), scored by the recipe's own run:

| decision | candidates | made in |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — schedule** | `s-cos` vs `s-base`, both at the sweep budget and the top rung | inside `tun01`, from `result.json` |
| **S2 — composed knobs** | every sweep arm vs `s-base`, §5.3's rule | `compose.py`, inside `tun01` |
| **S3 — the tuned recipe** | `tuned` and every sweep arm, all at the sweep budget and the top rung | after `tun01` |
| **S3b — the best POD recipe** | `pod-top` and the tuned POD arm, at the top rung | after `tun01` |
| **S4 — the reported best DeepONet** | S3's winner and S3b's winner, each retrained at 9000 s at the top rung in `fin01`, plus the **best-worst-case** arm of S3 if the budget allows; the reported checkpoint is the retrained one | after `fin01` |

The learned-trunk and POD-trunk variants are **reported side by side, never merged**: "the best
DeepONet this lane could build" names the better of the two under §5.1's metric and gives the
other beside it with its own numbers.

Ties go to the arm with the smaller worst case; if still tied, to the earlier arm in the spec.
A missing arm (skipped for budget, or failed) is not a candidate and is listed as such. Ladder
rungs are **not** selection candidates — they answer Q2 and are reported, never chosen between.
The 128-case controls are **not** candidates either. Nothing selected at one budget is reported
at another without being re-run at it.

**Selection surface.** Validation-32 only. The matched eight-case cohort is scored **once, after
every selection decision is fixed**, and is never used to choose anything. No final or held-out
cohort is opened; `dataset.py` refuses those splits by construction and `PROVENANCE.json`
records `final_cohort_opened: false`.

**Selection noise, declared in advance.** The full adaptive procedure — eleven sweep arms, a
schedule decision, a composition decision, per-arm checkpoint selection and a final retraining —
reuses the same 32 cases throughout. Consequently **the selected arm's validation mean is
optimistically biased for that metric**. That is the only statement made: it implies nothing
about the worst case, about the eight-case cohort, or about how the selected recipe would behave
on fresh data, and it can point either way for the paper's comparison. The report gives every
arm's full statistics so the spread is visible, and names a fresh confirmation cohort as the
missing piece.

### 5.2 Criteria

- **T0 (target-protocol control), and what it can and cannot decide.** `c-new128` and
  `c-pinned128` are reported with the signed relative difference
  $(\text{new} - \text{pinned})/\text{pinned}$ of their validation means, their paired per-case
  differences, and the measured per-time label discrepancy from §2.3. **This test cannot fail at
  the errors this lane currently sees, and that is stated rather than discovered**: the label
  perturbation an admissible setting introduces is at most 4.34e-3 (512) or 1.87e-3 (1024) in
  this lane's own metric, while a 10 % relative band around a 15 % error is 1.5e-2 — an order of
  magnitude larger. What T0 actually reports is therefore two things:
  (i) the **ratio** $\rho = \text{worst label discrepancy} / \text{arm validation error}$, and
  (ii) the trained pair, as a check that nothing unexpected happened. The label protocol is
  called **negligible for an arm** iff $\rho \le 0.2$ for that arm, and **material** otherwise.
  The corollary is the reviewer-facing one and is stated in the report beside the ladder: **the
  cheaper targets stop being negligible exactly where T3 would start to pass.** If any DeepONet
  arm reaches ~2 % validation error, its top-rung number must be re-derived on pinned-protocol
  data before it is quoted, and this lane will say so rather than quote it.
- **T1 (the data answer).** Reported as the **observed change in validation mean per rung at
  fixed wall budget**, with the caveat that more data at a fixed budget also means fewer passes
  per example. Two pre-chosen effect-size bars: a top-rung mean at or below **0.7×** the 128
  rung's is called a **large data effect**; a final rung-to-rung step under **10 %** is called
  **no further measurable gain at this compute**. Neither is called saturation, and a rung
  spacing below 2× or a regression is reported as **inconclusive**.
- **T2 (tuning).** Two separate statements at matched budget and data: **composition helped** iff
  `tuned` improves the validation mean by ≥ **10 %** relative to `s-base`; **the best tuned
  recipe helped** iff S3's winner does. Either can hold without the other.
- **T3 (competitive).** The best DeepONet is competitive with the FNO iff its validation-32 worst
  **and** median are each within **1.5×** `fno-large`'s (2.2811 % mean, 1.8054 % median,
  6.3825 % worst) — the parent's D1 bar, unchanged.
- **T4 (the matched cohort).** On the matched eight cases, whether the best DeepONet's worst
  fixed-initial error is below the ROM's 1.8671 %. These eight are **reused calibration and
  diagnosis cases**, already inspected by earlier lanes for other subjects; scoring them once
  here does not make them an independent test set, and "a fourth draw" means a fourth operator
  family, not a fresh statistical draw. One case decides this column.
- **T5 (the discretisation watch-item).** Reported as historical context only: 4.0265 % is the
  converged same-grid full-order model's worst error against the refined reference at 256
  intervals on the **6-case development cohort** of job bpn301. It is not a validation-32
  quantity, and `unet-medium` already sits below it (3.9622 % validation worst), so "every
  operator error exceeds the discretisation error" is already false as a general claim. If a
  DeepONet arm falls below it, that is flagged for the appendix with both cohorts named.
- **T6 (honest negative).** If the tuned, data-fed DeepONet is still the weakest family, that is
  the result and it is reported as such — explicitly not as an architecture ceiling: this lane
  tuned eleven one-factor knobs around one implementation, at one mesh, one PDE, one seed.

### 5.3 The composition rule, fixed before the sweep

`tuned` takes **every knob whose one-factor sweep arm improved the selection metric by at least
5 % relative to `s-base`**, each at the value that arm used; where two arms vary the same knob,
only the better one is taken. 5 % is chosen because the paired standard error between two arms
on these 32 cases, recomputed from the parent lane's own saved fields, is **2.0–3.8 % relative**:
a 2 % trigger would compose roughly a quarter of purely null knobs by chance. The schedule is not one of those knobs: it is carried by S1 and
applied to `tuned` whichever way S1 went. If no arm clears 2 %, `tuned` is `s-base`'s
configuration and is **not re-run**; the composition is then reported as "no knob cleared the
bar", not as a new measurement. If `tuned` is worse than the best single-knob arm, S3 selects
that single-knob arm and the composition is reported as a **failed combination**.

5 % is a pre-chosen practical trigger sized against that standard error, not a significance
test. The report gives every arm's continuous effect size rather than only its side of the line,
and states that arms within a few per cent of each other are not distinguished by 32 cases.

## 6. The jobs

Budget: **≤ 1 running, ≤ 6 total submitted**, counted without exception — a job that dies in its
preamble still counts, and is recorded with its logs. The account cap of 6 running is shared
with three other lanes, so `squeue -u tawal01` is checked before and after every submission. One
job per directory.

The order puts the reviewer-facing data question first, so a failure in the optional tuning work
cannot cost it:

| job | what it does | arms | wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gen01` | profile, then generate train cases 0…4607 at the selected reference setting; compare cases 0–127 with the pinned targets; pack the pool | — | ≤ 8.5 h |
| `lad01` | **the data ladder**: `c-pinned128`, `c-new128`, and the base configuration at 512, 2048 and the top rung, 3000 s each | 5 | ≤ 7 h |
| `tun01` | the sweep at the top rung: `s-base` plus eleven one-factor arms at 1500 s each, then the composed arm | ≤ 13 | ≤ 9 h |
| `fin01` | S4: the selected recipe and, if different, the best-worst-case arm, at 9000 s at the top rung; then the matched-cohort scoring of every checkpoint this lane produced | ≤ 2 | ≤ 7 h |

Two jobs are held in reserve. If `gen01` returns fewer than 2048 cases the ladder's rungs are
the milestones it did reach, and a top rung below 512 makes T1 inconclusive by §5.2.

**The sweep arms**, fixed here before `gen01` runs. Every arm is `configs/deeponet/base.json`
(the inherited `don-small` capacity and optimiser under §4.1's cadence) plus **one declared
override**, at the top rung, at the sweep budget:

| arm | knob | override |
|---|---|---|
| `s-base` | — | none: the sweep's own reference, at the sweep budget |
| `s-cos` | schedule | cosine to 1e-6, 2000-step warm-up |
| `s-lr3e-3` | learning rate | 3e-3 |
| `s-lr3e-4` | learning rate | 3e-4 |
| `s-pool8` | branch bottleneck | `pool_bins` 8 (an 8 × 8 branch code instead of 4 × 4) |
| `s-pool16` | branch bottleneck | `pool_bins` 16 |
| `s-freq` | trunk features | frequencies (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) |
| `s-trunk` | trunk capacity (**coupled**) | `trunk_width` 768, `trunk_layers` 4 — `trunk_width` also sets the branch's hidden layer and read-out, so this arm changes branch capacity too |
| `s-rank` | rank (**coupled**) | `rank` 512 with `trunk_width` 512, because `DeepONet2d` requires `trunk_width >= rank` |
| `s-pertime` | output normalisation | one output scale per evolved time |
| `s-batch32` | batch size | 32 (validation stays batched at 8) |
| `s-wd0` | weight decay | 0 |

After S1, every arm that follows `s-cos` is run with the winning schedule, so the capacity and
learning-rate knobs are not tested against a schedule already known to be worse; `s-base` is
re-used as their common reference only if S1 chose plateau, and otherwise `s-cos` is the
reference for those later arms. Which reference each arm was scored against is recorded in
`out/schedule-decision.json` and in every composed decision.

## 8. Gates — a job whose log fails any of these is void

1. `jax_backend=gpu` (generation) and `torch_backend=cuda` (training) in the logs; the run ends
   with `ALL-DONE`.
2. `sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256` passes, and every mounted cache verifies against its own
   `DATA.sha256` — which, for the generated cache, covers the per-case files, the index and the
   packed pool arrays.
3. The validation index hash equals the FNO job's `468b9e70…` for every arm. For pinned-data arms
   the train index hash equals `5333584b…`; for extended-data arms it equals the hash `gen01`
   recorded, and the audit checks **each arm against its own declared source and prefix** rather
   than one global literal.
4. The generation job's cases 0–127 reproduce the pinned cases' generation descriptors and input
   fields **bitwise**; only the evolved targets may differ, and their difference is measured. A
   mismatch voids the job. (Bitwise is reachable only because the cluster environment still
   matches the one that produced the pinned cache — numpy 2.5.0, scipy 1.18.0, python 3.13.2,
   checked in the job; the local box's numpy 2.4.4 already differs by 1 ULP in `exp`, which is
   why this gate is a cluster gate.) Because descriptors and input fields are **protocol
   independent**, that gate alone cannot detect a wrong reference setting, so the job separately
   records the protocol hash actually in use, asserts the anchor `refine.configure()` produced,
   records the chosen setting with both profiles, and reports the measured target discrepancy
   beside the calibration's own prediction for that setting. Every accepted case passes `data.py`'s residual, Newton-cap, boundary and
   dtype assertions and keeps its solver sidecar; a failing case **aborts generation** rather
   than being skipped, so an accepted prefix is contiguous and unbiased.
5. Every arm in the spec is present and complete, or explicitly recorded as skipped with its
   reason; a silently dropped arm fails the audit. An arm whose realised wall budget was
   shortened by the global reserve is flagged and is ineligible for matched-budget conclusions.
6. Every reported error is recomputed from the saved prediction fields by `audit.py`, which
   imports neither torch nor jax, and which also recomputes S1, S2, S3 and T0–T6 independently.
   The batch-8 selection score and the batch-1 reported score may differ by at most 1e-5
   **relative** (the parent's pre-registered tolerance, in the parent's own form).
7. The supplied initial state is returned bitwise and the boundary is exactly zero in every
   saved prediction.
8. Exactly one job per submit directory, confirmed in `squeue` before and after submission.
9. Collection materialises everything local recomputation needs (the validation index and its 32
   cases, the cohort, every `result.json` / `history.json` / `provenance.json` / prediction
   field / config, and the generated cache's index, generation report and solver sidecars) —
   symlinks are resolved, not archived as links. Nothing over 50 MB is committed. The remote job
   directory is deleted after a checksum-verified pull; **the generated cache is deleted only
   after `fin01`**, and its deletion is recorded with the seeds, commit and setting needed to
   regenerate it.

## 9. Risks

- **The cheaper reference protocol.** Measured on 128 real training cases and controlled by a
  trained arm (§2.3), with every evaluation cohort left at the pinned anchor — and with an
  explicit statement of what that does not establish.
- **Generation may not reach 4608.** The ladder is a nested prefix, so a short run yields a
  shorter valid ladder; the realised top rung is reported as such, and §6 says when T1 becomes
  inconclusive.
- **One-factor-at-a-time misses interactions.** Twelve arms is what the budget affords; §5.3
  tests exactly one interaction and reports it honestly if it fails.
- **Selection noise on 32 cases**, in the direction §5.1 states and no further.
- **Single seed.** No seed robustness is claimed. A spare job goes first to a two-seed repeat of
  the selected arm.
- **A larger training set changes host memory and I/O, not the science.** §4.4 and gates 2–3.
- **What this lane does not diagnose.** Why DeepONet is weak, beyond the per-time profile and
  the training-versus-validation gap it records: a trunk/branch representation diagnostic, error
  by physical regime, and independent seeds are named in `HANDOFF.md` as the shape of a
  follow-up.

- **A4 (2026-09-22, before `lad01` and before any training job) — a POD-DeepONet arm, added on
  the coordinator's instruction with the user's approval, and pre-registered here before it
  runs.**

  **Why.** A reviewer will ask why the paper compares against the 2021 branch/trunk DeepONet
  rather than the standard modern variant, and this project's own 3D lane already found that
  DeepONet needed a POD-initialised trunk to be competitive there. Comparing against the weaker
  variant alone is exactly the criticism this lane exists to forestall.

  **What it is.** POD-DeepONet (Lu et al., *CMAME* 2022): the trunk is **not learned**. It is the
  POD basis of the **training** output fields, held fixed, and the branch learns the
  coefficients:
  $$u_c(y) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{R} B_{ck}(u_0)\, \varphi_{ck}(y) \;+\; \mu_c(y) \;+\; \beta_c$$
  with $\varphi_{ck}$ the $k$-th POD mode of evolved output time $c$ and $\mu_c$ that time's
  training mean field. One basis per evolved output time, because the five times have different
  structure. Everything else is the vanilla arm's: the same convolutional branch, the same
  contract, the same boundary mask, loss, metric, data, split, cohorts and selection rule.

  **Construction, and the three things that keep it honest.**
  - The basis is built from the **training prefix only**, on the normalised targets, inside
    `train.py`; no validation or cohort field ever enters it. At each ladder rung it is rebuilt
    from that rung's own cases, so a POD arm at 128 cases has a 128-case basis.
  - The Gram matrix and the eigendecomposition are **float64 on the GPU** — this repository's
    `GRAM64` lesson, where an f32 Gram once floored POD near 2e-4 and faked an encoder wall. The
    smoke reports a top-mode orthonormality deviation of 1.8e-15.
  - **Rank 64, constant across every rung.** Mean subtraction costs one degree of freedom, so a
    128-case rung admits at most 127 modes and a rank near that limit would divide by numerically
    zero eigenvalues; 64 is comfortably inside it at every rung, and `train.py` refuses a rank
    above `cases - 1` rather than returning noise. The captured energy per output time and the
    retained spectrum ratio are recorded per arm, so a rank that outran the data is visible.

  **Arms and budget.** `pod-128` and `pod-top` join `lad01` at 3000 s each — the bottom and top of
  the ladder, which is what answers "what does the modern variant buy, and does it buy more or
  less when fed?" — and `fin01` runs the tuned POD recipe at 9000 s beside the tuned vanilla one.
  **This costs no extra job**: `lad01` goes from 5 arms to 7 (21000 s of training in a 25200 s
  budget, 8.5 h limit) and `fin01`'s second arm becomes the POD one. The lane's submissions
  remain `gen01` (failed), `gen02`, `lad01`, `tun01`, `fin01` — five of six, one spare. **Nothing
  is cut.** What is *not* done, and is declared rather than discovered: the POD variant gets two
  ladder rungs rather than four, and it is not swept — the eleven one-factor arms tune the
  learned-trunk variant, and `fin01`'s POD arm inherits whichever of those knobs apply to it
  (schedule, learning rate, branch bottleneck, batch size, weight decay; not the trunk knobs).

  **A coupled change, declared.** A POD arm differs from `base` in two config entries, not one:
  the trunk **and** the rank (64 against 256), because the rank of a POD-DeepONet *is* its number
  of modes. The sweep's `s-rank` arm (512 against 256) is what this lane has to say about the
  learned-trunk family's sensitivity to that knob; it does not isolate the effect at 64.

  **What it is given that the other three families were not** is now item 5 of §3.1.
