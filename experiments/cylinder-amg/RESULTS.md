# AMG instead of sparse LU in the accuracy-matched cylinder comparator: results

Final numbers for PROTOCOL.md of this lane (reviewer iMp8, multigrid). The tables are generated by `summarize.py`
from the run JSONs in `artifacts/` (`SUMMARY-final.md` / `SUMMARY-final.json`); the prose below only describes them.

## What was compared

The accuracy-matched FE comparator of the held-out case (P3/P2 Taylor-Hood on mesh c4, 16,912 unknowns, step 1/300,
510 steps, `frozen_oseen` with refresh every 30 steps; record median 42.25 s, rb-fb 7140851 on pax024) with its
UMFPACK LU replaced by AMG, everything else unchanged (subclass of the vendored stepper; `amg_stepper.py`):

- amg1: flexible GMRES on the full velocity-pressure system, upper block-triangular preconditioner, one smoothed-
  aggregation V-cycle (pyamg) per velocity component, Cahouet-Chabard Schur approximation (one SA V-cycle on the
  outlet-pinned pressure Laplacian + diagonal pressure mass), hierarchies rebuilt on the LU refresh schedule.
- amg2: the same, with the velocity and pressure-Laplacian systems SOLVED by AMG-preconditioned GMRES/CG (inner
  tolerance 1e-8), i.e. AMG wherever the direct solver was.
- Rejected on development data: Ruge-Stueben AMG (3/6 targets at 1e-6, pressure error 0.18) and the steady-Stokes
  pressure-mass-only Schur approximation (does not converge: residual ~2e-3 after 300 iterations, because the
  1.5/dt mass term dominates).

Tolerances were chosen on development Re 95 by the protocol rule (loosest tolerance whose six metrics are within 1 %
of LU's): amg1 1e-8, amg2 1e-8. amg1 at 1e-6 (the loosest tolerance meeting all six targets on Re 95) is a declared
secondary row, the setting most favourable to AMG.

## Results

Headline (all values from `artifacts/SUMMARY-final.md`): on held-out Re 105 every solver meets all six targets with
the same metric values as LU, and every AMG variant is slower than the sparse LU it replaces. Median wall seconds,
5 repetitions: LU 52.95 (ho-a) / 54.13 (ho-b, second LU block); amg1 at 1e-8 126.2 (2.38x the paired LU, 28.7
FGMRES iterations per step); amg1 at 1e-6 99.2 (1.87x, 16.7 iterations); amg2 at 1e-8 1158 (21.4x, 17.9 outer and
~1,490 inner iterations per step). AMG setup is cheaper than the LU factorisations (about 4 s vs 7-10 s over 18
refreshes) but the per-step Krylov solves cost far more than the reuse of the stored LU factors (88 s vs 18-21 s for
amg1). Claim: at this size the sparse direct solver is faster than the AMG solvers tested, so the reduced model's
speed-up over the best tested FE solver (LU) stands.

Full tables: `artifacts/SUMMARY-final.md` (development table, held-out tables with 5 repetitions per solver, ratios to the
paired LU of the same allocation and to the 42.25 s record, and the part of the wall time that no linear solver can
remove).

## Caveats

- Held-out timing ran in two allocations (amendment H1/H2) on batch Sapphire Rapids nodes (Xeon Gold 6438M), not the
  pax024 Gold 6448Y of the record; each allocation has its own paired LU. ho-b's node (pax012) was oversubscribed by
  other users (load 72 on 64 cores); its first LU block varied 61-151 s, its second (after amg2) 54-63 s, so amg2 is
  compared with the second block. Both LU blocks and both allocations give the same conclusion.
- pyamg and its Python-level Krylov loop are single-threaded; UMFPACK's factorisation and solve are effectively
  serial too. A compiled, threaded AMG (hypre BoomerAMG, PETSc fieldsplit, NGSolve's own preconditioners) would have
  lower per-iteration cost; it was not tested. At this size (16,912 unknowns) a direct solver is expected to win; for
  much larger meshes AMG's scaling could change the ranking.
- Only the linear solve changes. The solver-independent part of the stepper (convection application, outputs every
  step) is about 25 s of the ~53 s LU run on these nodes, so even a free linear solver could not make this comparator
  more than about 2x faster.
