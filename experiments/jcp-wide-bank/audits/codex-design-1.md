**Do not launch unchanged.** The experiment grid is useful, but solver eligibility, gate dependencies, and the trim accuracy/cost comparison need correction. Sections 6, 7 and H4 were excluded. No files were modified.

1. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — Answers to 1a and 1d.** The crossed grid answers how the **existing ordered linear span** behaves as \(R'\) and \(M\) change. It does not test a nonlinear-manifold correction dial or isolate bank width when H1 changes both \(R'\) and \(M\). More seriously, H3 judges accuracy using the converged rule but cost using a different, selected rule: \(\tau=10^{-3}=0.1\) pp already exceeds its 0.05 pp accuracy allowance. **Fix:** describe the dial as the linear-span setting \((R',M)\), and require the actual timed, selected-rule rollout to satisfy H3’s accuracy bar against a precisely specified baseline.

2. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — Controls and K-ctrl.** These are credible empirical bad rules: the prior 3D report records large failures in both distance and \(\rho\). But changing \(M\) or the state population can make one metric pass legitimately. The conjunction is sensible for asking whether **both** diagnostics detect a known bad arm; it is not necessary for rejecting that arm, since selection rejects failure of **either** criterion. A missing conjunction does not establish that \(m^\star\) is meaningless. **Fix:** report distance and \(\rho\) control outcomes separately, distinguish empirical expectations from implementation checks, and restore injected swapped-reference/perturbed-metric controls through the actual acceptance path.

3. **WRONG — \(m^\star\) eligibility and gate dependencies.** Selection avoids the historical test cohorts, which is correct; the certification draws are nevertheless selection data because their \(\rho\) determines eligibility. The principal defect is accepting finite but non-stationary solves: the prior 3D contract capped non-stationary steps at 1%, whereas this design drops that condition. Also, “withdraw the \(\rho\) criterion” after K-target fails could silently redefine the registered conjunction. **Fix:** specify convergence eligibility for candidates, targets, and certification rollouts; retain a justified stationarity limit; declare the primary \(m^\star\) unavailable whenever either required target is invalid. Report “no tested ladder member qualifies,” and treat any distance-only or \(\rho\)-only selection as a separately named diagnostic.

4. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — Metrics versus code.** The advection scalings, shared-node refined errors, evolved-time maxima, and storage formulas agree with the code. In 3D, explicitly use \(\|L^\top(c_m-c_{\rm conv})\|/\|u_0\|\), where \(LL^\top=\hat G^\top\hat G\); coefficient Euclidean distance is incorrect. Both reached-state populations **change**: [qstudy.py](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-08-jcp-wide-bank/experiments/jcp-wide-bank/vendor/quad2d/qstudy.py:368) uses lattice-reached states, while [qpanel.py](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-08-jcp-wide-bank/experiments/jcp-wide-bank/vendor/quad3d/qpanel.py:259) uses tensor-reached states. **Fix:** explicitly document and implement the switch to converged-rule states, identify medians as pooled-state versus per-case statistics, specify the zero-denominator policy, and avoid interpreting this sampled \(\rho\) threshold as certification of every candidate’s own trajectory.

5. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — Converged-rollout checks.** The proposed orders are plausible starting points, not established convergence guarantees for the new settings. Checking 640² versus 768² only on dev6 leaves val32—the population determining worst-case selection—unchecked. The 3D 40³–48³ comparison is useful empirical evidence, but agreement between two rules does not exclude common bias; the stronger 64³–80³ advection check helps only on its explicitly tested population. **Fix:** validate every setting on all selection cases and certification trajectories, require solver eligibility for both rollouts, and pre-register escalation or withdrawal when checks fail. Tie convergence tolerances to the 0.05 pp decision as well as to \(\tau\).

6. **CORRECT — Projection floor.** The stated quantity is a valid reference-relative lower bound for every rollout confined to the same sampled span, and is implementable without time stepping. **Concrete implementation:** evaluate the bank directly at the shared nodes, factor that sampled matrix once per \(R'\) using rank-revealing QR/SVD, and solve for each evolved reference snapshot. Record rank, conditioning, and residual checks; do not substitute the existing mesh Gram factor unless the sampled metric is identical. Compute both S and ST floors in 2D. The floor diagnoses representation error against that reference, not continuum error or a uniquely attributable “dynamics error.”

7. **WRONG — H1–H3 interpretations and resolution.** Failure to improve by 10% does **not** imply “saturates at 384”: a smaller improvement, solver failure, or opposing \(M\)-effects all fail H1. H2 is a legitimate descriptive hypothesis, but relative-\(\rho\) denominators and reached states change with \(M\), so monotonicity is not a mathematical expectation. The 0.05 pp bar is numerically measurable against fixed reference files; it is not established as a physical-accuracy distinction. The [prior 3D report](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/reports/2026-10-01-burgers3d-offmesh-quadrature.md:41) documents much larger refinement discrepancies, while its observed solver-tolerance discrepancy is smaller than 0.05 pp. **Fix:** replace “saturates” with “does not meet the registered improvement bar,” distinguish benchmark-relative from physical verdicts, add unresolved outcomes near numerical uncertainty, and report paired casewise changes alongside cohort worst/median values.

8. **WRONG — Timing protocol.** Same-job/GPU timing, warm compilation, per-call burn-in, and deterministic-output checks are sound. However, randomized interleaving and “last repetition / first repetition” do not specify the paired A–B–A protocol required by the lane rules; timing cases, repetition counts, synchronization, and aggregation are also missing. **Fix:** register paired baseline–trim–baseline blocks on identical cases, randomized pair order, explicit repetitions and burn duration, and `block_until_ready` on complete outputs. Persist every invocation and compute drift from matched block medians. Confirm the cheapest-family choice with a separate timing block so noise does not manufacture the 10% saving.

9. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — Mesh choices.** One mesh per dimension is defensible for a bounded development study, but prior invariance at the deployed settings does not establish invariance after adding columns or trimming tests. The prior 3D report explicitly warns that certification can change with mesh. **Fix:** scope all conclusions to 1024² and 64³, respectively; describe historical invariance as motivation only. If broader deployment claims are intended, add a pre-registered cross-mesh check for the largest width and most aggressive trim, including field distances and \(\rho\), rather than extrapolating existing results.

10. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — LSPG and shell completion.** \(M\ge R'\) is necessary for a full-column-rank residual Jacobian, not sufficient for identifiability or a well-conditioned solve; a regularized underdetermined least-squares problem can still be computed. Shell completion is appropriate. Evaluating the vendored `complete_M` at **\(n=65\)** gives actual \(M=(513,771,1027)\) for \(R'=256\), and \(M=(1027,1538,2052)\) for \(R'=512\), at nominal \(\kappa=(2,3,4)\). **Fix:** distinguish nominal \(\kappa\) from actual \(M/R'\), pin mode ordering and shell tolerance, use actual sizes everywhere, and check residual-Jacobian rank/conditioning. “64³” must map to 65 nodes, not `n=64`.

11. **NEEDS-RESTATEMENT — Memory and compute feasibility.** Individual arms look feasible on 80 GB, but the design does not bound peak residency. At nominal \(R'=512,M=2048\), rule blocks alone occupy **10.07 GB** for 640², **14.50 GB** for 768², and **2.72 GB** for 48³; dominant Jacobian products cost approximately **0.859, 1.237, and 0.232 TFLOP** respectively. The six shell-completed 3D tensor slices total **10.89 GB** if separately materialized. These exclude mesh banks, temporary concatenations, Jacobian workspaces, and compiled executables; the vendor drivers retain multiple arms. **Fix:** process settings sequentially, share maximal-prefix tables where practical, bound rule/state chunks, release caches explicitly, and measure peak memory plus compilation/rollout time on the largest smoke arm before accepting the budget.

12. **WRONG — Remaining reporting and budget gaps.** The estimates undercount arms: 2D has 16 candidates + two controls + gref = **19 full-cohort arms**, plus the dev6 check; 3D has 12 candidates + two controls + gref + tensor = **16**, with gl40 already included. No timing repetitions, target checks, projection floors, or compilation costs support the stated hours. The 3D timestep/trust/solver configuration also needs explicit pinning: [offmesh.py](/home/tahmid/Dev/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-10-08-jcp-wide-bank/experiments/jcp-wide-bank/vendor/quad3d/offmesh.py:115) uses adaptive initial steps followed by single sweeps, whose finite reason-0 exits can hide stalled dynamics. Richardson diagnostics require the additional 257-node reference, currently unspecified. **Fix:** freeze the complete solver/reference manifest, restore independent numerical audits and raw-data retention, enumerate every workload, and replace the hour estimates after measured largest-arm smoke tests. Qualify “linear storage” as conditional on fixed \(m\) and \(M/R'\); growth of \(m^\star\) can destroy linear scaling.
