# Adversarial cost review

## Findings

1. **Blocking — the \(N=16384\) to \(65536\) “exponents” are not valid scaling estimates.** The two points come from different jobs, nodes, freshly trained checkpoints, and commits ([TENSOR-NOTES.md:332](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/TENSOR-NOTES.md:332), [TENSOR-NOTES.md:409](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/TENSOR-NOTES.md:409), [TENSOR-NOTES.md:469](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/TENSOR-NOTES.md:469)). Same A100 model does not make wall-clock measurements cross-job comparable. With two observations, the fitted “exponent” has zero residual degrees of freedom: \(+0.31\) and \(-0.019\) are merely log-ratios, with no uncertainty or goodness-of-fit. The data qualitatively suggest the oracle is becoming more expensive—its solve rises 21.4→32.9 ms while both fixed-size arms stay near 14 ms—but do not support numerical large-\(N\) scaling exponents.

2. **Major — “constant online cost” is too broad; only the cached latent rollout is structurally \(N\)-independent.** \(A\), the Gram matrix, and \(Q\) are constructed before timing ([sep_b1d_ladder.py:91](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_ladder.py:91)); building \(Q\) explicitly loops over all grid rows ([b1d_tensor_common.py:36](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_tensor_common.py:36)). The timed IC fit still performs \(G^\top u_0\), hence \(O(NR)\) work ([b1d_fast_common.py:780](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_fast_common.py:780)), and the timed full decode multiplies all 51 head states by \(G^\top\), also \(O(NR)\) ([b1d_fast_common.py:108](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_fast_common.py:108), [sep_b1d_ladder.py:138](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_ladder.py:138)). Thus the implementation supports “grid-free latent solve after cached setup,” not asymptotically constant end-to-end evaluation.

3. **Major — the reported “µs per LM attempt” is mis-normalized.** Every one of the 50 time steps performs an initial residual/Jacobian evaluation before `att` is initialized to zero ([b1d_fast_common.py:594](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_fast_common.py:594)); only subsequent loop bodies increment `att` ([b1d_fast_common.py:606](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_fast_common.py:606), [b1d_fast_common.py:648](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_fast_common.py:648)). Nevertheless, total rollout time is divided only by `sum(atts)` ([sep_b1d_ladder.py:171](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_ladder.py:171)). The numerator also contains scan and previous-state overhead. Using the minimally corrected denominator `attempts + 50`, the oracle’s quoted \(+0.0487\) normalized slope becomes approximately \(-0.0014\), while the tensor’s \(+0.0095\) becomes approximately \(-0.041\). Therefore the claimed per-attempt trends are artifacts of the denominator and should be removed or renamed “rollout time amortized over attempted updates.”

4. **Major — the original four-job report falsely says the arms were interleaved.** The report states “arms interleaved” ([report:128](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/reports/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor-sample-free-burgers.md:128)), but the driver has arm outermost, then trajectory, then all repetitions ([sep_b1d_tensor.py:464](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_tensor.py:464), [sep_b1d_tensor.py:488](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_tensor.py:488), [sep_b1d_tensor.py:513](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_tensor.py:513)). The same IC routine varies by roughly 7–10% between arm blocks in that run, demonstrating material timing drift/noise. JOB A fixes the resolution ordering: repetitions are genuinely outermost and \(N\) alternates ascending/descending ([sep_b1d_ladder.py:127](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/sep_b1d_ladder.py:127)). Arms, however, remain in fixed oracle→NNLS→tensor order, not AB/BA balanced.

5. **Major — cost parity is supported only as coarse operational parity, not equality of evaluator cost.** JOB A gives tensor/NNLS end-to-end ratios from 0.928 to 1.070, rather than the headline’s narrower 0.98–1.05 band ([TENSOR-NOTES.md:392](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/TENSOR-NOTES.md:392)). The e2e measure includes roughly 7–10 ms of identical IC/decode work, which necessarily pulls ratios toward one. LM attempt counts also differ between sampled and tensor arms, so total solve ratios mix residual cost with solver-path differences. The defensible wording is: “No reproducible online speed difference was resolved; optimized e2e medians were within about 7% on these single-seed A100 runs.”

6. **Minor — the lean fold does not unfairly favor tensor against NNLS, but it does contaminate tensor/oracle attribution.** `lean` is enabled for both sampled and tensor branches ([b1d_fast_common.py:391](/home/tahmid/Dev/pod-ae-nmrom/Tunable-NM-ROM-Claude/worktrees/2026-08-29-b1d-tensor/experiments/separable-decoder/b1d_fast_common.py:391)), while the oracle is excluded. Therefore tensor/NNLS is a production-implementation comparison, but the reported tensor/oracle advantage combines removal of the grid operation with a fold unavailable in the oracle path. `tensor_nolean`, not `tensor`, is the cleaner implementation-matched oracle comparison.

## What the data actually support

The cached tensor residual and its Jacobian have fixed \(M,R,K\) shapes, so latent-solve arithmetic is \(N\)-independent by construction. JOB A finds no observed solve-time growth through \(N=4096\). The large-\(N\) jobs are consistent with the oracle entering a launch-floor-plus-\(O(N)\) transition; \(+0.31\) is an effective transition slope, not the oracle’s algorithmic exponent, which is asymptotically one for its grid projection. End-to-end cost remains \(O(N)\) because input projection and output decoding touch the grid.

**Verdict: legitimate with corrections (restrict constant-time to the cached latent solve; state cost parity as “within about 7% on these runs”; retract the original interleaving statement and per-attempt slopes; replace the cross-job \(16384\)–\(65536\) exponents with qualitative, indicative ratios).**
