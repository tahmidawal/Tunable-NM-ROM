**Overall verdict: NOT-READY.** The experiment is worthwhile, but the reference contract, qualification asymmetry, reuse assumptions, and stop predicates need correction before implementation. This was a read-only audit; no files changed or jobs submitted.

1. **Problem, families, pairing and cohorts — CORRECT, with safeguards to state explicitly.** The PDE, width distribution and viscosity levels reproduce the pilot’s Part-A family at \(W=0.4\). Pairing base uniforms across cells is beneficial: it controls case variation without causing train/validation/test leakage, because those splits use different seeds. `train-c` sharing training rows is also legitimate. The cells are correlated, however; any uncertainty estimate for differences or crossings must resample whole paired rows across cells, not treat cells or snapshots as independent. Width also changes wall proximity, so the experiment measures the combined effect of translation variability and wall interaction, not translation complexity alone. **Fix:** freeze descriptor hashes and assert split disjointness; freeze all retained cells before opening any test cell; state the paired inference policy and the wall-proximity interpretation. Clarify that the Gaussian is imposed in the interior with zero boundary traces—the stated tiny wall mismatch is real, though negligible at these targets.

2. **References — WRONG as a binding-contract claim; potentially defensible after an explicit amendment.** COMMON-BRIEF §2 requires second-order references with conditional Richardson estimates for new problems. Naming the spectral substitution a deviation does not establish that it satisfies that contract; the lane brief’s spectral-pilot lineage supplies motivation, but does not explicitly replace the uncertainty recipe. Moreover, exponential convergence alone does **not** guarantee that one \(3N/4\)-versus-\(N\) difference bounds the remaining error. The pilot’s `gen_a.gate_diff` uses coefficient-space, continuous-\(L^2\) differences; this design weakens that to a 257² lattice, which can hide high-frequency discrepancies, especially at \(N_{\rm ref}=768\). A4 was planned, not validated, in the pilot. Its `common.ic_panels` explicitly switches to 256 panels × 32 points above \(N=384\), because the smaller IC rule failed at 768. **Fix:** obtain an explicit reference-contract amendment or retain the required reference recipe; preserve coefficient-space gates, add a further-resolution/convergence or independent cross-check, and describe \(\varepsilon_{\rm ref}\) as a conditional estimate rather than a bound. Include padding and IC uncertainty, or justify their exclusion. Define separate test-time gates and allowances, evaluated only after freezing, with failed test references reported as unscorable. \(U=2\max\varepsilon_{\rm ref}\) is conservative **if** the underlying estimates are credible; it cannot make them credible by itself.

3. **Arms 4.1–4.6 — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT overall, with specific incorrect reuse claims.**

   - **4.1 F-SPEC — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The fixed-step ladder is a reasonable starting competitor, and charging IC evaluation, viscosity-dependent coefficients and field output is correct. There is no evidence yet that \(N\le256\) is sufficient at A4 or that the fastest feasible configuration lies away from the ladder boundary. A finite ladder is permissible, but cannot establish that the FOM is adequately tuned merely by being enumerated. **Fix:** register endpoint diagnostics and budget-controlled extension rules when resolution or step caps bind; report unresolved/censored FOM coverage explicitly. Define \(\delta_n\) and invalid-rung termination exactly instead of relying on an imported rule whose implementation has additional stopping conditions.

   - **4.2 F-SPEC-AD — NEEDEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Dyadic steps can hit output times exactly, but the algorithm is incomplete: initial level, rejected-step state handling, non-finite estimates, denominator floor, total attempt cap and cap-exhaustion behavior are unspecified. At level \(J\), two half steps require coefficients for level \(J+1\). “No level-cap hit” also ambiguously rejects an otherwise acceptable step at the finest permitted level. **Fix:** specify integer-tick scheduling, initial level, norm and normalization, rejection/coarsening rules, and failure only when required refinement exceeds the cap. Charge coefficients through \(J+1\), rejected trials, controller work and all accepted half steps. The raw difference is a valid conservative controller indicator; state whether the fourth-order factor \(1/15\) is intentionally omitted.

   - **4.3 R-BANK — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** Retraining on the eight prescribed cells and including both LSPG and ETDRK4 is faithful to the brief. However, “test prefix \(4r\)” is impossible with only 512 bank columns at the larger ranks: `rom.make_query` actually uses \(\min(4r,R)\), giving only 512 tests at \(r=384\). “Byte-identical” reuse also leaves geometry-dependent coordinates, ICs and envelopes needing explicit adapters. Training-snapshot norm integration does not certify integration of learned features, their derivatives or nonlinear products. **Fix:** pin the actual test-space policy, report \(M/r\), and provide additional test functions if \(4r\) is intended. Specify box adapters, training/ordering seeds, frozen reference rule and rank handling. Add bank-operator convergence checks; tune LSPG tolerance or explicitly scope the arm to the incumbent tolerance.

   - **4.4 R-PODG — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** A sine-coefficient POD with exact derivatives is a legitimate implementation of the stand-in. The proposed reuse is not automatic: `pod.py` currently constructs FD-grid POD spaces and FD derivatives. Also, composite-Gauss point evaluation is not the pilot’s original grid implementation; sharing a generic engine does not guarantee competitive performance. **Fix:** implement and audit the coefficient-space adapter, verify Parseval scaling and operator equivalence, and compare tensor/point outputs against the pilot on identical small cases. Describe this as the same mathematical model with a changed implementation, with performance limitations disclosed.

   - **4.5 R-TRANS — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT, not an illegitimate arm.** A parameter-dependent shifted basis is a legitimate transport-aware model, even without learned or evolving shifts. It is not an exact symmetry reduction of the bounded Dirichlet problem: translated centred solutions generally violate the original walls and sample the odd extension outside the centred box. This limitation may be substantial after diffusion and transport, despite the tiny initial wall values. **Fix:** label it a static translation approximation; specify its rule and step ladders, shifted IC projection and decoder, and report boundary defects alongside field error. Charge every query-dependent phase table and synthesis operation. Qualify the centred reduced dynamics and assess shifted outputs against the original box reference; do not silently clamp boundaries.

   - **4.6 R-ECSW — WRONG about the reused qualification routine.** The intended projected-training-state ECSW arm is sensible, but `run_cell.ecsw_q2` does **not** compare against the full parent rule: it compares \(\kappa\) with \(2\kappa\), reports rollout distance only, and is unavailable without a successor fit. The imported selector does not gate competitor selection on it. **Fix:** implement full-parent nonlinear-vector \(\rho\) and rollout checks on ECSW’s own reached states, gate selection on both, and qualify the parent rule against its successor as well. Pin rank, parent-rule, training-subset and fit-budget policies, recording support saturation and fit failures.

4. **Metric, matching, finalists, timing, crossing and \(r_\times\) — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The lattice metric, separate median/worst targets, three-way matching and minimum over fixed/adaptive FOM finalists are coherent. They match a common tolerance, not identical achieved errors. One-case screening, however, does not establish the fastest configuration for an adaptive/iterative cohort; the design’s \(t^\ast\) notation overstates that selection. Specify how many finalists survive and screen them on a representative fixed validation subset. Retain raw paired timings, timed-output identity checks, compilation checks and a duration/stability-based GPU warm-up: three short queries alone need not eliminate clock ramp. Separately timed components need not sum to a fused query, so report that discrepancy and keep end-to-end timing authoritative. Crossings are not identifiable for TRANS at one width, and BANK supplies only a broad two-endpoint comparison; multiple reversals must remain multiple observed transitions. A censored accuracy match has no speed-up and must not be assigned \(S<c\). Finally, \(r_\times\) must name the fastest FOM comparator, use representative same-job cost, and specify whether bank configurations need only numerical qualification or also target feasibility—the latter makes the projection-floor stop circular. **Fix:** register those policies and report missing crossings and missing \(r_\times\) as undefined.

5. **Q1/Q2 for every selectable arm — WRONG as currently specified.** The ETDRK4 coefficient and independent-update checks are useful discrete-update certificates; they are not a stationarity test for the continuous ODE, nor do they detect a shared wrong nonlinear operator. The proposed \(4n/8n\) anchors add time-accuracy evidence, but `finalize.q1` currently returns from its ETDRK4 branch without those comparisons. More seriously, the design imposes a \(2.5\times10^{-4}\) time-convergence requirement on reduced ETDRK4 while FOM ETDRK4 needs only finite outputs, and LSPG’s tight comparison keeps the same time grid. This can force reduced explicit arms to over-solve at 1–10% targets while competitors exploit time-error tolerance. **Fix:** explicitly separate algebraic/update correctness from time-discretization accuracy, and register a symmetric policy across steppers; if time-converged anchors remain mandatory, apply analogous requirements to FOMs and clarify LSPG treatment. Certify anchor validity, all stages and replay identity in-job. Replace the imported selector’s bank-only qualification gate with gates for every reduced arm, including test evaluation. Define Q2 state sampling, zero-denominator handling and successor availability; do not compare tensor entries in place of nonlinear vectors evaluated on reached states.

6. **Must-fail controls — WRONG as an unconditional suite.** Several controls could pass innocently: removing padding at an already highly resolved reference may cause negligible error; \(q=2\) on many elements may integrate adequately; “8 Gauss points” is ambiguous about panels; removing transport shifts need not worsen an already poor wall-limited model tenfold; and disabling adaptation need not harm a case whose coarse steps already suffice. Zeroing an inactive tensor slice may also be undetectable. Thus “a control that cannot fail is a bug” confuses an ineffective fault injection with a broken checker. **Fix:** retain physical ablations as diagnostics and use calibrated faults with demonstrably above-threshold effects on real reached states for mandatory checker tests. In particular, inspect adaptive accept/reject decisions under an intentionally unacceptable trial, corrupt an active tensor contraction, and independently verify the shifted decoder’s sign and zero-shift identity. Add controls for intermediate-stage NaNs, cap exhaustion, omitted viscosity-dependent setup, changed frozen artifacts, failed qualification exclusion, missing FOM matches, and exact stop-threshold boundaries. The current Richardson `score.selftest` is useful but does not test the proposed spectral reference estimator.

7. **Stop rules — WRONG operational translation.** The quoted brief text is accurate, but “no qualified \(S\ge0.5\)” is not equivalent to “every reduced model has \(S<0.5\).” The operational rule fires when references fail, timing is missing, qualification fails, arms are deferred, or no target is matched—even if there is no measured speed-up at all. This biases toward stopping. The pilot’s Part A′ wording explicitly restricted the comparison to targets reached by both methods; even that requires a non-vacuity/coverage policy. The bank rule compounds the problem: no affordable rank does not imply that the smallest rank’s projection floor exceeds the target, and an undefined rank is not evidence of a high floor. **Fix:** register three outcomes—fires, does not fire, unresolved—with explicit required arm/cell coverage, finite matched speed-ups, and treatment of deferred experiments. A measured \(S\ge0.5\) prevents the line stop; missing evidence must not count as a measured loss. Define bank stopping over the available wide-bank cells before narrow expenditure, using a defined \(r_\times\) and uncertainty-aware floor comparison. Any broader “stop for lack of feasibility” policy is a separate amendment, not the quoted speed criterion.

8. **Budget and defer order — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The 87 GPU-hour cap, lane concurrency cap and defer order match the brief, and the smoke-derived release condition is appropriate. The 54-hour estimate is not yet substantiated: the geometry projection is explicitly provisional, and cannot justify the assumed H200 bank-training cost by cross-device extrapolation. The plan must cover centred transport data, POD construction, all rule/rank/form/step combinations, ECSW fits, compilation, qualification replays, \(4n/8n\) anchors, test reference checks and failed jobs. The 384-trajectory experiment is budgeted but not operationally defined. **Fix:** specify which cells and arms receive the second data size and how its results enter selection; measure representative expensive operations, including A4 and the full-bank training phase, then reserve spent plus committed jobs plus projected remaining work and headroom. Apply the exact defer order, and propagate deferrals into stop/crossing coverage rather than treating them as failures.

9. **Other threats to interpretability or fairness — NEEDS-RESTATEMENT.** The fixed scoring grid is a valid declared metric, but without a finer-grid or continuous-norm diagnostic it cannot support unqualified field-accuracy claims at A4. Training the bank on a coarser sampling lattice than the coefficient POD is also a possible information bottleneck; a failed bank arm could reflect sampling or training rather than the bank architecture. One trained bank per cell supports a finding about those fitted models, not retraining robustness. Finally, the proposed framework lacks an explicit independent identity check for box scaling, viscosity, diffusion sign and advection projection, despite adapting a geometry-specific engine. **Fix:** add finer-grid/reference-norm diagnostics, training-representation and projection-floor reports, pinned training seeds and hashes, and manufactured small-case operator checks. Preserve invalid, unresolved, unqualified, censored and deferred statuses separately; freeze code, descriptors, trained artifacts, rules and selection policies before test opening.
