## What the numbers support, and what they do not

- Supported: at matched median error near 0.2 %, tuned operators answer in about 4–7 ms and our method needs 26 ms. The 45 ms figure of the rebuttal row is the price of a median near 0.026 %, not the price of operator-level accuracy.
- Supported: our method has a width setting that trades accuracy for time along a measured curve (0.53 % at 15 ms, 0.097 % at 26 ms, 0.048 % at 34 ms, 0.026 % at 46 ms) without retraining; an operator needs a new, larger training run for each lower error level.
- Not supported: any claim that we are faster than operators at equal median error. At the one level where both sides have test numbers (0.2 %), we are slower. At the lower levels there is no operator test number yet.
- Not supported: any worst-case claim. Operators lead on worst case at every width.
- Not supported: "wider is always better" (width 512).
- Caveats: one checkpoint, one mesh (256²), one GPU type, single runs per job. The "narrowest passing width" is a read-off of the test curve at fixed levels, not a setting chosen for deployment. The narrowest passing width for 0.2 % overshoots the level (it reaches 0.097 %); the sweep has no width between 128 and 192. Width 96 timed 15.27 ms in the sweep job against 14.37 ms in the reproduction job (same GPU type, different node and a larger round-robin); the other shared widths agree within 2 %. The per-invocation min–max range mixes case-to-case iteration-count differences with timing noise.
