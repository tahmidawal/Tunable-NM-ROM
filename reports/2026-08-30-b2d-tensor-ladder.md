# 2D Burgers with the advection term projected exactly: the tensor equals the full-grid oracle at N = 64–1024, costs the same as the sampled rule, and the O(N) oracle grows 33 → 670 ms while the tensor stays flat

Follow-up to `2026-08-29-b1d-tensor-sample-free-burgers.md`, user-directed ("do it for
Burgers 2D at multiple resolutions to see what improvement we get and how the cost scales").
Numbers are **final for these checkpoints, single seed, 8 test trajectories**: jobs 3038943
(N=64), 3038921 (256), 3038922 (512, decoder trained in-job), 3038923 (1024), 3039255
(R=512 stretch, stopped by design), branch `exp/2026-08-29-b2d-tensor` (results at
`ec2add0`). Tables are generated by `reports/gen_2026-08-30-b2d-tensor.py` from the branch's
`runs/b2dtensor/gen_tables.py`; the branch's `TENSOR2D-NOTES.md` has every table (per-trajectory,
full FOM ladders, stop histograms). **Every 1D review correction is applied here from the
start**: arms interleaved AB/BA with repetitions outermost; $Q = T + T^{(j\leftrightarrow k)}$;
blocked build; first-order undershoot framing; no exponents fitted across jobs (each $N$ ran on
a different GPU — ratios only, GPU named per row); like-for-like arms share IC fit, LM rule,
trust region, tolerances and decode bit-identically (gates STEP/ROLL = 0.0 against the
incumbent solver).

## What was built

Same construction as 1D, one dimension up. With the small bank ($K=16$, $R=64$, $M=64$) the
2D advection $u\,(u_x+u_y)$ under the FOM's per-axis sign-upwind stencil becomes, on
non-negative fields, one $64^3$ table (2 MB f64) covering both axes:
$$
T_{ijk} = \sum_x \Phi_{xi}\,G_{xj}\,\big[(D^-_x+D^-_y)G\big]_{xk},\qquad
\Phi^\top N(u) = \tfrac12\,h^\top Q h,\quad Q = T + T^{(j\leftrightarrow k)} .
$$
Built blocked over the up-to-$10^6$ interior points in 1.2–1.9 s (T-11); it replaces the
$m{=}256$ NNLS quadrature (an 11–26 s convex fit) and the full-grid path inside the
*same* solver. The positivity precondition was asserted, not assumed: the regenerated truth is
non-negative on every training and test snapshot at every $N$ (min $\ge -7\times10^{-31}$,
roundoff of the preconditioned solve; T-2).

## Findings

**F1 — the tensor equals the full-grid oracle at every resolution.** Per-trajectory rollout
error agrees to $8\times10^{-5}$ / $7\times10^{-6}$ / $2\times10^{-6}$ / $2\times10^{-5}$
absolute at $N = 64/256/512/1024$ on errors of $2.3$–$6.4\times10^{-2}$ (T-4), with
**identical stop-reason histograms and identical LM attempt counts** per trajectory at every
$N$ — the solver takes the same path. Gate T0 (tensor $=$ sign-upwind oracle on decoded
states that are entirely positive) holds to $\le 1.7\times10^{-15}$; the algebraic identity on
all training codes to $\le 1.9\times10^{-14}$ (T-3). Agreement is weaker than 1D
($10^{-4}$–$10^{-6}$ relative vs $10^{-6}$–$10^{-7}$) for the reason the 1D reviews
predicted: the 2D decoders undershoot far more — 15–17 % of decoded points sit at or below
zero (min $\approx -0.06$…$-0.15$), only 0.6–3 % of training states are all-positive, and
93–100 % of rollout states touch a $u\le0$ point (T-2). The first-order discrepancy
$-\Delta x\,\Phi^\top[\mathbb 1[u\le0]\,u\,\Delta_h u]$ is therefore larger, and it still
does not move the solver.

**F2 — no accuracy improvement over the sampled rule, because sampling was not binding.**
Tensor / NNLS-256 error ratio 0.996–1.003 (T-4): at $m = 256 = 4M$ the incumbent quadrature
already sits on the oracle, so the tensor cannot improve on it — it matches it with nothing
fitted. The learned-node sets at $m=64$ (`ex_learned`) are 5–18 % worse at $N\le256$ and tied
at 1024. The remaining error is the decoder floor of these $K=16$, $R=64$ checkpoints.

**F3 — cost: the same as the sampled rule, and the O(N) oracle finally shows its growth.**
Latent solve per 50-step trajectory (T-6, T-10): tensor 20–28 ms, NNLS-256 20–29 ms
(ratio 0.92–0.98), while the full-grid oracle goes **33 → 74 → 97 → 670 ms** from $N=64$
to $1024$ — $25\times$ the tensor at $10^6$ points. End-to-end tensor/NNLS 0.94–0.96. The
IC fit and decode (the $O(N)$ ends) are 1.6–4.8 ms and 0.4–1.2 ms. Each $N$ ran on a
different GPU (40 GB A100, 80 GB A100, H200, 80 GB A100), so these are within-job ratios,
not a fitted exponent.

**F4 — against the FOM, the tensor inherits the sampled rule's paired speedup exactly.**
At the matched-accuracy Newton rung on the same GPU (T-7): $0.42\times$ at $N=64$,
$0.58\times$ at 256, $0.98\times$ at 512, **$3.45\times$ at $N=1024$** (sampled rule:
$3.31\times$; the oracle: $0.18\times$). The crossover picture of the 2D campaign is
unchanged; what changes is that the residual is now exact and has no quadrature to fit or
tune. The FOM's tightest rung ($10^{-4}$, $5\times10^{-5}$) is $\sim$100× more accurate than
these ROMs at $16$–$231$ ms.

**F5 — the R=512 headline checkpoint does not compress by head-PCA (stretch, stopped by
design).** For the $K=32$, $R=512$ decoder at $N=1024$ the head outputs' singular values
decay slowly ($6.7\times10^{2}$ → $2.8\times10^{-2}$): keeping $1-10^{-8}$ of the energy
needs $K'=499$ of 512 — no compression (Q would be 122 MiB) — and even then the projection
tail leaves T0 at $4.3\times10^{-5}$ and the identity on raw codes at $2\times10^{-3}$ (T-12).
The tripwire fired as designed and the run stopped. Head-PCA is not the compression route for
that bank; a dense 270 MB tensor, a CP/Tucker fit of $T$ itself, or a smaller bank are the
remaining options.

## What this supports, and what it does not

- **Supports:** an exact, sample-free advection term in 2D at oracle accuracy and sampled-rule
  cost across $16\times$ in resolution; the removal of the NNLS fit and node choice from the
  2D pipeline for this bank size; the measured $O(N)$ growth of the full-grid alternative.
- **Does not support:** any accuracy or speed gain over the tuned $m=256$ rule (there is
  none); bit-exactness (undershoots are larger in 2D); the $R=512$ headline configuration
  (uncompressed); other seeds, other families, sign-changing data.
- The $N=1024$ checkpoint is an inherited decoder with a higher IC-fit error (0.15 vs 0.08)
  and a worse absolute error ($6.4\times10^{-2}$); that is a property of the checkpoint, not
  of the residual — the tensor-vs-oracle comparison is unaffected. Single seed throughout.

## Glossary

- **full / ex / tensor / ex_learned** — the four arms: full-grid sign-upwind advection
  (oracle, $O(N)$); the incumbent exact-linear residual with advection at $m=256$ NNLS nodes;
  the precomputed-tensor residual (0 sample points); the incumbent with $m=64$ gradient-learned
  nodes where a set existed.
- **Matched rung** — the cheapest (Newton tolerance, linear tolerance) pair of the standardised
  FOM ladder whose error is at least as small as the ROM's; the paired speedup is ROM time over
  that rung's time on the same GPU.
- **Positivity audit** — the numerical check that the truth is non-negative everywhere (the
  precondition for the tensor to equal the sign-upwind oracle), plus how far the *decoded*
  fields undershoot.
- **Head-PCA Tucker** — compressing the tensor by projecting the head output onto the few
  directions it spans; fails here because the $R=512$ head's outputs are not low-dimensional.
- **Gates STEP / ROLL** — bit-identity of the tensor driver's LM step and rollout against the
  incumbent solver functions (0.0 = the same code path).
- Other terms (bank, head, oracle, undershoot, launch floor, stop reasons) as in the 1D
  report's glossary.
