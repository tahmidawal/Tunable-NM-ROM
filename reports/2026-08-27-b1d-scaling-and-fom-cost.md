# 1D Burgers across resolutions: the node-learning result is scale-stable, and the honest cost story against the FOM

Follow-up to `2026-08-27-b1d-node-screening-and-poisson-qf.md`, user-directed:
extend the 1D screening to a resolution ladder $N = 128 \ldots 4096$ (32×),
measure how the ROM's online cost scales, and compare it to the FOM's cost.
Numbers are **final for these checkpoints, single seed** (jobs
2969373–2969381, all COMPLETED, branch `exp/2026-08-27-b1d-poissonqf`).
Tables are generated by `gen_2026-08-27-b1d-nodes-and-poisson-qf.py` (T-S*).
The cost instrument is new and cross-gated: an on-device LM rollout (gate V:
matches the screening's host-loop instrument to $6\times10^{-14}$), a
Gram-space IC fit (gate G: $10^{-15}$ vs the full-grid fit), and an analytic
tridiagonal FOM Newton (gates J/T2: $10^{-16}$ vs the dense Jacobian and the
dense-generator truth).

## Findings

**F1 — the accuracy result is resolution-stable across 32×.** Six for six
(T-S1): the decoder floor is flat (held recon $3.7\text{–}3.8\times10^{-3}$,
oracle rollout $4.5\text{–}6.2\times10^{-3}$); learned 32-node sets land on
the oracle at every $N$ (−0.6% to −8.4% vs same-budget NNLS); learned
16-node sets buy **−52% to −63% at every one of the six resolutions**. The
screening's conclusions were not a small-$N$ artifact.

**F2 — the ROM's online cost is flat in $N$, as the architecture promises.**
The latent solve (the per-step LM iteration over $K{=}8$ unknowns with the
32-node sampled advection and exact linear terms) costs 34–46 ms per
50-step trajectory with **no trend from $N{=}128$ to $N{=}4096$** (T-S2);
the $O(N)$ ends (IC projection ~13–20 ms, full decode <1 ms) are also flat
at these sizes. Note an honest deflation: even the *oracle* arm — whose
residual touches all $N{-}2$ grid points every iteration — is equally flat,
because at 1D sizes every kernel is launch-latency-bound on an A100. The
flatness is real and measured, but at these sizes it is not yet
*distinguishing*.

**F3 — there is no 1D wall-clock story against an honest FOM, at any
resolution measured.** The tolerance-terminated tridiagonal Newton FOM —
the $O(N)$ algorithm a real 1D code would use — solves a 50-step trajectory
in **8–9 ms** at every resolution except one (below), i.e. it is also
launch-bound and effectively flat through $N{=}4096$. The ROM end-to-end is
49–62 ms: **5–8× slower, and ~35× less accurate** ($5\times10^{-3}$ vs
$1.4\times10^{-4}$) (T-S3). No crossover exists in this range and none is
imminent: at 1D sizes the FOM's $O(N)$ growth has not even begun to bite.
This is the expected 1D analog of the 2D finding that the ROM's paired win
appears only at $1024^2$-scale meshes; the 1D testbed's value is
methodological (screening node-learning ideas cheaply), not speed.

**F4 — the $N{=}2048$ FOM row is an iteration-count anomaly, explained and
flagged.** At $N{=}2048$ the FOM needs ~4 Newton iterations per step (199
per rollout at tolerance $10^{-3}$) where every other resolution needs
exactly 1 (50), concentrated in the high-viscosity trajectories; its
per-iteration cost (0.16 ms) matches the neighbors, so this is Newton
convergence behavior near the upwind switching manifold at that particular
grid/front alignment, not a solver-speed effect. At the tight tolerance
$10^{-8}$, 6 of 8 trajectories **hit the 8-iteration cap** at $N{=}2048$
(worst step residual $1.3\times10^{-4}$) — that cell is capped, not
converged, and must not be quoted as a $10^{-8}$-tolerance cost. All other
cells converged (worst residuals at or under tolerance, recorded per
trajectory in the JSONs).

## What this supports, and what it does not

- **Supports:** the screening's node-learning conclusions at any practical
  1D resolution (scale-stable); the architectural claim that the ROM's
  online solve does not grow with the mesh (measured flat over 32×); the
  use of this 1D testbed as a cheap screening rig (a full six-arm
  experiment plus FOM baselines is ~2–4 min on one A100 at any $N$).
- **Does not support:** any 1D speed claim for the ROM. The FOM is
  strictly faster and strictly more accurate at every measured resolution.
  A speed story, if one exists, lives where it did in 2D — meshes large
  enough that the FOM's per-step cost dominates launch overhead — and 1D
  never gets there at these sizes.
- Single seed throughout; the per-mesh tight-budget percentages wobble at
  seed-noise scale (the −0.6% at $N{=}256$), while the 16-node −52..−63%
  band and the flat cost curves are far above it.

## Glossary — terms and columns, in plain language

- **Oracle / NNLS / learned nodes / m** — see the companion screening
  report's glossary; identical meanings. m=32 is the tight budget (= the
  number of weak test modes), m=16 the starved one.
- **IC fit** — solving for the latent vector that best matches the known
  initial condition; done here in a reduced (Gram) space so its
  per-iteration cost does not grow with the mesh, after one mesh-sized
  projection.
- **Latent solve** — the 50 implicit time steps in the 8-dimensional
  latent space; the part of the ROM whose cost should not depend on the
  mesh at all. **e2e** = IC fit + latent solve + decoding all 51 states.
- **Launch-bound** — at 1D problem sizes each GPU operation finishes
  faster than it takes to launch the next one, so wall time counts kernel
  launches, not arithmetic. It is why even mesh-sized operations look flat
  here.
- **Tridiagonal tolerance-Newton FOM** — the honest full-order baseline:
  Newton's method stopping when the step's relative residual reaches
  `ntol`, with the linear system solved by the O(N) tridiagonal algorithm
  (dense factorization would have inflated the FOM's cost and faked a ROM
  advantage).
- **Capped** — the solver used its maximum allowed Newton iterations
  without reaching the requested tolerance; the cell measures the cap, not
  the tolerance.
- **Gate V / G / J / T2** — new cross-checks for this study: the on-device
  rollout equals the screening's host-loop rollout; the Gram-space IC fit
  equals the full-grid fit; the analytic tridiagonal Jacobian equals
  automatic differentiation; the tridiagonal truth generator equals the
  dense one.

## Addendum (2026-08-28) — where the FOM's cost actually starts to grow

F3 above says the FOM is "effectively flat through $N{=}4096$"; a reader
(correctly) objected that a full-order solve must grow with resolution. It
does — linearly — and the ladder simply stopped inside the launch-latency
floor. Timing-only measurement of the same tridiagonal tolerance-Newton
instrument at larger $N$ (**local GB10, not the A100** — absolute times
~2.7× the A100's; the shape is the point; script and JSON in
`runs/b1dqf/fom_growth_local/` on the branch):

| grid points | FOM ms/traj (ntol 1e-3) | Newton/step |
|---|---|---|
| 510 | 22.2 | 1.0 |
| 4,094 | 25.5 | 1.0 |
| 16,382 | 29.8 | 1.0 |
| 65,534 | 36.1 | 1.0 |
| 262,142 | 67.6 | 1.0 |
| 1,048,574 | 207.0 | 1.0 |

The floor lasts to roughly 10–20k points, then the cost climbs ~linearly
(4× points → 3.1× time at the top). Read against the ROM's fixed ~22 ms
(A100, optimized), a 1D crossover would begin somewhere around
$10^5$–$10^6$ points — consistent with the 2D picture (win at $1024^2$,
loss at $256^2$). The correct wording for F3 is therefore "below the
latency floor in the measured range", not "flat".
