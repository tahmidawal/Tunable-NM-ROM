# Why the NS 2D reduced query is 30–120× slower than the FOM, and what would make it fast

Read-only investigation (2026-09-18) of lane `ns2d` at `9830d202`
(`worktrees/2026-09-17-ns2d/experiments/ns2d/`), job `ns304` (A100 80GB PCIe `pax105`, 3 timed
reps, medians). Numbers in the tables are generated by `cost_model.py` (beside this file, output
`tables.md`) from `artifacts/ns304/result.json` and the Burgers panel
`artifacts/bpn301/result.json` (job 3789570); the GPU structure checks are from
`profile_reduced.py` (`profile.json`, `profile.log`, local GB10, one run, ~1 minute). Nothing here
is a cluster result; the A100 constants are *fitted* to ns304's six rungs and checked out-of-sample
on the seven POD subjects.

**Status of the numbers:** the cost model is verified against the measured job; every per-fix time
below is a *prediction* from that model, not a measurement. The accuracy side of every fix is
unmeasured on NS.

---

## 0. The answer in five lines

1. **Dominant term:** every residual evaluation streams the whole dense advection tensor
   $Q\in\mathbb R^{M\times R\times R}$ (4.56 GB f64 at $M{=}2176$, $R{=}512$) once — 2.6 ms on the A100 —
   and `jax.jacfwd` streams it again with a batched $(R{\times}R)(R{\times}T)$ matmul that costs
   $0.08\,\mathrm{ms}\times T$ for $T{=}K{+}q$ unknowns. At ~7 residual passes and ~3 Jacobian passes
   per step (2.05 LM iterations/step) that is 27 ms/step at $q{=}0$ and 105 ms/step at $q{=}512$,
   ×500 steps. The residual is $M R^2$ **regardless of $q$** because the corrections live in
   $R$-space. The FOM residual is 3 FFTs on 65k points: the ROM residual has **73× the FLOPs and
   1450× the bytes** of the FOM residual, and the FOM takes 1 Newton/step at 0.84 ms.
2. **Three biggest levers** (multiplicative, at $q{=}0$): empirical quadrature (4.56 GB → ~0.19 GB per
   pass, ~25×), $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ instead of the fixed 2176 (34× less tensor at $q{=}0$; these two
   overlap — both attack the same pass cost), and dropping from ~7 residual + 3 Jacobian passes to
   1 + 2 per step (cap one LM iteration, no extrapolation acceptance test: ~2.4–3×). The analytic
   Jacobian (reuse `Q@cm`) is exact (parity $10^{-15}$) and cheap but buys nothing at $T{=}32$
   and 9× at $T{=}544$.
3. **Burgers is cheaper because of 50 vs 500 steps (10×), $M{=}64$ vs 2176 at $q{=}0$ (34×), and EQ
   rules of 1024–2560 nodes (its dense route is also 3–9× slower than its FOM).** NS adds *no*
   pressure and *no* second online field (vorticity form; $\Psi{=}-\Delta_h^{-1}G$ is folded into
   the tensor offline) and it is *not* iteration-heavier (2.05 vs Burgers' 3–4.5 LM it/step).
4. **Can any combination beat the 0.42 s FOM?** Marginally. The safe combination
   (certified EQ + $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ + analytic $J$ + 1–2 iterations at gtol $10^{-3}$, f64) predicts
   **0.26–0.5 s per query at $q{=}0$ (0.6–1.1× the FOM)** and ~0.4–1.0 s at $q{=}128$. The
   per-step launch-latency floor of this loop structure is 0.35 ms = 0.18 s/query, so
   **≤ 2.4× faster than the FOM is the ceiling at 256² with 500 steps**, at an error 4 orders of
   magnitude worse ($0.69$ vs $4\times10^{-5}$). No fix here changes the non-dominated set.
5. **Transferable in one job:** `q-ridge/eqcert.py` (GPU NNLS fitter, held-out-$\rho$ certifier,
   reachable-state collector) and b-eqtop's multi-draw protocol; the NS-specific glue is the
   18-array (ψ, ω × 9-point) node sampler and the Arakawa design rows (~150 lines).

## 1. Cost model of one reduced query, from the code

### 1.1 What one residual evaluation does (`ns2d_rom.py:210-217`)

```python
def weak(c, c_prev, nu):
    cm = 0.5 * (c + c_prev)
    qm = 0.5 * ((Q @ cm) @ cm)                       # = cm^T T cm        <- line 214
    am = A @ cm
    return (A @ c - A @ c_prev - dt * (qm - nu * lam * am)) / (1.0 + 0.5 * dt * nu * lam)
```

`Q` is the symmetrised tensor $(M,R,R)$; `Q @ cm` is a batched matvec over $M$: $M R^2$ MACs and one
full read of the tensor. With $M{=}2176$, $R{=}512$, f64: **4.56 GB per evaluation**
(`result.json: tensor.neural.bytes = 4563402752`). It is bandwidth-bound: 1.14 GFLOP is nothing,
4.56 GB at the A100's ~1.76 TB/s is **2.6 ms**.

The coefficient vector is `c = head_q(w) = D.head(params, w[:K]) + Cq @ w[K:]`
(`ns2d_phase3.py:341-342`), i.e. $c\in\mathbb R^R$ always. **The contraction is $M R^2$, not
$M(K{+}q)^2$, at every rung**, because the correction directions $C_q\in\mathbb R^{R\times q}$ and the
head output both live in bank-coefficient space; $q$ enters the residual cost nowhere. (The
`profile_reduced.py` residual at $q{=}0/128/512$: 6.09/6.14/7.37 ms on the GB10 — flat — and equal
to a bare `jnp.sum` pass over the same tensor, 6.15 ms.)

### 1.2 What the Jacobian does (`ns2d_rom.py:224-227`)

```python
def evaluate(z):
    r = fun(z, *args)
    J = jax.jacfwd(fun)(z, *args)
```

`jacfwd` pushes $T{=}K{+}q$ tangent columns through `Q @ cm`; JAX emits `Q @ dCm` with `dCm`
$(R,T)$: a batched $(R{\times}R)(R{\times}T)$ matmul over $M$, i.e. $2MR^2T$ FLOP, plus a re-read of the
tensor. Fitted on the A100: **0.0814 ms per tangent column = 14 TFLOP/s f64** (tensor-core DMMA;
peak 19.5). Crossover with the bandwidth floor at $T{=}32$, so every rung is compute-bound in its
Jacobian: 2.6 / 5.2 / 7.8 / 13 / 23.4 / 44.3 ms at $T{=}32/64/96/160/288/544$.

It never needs to be this: `Q` is symmetric in $(j,k)$ (`symmetrize`, line 197), so
$\partial q_m/\partial c = \tfrac12 (Q\,c_m)$ — the $(M,R)$ matrix `Q @ cm` the residual already
formed — and $J = \big[A - \Delta t(\tfrac12 X - \tfrac12\nu\lambda\odot A)\big]/(1{+}\tfrac12\Delta t\nu\lambda)\cdot \partial c/\partial w$
with $\partial c/\partial w = [\partial h_\theta/\partial z\ |\ C_q]$ an $(R,T)$ matrix from the tiny head.
One tensor pass gives $r$ *and* $J$; the extra cost is $MRT$ MACs. Verified in
`profile_reduced.py`: relative difference to `jacfwd` $5.5\times10^{-16}$, $8.6\times10^{-16}$,
$1.1\times10^{-15}$ at $T{=}32/160/544$; on the GB10 it costs 6.1/8.6/8.9 ms against jacfwd's
53/469/460 ms.

### 1.3 How many passes per step (`ns2d_rom.py:236-256, 303-311`)

Per time step, `step()` evaluates the residual at the previous iterate and at the linear
extrapolation `we = w + (w - wprev)` and keeps the better (lines 307-309: **2 residual passes**);
`step_lm` then evaluates $r$ and $J$ at the start (line 232: 1 residual + 1 Jacobian pass); each LM
iteration evaluates the trial residual (line 243) and, on acceptance, `evaluate(zn)` (line 245:
1 residual + 1 Jacobian). So

$$n_{\rm res} = 3 + 2\,{\rm it},\qquad n_{\rm jac} = 1 + {\rm it},$$

and the measured LM iterations per step (`iterations_total`/500) are 2.08/2.05/2.08/2.04/2.12 at
$q{=}0\ldots256$ and 1.08 at $q{=}512$ — every exit is reason 4 (relative gradient
$\le$ gtol $10^{-6}$), never the residual tolerance ($10^{-9}\|\omega_0\|$, `ns2d_rom.py:310`), never
the budget (200). POD-LSPG runs exactly 1.00 it/step.

The $(K{+}q)$-dimensional linear solve (`jnp.linalg.solve`, line 240, $\tfrac23 T^3$ FLOP: 22 kFLOP at
$T{=}32$, 107 MFLOP at $T{=}544$) is negligible at every rung — it is the one part of the query
that *is* $(K{+}q)$-sized.

(two constants, least-squares on the six neural rungs using each rung's *measured* iteration count).

### 1.5 Why POD-544 costs the same as $q{=}512$

Both run `jacfwd` with 544 tangent columns through an $(M,D,D)$ tensor with $D\approx512$: the
neural rung costs $2\cdot2176\cdot512^2\cdot544 = 6.2\times10^{11}$ FLOP per Jacobian, POD-544
$2\cdot2176\cdot544^3 = 7.0\times10^{11}$ (13 % more), and the neural rung runs 1.08 it/step against
POD's 1.00: 105 vs 108 ms/step. **The cost of a rung is set by $(M, D, T)$ — the tensor's bank
dimension and the number of unknowns — and the head is invisible to it.** At $q{=}R$ the neural rung
*is* an $R$-dimensional linear ROM with a redundant parametrisation, and it costs like one.

### 1.6 Why the FOM is 0.84 ms/step

`ns2d_fom.make_fom` (`ns2d_fom.py:110-183`): one Newton per step at ntol $10^{-3}$ (500 iterations
total), BiCGStab preconditioned by the *exact* FFT Helmholtz inverse (the linear part of the
implicit-midpoint operator), so only the advection part $\sim\mathrm{CFL}\lesssim0.5$ is left to
Krylov: 2–4 matvecs. One residual = one FFT Poisson solve (2 FFT2) + Arakawa stencil + Laplacian
$\approx 16$ MFLOP, ~3 MB. The whole step is ~15–20 FFT2's of 256² and a handful of stencils —
essentially at its own latency floor. **For a Fourier test space and a bank made of Fourier
features, the FFT pseudo-spectral evaluation *is* the optimally contracted bilinear form**; a
dense $(M,R,R)$ tensor throws that structure away and pays $MR^2$ for what costs $n\log n$.

## 2. Why the Burgers ROM is 10–50× cheaper at similar $R$

Burgers panel `bpn301` (same GPU class, $L{=}256$, $K{=}16$, $R{=}512$, **50 steps** of
$\Delta t{=}0.005$), per step:

| arm | $M$ | $m$ | $T$ | it/step | ms/query | ms/step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| q0 dense | 64 | – | 16 | 3.0 | 285 | 5.7 |
| q0 dense, $M{=}256$ | 256 | – | 16 | 3.0 | 341 | 6.8 |
| q128 dense | 576 | – | 144 | 3.0 | 1191 | 23.8 |
| q256 dense | 1088 | – | 272 | 4.5 | 3945 | 78.9 |
| q0 EQ (certified) | 64 | 1024 | 16 | 3.0 | 60 | 1.2 |
| q0 EQ, gtol $10^{-3}$ | 64 | 1024 | 16 | 2.0 | 44 | 0.87 |
| q0 EQ, `fast` L4 kernel | 64 | 1024 | 16 | 3.0 | 40 | 0.81 |
| q128 EQ | 576 | 2048 | 144 | 3.0 | 248 | 5.0 |
| q256 EQ | 1088 | 2048 | 272 | 4.5 | 697 | 13.9 |
| FOM ntol $10^{-3}$ | – | – | – | – | 32 | 0.64 |
| FOM `fft_tight` | – | – | – | – | 91 | 1.8 |

Reading against NS ($q{=}0$: 26.8 ms/step; $q{=}256$: 92.5 ms/step):

- **Steps: 50 vs 500 — 10×** of the per-query gap, before anything else.
- **Test space: $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ (64 at $q{=}0$) vs $M_{\rm FIXED}{=}2176$ at every rung.** The NS
  job fixed $M$ at $4(K{+}q_{\max})$ so all rungs share one tensor (§A9, `ns2d_phase3.py:322`);
  the pre-registered Phase-3 contract, never run, was $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ like Burgers. At $q{=}0$ that is
  34× more tensor per pass than needed. Per step at the *top* rung the two PDEs are already close
  (79 vs 92 ms; $M$ 1088 vs 2176, $T$ 272 vs 288) — and per LM iteration NS is only ~2× Burgers
  (30 vs 14 ms), which is the 2× in $M$.
- **Residual route.** Burgers' dense route (`arms.weak_dense`, `head-ablation/arms.py:264-269`)
  evaluates the nonlinearity on the full grid and projects with $\Phi^\top$: $n(R{+}M)$ per residual
  and $8n(R{+}M)$ bytes — 0.3 GB at $q{=}0$, 0.84 GB at $q{=}256$. NS's tensor route is 4.56 GB at
  every rung. Note the tensor route is *not* intrinsically worse: at $M{=}64$ it would be 134 MB per
  pass, cheaper than Burgers' full-grid route. It is $M_{\rm FIXED}$ that makes it 4.56 GB.
- **EQ.** Burgers' timed arms use certified rules of 1024–2560 nodes: `weak_eq` (lines 254-261)
  reads $8m(5R{+}M)$ bytes — 21 MB at $q{=}0$ — so it is latency-bound (0.8–1.2 ms/step). NS has no EQ
  at all (DESIGN: "there is no positivity restriction and no quadrature rule anywhere").
- **Iterations: NS is not iteration-heavier.** 2.05 LM it/step vs Burgers' 3.0–4.5; NS exits on
  gtol every step with zero budget exits. This hypothesis is refuted by the data.
- **Two components / pressure: NS adds nothing online.** The vorticity–streamfunction form has no
  pressure; $\psi$ comes from $\Psi = -\Delta_h^{-1}G$ precomputed into the tensor
  (`build_T_fft`, lines 141-194). The 24-term Arakawa bilinear form vs Burgers' 4-term one costs
  nothing once folded into $T$; it matters only for EQ sampling (18 sampled arrays vs 5).

So the honest decomposition of the ~50× per-query gap at $q{=}0$ (13.4 s vs 0.285 s dense): 10×
steps × ~4.7× per step, and the per-step factor is the $M$ (2176 vs 64) partially offset by NS's
fewer iterations. Against Burgers' *EQ* arm (0.044–0.060 s) the gap is 220–300×, of which EQ itself
is ~5–7×.

### F1 — analytic Jacobian (reuse `Q@cm`)

- **Gain:** none at $T{=}32$ (jacfwd sits exactly at the bandwidth crossover), 1.5× at $T{=}96$,
  2.2× at 160, 3.3× at 288, **9× at $T{=}544$** per Jacobian; per query 1.0/1.2/2.2/3.4/7.8× at
  $q{=}0/64/128/256/512$ with everything else unchanged.
- **Accuracy risk: none.** Same numbers to $10^{-15}$ (measured). ~15 lines in `make_lm`/`make_weak`.
- Also removes the $(M,R,T)$ intermediate (1.2 GB at $T{=}544$).

### F2 — $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ instead of $M_{\rm FIXED}{=}2176$

- **Gain:** tensor 4.56 GB → 0.27 GB at $q{=}0$ (17×), 1.34 GB at $q{=}128$ (3.4×); per query 13×
  at $q{=}0$ (to 1.03 s), 7× at $q{=}128$ (with F1).
- **Accuracy risk: moderate, unmeasured on NS.** This *is* the pre-registered NS contract and the
  Burgers convention (b-seeds: the scheduled-$M$ ladder is monotone on 3 seeds + sealed cohort);
  Burgers b-qxm found fixed-$M$ and scheduled-$M$ both pass. But fewer test modes = a weaker
  projection, and ns304 showed layer 3 (solve) sits 4–10× above layer 2 (manifold) already. The
  ns304 ladder's non-monotone first rung was measured at $M{=}2176$; at $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ the errors will
  differ and must be re-measured. Comparability across rungs is lost (that was the reason for
  $M_{\rm FIXED}$).

### F3 — pre-contract in $(K{+}q)$ coordinates: **not possible with nested corrections** (derivation)

With $c = h_\theta(z) + C_q y$,

$$T(c,c) = T(h,h) + 2\,T(h, C_q y) + T(C_q y, C_q y).$$

The last term pre-contracts to $(C_q^\top \cdot T \cdot C_q)\in\mathbb R^{M\times q\times q}$ ($Mq^2$ per
evaluation); the middle to $(T\cdot C_q)\in\mathbb R^{M\times R\times q}$ ($MRq$); the first is the full
$(M,R,R)$ tensor on $h_\theta(z)\in\mathbb R^R$, which is a generic point of $\mathbb R^R$ (the head's
output layer is $R$-wide, `ns2d_decoder.py:40`). Pre-contracting $T(h,h)$ needs $h\in\operatorname{span}(U)$
for a fixed $U\in\mathbb R^{R\times d}$, and then $c\in\operatorname{span}[U\,|\,C_q]$: the model becomes a
*linear* $(d{+}q)$-dimensional subspace ROM with a nonlinear reparametrisation inside it, whose
representation floor is the linear one and whose residual cost is $M(d{+}q)^2$ — exactly POD-LSPG at
$k'{=}d{+}q$, which is already in the job (0.21 s at 32 dims with error 0.55; 10 s at 288 with 0.095;
the bank floor 0.0023 needs $d{=}R$). **Conclusion:** the quadratic term of a nonlinear head in
$R$-space costs $MR^2$ per evaluation, full stop; only the Jacobian (F1) can be brought to $MRT$.
The way to get below $MR^2$ *exactly* is to keep the bank in Fourier space and evaluate by FFT —
which is the FOM. The way to get below it *approximately* is sampling (F4).

### F4 — empirical quadrature on NS

Sampled residual at $m$ nodes: $r_s(c) = J_A(\psi(c),\omega(c))(x_s)$ needs $\psi$ and $\omega$ at
$x_s$ and its 8 neighbours: precomputed $\Psi_S, G_S\in\mathbb R^{9\times m\times R}$ (gathered from the
grid bank and its FFT Poisson solve — both already computed in `build_T_fft`), then 24 bilinear
products and a weighted projection $P_q^\top r_s$ with $P_q\in\mathbb R^{m\times M}$. Bytes per pass:
$8m(18R + M)$ = 93 / 186 / 373 MB at $m{=}1024/2048/4096$ with $M{=}2176$ (vs 4.56 GB): **25× less
traffic at $m{=}2048$**; measured on the GB10: 0.98/1.35/2.35 ms per residual against 24 ms for the
dense route at the same $M$ (extrapolated from 6.1 ms at $M{=}544$). Its analytic Jacobian is
bilinear (two gathers), $\sim 24mR + mRT + MmT$ MACs — 0.05–0.3 ms on the A100.

- **Gain:** per query 2.0× at $M{=}2176$ with the current iteration policy (0.84 s), **1.0×** with
  1 it/step; with $M{=}4(K{+}q)$ and $m\sim512$ the query is latency-bound at ~0.26 s (0.6×).
- **Node count.** Burgers certified (held-out $\rho_{95}\le0.116$, q-ridge) at $m{=}1024$ for
  $M\le320$ ($m/M\ge3.2$), needed $m\approx2048$–2319 for $M{=}576$ and $m\approx2522$–2560 for
  $M{=}1088$ ($m/M\approx2.3$). By that ratio NS needs **$m\approx5000$–7000 for $M{=}2176$**,
  ~400–500 for $M{=}128$ ($q{=}0$), ~1500–2048 for $M{=}640$ ($q{=}128$). NS's reachable set is
  richer (14-dim family × 500 steps × LM iterates, a 24-term bilinear functional), so treat these as
  lower bounds; $m{=}4096$–8192 at $M{=}2176$ is the honest first sweep.
- **NNLS scaling.** Design = (fit states × $M$) rows × candidates: 64 × 2176 = 139k rows × 8192
  candidates = **9.1 GB f64** at $M{=}2176$ (Burgers' largest was 64 × 1088 = 70k × 8192 = 4.6 GB);
  at $M{=}128$ it is 8k rows — trivial. `eqcert.gpu_nnls` (projected gradient + exact drop-loop on
  the support) reaches $m{=}8192$ "inside a job"; scipy Lawson–Hanson took ~1000 s at $m{=}2048$
  (`checks/nnls-size.json`: 87 s for a 70k × 2048 fixed-support refit) and scales roughly
  rows × cols × $m$ — hours at $m{=}5000$. Use the GPU fitter.
- **Accuracy risk: real and already burned once.** q-diag/q-ridge: an EQ ladder regressed
  ($0.116\to0.185$ in $\rho$ with $q$ at fixed $m$) while its NNLS fit looked fine — **certify only by
  held-out $\rho$ on reachable states**, never by fit residual. On NS the rule error is judged
  against a ROM error of 0.06–0.7, so $\rho\sim0.1$ is tolerable in absolute terms; but the
  Burgers experience is that $\rho$ tracks the ladder's monotonicity, which ns304 already fails at
  the first rung.

### F5 — warm start across steps with an iteration cap

The code already warm-starts by linear extrapolation (`we = w + (w - wprev)`, line 306) but pays
**two residual passes per step to decide between `w` and `we`** and then 2.05 LM iterations because
gtol $10^{-6}$ is the binding exit. Passes per step $7.1 + 3.1$ → with a 1-iteration cap and no
acceptance test, $1 + 2$: **~2.4–3.3×** on the streaming-bound designs (13.5 → 4.1 s at $M{=}2176$;
1.03 → 0.46 s at $M{=}128$).

- **Accuracy risk: moderate.** One Gauss–Newton step per time step from an extrapolated start is
  a different solver; drift accumulates over 500 steps. Burgers evidence is encouraging: the
  gtol $10^{-3}$ EQ arms ran 2.0 vs 3.0 it/step with errors equal to 4 digits
  (q0: $4.552\times10^{-2}$ vs $4.553\times10^{-2}$; q128: $4.079$ vs $4.081\times10^{-2}$). The R-CONV
  gate (zero budget exits) and the "stationary" exit semantics would have to be restated. A
  cheaper variant with less risk: keep 2 iterations, drop only the extrapolation *test* (always
  start from `we`): $7.1{+}3.1\to5{+}3$ passes, 1.3×.

### F6 — mixed precision in the contraction

f32 tensor: 2.28 GB per pass (2×; GB10 measured 1.6×), TF32 `jacfwd` 8–16× — but after F1/F4 the
Jacobian is not the bottleneck and after F4 the residual is latency-bound, so **f32 buys ≤2× only on
the dense route and nothing on the recommended one.**

- **Accuracy risk: the stopping rule breaks.** The step tolerance $10^{-9}\|\omega_0\|$ and gtol
  $10^{-6}$ are below f32 rounding of a 2176-term sum; the LM would stagnate to *rejected* or
  *budget* (200 iterations) exits — i.e. **slower**, not faster — unless gtol $\ge10^{-4}$ and an
  iteration cap are set first. The error impact itself is negligible against 0.06–0.7. Not worth
  it on its own.

### F7 — fewer time steps

$\Delta t{=}2\times10^{-3}$ (500 steps) was chosen for the *FOM's* temporal convergence gate. Implicit
midpoint is A-stable; the ROM's temporal error at $\Delta t{=}10^{-2}$ (50 steps) would be
$\sim25\times$ the FOM's $4\times10^{-5}$ = $10^{-3}$, invisible under 0.06–0.7. **10× per query — but
the FOM takes the same 10× (Burgers: `nt1e-3_dt01` 20 ms vs `dt005` 32 ms; only 1.6× for 2× fewer
steps because larger steps cost more BiCGStab iterations), so the ratio does not move** unless the
FOM is denied the larger step, which is not a fair same-job comparison. Larger ROM steps also make
the extrapolated warm start worse (more LM iterations). Neutral for the claim.

## 4. Recommended combination and its expected time

**Safe set:** F1 (exact, free) + F2 ($M{=}4(K{+}q)$, the pre-registered contract) + F4 (certified EQ,
$m$ swept, held-out $\rho$) + the mild form of F5 (2 iterations, gtol $10^{-3}$, no extrapolation
test), f64 throughout, 500 steps.

| rung | tensor / rule | predicted s/query | vs FOM 0.42 s | as measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $q{=}0$, $M{=}128$ | EQ $m{\approx}512$–1024 | **0.26–0.48** | 0.6–1.1× | 13.4 s |
| $q{=}128$, $M{=}640$ | EQ $m{\approx}2048$ | **0.42–1.0** | 1.0–2.3× | 29.0 s |
| $q{=}512$, $M{=}2176$ | EQ $m{\approx}5000$+ | ~1–1.5 | 2.5–3.5× | 52.6 s |

With b-panel's `fast.py`-style kernel work (fusion, unrolled inner loop, closed-form small solve —
1.5× on Burgers, 60 → 40 ms) the $q{=}0$ rung could reach ~0.2 s. **The floor is 0.18 s (latency), so
the best case at 256² and 500 steps is ~2× faster than the FOM at ntol $10^{-3}$, with an error of
0.69 against $4\times10^{-5}$** — 30–120× slower becomes 0.5–2× faster, and the neural rung is still
nowhere near the (ms, error) frontier, which the FOM ladder and POD-32 own. A >10× speedup for this
model family needs a FOM that is far from its latency floor (1024² or 3D, where the EQ-ROM's cost is
mesh-independent and the FFT FOM is not) — and Burgers at 1024² measured only 1.82× (H200) on that
route, so that is not a promise either.

**Not recommended:** F3 (impossible with a nonlinear head — see derivation), F6 alone (breaks the
stopping rule, no gain after F4), F7 as a ROM-only lever.

## 6. Caveats

- The GB10 profile validates *structure* (pass counts, $q$-independence, jacfwd vs analytic,
  sampled vs dense), not A100 numbers: the GB10's f64 matmul is ~100× slower than the A100's tensor
  cores and its kernel latency is 2–3× higher, so its jacfwd ratios overstate F1's gain.
- The A100 constants $a$, $c$ are a 2-parameter fit to 6 points; they reproduce the 7 POD subjects
  out-of-sample to 5–11 % at large $k$. The latency floor is inferred from one subject (POD-32).
- The EQ node counts for NS are extrapolated from Burgers' $m/M$; the sampled-residual timings in
  `profile_reduced.py` use random operands of the right shapes.
- None of this changes the lane's verdict: the $K{=}32$ head fails H-ORACLE, and every neural rung is
  dominated by POD-LSPG on error. Making it 30× faster makes it *cheap and wrong* rather than
  *slow and wrong*.

## Glossary

- **Reduced query**: one ROM solve from a dense $\omega_0$ to 26 dense output fields over 500 steps.
- **$K, q, R, M, m, T$**: latent dimension of the head; correction rank; bank size (columns of $G$);
  number of Fourier test modes; number of quadrature nodes; $T{=}K{+}q$ unknowns per step.
- **Tensor route**: precomputing $T_{mjk} = \sum_x\phi_m J_A(\psi_j,g_k)$ so the projected advection
  is $c^\top T c$ — exact, but $MR^2$ per evaluation.
- **Pass**: one full read of the tensor from GPU memory (bandwidth-bound).
- **`jacfwd`**: JAX forward-mode Jacobian: one tangent column per unknown, pushed through the residual.
- **LM**: Levenberg–Marquardt, the damped Gauss–Newton loop solving each implicit step; "it/step" =
  its iterations per time step; exit reason 4 = relative gradient below gtol.
- **Extrapolation test**: evaluating the residual at both the previous solution and its linear
  extrapolation to pick the starting iterate.
- **EQ / hyper-reduction**: replacing the full-grid (or full-tensor) nonlinear term by a weighted sum
  over $m$ sampled nodes, weights fitted by NNLS on training states.
- **NNLS rel-fit vs $\rho$**: the rule's fit residual on its own snapshots vs its true relative
  error on held-out states the solver actually visits; only $\rho$ certifies.
- **POD-LSPG**: the linear control — POD basis of the snapshots, same weak residual, identity head.
- **FOM ntol**: full-order Newton stopping tolerance (relative residual); $10^{-3}$ is the cheapest
  rung that still reproduces the converged solution to $4\times10^{-5}$.
- **Latency floor**: time per step spent launching kernels and running the loop machinery,
  independent of problem size.
- **H-ORACLE**: the Phase-2 gate the head failed (must beat POD-$K$ 2× on held-out states).
